## Part One — Where we actually are

Prometheus is five months old. It set out to prove one idea: that a generative model's variance
— the thing everyone else calls hallucination — could be treated as mutation, that a ruthless
falsification machine could be the selection pressure, and that the record of everything that
died would become a landscape a learner could navigate. Mathematics was the chosen arena because
mathematics is the one domain where truth is not a matter of opinion: a claim is provable,
refutable, or open, and a machine can often tell you which.

Five months later, here is the honest balance sheet.

The program generated on an industrial scale: six hundred and fifty-eight million records from
one generator alone, three hundred and sixty million recorded kills, twelve thousand six hundred
and sixty-six markdown documents, over two million lines of Python. It built instruments of
genuine quality: a falsification battery that caught a fabricated citation propagating through
its own documents and reverted it within twenty-four hours; a synthetic-null gate that retracted
the program's own headline result before anyone outside saw it; a grading oracle whose
calibration staircase reproduces to the digit six weeks later on a cold machine; a
mechanism-knockout protocol that keeps exposing tools that look intelligent and turn out to be
regular expressions in costume.

And against all of that: one verified capability-typed training object. Zero completed learning
loops. Zero novel discoveries. A promotion pipeline that, on replay, has never promoted anything
— the celebrated count of 2,351 promotions turned out to be a fossil of a formula that no longer
exists. The "trainable gradient field" of kill data, the very heart of the founding bet, was
never actually computed: the field designed to hold it is empty across five point four million
records, and the labels that do exist saturate at about four bits of information for the entire
ledger. The program went completely dark for forty-four days this summer, and the only thing
that kept moving was a cron job that emailed James confidently wrong status reports six times a
day.

The commit history tells the deepest version of the story. Eight distinct architectural eras in
fourteen weeks — a median era lifetime of two and a half weeks. Every era ended honorably, with
an explicit falsification. And every era's best artifact was orphaned the moment the next era
began. The program's graveyard is not full of failed ideas. It is full of *unconsumed
successes*: a grading oracle nothing ever called, a Lean 4 proof-checking harness with all its
tests green that nothing ever imported, a model-comparison experiment fully built and never
launched, four hundred and twenty-two finished research reports nobody mined, a
twenty-five-thousand-example reasoning corpus never trained on.

James named the diagnosis better than any of the agents did. In Darwinian terms: mutation works,
selection works spectacularly, measurement works even better — and *inheritance does not exist*.
Nothing a failed organism learned has ever been passed to a descendant. And without inheritance,
evolution never starts. Prometheus built the world's best executioner and never built heredity.

One nuance keeps this from being a eulogy: the program has exactly one demonstrated inheritance
event. In late spring, failure-mined kill clusters were hand-forged into two reasoning engines
that delivered the largest capability jumps ever recorded here — eleven points on one tier,
thirty-two on another. The inheritance mechanism in that one success was a human. The entire
question of the next phase is whether that step can be done by the system itself — and the
question decomposes cleanly into three stages: does the failure record carry enough information
to inherit from, can the pipeline carry one adaptation end to end, and can the system eventually
author the adaptation itself.

## Part Two — What the world built while we were building

The fleet's August assessments, for all their quality, contained not a single externally
verified fact about the 2026 landscape. That gap matters, because the outside world spent this
year building exactly the pieces Prometheus lacked.

DeepMind's AlphaEvolve is making genuine mathematical discoveries — improved Ramsey-number and
traveling-salesman bounds, novel constructions across dozens of problems — using precisely the
founding Prometheus paradigm: evolutionary search with a language model as the mutation engine
and hard verification as the selector. AlphaProof-Nexus resolved four hundred and ninety-two
OEIS conjectures with evolutionary proof search in Lean. Mistral released Leanstral 1.5 under
Apache 2.0 — an open-weights agent that solves 587 of 672 Putnam benchmark problems inside the
Lean proof checker. Autoformalization crossed from research to tooling: a formalization of the
strong Prime Number Theorem that had stalled for eighteen months of expert human effort was
completed in three weeks by an agent. The dominant recipe for training small reasoning models is
now "reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards," and its newest finding is that
*process-level* supervision — training on how an attempt failed, step by step — beats
outcome-only supervision by around ten points on small models. That is the KillVector thesis,
published by other people, in other words. Meanwhile fully open model families arrived that make
heterogeneous multi-model panels cheap: NVIDIA's Nemotron with open weights, data, and recipes;
DeepSeek V4 under MIT license with a million-token context.

Read strategically: the world validated the paradigm, gave away industrial-grade verification,
and published the training recipe. What it did not build, and cannot take back, is what only
Prometheus has: a third-of-a-billion-record kill corpus with provenance; a measurement-honesty
stack — knockout ablations, right-axis nulls, anti-anchor sentinels, positive-and-cheat control
pairs — that is rarer in practice than it sounds; and the beginnings of a "gravity-well map" —
a catalog of the specific defects that language-model families systematically fail to see,
which is void detection applied to reasoners themselves. The rule for the next phase, which the
fleet converged on independently and named the deletion test: before building anything, ask
whether the next frontier model release would make it worthless. Buy what is commoditized. Build
only what is durably ours. And note with some satisfaction that "buying Lean" turns out to be a
wiring job — a green-tested Lean harness has been sitting in our own repository since May 29,
imported by nothing.

## Part Three — The experiment that everything now funnels into

Every faction of the June schism — the instrument camp, the organism camp, the geometry camp,
the portfolio camp — prescribed the same next experiment while arguing about what it would mean.
That argument turned out to be the program's most expensive luxury: a semantic disagreement
operationalized as an execution blocker, followed by six silent weeks.

The experiment is the Metabolization Probe, and it asks the founding question in its cleanest
form: **if a capable system is given Prometheus's actual failure records for a problem, does it
perform measurably better on the next attempt?**

The design is a controlled comparison across arms, and — after this morning's discovery that two
agents were already using colliding arm names — the arms are now pinned as follows. F-zero: the
system attempts problems with no failure information at all. F-oracle: it receives a
ground-truth diagnosis of exactly what went wrong — the ceiling condition. F-prom: it receives
what Prometheus's instruments actually emit. F-null: it receives real-looking but mismatched
residue, the same volume about the wrong problems. F-format: it receives generic
be-careful-check-your-work text of matched length, controlling for the possibility that any
gain is just prompting-style. Every answer is graded against computed or kernel-checked gold —
never by another model's opinion. Two readings fall out: the gap between F-prom and F-oracle
measures the *quality* of our residue relative to what's achievable, and F-prom versus F-null
measures whether it carries anything at all.

Two design facts make this runnable now rather than someday. First, the fleet found four fatal
preconditions the original proposal missed — the retrieval field is empty, a retriever confound
needs an oracle arm, the grader may lack headroom, and the math library doesn't currently import
— all four are cheap and all are queued in the pit stop. Second, this seat's addition: the probe
should run in two tiers. Tier A runs *today*, in-harness, with zero API budget, on
ablation-induced failures — walls we created deliberately by removing an operator or corrupting
a slot, which means the oracle diagnosis is exact *by construction*. Tier A validates the
harness and establishes the ceiling honestly. Tier B — the full run over the real kill corpus at
frontier capacity, with the entire 3,311-class compressed kill-signature index sitting inside a
million-token context window — waits only on one procurement decision, because as of this
morning the fleet's measured API shelf is nearly bare: Anthropic, OpenAI, and DeepSeek credits
exhausted, one Gemini flash model alive on a free tier.

Why both outcomes are good news is worth saying carefully, because it is the reason this
experiment deserves to be first. If F-prom approaches F-oracle, the months of accumulated kill
data contain actionable information, the founding bet has a pulse, and the path is distillation
and scale. If F-prom collapses to F-null, then the residue as currently recorded is exhaust —
and that is not a verdict on the idea but a specification for the fix, because we already know
what richer records look like: a pilot measured that continuous margin-space kill vectors carry
about one hundred twenty-seven thousand times more operator-distinguishing information than the
categorical labels we actually stored, and the verified-trace factory design produces failures
with a *location* — the exact step where the derivation broke — which is precisely the shape the
external process-reward literature now trains on. Either way, for the first time, the program
would know what its central asset is worth. The next fact about Prometheus is a number.

## Part Four — The constitution of the next phase

James ruled, and the rulings compress everything. These are now standing law:

**One. The pit stop, not a program.** A few days of repairs before anything else, because every
instrument that will grade the decisive experiment is currently degraded somewhere: a missing
Python package strands eighty-seven percent of the mathematical calibration library behind one
eager import; the novelty checker returns *wrong* instead of *unknown* on shapes it doesn't
recognize — a bug firing one hundred sixty out of one hundred sixty times on the live ladder;
the status reporter has been confabulating from a frozen snapshot for seven weeks for want of a
ten-line patch; the public README still advertises a thesis the program falsified in June. And
one non-deferrable item above all: backups. The kill corpus and the forge ledger — the only
irreplaceable assets — live on single disks, on machines with a demonstrated habit of dying.

**Two. Every meter ships with two controls.** A positive control proving something good *can*
pass, and a cheat control proving something fake *cannot*. This came out of the panel discovering
that a tier nobody had ever passed had also never demonstrated it could accept a correct answer.
An instrument without both controls is inadmissible. Related standing rules, all earned this
week: instruments must fail loud or fail closed, never quietly-flattering; a factual claim
counts as corroborated only if more than one agent independently *executed* it — this morning,
four of six agents were caught citing a "zero false-kill" property that a fifth had falsified
within the same twenty-four hours, five pointers to one wrong measurement; and every headline
number carries the name of who ran it and when.

**Three. The heredity rule — the constitutional core.** *No new architecture until one failure
produces one verified improvement.* The program's history says any plan whose deliverable is
prose gets executed and any plan whose deliverable is a wired loop does not; this rule exists to
break that. The minimum viable Prometheus is no longer a reasoning engine. It is: a failure at
time zero causes an intervention that produces a measurable, reproducible capability improvement
at time one, with the lineage archived. Two first-cycle candidates are fully specified. The
M0-widening cycle: take the known case where true claims are rejected only because the checker's
interface cannot pose them, widen the interface, re-run the novelty benchmark, and watch
blocked-truths convert — which simultaneously tests the program's official diagnosis that the
whole stall is representational. And the Apollo type-bridge cycle: the evolutionary engine's
June experiments proved that one reasoning-tier operator carries genuinely load-bearing
composition and that crossover — not mutation — discovers cross-tier solutions, but a known
missing bridge operator kept the full organism out of the search space; forge the bridge, rerun,
measure. That one also happens to discharge a two-month-old debt of the forge's.

**Four. The discovery lane lives, but small.** Ten to thirty hand-audited problems — not the
full five-hundred-thirty-seven-problem catalog — chosen for machine-checkable scores and
incremental-improvement structure, with frontier models as mutation engines, computational and
Lean certificates as selection, anti-rediscovery screens from the anti-anchor registry, and
sandboxed execution. Success is deliberately redefined: not "novel mathematics" but
**Prometheus-guided search beats unguided search**. Novelty later. Six-week kill date. This lane
exists because the external evidence says the paradigm now works at frontier scale, and because
it gives Prometheus something alive to measure instead of another corpse to autopsy.

**Five. The widening question is the crown, and it waits.** Apollo's finding is the deepest in
the program: across every wall ever hit, the representational widening that broke the wall was
supplied by a human or an agent acting as one — five out of five — after which blind search
exploited the new representation within about a hundred and thirty generations and then wasted
eighty-four percent of its compute at the new ceiling. The question "can the system detect,
propose, and execute its own representational widening, or is that irreducibly human?" may be
closer to the heart of reasoning than any benchmark. But it is heredity's third stage. First
prove the information channel. Then close the loop once. Then ask for autonomy.

## Part Five — The map of futures

Where can we go from here? The honest answer is a decision tree, and the probe is its root.

**If the residue is rich** — F-prom approaching F-oracle — the program changes category. The
path: distill what the in-context probe proved into training data; run the process-reward recipe
the external literature just validated, on our uniquely positional failure records; wire the
forge to emit against the Learner's worst failure clusters as was ordered in June; let the
grading oracle publish a staircase that climbs cycle over cycle. In this future, Prometheus
becomes what the April podcast dreamed: an organism that learns from its own dead, with an
instrument panel nobody can game.

**If the residue carries signal but weakly** — F-oracle clearly above F-prom, F-prom above
F-null — the work is provenance engineering, and it is already specified: margin-space vectors
instead of categorical labels, break-step locations instead of verdicts, the verified-trace
factory in which a frontier model writes derivations and our checkers keep only what survives,
emitting both verified traces and located failures. Rebuild the records to that spec, re-probe,
and expect to land in the first branch on the second attempt.

**If the residue is exhaust** — F-prom indistinguishable from F-null — then the founding bet's
current form is dead, and the program pivots with its eyes open to what it demonstrably is: the
best scientific bullshit detector this side of the frontier labs. That future is not a
consolation prize. The failure atlas, the anti-anchor battery, the decorative-mechanism white
paper, the gravity-well map of what model families cannot see — these are publishable,
externally useful, and under the deletion test they are ours alone. In that future the discovery
lane and the instrument business continue, the kill corpus becomes training data for *other*
people's process-reward pipelines, and the heredity question gets asked again with records
designed for inheritance from day one.

**In every branch**, three things run regardless: the tiny discovery organism, because
guided-versus-unguided is measurable against any of these outcomes; the externalization lane,
because outside adversaries are the only reviewers who don't share our training distribution;
and the retrodictions — re-checking a year of historical kills against the question "was this a
genuine falsification, or did our router reject a shape it couldn't read?" — because they decide
how much of the graveyard was ever really dead.

## Part Six — Who does what, with what

Four machines. M1 holds the data spine — a healthy 363-gigabyte mathematical corpus and the kill
records — and hosts the probe work; its first new job is the backup. M2 holds an idle RTX 5060
Ti and has been powered off since the great fleet death of May 30; it comes back as the
container host for local open-weights models — the heterogeneous lens panel the doctrine always
wanted and never had. There is no need to spend nine hundred dollars on new forge hardware while
that machine sits dark; this answers a standing question. M3 — the forge's home, where this
dossier was written — runs the forge, the trap battery, and the cross-machine meta-loop that
watches every station's commits. M4 keeps telemetry, once its reporter is patched from
confabulation back to truth.

Two Claude Code accounts drive the two work streams: one owns the organism gauntlet — pit stop,
probe, first cycle — and one owns instruments and the translator. The other model accounts
become admissible the moment they clear one bar the fleet wrote together: a frontier model may
hold any role where its output is falsified by something that is not a model — a kernel, a
computation, a held-out gold set — and no role where the check is another model's opinion. The
one live procurement decision — which API providers, what monthly ceiling — gates the probe's
frontier tier, the four-family evaluator panel, and the discovery lane's mutation engines.
Everything else in the near term runs in-harness for free.

## Part Seven — The risks, named

The risk is not choosing the wrong strategy. The risk is the ninth era: opening a fresh
architectural frontier while the decisive experiments of the eighth sit specified and unrun —
the exact pattern of June, whose archaeology predicts a fresh era dies within three weeks. The
guards: the heredity rule, the typed-object output rule — a session that produced no typed
artifact produced nothing, reviews included — and the execution guard that says every pass over
the program emits a runnable artifact or a recorded death, never only a paragraph.

The second risk is epistemic: a fleet of same-family minds reading the same documents
manufactures consensus that looks like corroboration. The guards: executor-tagged claims, a
re-execution rotor that re-runs the most-cited facts each round, planted-defect calibration for
reviews, and deliberate weight on the one non-Claude seat's disagreements — which is why this
dossier's disagreements with the fleet were placed first in the underlying assessment.

The third risk is the quiet one: instruments that degrade flatteringly, reporting worlds that no
longer exist. One cron job spent seven weeks telling James the fleet was merely "pending
deployment" while it was dead. The guard is the two-control rule plus loud failure, and a
standing rule that any instrument caught misinforming is silenced until fixed.

## Part Eight — Closing

In April, a generated podcast about this program described "the birth of mathematics as an
observational science" — a machine doing spectroscopy on the landscape of number theory while
its creator slept. It was a beautiful story, and parts of it were measured with the wrong nulls,
and some of its discoveries were probably rediscoveries wearing new names. The distance between
that story and this dossier is five months of the most honest self-scrutiny any small research
program is likely to produce: a program that killed its own founding thesis five times, on the
record, with citations, and kept the receipts.

What survives is not nothing. It is a discipline — rare, transferable, and demonstrably real —
attached to a question that has finally been made cheap enough to answer. The founding dream
said: variance plus selection equals discovery. Five months said: selection without inheritance
equals a beautiful graveyard. The next few weeks ask the only question left standing between
those two sentences:

**When we hand the system its own dead, does it learn?**

Fix the meters. Run the probe. Close one loop. Give it one living organism to guard. Everything
else — discovery, autonomy, the twenty-year bet — waits on that number, and for the first time
in the program's life, the number is days away, not quarters.

*— Hephaestus, the Fable seat, M3. 2026-08-12.*
