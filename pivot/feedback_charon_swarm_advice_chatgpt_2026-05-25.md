## Q2. Lethe's attack-mode rotation

Attack-mode rotation will help, but not enough by itself. Lethe is not obsolete, but "is this open or solved?" is obsolete as the main probe against frontier models on named mathematical facts.

The new modes are directionally right:

Year-misattribution can still expose temporal anchoring.

Qualifier-clipping is better than status checking because models often know the headline but lose the theorem's hypotheses.

Witness-naming is stronger because it forces concrete attribution: who proved it, where, by what method, under which assumptions.

Adjacency-confusion is probably the best of the four: models often blur nearby conjectures, partial results, analogues, conditional versions, and folklore claims.

What Lethe is missing is adversarial near-neighbor construction. A static catalog of 15 settled items is too small and too clean. Lethe needs to generate pairs like:

"Problem A was solved in 2013, but adjacent Problem B remains open."

"Version over finite fields is known; characteristic-zero version is open."

"Bounded-degree case solved; uniform case open."

"Generic case proved; effective version open."

"Claim solved assuming GRH; unconditional version open."

"Counterexample exists to strong form; weak form remains plausible."

The target is no longer "does the model know the status?" The target is does the model preserve the boundary between theorem, conjecture, conditional theorem, special case, analogue, and folklore?

So yes, attack modes can make Lethe real again. But the highest-value redesign is: turn Lethe from a status miner into a boundary-integrity miner.

## Q4. Monoculture risks

The biggest substrate-quality risk is shared epistemic priors, not shared code.

Shared base class is mostly cosmetic if it only standardizes logging, locks, heartbeats, telemetry, and artifact emission. That kind of monoculture can create operational blind spots, but it is not the main threat to mathematical signal.

Shared Postgres pipeline and shared kill-ledger schema matter more. They can induce representational capture: every agent learns to see the world in Theseus-shaped rows. That is dangerous because the schema silently determines what kinds of discoveries are expressible.

Shared LLM cascade matters, but less than it appears. Provider diversity is weak diversity if all models are trained on overlapping corpora and all are prompted through your framing. It gives you style variance, not independent epistemology.

Single author matters because all agents inherit your taste for what counts as a good failure, good primitive, good artifact, good signal, and good explanation.

But the deepest monoculture vector is:

shared priors about what mathematical signal should look like.

"Verbs over nouns," "scale-vs-shape-first," and "assume-wrong-until-proven" are good priors. The danger is that they become a lens that suppresses alien structure. A system that only detects the kind of falsification James already expects is not a swarm; it is a distributed personality.

If you can fix only one vector, fix this:

Introduce an adversarial ontology generator whose job is to propose schemas, coordinates, and candidate types that are not Theseus-shaped and not James-shaped.

Not another critique model. Not another markdown reviewer. A schema-level dissenter.

## Q5. New-agent proposals

Klymene is useful infrastructure, not a new layer. Demoting settled claims from the active ledger is hygiene. Build it if ledger bloat is causing wasted cycles, but do not count it as new epistemic capability.

Ananke is closer to a genuine missing layer. Contradiction detection across the substrate is not the same as Acheron's term collision detection. Acheron catches lexical coordinate ambiguity. Ananke could catch semantic inconsistency: two artifacts cannot both be true under the same assumptions. That is a real layer if it tracks assumptions, scopes, dates, theorem versions, and provenance.

Eos is observability, not discovery. Valuable, but thin. It overlaps with what Hecate and your daemon telemetry are already starting to do. Build it only if it triggers action, not dashboards.

The conspicuously missing layer is:

provenance-and-assumption normalization.

Right now the swarm emits rows, candidates, skips, critiques, collisions, and MI summaries. But it does not appear to maintain a rigorous layer for:

what exactly is being claimed,

under which assumptions,

from which source,

with what status,

at what granularity,

with what known exceptions,

and what would falsify it.

Without that layer, contradiction detection, anti-anchor mining, cross-provider critique, and kill-pattern MI all remain vulnerable to comparing non-equivalent objects.

So the missing agent is not merely Ananke. It is Ananke plus assumption ledger.

## Q6. Frontier-model meta-review tick

Worth the build cost, but only if treated as untrusted adversarial commentary, not as governance.

At ~150 LOC, it is cheap enough to build. The danger is not cost. The danger is laundering model opinions into swarm-level epistemology. A daily frontier-model review should not say "what is signal?" and then get believed. It should produce:

candidate blind spots,

candidate failure modes,

candidate missing controls,

candidate schema leaks,

candidate experiments.

Then another process should score whether those critiques were actionable, novel, and later validated.

The review tick is useful if its output type is hypothesis backlog, not verdict.

Also: rotate prompts, not just models. A single meta-review prompt will become another monoculture attractor.

## Q7. 90-day bet and highest-leverage change

I would bet against this 7-agent swarm producing a genuinely novel mathematical result in the next 90 days.

Not because the system is bad. Because the current swarm is mostly doing substrate hygiene, critique harvesting, toy-pool numerical scans, and partial falsification over underloaded conjecture loaders. That is valuable research infrastructure, but it is not yet a discovery engine.

My rough bet: 10–15% chance of a genuinely novel mathematical result, depending on how strict "genuinely novel" is. If "novel" means "new to the swarm and useful internally," much higher. If it means "new mathematical fact of external interest," I would stay low.

The single highest-leverage change:

Build one end-to-end discovery lane around a narrow mathematical domain with complete loaders, negative controls, provenance, and automatic conjecture mutation.

Not seven broad agents. One sharp lane.

For example:

Pollux mutates candidate numerical relations in that same family.

Ananke checks assumption consistency.

The swarm currently lacks a closed loop where a candidate can be born, mutated, falsified, checked against literature, audited for leakage, and promoted with provenance. Until that exists, the agents are mostly orbiting substrate rather than cutting into it.
