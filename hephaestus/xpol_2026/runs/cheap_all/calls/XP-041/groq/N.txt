**1. Emerging computational mechanism**  
The synthesis yields a **Functor‑Based Incentive‑Compatible Experimentation Engine (FICE)**.  In FICE a *structural causal model* (SCM) is treated as an object in a category **Caus**, with arrows representing admissible interventions (do‑operators).  A *mechanism* (e.g., a Vickrey‑Clarke‑Groves (VCG) auction) is a functor **M : Caus → Pay**, mapping each intervention to a payment rule that guarantees incentive‑compatibility for self‑interested “experimenter agents” (which may be sub‑modules of the reasoning system).  Natural transformations between functors encode *policy updates*: the system can replace one experiment‑selection mechanism with another while preserving the same causal semantics.  The Kleisli category of the *intervention monad* formalises sequential do‑calculi, allowing compositional planning of multi‑step experiments.

**2. Advantage for a self‑testing reasoning system**  
FICE lets the system *auction* its own experimental resources (e.g., simulation time, data queries) to internal hypothesis‑generation agents.  Because the VCG‑style payment rule aligns each agent’s utility with the *expected causal information gain* (quantified by do‑calculus‑derived Shannon‑information metrics), the system automatically selects interventions that are both maximally informative and robust to strategic manipulation of data sources.  This yields a metacognitive loop where hypothesis testing is governed by provably optimal, incentive‑compatible experiment design rather than ad‑hoc heuristics.

**3. Novelty assessment**  
Elements exist in isolation: categorical treatments of SCMs, causal mechanism design, and incentive‑compatible experiment selection.  However, a full functorial pipeline that couples *do‑calculus*, *VCG‑style payment*, and *category‑theoretic composition* into a single executable architecture has not been formalised as a distinct field.  Thus the combination is **largely novel**, though it builds on scattered literature in “causal mechanism design” and “categorical probability”.
