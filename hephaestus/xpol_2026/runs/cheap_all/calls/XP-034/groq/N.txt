The network maintains an internal “hypothesis bank” – a set of parametric generative models that predict future observations. After each prediction cycle the residual error signal is transformed with a fast Fourier transform; a periodogram (Welch’s method) provides a power‑spectral density (PSD).  

A Popperian falsification loop evaluates each hypothesis by testing whether its residual‑error PSD deviates significantly from the null distribution expected under that hypothesis (e.g., using a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test on the cumulative PSD or a likelihood‑ratio test on fitted 1/f^α spectra). Hypotheses whose error spectra show excess low‑frequency power (signalling long‑range correlations) are flagged for rejection, while those whose PSD matches the critical‑state baseline (α≈1) are retained and refined. The critical dynamics guarantee that the system’s internal sensitivity (susceptibility) is maximal, so even tiny systematic mismatches generate measurable spectral signatures.

**Advantage for a self‑testing reasoner**

Because the engine operates at criticality, it amplifies minute, structured prediction failures into macroscopic spectral anomalies. The spectral test isolates the *shape* of the error—not just its magnitude—allowing the system to falsify hypotheses that are otherwise indistinguishable by scalar loss functions. This yields a principled, quantitative falsification criterion that scales with the complexity of the data stream and automatically focuses computational effort on the most informative frequency bands.
