**Computational mechanism** – *Chaotic Spectral Type‑Driven Proof Search (CST‑PFS)*.  
A CST‑PFS engine treats each proof‑search state as a high‑dimensional vector and stores it as a complex‑valued *spectral embedding* (via a short‑time Fourier transform of the term‑tree’s depth‑first traversal). The embedding is typed by a dependent‑type system that indexes the vector with *spectral invariants* (e.g., dominant frequencies, power‑law exponents). A *chaotic iterator*—a discrete‑time dynamical system such as a logistic‑map‑based sampler—perturbs the embedding according to its Lyapunov exponent, guaranteeing exhaustive, yet highly diverse, exploration of the proof space. The search algorithm (e.g., a Monte‑Carlo Tree Search) is constrained by the type‑checker: any candidate transition whose spectral signature violates the dependent‑type constraints is rejected at compile‑time, while admissible moves are those whose *spectral distance* (measured by a periodogram‑based kernel) stays within a Lyapunov‑controlled bound. The resulting architecture is an *autonomous, type‑safe, chaotic explorer* that can be run inside a proof assistant such as Coq or Agda.

**Advantage for self‑testing hypotheses** – The system can *spectrally fingerprint* each hypothesis and its attempted proof. By monitoring the Lyapunov exponent of the chaotic iterator, it detects when a proof attempt becomes unstable (exponential divergence of the spectral embedding), signalling a likely inconsistency. The type‑level spectral invariants provide a formal “audit trail” that can be compared across runs; a hypothesis that repeatedly yields low‑entropy, low‑frequency spectra is flagged as over‑constrained, while high‑entropy, broadband spectra indicate under‑constrained or false conjectures. This dual feedback loop lets the reasoning engine prune dead‑end hypotheses early and allocate resources to promising regions of the search space.

**Novelty** – Elements exist separately: chaotic samplers in probabilistic programming, spectral program analysis, and dependent‑type guided synthesis. No published framework unifies all three under a single proof‑search engine, so CST‑PFS is a novel synthesis rather than a re‑branding of an existing field.
