**Algorithm Overview**  
The evaluator builds three tensors:  
1. **Parse‑tensor P** (shape *L × R*) where *L* indexes lexical slots (subject, verb, object, modifier) and *R* indexes relation types (negation, comparison, conditional, numeric, causal, order). Each cell holds a binary flag (0/1) indicating the presence of that relation in the candidate answer.  
2. **Reference‑tensor R** (same shape) constructed from the gold‑standard answer using the same slot‑relation mapping.  
3. **Weight‑tensor W** (shape *L × R*) generated by a low‑rank CP decomposition of a corpus‑derived co‑occurrence matrix; the decomposition yields factor matrices *A* (lexical importance) and *B* (relation importance). The outer product *W = Σ_k a_k ⊗ b_k* gives a compact weighting scheme that captures how strongly each slot‑relation contributes to overall correctness.

**Scoring Logic**  
- Compute the element‑wise error tensor *E = P ⊕ R* (XOR) to identify mismatched relations.  
- Apply a feedback‑control loop:  
  - **Proportional term** *Pₜ = α·‖E‖₂* (raw mismatch magnitude).  
  - **Integral term** accumulates past errors *Iₜ = Iₜ₋₁ + β·‖E‖₂* to penalize systematic omissions.  
  - **Derivative term** *Dₜ = γ·(‖E‖₂ – ‖Eₜ₋₁‖₂)* to reward rapid convergence.  
  - The control signal *Cₜ = Pₜ + Iₜ + Dₜ* is subtracted from a baseline score.  
- Weight the error by the Nash‑equilibrium payoff matrix *U* built from a two‑player game: the evaluator (player 1) chooses a weighting strategy *w* ∈ Δ (simplex over tensor slices), the candidate (player 2) chooses a response pattern *p* ∈ {0,1}^{L×R}. Solving for the mixed‑strategy Nash equilibrium (using Lemke‑Howson on the bilinear payoff *wᵀ · U · p*) yields optimal *w* that minimizes worst‑case loss. The final score is *S = 1 – Cₜ·(w ⊙ W).sum()* (clipped to [0,1]).

**Parsed Textual Features**  
- Negation markers (“not”, “never”) → slot = verb, relation = negation.  
- Comparative adjectives/adverbs (“greater than”, “less efficient”).  
- Conditional clauses (“if … then …”).  
- Numeric expressions (quantities, percentages).  
- Causal connectors (“because”, “therefore”).  
- Ordering relations (“first”, “subsequently”).  

**Novelty Assessment**  
The exact combination—tensor‑decomposed weighting, PID‑style feedback on logical error, and a game‑theoretic equilibrium for adaptive weighting—does not appear in existing open‑source evaluators. Similar components exist separately (tensor‑based importance, control‑theoretic error correction, Nash‑equilibrium weighting), but their integration for pure‑algorithmic reasoning scoring is novel.
