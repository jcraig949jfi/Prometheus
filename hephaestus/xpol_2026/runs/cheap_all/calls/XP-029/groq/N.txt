**1. Emergent computational mechanism**  
Combining the three ideas yields an *information‑theoretic, bandit‑driven sparse coding loop*. A sparse autoencoder learns a high‑dimensional dictionary \(D\) and produces a binary (or near‑binary) latent code \(z\) with a strong ℓ₁ or KL‑sparsity penalty. Each active unit of \(z\) is interpreted as a candidate hypothesis about the current observation. A multi‑armed bandit (e.g., KL‑UCB or Thompson‑sampling with a Beta‑Bernoulli prior) treats each latent unit as an arm and selects a subset to query (activate, test, or request additional data). The reward signal is the *information gain* \(IG = H_{\text{prior}}-H_{\text{posterior}}\) computed from Shannon entropy or mutual information between the autoencoder’s reconstruction error and the selected hypothesis. Thus the system continuously refines the sparse representation by pulling the most informative arms, while the autoencoder’s reconstruction loss supplies the likelihood needed for the bandit’s posterior update.

**2. Advantage for self‑hypothesis testing**  
The bandit focuses computational budget on the few latent dimensions that promise the largest reduction in uncertainty, exploiting sparsity to keep the hypothesis space tractable. Because the reward is directly tied to information gain, the system automatically balances exploration (testing rarely‑used latent units) against exploitation (refining well‑supported hypotheses). This yields faster convergence to a compact, disentangled model of the world and reduces the number of samples needed to falsify or confirm a hypothesis.

**3. Novelty**  
Active‑learning bandits have been applied to feature selection and to query‑efficient representation learning, and information‑theoretic objectives are common in variational autoencoders. However, a concrete architecture that (i) uses a sparse autoencoder as a hypothesis generator, (ii) quantifies each hypothesis by Shannon‑entropy reduction, and (iii) closes the loop with a KL‑UCB/Thompson‑sampling controller is not standard. We can call it **Info‑Bandit Sparse Autoencoder (IBSA)**; it sits at the intersection of representation learning, information‑theoretic decision theory, and sequential experimentation, and thus is largely novel.
