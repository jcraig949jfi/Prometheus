**1. Algorithm derived from Thermodynamics + Gene‑Regulatory‑Networks + Active‑Inference**  
The scorer builds a *thermodynamic free‑energy graph* (FE‑graph). Nodes represent elementary logical propositions extracted from the answer (e.g., “X > Y”, “¬A”, “if B then C”). Each node stores a **state vector** `s = (energy, entropy, belief)` where:  
* **energy** = a penalty proportional to violation of hard constraints (e.g., contradictory statements, broken transitivity).  
* **entropy** = uncertainty derived from the number of compatible interpretations (more ambiguous phrasing → higher entropy).  
* **belief** = a prior probability from a shallow lexical‑frequency model (counts of domain‑specific terms).  

Edges encode regulatory‑like relationships: activation (support), inhibition (contradiction), and feedback loops (self‑referential claims). Edge weights are computed from *expected free energy* (EFE) used in active inference:  

\[
\text{EFE}_{ij}= \underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_i||P_j)}_{\text{epistemic cost}} + \underbrace{C_{ij}}_{\text{pragmatic cost}}
\]

where `D_KL` is the KL‑divergence between belief distributions of connected nodes and `C_ij` is a domain‑specific cost (e.g., violating a known causal law).  

The scoring loop iteratively **relaxes** the FE‑graph: node energies are updated by summing incoming EFE, entropy is reduced by applying a simple Gibbs‑sampling step, and belief is renormalized. Convergence yields a scalar *total free energy* `F = Σ (energy + entropy)`. The final score is `score = 1 / (1 + F)`, bounded in [0,1]; lower free energy = higher reasoning quality.

**2. Parsed structural features**  
- Negations (`not`, `¬`)  
- Comparatives (`greater than`, `≤`)  
- Conditionals (`if…then`, `=>`)  
- Numeric values and units  
- Causal claims (`causes`, `leads to`)  
- Temporal/ordering relations (`before`, `after`)

Regular‑expression pipelines tag these tokens, map them to proposition nodes, and attach appropriate edge types (activation, inhibition, feedback).

**3. Novelty**  
The exact combination—treating logical propositions as thermodynamic particles, wiring them with gene‑regulatory‑style activation/inhibition, and driving convergence with active‑inference free‑energy minimization—is not present in the literature. It parallels constraint‑propagation and belief‑propagation methods, but the explicit thermodynamic bookkeeping and regulatory‑graph metaphor constitute a novel algorithmic synthesis.
