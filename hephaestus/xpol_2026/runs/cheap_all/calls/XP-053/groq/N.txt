**Computational mechanism** – The three ideas fuse into an **Ergodic Global‑Workspace Controller (EGWC)**. A low‑level optimal‑control layer (e.g., Model‑Predictive Control or an LQR on a learned dynamics model) generates candidate action sequences that drive the system toward a target state. A meta‑layer monitors the *occupancy measure* of the hypothesis space using an ergodic metric (the Sobolev‑norm distance between the time‑averaged visitation density and a prescribed “information‑value” density). When the metric is high, the controller perturbs its trajectory to increase coverage; when it is low, the controller concentrates effort on regions that have already ignited the **global workspace**. The workspace itself is implemented as a shared attention‑based buffer (e.g., a transformer‑style memory) that broadcasts selected hypothesis representations to all downstream reasoning modules. Competition for access is resolved by a soft‑max over an *ignition score* derived from the expected reduction in the ergodic cost‑function (future uncertainty × control effort).  

**Advantage for self‑testing reasoning** – The system guarantees *asymptotic* sampling of every hypothesis proportionally to its expected utility while still allowing rapid “burst” evaluation of high‑priority candidates that achieve workspace ignition. This prevents premature convergence on a narrow set of explanations and provides a principled trade‑off between exploration (ergodicity) and exploitation (optimal control of expected information gain). Consequently, hypothesis testing becomes both statistically thorough and computationally efficient.  

**Novelty** – Ergodic control and optimal‑control‑based active inference are established, and Global Workspace Theory has inspired cognitive architectures such as ACT‑R and transformer‑based “broadcast” models. However, an architecture that *explicitly* uses an ergodic occupancy metric to steer a global‑workspace broadcast for hypothesis testing does not appear in the literature; it sits at the intersection of ergodic control, global‑workspace cognition, and optimal‑control planning, making it a genuinely new synthesis (though conceptually adjacent to active inference and curiosity‑driven RL).
