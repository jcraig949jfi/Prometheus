**1. Emerging computational mechanism**  
By treating a reasoning system’s hypothesis space as a *quantum game* the three pillars fuse into a **Quantum‑Information‑Game‑Theoretic Inference Engine (QIGIE)**.  The engine encodes each candidate hypothesis in a basis state of a variational quantum circuit.  A *mutual‑information* loss \(L_{\text{info}} = I(X;Y)-\lambda D_{\text{KL}}(p_{\theta}\|p_{\text{prior}})\) quantifies how much a measurement outcome reduces uncertainty about the data.  Simultaneously, the system plays a *self‑game*: each “player” is a possible internal model; the payoff matrix is the expected reduction in entropy minus a penalty for deviating from a mixed‑strategy equilibrium.  A Nash‑equilibrium solver (e.g., Lemke‑Howson or differentiable fictitious play) runs on the classical side, while the quantum circuit supplies a superposition of strategies that the optimizer (QAOA‑style) collapses into a *correlated equilibrium* that maximizes the information‑theoretic objective.

**2. Advantage for a self‑testing reasoning system**  
The quantum superposition lets the system evaluate exponentially many hypotheses in parallel, while the information‑theoretic loss guarantees that only those reducing Shannon entropy survive.  The Nash‑equilibrium regularizer prevents “over‑fitting” to a single hypothesis by enforcing stability: no single belief can improve the payoff without a coordinated shift of the whole belief mixture.  Consequently the system can *self‑audit* its predictions, detect internal contradictions, and converge to a belief profile that is both information‑optimal and strategically robust.

**3. Novelty**  
Quantum game theory and quantum Bayesian inference exist, and Nash‑equilibrium regularization appears in multi‑agent reinforcement learning.  However, an architecture that **jointly optimizes a quantum variational circuit, an information‑theoretic objective, and a Nash‑equilibrium constraint for metacognitive hypothesis testing** is not documented as a distinct field.  It therefore counts as a novel synthesis, albeit built from known components.
