**1. Emerging computational mechanism**  
By indexing each candidate hypothesis on a *prime‑number lattice* and letting the system treat its own future belief states as *agents* in a recursive “mind‑reading” game, we obtain a **Prime‑Indexed Recursive Game‑Theoretic Meta‑Learner (PIRGT‑ML)**. The hypothesis space is mapped to the sequence of primes (e.g., hypothesis hₖ ↔ pₖ). A belief‑graph is built where edges encode *mental‑state recursion* (I‑POMDP style) and the payoff of selecting a hypothesis is the expected reduction in prediction error. The system then computes a **Nash equilibrium** over mixed strategies on this graph using regret‑matching or fictitious play, while weighting each hypothesis by a zeta‑based prior 1/ζ(s) that reflects the density of primes (s≈2). The equilibrium strategy selects a distribution over prime‑indexed hypotheses that is stable against unilateral “self‑deviation” (i.e., the system cannot improve its own loss by abandoning the chosen mixture).

**2. Advantage for self‑testing**  
Because each hypothesis is uniquely identified by a prime, collisions in the hypothesis hash are impossible, allowing fast O(1) lookup and deterministic merging of evidence. The game‑theoretic equilibrium guarantees that the system’s belief update is *self‑consistent*: no internal “agent” (a future version of the system, a simulated opponent, or a counter‑hypothesis) can unilaterally increase expected utility. This yields a built‑in sanity check: any hypothesis that would break the equilibrium is automatically down‑weighted, preventing confirmation bias and helping the system detect false‑belief cycles that plague standard Bayesian updates.
