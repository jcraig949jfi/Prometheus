**Computational mechanism**  
A *criticality‑driven adaptive synthesis engine* (CA‑SYN) can be built by layering three concrete components:  

1. **Neural program synthesis** (e.g., DreamCoder’s library‑learning + transformer‑guided search) that maps a formal specification to a candidate abstract syntax tree (AST).  
2. **Self‑Organized Criticality (SOC)** implemented as a sand‑pile–style resource allocator: each synthesis step deposits “energy” (search budget) on a lattice of program fragments; when a local threshold is exceeded a cascade rewrites a subtree, producing a power‑law distribution of program “avalanches.”  
3. **Adaptive control** using a Model‑Reference Adaptive Controller (MRAC) that continuously tunes the SOC thresholds and the neural guide’s temperature/beam‑width based on online performance error (e.g., mismatch between generated program behavior and the specification).  

The three interact: the MRAC observes the error signal, adjusts SOC parameters to keep the system at the edge of a phase transition, and the SOC‑driven cascades supply diverse, non‑local program mutations for the neural synthesizer to evaluate.

**Advantage for a self‑testing reasoning system**  
A system that must generate, test, and discard its own hypotheses benefits from *scale‑free exploration*: most edits are small (fine‑tuning) while occasional large avalanches produce radical program restructurings, preventing the search from getting trapped in local minima. The adaptive controller guarantees that the avalanche rate stays near the critical point, automatically balancing exploitation and exploration without hand‑crafted schedules. Consequently, hypothesis testing becomes both rapid (small errors are corrected instantly) and robust (large conceptual shifts occur when needed).

**Novelty assessment**  
No established field unifies neural program synthesis, SOC, and adaptive control. Related work exists separately: DreamCoder (meta‑learning synthesis), criticality‑driven reinforcement learning, and MRAC for system identification, but their integration into a single hypothesis‑generation loop is, to my knowledge, unprecedented.
