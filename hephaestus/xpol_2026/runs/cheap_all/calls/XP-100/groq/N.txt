**1. Algorithmic core**  
The combined algorithm treats a candidate answer as a *super‑position* of discrete logical propositions extracted from the text. Each proposition is stored as a node in a directed graph `G = (V, E)`. The node payload is a NumPy float‑vector **ψ** that holds a probability amplitude for the proposition being true at a given *scale* (e.g., sentence‑level, paragraph‑level, whole‑answer).  

*Renormalization* is performed by repeatedly **coarse‑graining** the graph: lower‑level nodes are aggregated into higher‑level meta‑nodes, their amplitudes summed and then *renormalized* so that the ℓ₂‑norm of the amplitude vector at each scale equals 1. Fixed‑point detection (‖ψₖ₊₁ − ψₖ‖ < ε) yields a stable scale‑independent representation of the answer’s logical content.

*Symbiosis* enters as a mutual‑reinforcement step. For every edge (e = (u→v)) representing a logical implication, a **constraint‑propagation** pass updates the amplitudes of u and v simultaneously:  
```python
ψ_u += λ * (ψ_v * w_e)      # λ∈[0,1] controls symbiotic strength
ψ_v += λ * (ψ_u * w_e)
```  
The weights w_e encode the strength of the relation (e.g., causal, conditional). After each pass the amplitudes are renormalized again, allowing “interference” – overlapping evidence can amplify or cancel scores, mimicking quantum superposition.

The final **score** for the answer is the summed squared amplitudes of propositions that match a gold‑standard logical template, i.e. Score = ∑ₖ‖ψₖ‖² for matched nodes.

**2. Parsed structural features**  
The parser extracts:  
- Negations (¬) and double‑negatives,  
- Comparatives/ superlatives (>, ≥, <, ≤),  
- Conditionals (if … then),  
- Numeric values and units,  
- Explicit causal claims (because, leads to),  
- Temporal and ordering relations (first, before, after).  

These are encoded as edge types and weight modifiers in G.

**3. Novelty**  
The pipeline resembles existing constraint‑propagation and probabilistic logic systems, but the explicit use of a quantum‑inspired amplitude vector together with a renormalization hierarchy and a symbiotic mutual‑reinforcement update is not present in standard NLP evaluators. It therefore maps partially to prior work (e.g., belief‑propagation, multi‑scale reasoning) while introducing a novel hybrid formalism.
