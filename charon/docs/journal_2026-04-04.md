## The Finding (As Of End Of Day)

The spectral tail of elliptic curve L-functions (zeros 5-19) exhibits rank-dependent
gap compression that **contradicts the sign prediction of finite-matrix RMT**.

Random matrix theory predicts that rank-1 curves (one fewer free eigenangle) should
have wider gaps in the spectral tail. The empirical data shows the opposite: rank-1
curves have uniformly tighter gaps, with Cohen's d ~ -0.05 to -0.11 across all 15
gaps.

This sign inversion:
- Survives 15 mechanism-stripping attempts
- Is stable across stratum sizes, clustering methods, and train/test splits
- Is identical on raw (unnormalized) zeros (r = 0.994)
- Is not predicted by SO(120) simulation (pattern correlation r = 0.15)
- Gets stronger (d-values double) when outliers are removed
- Is confirmed by permutation test (p = 0.001)

Additionally:
- BSD invariants have monotonically decaying influence from z1 to z15 (RF R^2
  goes from +0.52 to -0.50)
- Tamagawa has a novel two-hump spectral fingerprint (z1-3 and z10-16) but is
  orthogonal to rank
- Galois image and torsion also affect zeros but are orthogonal to rank

## Evening Battery (Autonomous Exploration)

### Compression Gradient: Strongest Near Central Point
| Window | Mean d |
|--------|--------|
| z1-z8 | -0.125 |
| z3-z15 | -0.067 |
| z5-z20 | -0.045 |
| z8-z20 | -0.038 |

Compression propagates outward from s=1/2 and weakens with distance. Consistent
with forced-zero repulsion having longer range than RMT predicts.

### Rank-2 Scaling: Compression Scales With Rank
- Rank-0 vs Rank-1: d = -0.045
- Rank-0 vs Rank-2: d = -0.188 (4x stronger)
- Rank-1 vs Rank-2: d = -0.147

More forced zeros = tighter gaps. Exactly opposite to RMT prediction. The effect
is proportional to rank, not just present/absent.

### Bad Primes: Strong Zero-Gap Correlation
omega(N) vs mean gap: rho = -0.231 (p = 5e-83). More bad primes = tighter gaps.
New mechanism candidate -- needs rank-orthogonality test.

### Small-N RMT: Gap 1 Is Special
At SO(20) and SO(40), gap 1 (adjacent to pinned zero) has d = -2.2 (compressed),
but all subsequent gaps have d ~ +0.1 to +0.3 (expanded). RMT predicts compression
only in the immediate neighborhood of the pinned zero. The empirical data shows
compression everywhere -- the effect propagates much further than RMT expects.

### Paper Implications
The rank-2 scaling and the compression gradient strengthen the narrative. The story
is: the forced zero at s=1/2 compresses nearby gaps (RMT agrees on gap 1), but the
compression propagates much further into the tail than RMT predicts, and the effect
scales with the number of forced zeros. This is a "long-range repulsion" finding.
