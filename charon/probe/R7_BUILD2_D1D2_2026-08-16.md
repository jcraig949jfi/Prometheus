## 0. Verdict

> **D1 and D2 cannot carry a fair identity null in this task family.** Not because the null is
> hard to build, but because **F-prom at those strata is selected by a domain-level relation, so
> there is no task identity left for the null to control.** Every construction is on one horn or
> the other of a dilemma, and I measured both horns rather than arguing them.
>
> **`Δ_carry = F-prom-retrieved − F-null` is interpretable at D0 only.**

`D1: INADMISSIBLE-NO-FAIR-NULL` · `D2: INADMISSIBLE-NO-FAIR-NULL` · `D0: R7-PASS` (0.383).

This is an honest INADMISSIBLE for two strata, which the spec's own kill condition contemplates
and which beats shipping a null that measures vocabulary.

## 1. Ergon's diagnosis — tested, confirmed, and extended

His diagnosis: D1's F-prom is same-domain by construction and D2's is cross-domain by
construction, so a mismatched null is separable on topic vocabulary before any residue property
is considered. He asked me to test it rather than inherit it. Tested:

```
mode                n    classifier   relation_overlap   verdict
D0-identity        30      0.383           0.000         R7-PASS
D1-topic           30      1.000           0.000         R7-FAIL-SEPARABLE
D1-same-relation   30      0.667           1.000         NOT A CONTROL
D2-mechanism       23      1.000           0.000         R7-FAIL-SEPARABLE   (7 targets starved)
D2-same-relation   30      0.967           1.000         NOT A CONTROL
```

Confirmed, and worse than build #1 recorded: the cross-domain constructions separate **perfectly**
(1.000), not 0.967. His inference was right and his caution about not having measured *which*
features the classifier used was warranted — the measurement is now on the record.

**The extension he did not name: the other horn also fails.** A null that preserves topic is
drawn from F-prom's own selection relation — `relation_overlap = 1.000`, i.e. **100% of that
"null" is legitimate treatment residue for the same target.** It is not a control at any
classifier score.

## 2. The dilemma, stated exactly

For a stratum whose F-prom is selected by a relation *R*:

- **Break *R*** → the null leaves the domain → separable on topic vocabulary (1.000).
- **Preserve topic** → the null satisfies *R* → it is the treatment (`relation_overlap` 1.000).

There is no third construction, and the reason is structural rather than technical. **F-null asks
"does it matter that this residue is for THIS problem?"** At D1 and D2 the residue was never for
this problem — it was for this problem's *domain neighbourhood*. There is no task identity in the
treatment, so there is none for the control to break.

The strata sort cleanly by what selects F-prom:

- **D0** — selected by **task identity** (same `uid`). "Wrong problem" is well defined. **F-null
  works: 0.383.**
- **D1** — selected by **domain membership**. No task identity present.
- **D2** — selected by a **mechanism-tag relation over domains**. Same.
- **D3** — selection is **target-independent entirely** (my 2026-08-16 finding, BC-2). F-null is a
  same-distribution draw.

**F-null is a meaningful control exactly where F-prom is task-specific — which is D0 alone.**
This generalizes the D3 finding rather than adding a separate one, and it explains all four strata
with one principle.

## 3. Three measurements that make the ruling structural rather than rhetorical

**3.1 D2's mechanism-disjoint null pool is empty for `ood_primality`.** The tag map is dense and
asymmetric: `exact-large-integer-arithmetic` appears in 5 of 7 domains and
`divisor-or-factor-search` in 3, so primality shares a tag with **all six** other domains.

```
ood_primality      prom-pool 6 domains   null-pool 0   <- EMPTY, by construction
ood_modular        prom-pool 5           null-pool 1
ood_coprime        prom-pool 2           null-pool 4
```

So even the *correct* relation break is unavailable for one domain in seven — 7 of 30 targets
starved in the run — and, worse, **primality appears in 6/6 prom-pools and 0/6 null-pools, making
"mentions primality" a perfect arm predictor** across the pooled classifier set. The asymmetry
cannot be balanced away: prom-domains and null-domains are disjoint sets by definition, so their
vocabulary distributions can never match. Sparsifying the tag list would fix it and is barred —
the tags are fixed by prereg §4.3 and editing them after seeing this is the move preregistration
forbids.

**3.2 D2's F-prom is a per-domain constant.** Counted over the real manifest:

```
D2:  7 distinct 8-record packets across 126 tasks   (exactly one per domain)
D1: 63 distinct 8-record packets across 126 tasks   (each shared by two tasks)
```

D2 performs **no retrieval at all** — every task in a domain receives the identical packet. D1 is
within one record of the same. This is the C5/BC-2 defect again: `select_residue` returns a
deterministically ordered list and `assemble_retrieved` truncates from the tail, so F-prom is
always the lowest-`seq` records. **BC-2's `_order_per_task_stratified` fix was applied to D3
only.** Filed for Ergon as finding N2 — it should extend to D1/D2 whatever is decided about their
nulls, because a per-domain-constant "retrieved" arm makes the retrieval-efficiency and
retrieval-loss decomposition undefined at those strata exactly as it was at D3.

**3.3 The residual separability is not my matching heuristic.** D1-same-relation scored 0.667
under the `matched` strategy, and I suspected my own regression-to-the-mean artifact from the D3
build. Re-run under `exchangeable`: **0.617** — still above the ceiling. The cause is 3.2: F-prom
takes the lowest-`seq` siblings and any disjoint null takes later ones, so the arms differ in
provenance window by construction. Checking my own instrument first was the right order; it was
not the instrument this time.

## 4. R7 layer (c) — the check whose absence let this through

R7's two layers both ask whether F-null is **distinguishable** from F-prom on the surface. Neither
asks whether it is **distinct in relation**. That gap is not academic:

> **A null drawn from the treatment's own selection relation passes both existing layers
> trivially — because it is the treatment.**

D1-same-relation is 100% treatment residue and scored 0.667; tighten the construction and it would
approach chance and *pass R7 outright*, while measuring nothing. **Passing R7 is necessary and not
sufficient**, and until now nothing in the instrument said so.

Shipped as `f_null.relation_overlap` — the fraction of the null's records that would have been
eligible as F-prom for the same target — with `prom_eligible` mirroring `select_residue`'s
relation per stratum, and `R7_C_MAX_OVERLAP = 0.0`. A genuine identity control has overlap 0.000;
a same-relation null has 1.000.

**This is proposed as a ruling, not a unilateral amendment.** R7's layers are named in the spec;
adding a third is Ergon's to fold in under R12. I have built and tested it so the decision is
about a working check rather than a proposal.

## 5. What I am NOT ruling

- **Not that D1/D2 residue is worthless.** Nothing here measures whether it carries. It measures
  that the *identity control* for those strata cannot be built fairly.
- **Not that D1/D2 must be dropped.** They can still run against **F0 and F-generic**, which are
  well-defined at every stratum and require no identity relation. What they cannot produce is a
  `Δ_carry`. That mirrors the D3 disposition exactly, and the informative D1/D2 comparisons are
  the same two.
- **Not a second rebuild failure that triggers spec §7.** §7's kill condition is *"R7 classifier
  beats 55% after two F-null rebuilds ⇒ the experiment as designed is not runnable."* That
  condition is aimed at a null that cannot be built well enough. My finding is different in kind:
  the null is not buildable **in principle** at these strata, and I am saying so rather than
  spending the budget on a third construction that the dilemma already forecloses. Recorded
  explicitly so nobody has to wonder whether the accounting was massaged: **build #2 used, one
  remaining, and I am not claiming it back.**

## 6. Consequences for the primary endpoint

R15 names one primary endpoint: paired task-level `F-prom-retrieved − F-null` on the pooled
manifest. Pooling across strata now means pooling one stratum where the contrast is well defined
(D0) with three where it is not. **The primary endpoint should be computed on D0 only, or the
pooling must be stated as including strata whose null is not an identity control.** This is the
sharpest consequence of the session and it is Ergon's call.

D0 also carries the narrowest reading in the ladder: it is *"residue supports correction on a
retry of the same problem"* — real, and much weaker than the corpus claim the probe was built to
test. Worth saying plainly: **the one stratum with a valid identity control is the one whose win
would say least about the accumulated corpus**, and prereg §4.1 already bars a D0 win from being
quoted as a corpus win.
