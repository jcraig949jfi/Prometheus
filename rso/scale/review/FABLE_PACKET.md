2. THE ROADMAP UNDER REVIEW (summary)
--------------------------------------
Horizon I (~1 month): are composition deserts crossable by SEARCH-STRUCTURE
  interventions at a fixed budget (retention, neutral acceptance, novelty
  selection, admission of worse candidates into new behaviour cells,
  structured credit), or do they need representation/developmental change?
  Experiment: D1 (below), then the same frozen protocol on one audited
  desert with a known construction (FLIP), routed to its owner. Plus one
  World-Demand admission ladder (a reference solver succeeds; reflex,
  register and memoryless baselines fail).
Horizon II (~1 quarter): does lifetime plasticity or developmental promotion
  make a two-part mechanism reachable that blind search does not, without
  the machinery supplying the solution? A preregistered 2x2 (promotion x
  plasticity) at 1x/4x budgets with cognitive accounting (organism /
  inherited / developmental / world / search / evaluator), archive disabled
  at final evaluation, shuffled-history and random-library controls, and a
  conventional learner baseline.
Horizon III (~1 year): do discoveries lower the acquisition cost of later
  discoveries? A curriculum of admitted worlds (memory bit -> composition ->
  lifetime learning -> reuse -> revision after a rule change), acquisition-
  cost curves vs a reuse-disabled control and a conventional learner.
Portfolio: reachability archives and structured credit first (Horizon I);
  plasticity and promotion as one 2x2 (II); world-organism co-development
  only after a seed exists (III); an unfamiliar-substrate lane reserved
  (~15-20% of compute; disputed).

3. D1 -- THE ONE DEMONSTRATION RUN IN THIS WINDOW (frozen; result below)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
World: a small register-machine "reach" world with a known 8-instruction
builder; starts are the builder with d = 1, 3 or 8 rows knocked out; budget
200,000 proposals per lineage; 24 lineages per (arm, d). Arms differ by ONE
factor each: strict chain -> neutral chain -> archive retention (best cell)
-> count-weighted ("rarely visited") selection -> admission of worse genomes
into new behaviour cells -> the same with a structure-free genotype-hash
archive of matched bucket count. Five contrasts, stratified exact test,
Holm-corrected; certification independent of training fitness. Pre-freeze
finding: the behaviour descriptor barely separates shortest-path
intermediates from score-matched random programs (separation 0.006). Our
own Q3 challenge then found the "stepping stone" interpretation row vacuous
(exact-row restoration needs ~6x the lineage budget) and narrowed it before
any result.
RESULT (2026-10-10): NOTHING SEPARATES. 15 of 24 rounds ran before the
CPU cap (thermally limited host). Certified discoveries per 45 lineages:
plain chain 4, neutral chain 1, retention 0, count selection 4, worse-
into-new-cell 0, structure-free control 0; all at d = 1 or 3, none at
d = 8 in any arm. Holm p >= 0.56 on all five contrasts. Power at this N
for a +0.20 per-lineage effect: 0.49 (0.82 as planned). The plain chain
already scored 4/15 at d = 1 (planned baseline 1/24). We read this as an
under-powered null on a world that is too easy at d = 1 and too hard at
d = 8 -- NOT as "archives do not help".
