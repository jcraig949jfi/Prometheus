# D-17 — the evidence, and a correction to the record the operator is about to make

Techne, 2026-09-10. Written because the operator's D-17 ruling rests on a framing **I** supplied
and then corrected, and the superseded version is the one that reached them. The instinct in the
ruling is right — *don't turn a provenance unknown into a fake blocker, but don't turn it into
fake certainty either* — and applied to the measured facts it produces a different decision.

## The ruling as proposed, and why each half is inverted

> "MIT source grant exists at revision 350804b7b358, but no upstream tag matches wheel 0.1.29, so
> you cannot prove that this source revision produced the installed wheel. Record: source pin =
> 350804b7b358; license = MIT at pinned source; binary/source correspondence = UNESTABLISHED."

Three things are wrong, and they are my errors, not the operator's.

**The revision is the wrong repository.** `350804b7b358` is `mlb2251/stitch` — the **Rust core**
— at its main HEAD on 2026-09-09. The Python wheel is built from **`mlb2251/stitch_bindings`**, a
separate project. A grant covering the core does not extend to a separate bindings package. (It
is also not even the core revision in play: the bindings pin the core at `0ef5ec7`, not at main's
head.)

**Provenance is ESTABLISHED, not unestablished.** I reported "no upstream tag matches 0.1.29"
after searching `mlb2251/stitch`, which publishes one tag. `stitch_bindings` publishes **21**,
including `v0.1.29` → `8ba2c1c041ab384ccaa11e68226d5654474d161a`, whose `Cargo.toml` declares
`version = "0.1.29"` — an exact match with the installed distribution. The tag was never missing;
I was looking in the wrong repository.

**The licence is UNRESOLVED, not MIT.** Verified exhaustively today against the full tree at
`v0.1.29`: **128 entries, zero licence-like paths** — no `LICENSE`, `COPYING`, `NOTICE` or
`COPYRIGHT` anywhere, recursive, untruncated. No `license` key in `Cargo.toml`. No `license` key
in `pyproject.toml` — which is *why* maturin put nothing in the wheel. The wheel and sdist carry
nothing either.

So the two halves are the other way round from the proposed record:

```
                      proposed            measured
source correspondence UNESTABLISHED   ->  ESTABLISHED  (tag v0.1.29 == version 0.1.29)
licence               MIT             ->  UNRESOLVED   (no grant in 128 files, or either artifact)
```

## What I would record as D-17 v1

Two artifacts, two rows, because conflating them is what produced the error:

```
stitch_core 0.1.29  (Python bindings)
  source pin        mlb2251/stitch_bindings @ tag v0.1.29 = 8ba2c1c041ab384ccaa11e68226d5654474d161a
  version match     Cargo.toml version = "0.1.29" == the installed distribution
  correspondence    ESTABLISHED by tag and version; NOT by a reproducible build
  licence           UNRESOLVED -- no grant in the repository tree (128 entries), none in the
                    wheel, none in the sdist, no license key in Cargo.toml or pyproject.toml
  disposition       development-only. The block is a LICENCE block, not a provenance block.

stitch_core (Rust core, the crate the bindings depend on)
  source pin        mlb2251/stitch @ 0ef5ec7f17091d22b8fa959fb5705e359d735a47
                    -- the revision the BINDINGS pin, not main's head
  licence           MIT, Copyright (c) 2021 Matthew Bowers; LICENSE present at that revision and
                    Cargo.toml declares license = "MIT"
  disposition       UNBLOCKED for internal use and for export, with the notice kept beside the copy
```

**And the distinction the operator asked to preserve is already moot for capability purposes.**
The MIT core is built here and produces **byte-identical results** to the bindings on one
hash-pinned input — `1919558 -> 316890`, three abstractions, verbatim bodies
(`ROUTES_AGREE`, receipt `paper_reproduction-stitch_rust_core-20260910T161201Z.json`). So nothing
needs the unlicensed layer. "This exact source produced these exact Stitch results" is a claim I
**can** make, through the core, with a pinned revision and an MIT grant.

Concretely: anything that must leave the host, or that must carry a provenance claim, goes through
the core route. The bindings stay development-only on licence grounds alone, and that costs the
programme nothing measurable, because the H0/H2 library is already produced the other way.

## What would close the bindings row

One of:

1. upstream adds a `LICENSE` to `stitch_bindings` and a `license` key to its `pyproject.toml`
   (the packaging fix that would also put the notice in future wheels); or
2. the maintainer states the bindings' licence in writing — Matthew Bowers, `mlbowers@mit.edu`
   per the core's `Cargo.toml`; or
3. the operator records a deliberate acceptance of the unresolved state with its scope.

Cost in all three cases: **$0.** There is no licence to buy. TECHNE-06 on my backlog is the ask,
and it is blocked only on whether this seat may open an upstream issue in the programme's name.
