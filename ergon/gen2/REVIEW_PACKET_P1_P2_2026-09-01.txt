HEADLINE

    PROJECT 1 -- SELECTION_NOT_DISTINGUISHABLE_FROM_CHURN.

    Principled selection does NOT beat arbitrary churn. At n = 100 fresh
    paired lineages the contrast is -0.31 pp with 95% CI [-1.12, +0.55] pp
    and p = 0.473. The upper bound EXCLUDES the preregistered 2.00 pp
    strategic threshold, so this is a BOUNDED null: the data rule out a
    meaningful selection advantage rather than merely failing to find one.

    Gen-1B measured +1.51 pp on this contrast at n = 30. The replication
    REVERSES the sign, and its CI excludes the Gen-1B point estimate.

    PROJECT 2 -- TRAJECTORY_INSTRUMENT_CLEAN, with a methodological finding
    that matters more than the gate: the parity test passed on an instrument
    that recorded nothing.

======================================================================
1. WHAT PROJECT 1 WAS, AND WHY IT WAS NOT A POWER PROBLEM
======================================================================

Gen-1B reported I1 MUT_REDUNDANT - I3 RANDOM at +1.51 pp, uncorrected
p = 0.0228, dying only after Holm correction across five contrasts
(adjusted 0.0912). The natural reading -- and the one the Gen-1B packet
offered -- was "suggestive, underpowered, rerun it bigger."

That reading was wrong, and the pre-run power analysis said so BEFORE the run:
recomputing from the contrast's own per-lineage SD (sigma = 0.03218),

    n = 30    MDE80 1.65 pp    MDE90 1.90 pp
    n = 100   MDE80 0.90 pp    MDE90 1.04 pp

n = 30 ALREADY had 90% power against a 2.00 pp effect. The binding constraint
in Gen-1B was MULTIPLICITY, not lineage count. Project 1 therefore isolates a
contrast that was diluted by being run beside four others; with one
preregistered contrast it takes no correction, and that was recorded as a
design change in advance rather than discovered as an analysis change.

THE THRESHOLD, frozen before data and justified without reference to the
observed effect:

    T = 2.00 pp

  a. GRANULARITY. CFR is over 42 tasks, so one task is 2.38 pp. Below T an
     effect cannot reliably move even one task in forty-two within a lineage.
  b. PROPORTION. The whole accumulation effect is 10.95 pp and its
     content-adaptedness component 6.7 pp. T is ~18% and ~30% of those.
  c. DECISION RELEVANCE. Nothing downstream in the queue is worth building on
     a selection effect that cannot clear one task in forty-two.

T is STRATEGIC, not statistical. A real effect below T is reported as real and
below scale, never as absent.

======================================================================
5. THE REPLICATION REVERSAL, AND WHAT IT COSTS
======================================================================

    Gen-1B   n = 30    I1 - I3 = +1.51 pp   uncorrected p 0.0228
    Project1 n = 100   I1 - I3 = -0.31 pp   p 0.473, CI [-1.12, +0.55]

The sign reversed and the new CI excludes the old point estimate. Both arms
moved toward each other rather than one collapsing:

    I1   0.3135 -> 0.3076
    I3   0.2984 -> 0.3107

That is the signature of regression to the mean on a contrast selected for
attention because it was marginal.

HOLM WAS RIGHT AND THE GEN-1B PACKET TALKED PAST IT. That packet called the
contrast "suggestive, underpowered" and recommended a bigger rerun. The
correction was not being conservative; it was being correct, and the
replication now confirms the corrected reading over the uncorrected one. The
pre-run power calculation was the available clue and it pointed the same way.

The transferable rule: when a marginal contrast dies on multiplicity, the
first question is whether the correction was RIGHT, not how to get more n.

======================================================================
6. WHAT PROJECT 1 DOES NOT KILL
======================================================================

Gen-1B's I1 - I0 = +2.78 pp (Holm 0.0040) is UNTOUCHED by this run, because
I0 was not an arm here. Combined with I1 ~= I3, the surviving hypothesis is:

    ANYTHING beats the inherited MRU rule, and the specific selection
    criterion does not matter -- i.e. most-recent-first eviction is ACTIVELY
    HARMFUL rather than principled retention being helpful.

That is a different claim from the one the queue was built on. It is
consistent with Gen-1B's I3 - I0 = +1.27 pp (p = 0.0540, just missing at
n = 30) and with the fact that in Project 1 the RANDOM arm sits at 0.3107,
well above Gen-1B's MRU arm at 0.2857.

It is NOT confirmed. Confirming it needs I0 at n = 100, which Project 1
deliberately did not run because the prereg fixed two arms.

CONTROLS:
    POSITIVE  24-task fixture that reaches the cap: divergence MUST fire, and
              does, at evaluation index 33999 (identity Jaccard 0.6347,
              behaviour Jaccard 0.6667)
    NEGATIVE  same arm, same seed: no divergence. PASS
    ANCESTRY  1.0000 of scored candidates carry a parent. PASS

THE METHODOLOGICAL FINDING, which is the part worth keeping. The parity gate
passed on the FIRST version of this instrument, and that version was broken:
97% of scored candidates had NO parent recorded. The cause is structural --
m1_rx builds a whole generation of 32 children and only THEN scores them, so a
single "pending parent" slot is overwritten 31 times before the first dist()
call.

    Parity tests that an instrument does not CHANGE the phenomenon.
    It does not test that the instrument CAPTURES it.

Both gates are needed. The Gen-1A persistence instrument had only the first
kind, and passed.

A second instance of the same shape nearly slipped through: the first
divergence run reported None for I1 vs I3, which looked like a clean result
and was actually a 6-task fixture where the cap is never reached, so no
eviction has yet differed and the policies are still identical. A broken
detector and a not-yet-diverged pair are indistinguishable without a positive
control.

DENOMINATOR FOR ANY FUTURE MECHANISM CLAIM: only about 5.5% of scored
candidates descend directly from a library draw
(LIB 2419, XOVER 19822, POP 19212, REP 2541 on the 24-task fixture). Whatever
retention does, it acts through that narrow channel.

9. CLAIM CEILING
======================================================================

  - The null is BOUNDED, not absolute: effects below +0.55 pp remain possible
    and are excluded only relative to T = 2.00 pp.
  - This is one contrast between two specific policies on one consumer, one
    42-task battery, one budget. It does not show that no retention policy can
    beat random; it shows that THIS one does not, at this scale.
  - I0 was not run. "MRU is actively harmful" is the surviving hypothesis, not
    a result.
  - Project 2 certifies the instrument, not any mechanism. No trajectory claim
    is made anywhere in this packet.
  - The 5.5% library-descended fraction is measured on a 24-task fixture at
    budget 2000, not on the full experiment.

11. RECOMMENDATION -- THE QUEUE NEEDS RE-GATING
======================================================================

Project 1 was declared the gate for almost everything downstream, and it
returned negative. Taking that seriously:

STOP, do not run as designed:
  - PROJECT 3 (where does the +2.78pp enter?) as a SELECTION mechanism study.
    There is no selection effect to trace. The question survives only in its
    reformulated version below.
  - PROJECT 7 (transplant). It was already gated on "a selection effect worth
    transplanting". There is none.

RUN NEXT, and it is one cheap discriminating experiment:
  - I0 MRU vs I3 RANDOM at n = 100, same design, one contrast, no correction.
    This is the ONLY experiment that can distinguish the two surviving stories:
        (a) MRU is actively harmful, and any churn beats it
        (b) Gen-1B's I1-I0 was itself inflated the way I1-I3 was
    It costs about the same as Project 1 and it decides whether ANY retention
    result in this programme survives. If I3 - I0 is null, then Gen-1B's
    headline goes too and the honest position becomes "retention policy does
    not measurably matter in this consumer at this scale."

THEN, conditional on I3 > I0:
  - Reformulated PROJECT 3: not "why does selection help" but "what is MRU
    doing wrong?" Use the Project 2 instrument on paired I0 vs I3 lineages.
    The mechanism question becomes tractable and specific: most-recent-first
    eviction has a systematic defect, and the trajectory instrument can now
    say what it is.

DEFER: Projects 4, 5, 6, 8, 9. Project 4's forgetting rule was motivated by
the hazard structure, which stands on its own, but building a retirement
policy is not worth it until we know whether ANY eviction rule beats another.

The Phase 7 hazard result from Gen-1B is unaffected by all of this and remains
the most reusable thing the programme has produced on memory: usefulness does
not decay, 44% of artifacts never earn a first credit, and 24% of used
artifacts revive after five or more dormant tasks.
