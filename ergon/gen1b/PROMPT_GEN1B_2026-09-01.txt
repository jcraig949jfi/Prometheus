PHASE 1 - MUTATIONAL REDUNDANCY AUDIT
======================================================================

Gen-1A found:

    834 admitted artifacts
    347 distinct behaviour fingerprints
    58% of artifacts behaviourally duplicate another admitted artifact

This is sufficient to establish CURRENT-BEHAVIOUR redundancy.
It is NOT sufficient to establish EVOLUTIONARY redundancy.

The library contains executable genotypes that are later used as mutation
seeds. Two artifacts with identical present behaviour may expose very
different future mutation neighborhoods.

Therefore test:

    SAME PHENOTYPE != SAME MUTATIONAL AFFORDANCES?

For pairs or groups sharing an identical behaviour fingerprint, measure their
controlled mutation neighborhoods.

Use matched mutation budgets and controlled/randomness-preserving procedures
wherever mechanically possible.

At minimum report pairwise/group similarity in:

  - offspring behaviour fingerprints;
  - number of distinct offspring behaviours;
  - offspring score distributions;
  - probability of producing a strict improvement;
  - probability of producing an effective-use event;
  - reachable novel behaviours;
  - operator-specific offspring differences;
  - genotype/edit distance;
  - offspring overlap as mutation radius/budget increases.

Define and justify a quantitative measure of:

    MUTATIONAL REDUNDANCY

The purpose is to distinguish:

    behaviourally equivalent AND evolutionarily redundant

from:

    behaviourally equivalent BUT evolutionarily distinct.

Report what fraction of the apparent 58% redundancy survives this stronger
test.

If behavioural duplicates routinely expose different mutation neighborhoods,
do not silently treat them as disposable.

In that case either:

A. redefine I1 REDUNDANCY so eviction requires both behavioural redundancy and
   sufficient mutational-neighborhood redundancy;

or

B. retain simple behavioural redundancy as the arm but explicitly classify it
   as a test of phenotype-level compression, not genuine evolutionary
   redundancy.

Simulate the final I1 policy against the recorded admission stream before
preregistration.

Report:

  - fraction of eviction decisions differing from baseline;
  - mean and terminal library Jaccard overlap;
  - behavioural diversity;
  - mutational-neighborhood diversity;
  - age distribution;
  - effective-use distribution;
  - fraction of unique mutation neighborhoods removed.

If I1 does not create meaningful policy divergence, drop it rather than
running a ceremonial arm.

PHASE 2 - EFFECTIVE-USAGE OPPORTUNITY AUDIT
======================================================================

Gen-1A established that raw draw count is non-discriminating and that
effective uses are much more dispersed:

    median raw draws ............ 286
    zero raw draws .............. 4%
    median effective uses ....... 1
    zero effective uses ......... 42%

That establishes a sorting signal.

It does NOT yet establish that absolute effective-use count is free of
exposure/residence bias.

Test whether EFFECTIVE_USAGE itself is substantially an artifact-age or
opportunity signal.

Using the existing baseline corpus, analyze relationships among:

  - effective-use count;
  - total draw opportunities;
  - residence duration;
  - admission position;
  - genotype length;
  - task family;
  - behaviour fingerprint;
  - artifact age;
  - score at admission.

At minimum compare:

1. absolute effective-use count;
2. effective uses / draw opportunity;
3. probability of at least one effective use after matched opportunity counts;
4. a reasonable shrinkage/Bayesian or empirical-Bayes estimate of
   effective-use rate, if useful and implementable without oracle leakage.

Pay particular attention to artifacts with ZERO effective uses.

Determine whether they are:

    genuinely inert

or disproportionately:

    young / underexposed / rarely drawn.

Do not optimize the policy retrospectively for CFR.

The purpose is diagnostic: determine whether the current I2 semantics match
the name EFFECTIVE_USAGE.

If absolute counts remain defensible, retain them.

If opportunity-normalized credit is clearly more faithful and can be
maintained ONLINE using learner-visible information only, propose the minimal
repair BEFORE viewing Gen-1 policy outcomes.

Any repair changes the preregistration and therefore must be frozen and hashed
before Gen-1 begins.

PHASE 6 - RESULT CLASSIFICATION
======================================================================

Classify the outcome using the strongest mechanically defensible category.

Candidate categories include:

SELECTIVE_MEMORY_EFFECT
    A retention policy changes future CFR and the retained population
    demonstrably contains more reusable/effective capability.

DIVERSITY_PRESERVATION_EFFECT
    A policy changes CFR primarily by preserving behavioural or
    mutational-neighborhood diversity.

REDUNDANCY_COMPRESSION_EFFECT
    Removing genuinely redundant artifacts creates measurable future
    capability or compute efficiency without sacrificing reach.

AGE_OR_RESIDENCE_EFFECT
    Apparent memory-quality effects are predominantly explained by
    artifact age/exposure.

TRAJECTORY_EFFECT_UNRESOLVED
    CFR changes but measured retained-library properties cannot explain
    the effect; policy-induced search trajectory remains the leading
    unresolved mechanism.

POLICY_DIFFERENCE_NO_CAPABILITY_EFFECT
    Policies produce substantially different libraries and trajectories
    but no meaningful CFR difference.

RETENTION_POLICY_EFFECT_NEAR_ZERO
    Gen-1 is adequately powered and places a useful upper bound on
    retention-policy value.

Do not force the result into one of these names if another description is more
mechanically accurate.

PHASE 7 - EXPERIENCE HALF-LIFE
======================================================================

Use the new persistence ledger to produce the first empirical
survival/recurrence analysis of executable experience.

For each artifact determine, where observable:

  - admission time;
  - draw opportunities;
  - first effective use;
  - last effective use;
  - time between effective uses;
  - whether usefulness recurs after long dormancy;
  - age at retirement;
  - whether a retired artifact would later have become useful under another
    observed policy/counterfactual replay where legitimately measurable;
  - task-family distance between uses.

Estimate quantities of the form:

    P(future effective reuse |
      artifact age,
      previous effective uses,
      draw opportunities,
      behaviour family,
      genotype properties,
      task distance)

Produce an empirical EXPERIENCE HALF-LIFE or, if the distribution does not
admit a meaningful half-life, a survival/hazard representation that does.

Ask specifically whether usefulness has:

  - short-lived decay;
  - heavy tails;
  - resurrection events;
  - distinct artifact subpopulations.

Do not fit an elaborate learned eviction policy in this generation.

The objective is to determine whether forgetting could eventually be based on
an empirically measured hazard rather than a hand-selected retirement
threshold.

PHASE 8 - MEMORY TRANSPLANT DESIGN
======================================================================

Do NOT automatically spend another large experimental budget.

Instead, based on Gen-1 results, design the next falsification experiment:

    MEMORY TRANSPLANT / RESIDUAL CAPABILITY TEST

Concept:

Take mature libraries produced under the competing Gen-1 retention policies.
Freeze them.
Expose them to a fresh held-out challenge battery under otherwise matched
conditions.

Ask whether libraries created by a superior retention policy carry
transferable future capability when separated from the exact developmental
trajectory that created them.

The intended distinction is:

    GOOD POLICY -> LUCKY DEVELOPMENTAL TRAJECTORY

versus

    GOOD POLICY -> BETTER PERSISTENT SUBSTRATE
                   -> FUTURE TRANSFERABLE CAPABILITY

Return a concrete design, power estimate, stop conditions and expected cost.

Do not execute this follow-on unless it is extremely cheap and unambiguously
authorized by an existing budget. The deliverable for this phase is primarily
the design.

======================================================================
CLAIM DISCIPLINE
======================================================================

Maintain the existing Ergon standard.

In particular:

  - A behaviour fingerprint is not automatically an evolvability fingerprint.
  - Dispersion is not automatically utility.
  - Retention is not automatically memory quality.
  - Correlation between a mechanism variable and CFR does not establish
    mediation.
  - Policy-induced trajectory differences remain part of the causal treatment.
  - A null at n = 30 means something very different from the previously
    rejected null at n = 5.
  - A policy can generate radically different libraries while leaving
    capability unchanged.
  - A winning policy need not imply that the selected artifacts themselves
    carry portable capability.

Negative results are first-class results.
