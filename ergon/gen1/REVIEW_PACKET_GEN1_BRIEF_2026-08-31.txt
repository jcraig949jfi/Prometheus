3. FINDING 1 -- ATTAINABLE RANGE IS NONTRIVIAL (SECTION 5 SATISFIED)
====================================================================

Section 5 requires, before any policy design, an answer to: CAN TWO
LEGITIMATE POLICIES ACTUALLY PRODUCE DIFFERENT LIBRARIES? If all
reasonable policies collapse to nearly the same 64 entries, Gen-1 is
bounded at approximately zero and section 21 says STOP.

Answered read-only from data already on disk, at zero compute cost,
using library_size_at_start across the 290 committed M1 rows:

    lineages .................................. 5
    tasks per lineage ......................... 58
    library starts ............................ EMPTY (size 0)
    first saturated position, per lineage ..... 16, 15, 16, 16, 16
    tasks post-saturation, per lineage ........ 42, 43, 42, 42, 42
    admissions/task pre-saturation ............ 4.05 mean
    tasks running saturated ................... 211 of 290 (72.8%)
    eviction decisions per lineage, est ....... ~171
    library turnover per lineage, est ......... ~2.67x

Reading. The cap is reached about a quarter of the way through each
lineage, with remarkable consistency across all five, and eviction is
then live for the remaining three quarters. Roughly 171 eviction
decisions occur against a 64-slot library, so the library turns over
about 2.7 times. Two policies making different choices at those
decision points have room to end up with substantially different
libraries.

VERDICT: RETENTION_READOUT_RANGE_NONTRIVIAL. The section 21 stop
condition does not fire on this axis.

CLAIM CEILING, and it matters. Turnover measures the OPPORTUNITY for
divergence. It does NOT show that two legitimate policies will
actually produce different libraries, and it does NOT show that
different libraries move CFR. Two of the three links in that chain are
untested. This is a pre-screen that clears a STOP condition; it is not
a substitute for the full section 5 analysis over reuse, score,
behaviour, age, redundancy and recurrence.

4. FINDING 2 -- EXACT STREAM FREEZING IS ARCHITECTURALLY IMPOSSIBLE
====================================================================

Section 6 calls stream freezing load-bearing, states the preferred
design as one canonical candidate stream replayed through several
policies, and then says: if exact stream freezing is not possible,
STATE THAT LIMITATION BEFORE RUNNING. This is that statement.

It is not possible under the frozen D-5 search core, and the reason is
mechanical rather than a matter of care.

The library is read at exactly one place, m1_rx's fresh():

    if extra_pool and rng.random() < 0.5:
        return mutate(extra_pool[rng.randrange(len(extra_pool))], rng)
    return mutate(rep[rng.randrange(len(rep))], rng)

Two consequences follow immediately.

  a. rng.randrange(len(extra_pool)) selects an index into the library.
     Two policies holding different libraries return DIFFERENT
     GENOTYPES from the same random draw.

  b. mutate() consumes a VARIABLE number of rng values. The applicable
     mutation classes are filtered by program length (INSERT and
     DUP_BLOCK require len < MAX_LEN, DELETE requires len > 1, SWAP
     requires len >= 2), and the classes themselves consume different
     amounts -- OP_REPLACE draws an index, an opcode and a typed
     operand, while DUP_BLOCK draws a block length and two positions.

So a different seed genotype produces a different rng consumption
count, the shared random stream desynchronises at the first divergent
library draw, and every subsequent candidate in the run differs. The
divergence is immediate and irrecoverable.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE DESIGN. The clean diagram in section 6 --

    CANDIDATE STREAM
          |
          +--> policy A
          +--> policy B

-- is unavailable without modifying the frozen search core, which
section 22 forbids and which would break the identity gate that makes
M1 reproduce M0c-RX exactly on an empty library.

Gen-1 therefore falls to the fallback the brief names: paired
same-seed runs with demonstrated stream comparability. Attack F1
(POLICY CHANGES INCOMING STREAM) is consequently NOT a risk to be
mitigated but a CERTAINTY to be bounded. The honest framing is that
attribution runs to the policy-plus-its-downstream-stream jointly, and
the packet should say so rather than claim isolation it cannot have.

What can still be done, and should be preregistered:

  - report stream divergence as a measured quantity (first divergent
    evaluation index, fraction of shared candidates) rather than
    assuming it away
  - pair on task and seed so the comparison is within-task
  - treat the shuffled-history arm as the calibrator for how much CFR
    moves under stream divergence WITHOUT a content difference, since
    D-5 already showed that arm diverged (94/290 rows differ) and
    re-converged to 100% retention of the advantage

That last point is the strongest available control and it already
exists in the inherited evidence.

5. FINDING 3 -- I2 AS SPECIFIED IS AGE-WEIGHTING, NOT UTILITY
====================================================================

Section 7's I2 is "USAGE-WEIGHTED RETENTION: prefer artifacts that
have actually been reused by future mutation draws."

Under the frozen draw rule, usage carries no quality signal, because
the draw is UNIFORM:

    extra_pool[rng.randrange(len(extra_pool))]

Every resident entry is equally likely on every library draw. Usage
count for each entry is therefore Binomial(n_draws, 1/64) -- the same
distribution for every entry -- and the only systematic differentiator
is RESIDENCE TIME.

The arithmetic, from the frozen defaults psize=32, immigrant=0.10 and
the top ladder rung of 30000 evaluations:

    generations ..................... ~937
    immigrant draws ................. ~3000   (32 x 0.10 per gen)
    of which from the library ....... ~1500   (the 0.5 branch)
    draws per resident entry ........ ~23     (1500 / 64)
    noise on that count ............. ~5      (sqrt 23)

So differences in usage between co-resident entries are roughly one
standard deviation of pure multinomial noise. Usage separates entries
only to the extent their residence times differ.

CONSEQUENCE. I2 as literally specified is approximately AGE-WEIGHTED
RETENTION, which is close to the INVERSE of I0's most-recent-first
eviction. That may still be a worthwhile arm -- an inverted-age
comparator is informative -- but it should be named for what it is,
and it is not a utility signal.

This is attack F5 (USAGE CIRCULARITY) arriving structurally rather
than statistically, and it is better fixed in design than disclosed
in analysis.

TWO REPAIRS A REVIEWER SHOULD CHOOSE BETWEEN. Both stay inside the
learner-visible boundary of section 13:

  R1  redefine usage as EFFECTIVE usage -- draws whose mutated child
      scored better than the parent population median, or better than
      the drawn artifact itself. This is learner-visible at draw time
      and does carry a quality signal.
  R2  keep usage as drawn-count but rename the arm AGE-WEIGHTED and
      pair it explicitly against I0 as an order-inversion control.

R1 is the scientifically interesting one. R2 is the honest minimum. I
would not run I2 as currently worded under either name.

6. FINDING 4 -- SECTION 8 HAS A LONG ENOUGH WINDOW TO TEST FORGETTING
====================================================================

Retirement needs a saturated library and enough subsequent
opportunities for a never-reused rule to fire. The saturation profile
in section 3 supplies both: saturation at position ~16 of 58 leaves
42 tasks in which a retirement rule can act and its consequences can
be observed.

The brief's example rule -- retire never-reused artifacts after N
future opportunities -- must have N preregistered before CFR is seen.
Given ~23 draws per entry per task at the top rung, N should be
expressed in DRAWS or in TASKS, not left ambiguous, because those two
units differ by a factor of about 23 and the rule's aggressiveness is
entirely determined by which is meant.

7. WHAT THE BRIEF GETS RIGHT, EXPLICITLY
====================================================================

Recorded because a review packet that only lists objections
misrepresents the document.

  - Persistence is correctly sequenced FIRST, and correctly specified
    as OBSERVATIONAL ONLY with a named failure state
    (PERSISTENCE_INSTRUMENT_SUSPECT). Same-seed parity plus round-trip
    plus oracle-leakage checks is the right validation triple.
  - Schema minimality (section 4D) is the correct instinct and the
    opposite of the usual failure.
  - Section 12's SAME-LIBRARY-SIZE control forecloses the most obvious
    cheat, an arm winning by retaining more.
  - Section 13 draws the online/retrospective line exactly where it
    belongs: retrospective analysis may examine future reuse, the
    online policy may not.
  - Section 14's vocabulary includes CONSUMED_NO_EFFECT for an
    artifact that is stored and repeatedly retrieved yet changes
    nothing on removal. That distinction is the whole point of a
    leave-one-out and most memory work elides it.
  - Section 24 pre-commits to the negative result being equally
    valuable, and says what it would mean: the signal is content
    accumulation rather than memory management.

8. RISKS THE BRIEF DOES NOT YET COVER
====================================================================

  U1  ADMISSION AND EVICTION ARE CONFLATED. The brief calls the
      treatment "admission/retirement policy" throughout, but these
      are separable levers with different failure modes. D-5's
      admission rule (solver + 4 behaviour-distinct) and its eviction
      rule (most-recent-first) can be varied independently, and an
      arm that changes both cannot attribute to either. Recommend a
      factorial or an explicit statement that only eviction varies in
      Gen-1.

  U2  NO MDE IS STATED. Section 19 requires an MDE at freeze but the
      brief does not supply one, and the inherited precedent is
      unhelpful: D-5's G4 cleared its 10pp floor by 0.95pp against SE
      3.4pp. A retention effect is plausibly SMALLER than the
      accumulation effect it modulates, so the design may need more
      lineages rather than more evaluations. Compute the SE before
      choosing the line.

  U3  FIVE LINEAGES MAY BE THE BINDING n. The pairing unit is the
      task, but retention policy acts at the LIBRARY level, and the
      library is a lineage-scoped object. If the true independent unit
      is the lineage there are 5 of them, not 290 rows. This is the
      same wrong-unit error that once inflated a precision estimate
      57x elsewhere in this programme. Decide the unit before running.

  U4  SECTION 17 IS CORRECTLY SEQUENCED BUT UNREACHABLE UNTIL
      PERSISTENCE LANDS. The Family-B shortest-equivalent pre-screen
      is to use "the admissible persisted search-discovered corpus
      from Gen-1", which does not exist yet. That is the right
      dependency; a reviewer should simply expect section 17 to be
      the last thing in the generation, not a parallel track.

  U5  CROSS-BENCH OUTPUT MAY LEAK SELECTION. Section 16's Harmonia
      corpus would be emitted for artifacts found "consistently
      retained, reused, load-bearing". Selecting the corpus on the
      Gen-1 outcome and then handing it to another bench propagates
      Gen-1's selection relation into Harmonia's population. If
      Harmonia's question is representation-conditional reachability,
      that could be exactly the wrong sample. Recommend emitting the
      FULL persisted corpus with retention status as a FIELD, not a
      filter.
