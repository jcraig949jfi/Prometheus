## Phase 1 -- what scored fossils imply (exact)

A fossil (x, s) on a length-L landscape is the constraint d_H(x, t) = m,
m = L(1 - s), an integer. One fossil: C(L, m) targets; no bit fixed unless
m in {0, L}. Two fossils differing on k positions (set D), agreeing on A:
the score difference identifies b1 = (k + m1 - m2)/2 = how many of D the
target takes from x1, and a = m1 - b1 mismatches on A -- COUNTS, not
positions. Bits on D are fixed only when b1 in {0, k}; on A only when
a in {0, L-k}. Odd (k + m1 - m2), or b1/a out of range, is a
CONTRADICTION. N fossils: partition positions into blocks by their column
pattern; the mismatch counts are an integer linear system in u_B (ones per
block); the feasible set is the union of solutions weighted by
prod C(n_B, u_B); a block is FIXED when every solution has u_B in {0, n_B},
COUNT-KNOWN when u_B is the same strictly-interior value in every
solution, AMBIGUOUS otherwise. P(t_j = 1) under a uniform prior is exact
per block. Controls: positive (one-bit-apart fossils fix that bit and
nothing else; a basis of L+1 fossils identifies t), ambiguous (random
pairs leave 2^7..2^26 targets), contradictory (the same candidate at two
distances admits no target; a non-integer distance is refused), baseline
(block solver == brute-force enumeration on counts, fixed bits and
per-position frequencies over 40 random instances), order invariance
(all 24 permutations of 4 fossils identical; a duplicate adds nothing).

## Licensing

S2 is NOT_LICENSED on the current corpus:

1. On the operational source there is no combinable information at all
   (one duplicate pair). Emitting S2 rows from the raw ledger would use a
   source the cutover just retired.
2. Even where the raw accounting shows multi-fossil landscapes, the
   information S1 did not use is ONE fixed bit per landscape (the S1 flip
   itself) plus a count constraint from C. That is what two fossils one
   bit apart and one random fossil can give; the mathematics says so and
   the census confirms it. An F2 built on it would differ from the
   one-bit rule by less than one bit's expected score on 7 of 12
   landscapes. That is dilution, not a treatment.
3. Nothing may be manufactured to change (1) or (2) before this scarcity
   is on the record (this file).

What would license S2 honestly: fossils that are NOT one bit apart and
NOT uniform-random -- e.g. a landscape carrying two candidates that
disagree on a SMALL set D with a known score difference (b1 in {0, k}),
which fixes |D| bits at once. Such pairs arise from any policy that
proposes bounded-distance neighbours of a fossil and records the result;
they do not arise from S1's single flip or from uniform draws. That is a
design decision for the operator, not a row to emit now.
