0. ORIENTATION (self-contained; no prior context assumed)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Prometheus runs a CONSERVED-RISK ADMISSION LEDGER. A scientific test purchases
a threshold K at price 1/K from a permanent lifetime budget ALPHA_LIFE = 0.1
(~100 tests EVER at the scientific floor K_MIN = 1000). Purchases are
irrevocable. Admission evidence must derive from a public randomness beacon
whose value did not exist until after the registration was committed and
externally anchored.

THE SUBSTRATE. stackvm-v1 is a metered stack/register VM whose design charter
makes EVERY BYTE SEQUENCE A LEGAL PROGRAM (opcode = byte % 33; operand bytes
past the end read 0; empty-stack pop = 0; div/mod by zero pushes 0). It is
bit-deterministic in (code, inputs, max_steps). A historical corpus of 183,856
hash-chained records holds 71,683 executions, 30,121 artifacts, 40,796 lineage
edges, two driver eras, and 7 successes. Five candidate phenomena were frozen
from it in an earlier pass and are currently held as
PRESERVED_UNREGISTERABLE_UNDER_CURRENT_ADMISSION_LANGUAGE.

THE QUESTION THIS PASS INVESTIGATED, exactly: can an already-selected
historical artifact have a non-vacuous admission null WITHOUT reproducing the
process that caused it to become interesting?

2. THE THEOREM, AND THE THREE WAYS I HAD IT WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let D be the selection sigma-field -- EVERYTHING knowable before a candidate
became a candidate, including human decisions and adaptive mining. Let C be
D-measurable (chosen by any rule, however hindsight-laden). Let U be
post-commit beacon randomness, independent of D. Let theta be the ENTIRE test
object: statistic, threshold, decoder from beacon bytes to intervention,
support and law of U, conditioning event, tie rule, censoring rule, stopping
rule.

  THEOREM 1. If P_U(phi_theta(c,U)=1) <= alpha AT THE REALIZED c, then
  P(reject) <= alpha for ANY selection rule producing C.

CORRECTION 1 -- "sup over c" was the wrong premise. It is simultaneously TOO
STRONG (sup_c = 1 for most tests worth running, so it rejects valid tests) and,
as practised, TOO WEAK (registrants verify on average and sincerely believe
they have it pointwise). The right premise is the level at the REALIZED
candidate -- and it is certifiable (section 5).

CORRECTION 2 -- MY CENTRAL DIAGNOSTIC WAS FALSE. I claimed a clean dichotomy:
selection is free when the null's randomness is created after the candidate is
fixed, and fatal when the null is a measure over the space the candidate was
selected from. FRESHNESS OF U IS NECESSARY, NEVER SUFFICIENT. Three
counterexamples, all realizable here:
  * SELECTED SUPPORT -- draw a fresh beacon-uniform byte position, but narrow
    the intervention class to "positions in the executed region" after mining
    the corpus. U is fresh, independent, applied to a fixed c, and the
    rejection probability goes to 1.
  * SELECTED STATISTIC/THRESHOLD -- fresh beacon inputs, threshold set at a
    corpus quantile. No base measure anywhere; the threshold is D-measurable.
  * SELECTED CONDITIONING EVENT -- condition on the candidate's archive cell,
    when the archive is itself a selection device on that event.
In all three a registrant can truthfully say "my null uses randomness created
after the candidate was fixed" and be wrong.

  REPLACEMENT CRITERION:
  SELECTION OVER c IS FREE. SELECTION OVER theta IS NOT.
  theta must be committed and hash-anchored BEFORE the beacon, and the level
  must hold at the realized c.

CORRECTION 3 -- GENERICITY PREMISES ARE BANNED. Practical "pointwise" proofs
here almost always hide a "for a generic program" step. Because every byte
sequence is a legal program, the corpus is a search engine for the exceptional
set. MEASURED EXACTLY on the real interpreter, enumerating the entire
intervention support (64 positions x 255 replacements = 16,320 mutants), for
the test "reject iff a single-byte perturbation leaves (steps,output,halt)
unchanged":

     halts immediately (JMP self + padding)   alpha = 0.9694
     random 64B                               alpha = 0.8398
     random 64B (other seed)                  alpha = 0.8072
     loop-heavy                               alpha = 0.1837
     all-NOP (every byte executed)            alpha = 0.1176
     dense arithmetic (ADD chain)             alpha = 0.0243

  RANGE 0.024 TO 0.969 -- A FACTOR OF 40. No single pointwise claim covers
  them. Note the direction that matters: a GENERIC random program is already
  ~84% inert, so a test premised on "few inert bytes" is mispriced for
  essentially EVERY program, not merely for adversarially chosen ones.

  ADMISSIBILITY RULE: a level argument is admissible only if it is (i) exact
  finite enumeration over supp(U); (ii) an exchangeability/orbit argument with
  an EXPLICIT STABILIZER CLAUSE and a conservative tie rule (>=, never a seeded
  tie-break); or (iii) a per-candidate certificate. Any proof containing
  "generic", "typical", or "almost every" is an average-case bound and is
  rejected.

3. WHAT SURVIVES: THE CANDIDATE-CONDITIONAL ROUTE (measured)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Specimen chosen as the MAXIMUM of n draws ON THE VERY STATISTIC LATER REPORTED
-- the most hindsight-laden selection possible -- then a fresh randomized
intervention on clones, assignment drawn after the specimen is fixed, inference
by permuting the assignment labels:

     selection depth n = 1        -> rejection 0.0533
     selection depth n = 100      -> rejection 0.0553
     selection depth n = 1,000    -> rejection 0.0567
     selection depth n = 200,000  -> rejection 0.0647

  LEVEL IS FLAT IN SELECTION INTENSITY (endpoints ~2 SE apart at 1,500
  trials). Power: 0.215 / 0.585 / 0.979 at effects 0.5 / 1.0 / 2.0 -- not
  vacuous.

  THE BOUNDARY INSIDE THIS CLASS: report the best of 20 observables on the
  same specimen -> 0.6475. Let the beacon select the observable -> 0.0525.
  CLASS CC IS FREE WITH RESPECT TO SPECIMEN SELECTION, NOT TEST SELECTION.

4. WHAT DOES NOT SURVIVE: RARITY, AND WHY MY REPLAY WAS THE WRONG OBJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CORRECTION 4. I proposed a single "selection-replicating null" whose unit is
"the artifact the complete pipeline nominates". THAT IS NOT AN ARTIFACT TEST.

Let the pipeline propose X_1..X_N and nominate C* = argmax_i S(X_i). If the
null is the law of a fresh pipeline's nominee, then C* and C'* are i.i.d.
draws from that law, so the p-value is EXACTLY Uniform(0,1) for every artifact
and every statistic. It never conditions on C*. Its only power is against
"the pipeline is not the pipeline you modelled."

  D1 REPLICATION NULL ......... tests pipeline misspecification; unit of
                                inference is THE PIPELINE; never licenses an
                                artifact claim
  D2 CONDITIONAL/SELECTIVE .... tests the artifact; valid where the selection
                                is a measurable event
  D3 RARITY AMONG OUTPUTS ..... PROVABLY VACUOUS when the statistic is
     with T measurable wrt S     measurable wrt the selection functional:
                                 conditional on nomination T(C*) = max_i S,
                                 Var(T|S) = 0, p ~ Uniform with power exactly
                                 alpha against EVERY alternative.
                                 Escape requires an ORTHOGONALITY CERTIFICATE.

THE INFLATION LAW FOR INCOMPLETE REPLAY. Matching operator chain, generation
count and population size fixes only the proposal kernel. Omitting a stage that
retained competition fraction q inflates the level by ~1/q, and omissions
compose multiplicatively. Measured, true search depth N = 100:

     replay assumes n = 100 (correct) -> 0.052    <-- target 0.05
     replay assumes n = 50            -> 0.100
     replay assumes n = 20            -> 0.220
     replay assumes n = 10            -> 0.338
     replay assumes n = 1             -> 0.830

  UNDERCOUNTING THE HISTORICAL SEARCH IS DIRECTLY AND MONOTONICALLY
  ANTI-CONSERVATIVE. Two-stage selection shows the same law: replaying only
  the inner stage gives 0.506 against 0.0495 for the full replay.
  REQUIREMENT: every registered rarity claim publishes a RETENTION LEDGER --
  per stage, cardinality in and out, hence the factor, hence
  K_eff = K / prod(1/q_i).

5. THE CONSTRUCTIVE RESULT: REGISTERED CONDITIONAL LEVEL CERTIFICATE (RCLC)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Theorem 1's premise is NOT unverifiable. At registration c is already known (it
is D-measurable) and the law of U is declared. So

      alpha(c) = P_U( phi_theta(c,U) = 1 )

is a deterministic functional of two known objects. Evaluating it requires NO
FRESH RANDOMNESS -- the beacon is needed only for the single real draw. For
single-byte interventions the support is 16,320 and alpha(c) is computed
EXACTLY, in seconds (section 2's table IS that computation).

  RCLC: before the beacon, publish hash(c), hash(theta), and alpha_hat(c) --
  exact where enumerable, else a Clopper-Pearson upper bound -- plus the hash
  of the certifying computation. THE COURT DEBITS max(alpha_nominal,
  alpha_hat_upper). If alpha_hat_upper > alpha_nominal the test is mispriced
  and must be re-specified before the beacon.

  ECONOMIC COROLLARY: certifying a 1/K tail costs ~K evaluations, so
  CERTIFICATION COST LOWER-BOUNDS THE PRICE. A test you cannot afford to
  certify is a test you cannot afford to buy. The exposure this closes is
  large: a test priced at 2.4e-4 whose true level at a searchable candidate is
  ~0.97 would consume NINE TIMES THE ENTIRE LIFETIME BUDGET in one purchase.

6. HUMAN ATTENTION, AND THE CORRECTION THAT MATTERS MOST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CORRECTION 5. I argued: the five candidates were nominated by adaptive
model-driven mining; that is not a function of preserved inputs; therefore
rarity claims about them are retrospectively unstateable. THE MIDDLE STEP IS A
NON SEQUITUR.

Selective inference does not replay the analyst. It conditions on the selection
EVENT.

  THEOREM 2. If the selection S is measurable with respect to ANY sigma-field
  G, a null computed conditional on G has valid level for EVERY selection rule
  in G. One never needs to know which rule was used, and conditioning on a
  SUPERSET is conservative, not invalid.

So the operative question is not "was the nomination rule a function?" but
"IS THE SIGMA-FIELD THE MINER CONSULTED BOUNDED STRICTLY INSIDE sigma(D)?"

For the five frozen candidates the answer is factual and it is NO: the
archaeological mining READ THE RAW JSONL LEDGER FILES DIRECTLY with ad-hoc
code, bypassing any query interface, so no access log bounds G. Correct status:

      INADMISSIBLE FOR LACK OF A BOUNDED SELECTION SIGMA-FIELD

Factual and REMEDIABLE, not metaphysical. Representability of human attention:

  contemporaneous logged rule .... recovers the rule IF one existed; none was
  reconstructed ranking function . recovers a MODERN rule -- tests a DIFFERENT
                                   claim; it did not generate the artifact
  blinded replay panel ........... recovers a fresh nomination distribution,
                                   not the original UNBLINDED decision
  external frozen procedure ...... fixes the future, recovers nothing past

10. LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  L1 The toys are Gaussian/permutation models, not stackvm executions. They
     establish the LAWS (inflation, correction, level-flatness); they do not
     establish magnitudes for any real stackvm claim.
  L2 The RCLC was demonstrated only for single-byte interventions, where the
     support is small enough to enumerate. Richer intervention laws need
     Clopper-Pearson bounds and the ~K certification cost is then real.
  L3 Theorem 2 (G-conditional validity) is stated and used but not
     independently re-derived here; it is standard selective inference and
     should be checked by the reviewer against a source they trust.
  L4 The 0.0533 -> 0.0647 drift across selection depth is ~2 SE. I report it as
     flat; a reviewer may reasonably want 10x the trials before accepting that.
  L5 I did NOT open the observer store index (var/observer/index.sqlite), which
     appears live. If it turns out to log queries, section 6's factual finding
     for FUTURE mining changes -- though not for the five candidates, whose
     mining bypassed it.

  CLOSING
  The mission warned against two opposite errors, and both were live risks.

  The first is believing that a historical specimen can be shown RARE by
  comparing it to fresh random draws. It cannot: that null is a statement about
  the specimen's provenance, which selection falsifies for every selected
  specimen, and the naive version of the repair turns out to test the pipeline
  rather than the artifact.

  The second is concluding that retrospective candidates can never be tested at
  all. They can. A specimen chosen by unlimited hindsight remains a perfectly
  valid subject for a NEW randomized causal experiment, and the level of that
  experiment is flat in how extremely the specimen was selected -- measured
  here across five orders of magnitude of selection depth.

  The line between those two is the deliverable, and it is not where I first
  drew it. My original criterion turned on where the randomness came from.
  The correct criterion turns on what was committed: the specimen may be chosen
  by hindsight; the test may not.
