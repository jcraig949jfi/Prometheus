## RAW INTERPRETATIONS
- [spec] I1: A decoder that is driven by slowly injected noise and relaxes between injections is a sandpile: unsatisfied checks are stress, bit flips are topplings. The decoding threshold (a phase transition) may be the self-organized attractor of such a driven decoder rather than a parameter someone tunes.
- [spec] I2: A system rewarded for throughput and punished for decoding failure will spend redundancy down until it sits at the edge of its code's threshold; the rate-noise phase boundary becomes an operating point reached by self-organization, and failures there come in bursts.
- [spec] I3: A medium at criticality has long-range correlations and so spreads any imprint nonlocally, which is what redundancy does; a critical medium may therefore act as an unengineered distributed code whose recoverability after local damage peaks at the critical point.
- [spec] I4: SOC is a noise source, not a mechanism of protection: a critical world produces power-law bursts of damage, and no fixed-depth code or interleaver suffices against scale-free bursts; codes that survive in a critical world must be multi-scale, and the need for a hierarchy of redundancy is the signature.
- [spec] I5: The abelian sandpile's recurrent configurations form a finite group equal to integer vectors modulo the graph Laplacian lattice; sandpile relaxation therefore computes a coset representative, i.e. a syndrome, for free. A sandpile is a syndrome computer for a lattice code whose codewords are toppling vectors.
- [spec] I6: Codes conserve parity the way sandpiles conserve grains: errors create defects in pairs and local decoders move and annihilate them. Bulk conservation is what makes sandpiles critical; parity conservation might make driven local decoders critical, and breaking it should remove the criticality.
- [spec] I7: A population whose genomes carry evolvable redundancy under mutation has an error threshold (a phase transition in heredity); if redundancy costs fitness, the population self-organizes to the edge of that threshold, with extinction and sweep events of scale-free size.

## CANDIDATE PHYSICS
- [spec] M1 (dynamical law): Syndrome sandpile: on a sparse Tanner graph, noise is injected one bit at a time and a threshold bit-flip rule (flip a variable when at least t of its checks are unsatisfied) runs to quiescence between injections. The stationary residual-error density self-tunes toward the ensemble's bit-flip decoding threshold, because below it relaxation removes errors and above it stalled trapping sets accumulate and flips create new errors.
- [spec] M2 (learning pressure): Redundancy thermostat: a storage agent lowers its protection (scrub effort or transmit power) by a small step after every successful epoch and raises it by a large step after every failure, with residual errors persisting between epochs. The operating point is driven to the edge of the scrubbing threshold and failures arrive in heavy-tailed bursts because persistent residue couples consecutive epochs.
- [spec] M3 (representation): Critical medium as distributed code: a pattern is imprinted on a threshold medium by biased driving; after erasing a random fraction of sites, the imprint is recovered by a linear readout of the remaining sites. Recoverability peaks near the critical point of the medium because correlations spread the imprint without the medium forgetting it.
- [spec] M4 (computational primitive): Sandpile syndrome computer: take the graph Laplacian lattice as a lattice code (codewords = integer toppling vectors times the reduced Laplacian). Relaxing a received grain vector to its recurrent representative computes its coset, i.e. its syndrome, by purely local threshold dynamics; decoding reduces to a lookup from recurrent configuration to minimum-weight grain error.
- [spec] M5 (selection mechanism): Error-threshold seeking: genomes carry an evolvable repetition factor on their functional bits; redundancy costs replication speed. The population evolves toward the minimum redundancy that keeps it below its heredity error threshold, and extinction or sweep events near that edge have scale-free size distributions.
- [spec] M6 (error-correction mechanism): Failure-size criticality at threshold: the number of wrong bits left after iterative decoding is bimodal away from threshold (zero or large) and broad, possibly power-law, exactly at threshold, so the size distribution of decoding failures is an order-parameter probe of the code's phase.
- [spec] M7 (memory structure): Scale-free burst memory: marks written into a sandpile are erased when their site topples. At criticality erasures come in spatially compact bursts of every size, so the benefit of scattering an erasure-code's symbols (interleaving) over placing them contiguously grows with system size, whereas in a subcritical pile the benefit saturates once the spread exceeds the characteristic avalanche size.
- [spec] M8 (computational primitive): Critical slowing down as noise estimator: the number of iterations an iterative decoder needs grows sharply as noise approaches threshold, so an agent can read its own distance-to-threshold from convergence time and adjust protection without ever observing a failure.
- [impl] M9 (causal constraint): Parity-conserving lazy decoder: on a planar surface-code-like lattice, edge flips create defect pairs; a lazy local decoder acts only where a neighbourhood holds at least theta defects, and its moves either annihilate adjacent pairs or hop a defect (parity preserving). Defects leave only at the boundary. Parity conservation plus threshold laziness plus boundary-only loss should make decoding avalanches scale-free; allowing bulk single-defect deletion should destroy it.
- [spec] M10 (developmental process): Failure-driven code growth: a code grows by adding a new check node over the bits involved in each decoding failure; the grown Tanner graph self-organizes to a structure whose threshold sits just above the ambient noise, and failure sizes become scale-free as the code approaches that edge.
- [spec] M11 (information bottleneck): Coarse-graining as decoding: majority-rule block coarse-graining is a concatenated repetition decoder; its fixed-point structure (flow to all-correct or to random) is the threshold of the concatenated code, so a system that repeatedly coarse-grains its own state keeps information iff noise is below an RG critical point.
- [spec] M12 (mutation operator): Avalanche mutation: mutations hit genomes as sandpile avalanches mapped onto the genome layout (correlated bursts with scale-free sizes) rather than iid with the same mean rate. Populations with evolvable symbol layout evolve multi-scale interleaving, which iid-mutated populations never do.
- [spec] M13 (world rule): Throughput heats the channel: the world's noise rate rises with the information rate pushed through it and relaxes slowly; a transmitter maximising goodput drives the channel to the code's threshold, where noise fluctuations are amplified by the steep failure curve.
- [spec] M14 (organism architecture): Split-phase organism: an organism that must both detect weak signals (favoured at criticality) and retain states (favoured in an ordered, code-protected phase) evolves a partition into a critical sensing module and an ordered, redundant memory module, when each module has its own control parameter; with one shared parameter it settles at a compromise that is worse at both.
- [spec] M15 (interaction law): Miscorrection contagion: a network of storage nodes each scrubs its own codeword, but a miscorrection at one node leaks corrupted symbols into its neighbours' codewords. The branching ratio of miscorrection set by code distance and coupling determines whether failures stay local or cascade, and repair policies that reduce scrub effort after quiet periods poise the network at branching ratio one.
