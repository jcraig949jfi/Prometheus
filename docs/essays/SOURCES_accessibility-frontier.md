## The closest experimental relative (r1)

    Richard E. Lenski, Charles Ofria, Robert T. Pennock, Christoph Adami
    "The evolutionary origin of complex features"
    Nature 423, 139-144 (8 May 2003); doi:10.1038/nature01568
    https://www.nature.com/articles/nature01568   (full text paywalled)

The essay says: digital organisms evolved a complex logic function (EQU) in 23 of 50
populations when its simpler components were also rewarded, and the feature "never
evolved when simpler functions were not rewarded."

**VERIFIED with one caveat.** The abstract (PubMed 12736677) supports the mechanism
claims used in the essay: "complex functions evolved by building on simpler functions
that had evolved earlier, provided that these were also selectively favoured", and
"in some cases, mutations that were deleterious when they appeared served as
stepping-stones". The phrase "never evolved when simpler functions were not
rewarded" is quoted from the paper as reproduced in a secondary source (The Panda's
Thumb, 2014, quoting the authors), which also reports "23 lineages that evolved to
perform EQU". I could not read the full text directly. The essay therefore attributes
the sentence to the authors "in their words" and gives 23 of 50 for the standard
treatment; it does NOT state a population count for the EQU-only treatment, because
I could not verify that count from a primary page.

## Evolutionary accessibility in real landscapes (r2, r3, r5)

    Daniel M. Weinreich, Nigel F. Delaney, Mark A. DePristo, Daniel L. Hartl
    "Darwinian evolution can follow only very few mutational paths to fitter proteins"
    Science 312, 111-114 (7 April 2006); doi:10.1126/science.1123539
    PubMed 16601193

**VERIFIED.** Five point mutations in a beta-lactamase allele; 120 possible
trajectories; "102 trajectories are inaccessible to Darwinian selection" because four
of the five mutations fail to increase resistance in some combinations. The essay's
"102 of 120" is the paper's own figure.

    Frank J. Poelwijk, Daniel J. Kiviet, Daniel M. Weinreich, Sander J. Tans
    "Empirical fitness landscapes reveal accessible evolutionary paths"
    Nature 445, 383-386 (25 January 2007); doi:10.1038/nature05451
    PubMed 17251971

**VERIFIED.** Cited for the framing that intermediates, not endpoints, determine the
course of evolution.

    Jasper Franke, Alexander Kloezer, J. Arjan G. M. de Visser, Joachim Krug
    "Evolutionary accessibility of mutational pathways"
    PLoS Computational Biology 7(8): e1002134 (18 August 2011)
    doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002134; arXiv:1103.2479; PubMed 21876664

**VERIFIED.** Open access. Cited for "evolutionary accessibility" as an established
quantitative term.

## The formal signature of the recorder/invoker pair (r4)

    Frank J. Poelwijk, Sorin Tanase-Nicola, Daniel J. Kiviet, Sander J. Tans
    "Reciprocal sign epistasis is a necessary condition for multi-peaked fitness
     landscapes"
    Journal of Theoretical Biology 272(1), 141-144 (7 March 2011)
    doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2010.12.015; PubMed 21167837

**VERIFIED.** The paper shows that a multi-peaked landscape must contain reciprocal
sign epistasis -- "genetic changes that are separately unfavorable but jointly
advantageous" -- and that the condition is necessary but not sufficient. The essay
uses exactly that characterisation and says "necessary condition"; it does not claim
sufficiency. The Crius pair (recorder -0.001, invoker -0.002, both together +1.138 on
the same streams) fits the definition; the essay calls it "a textbook instance", which
is a description of the measured signs, not a claim that the whole Crius landscape is
multi-peaked.

## Valleys can be crossed: the counter-evidence (r6)

    Arthur W. Covert III, Richard E. Lenski, Claus O. Wilke, Charles Ofria
    "Experiments on the role of deleterious mutations as stepping stones in adaptive
     evolution"
    Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 110(34), E3171-E3178 (2013)
    doi:10.1073/pnas.1313424110; data at doi:10.5061/dryad.4hp5n

**VERIFIED** from the PNAS record and the Dryad data record. Populations in which
deleterious mutations were disallowed reached significantly lower long-term fitness
than controls in which they were allowed, "because some of the deleterious mutations
served as stepping stones across otherwise impassable fitness valleys". Cited in the
essay's second counter-argument against itself. The volume/issue/page numbers are
from the journal record; if a reader finds the issue number differs, the DOI is the
authority.

## Landscapes, ruggedness, holes (r7, r8)

    Stuart A. Kauffman, Simon Levin
    "Towards a general theory of adaptive walks on rugged landscapes"
    Journal of Theoretical Biology 128(1), 11-45 (7 September 1987)
    doi:10.1016/S0022-5193(87)80029-2; PubMed 3431131

**VERIFIED.** The NK model. Cited for ruggedness as a tunable landscape property.

    Sergey Gavrilets
    "Evolution and speciation on holey adaptive landscapes"
    Trends in Ecology & Evolution 12(8), 307-312 (August 1997)
    doi:10.1016/S0169-5347(97)01098-7; PubMed 21238086

**VERIFIED.** The argument that in high-dimensional genotype space the
valley-crossing problem "may be non-existent". Cited as an objection the essay must
survive, and stated as such.

## Neutral networks and navigability (r9, r10, r11)

    Peter Schuster, Walter Fontana, Peter F. Stadler, Ivo L. Hofacker
    "From sequences to shapes and back: a case study in RNA secondary structures"
    Proc. R. Soc. Lond. B 255, 279-284 (22 March 1994)
    doi:10.1098/rspb.1994.0040; PubMed 7517565

**VERIFIED.** "All common structures can be accessed from an arbitrary sequence
through a number of mutations much smaller than the chain length." The essay's
gloss follows this sentence.

    Andreas Wagner
    "Robustness and evolvability: a paradox resolved"
    Proc. R. Soc. B 275(1630), 91-100 (7 January 2008)
    doi:10.1098/rspb.2007.1137; PMC2562401

**VERIFIED.** Phenotypes with large connected neutral networks can be both robust
and evolvable. The essay's one-line gloss is a paraphrase of the abstract.

    Sam F. Greenbury, Ard A. Louis, Sebastian E. Ahnert
    "The structure of genotype-phenotype maps makes fitness landscapes navigable"
    Nature Ecology & Evolution 6(11), 1742-1752 (November 2022)
    doi:10.1038/s41559-022-01867-z; PubMed 36175543; bioRxiv 2021.10.11.463990

**VERIFIED.** For RNA secondary structure, protein tertiary structure and protein
complexes, "even under random fitness assignment, fitness maxima can be reached from
almost any other phenotype without passing through fitness valleys". This is the
strongest form of the essay's first counter-argument and is presented as such.

## How nature crosses assembly valleys (r12, r13)

    Stephen Jay Gould, Elisabeth S. Vrba
    "Exaptation -- a missing term in the science of form"
    Paleobiology 8(1), 4-15 (1982); doi:10.1017/S0094837300004310

**VERIFIED.** Features "non-adapted but available for useful cooptation in
descendants". Cited for dial A of the closing experiment.

    John Gerhart, Marc Kirschner
    "The theory of facilitated variation"
    Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 104(suppl. 1), 8582-8589 (15 May 2007)
    doi:10.1073/pnas.0701035104; PubMed 17494755

**VERIFIED.** Regulatory change in the usage of conserved core components as the
source of most post-Cambrian phenotypic variation. The essay's phrase "large
coordinated phenotypic change available as small regulatory change" is a gloss on
that thesis. The 2005 book (Kirschner & Gerhart, *The Plausibility of Life*, Yale)
is the longer statement and is not separately cited.

## Deception, novelty, credit, options (r14-r17)

    David E. Goldberg
    "Simple genetic algorithms and the minimal, deceptive problem"
    in L. Davis (ed.), Genetic Algorithms and Simulated Annealing,
    Morgan Kaufmann, Los Altos CA, chapter 6, 74-88 (1987)

**VERIFIED** (book chapter; no DOI). The origin of "deception" in genetic algorithms
and of the minimal deceptive problem.

    Joel Lehman, Kenneth O. Stanley
    "Abandoning objectives: evolution through the search for novelty alone"
    Evolutionary Computation 19(2), 189-223 (2011); doi:10.1162/EVCO_a_00025

**VERIFIED.** "Objective functions themselves may actively misdirect search toward
dead ends." Cited for the existence of a whole search tradition built around the
failure the essay describes.

    Marvin Minsky
    "Steps toward artificial intelligence"
    Proceedings of the IRE 49(1), 8-30 (January 1961); doi:10.1109/JRPROC.1961.287775

**VERIFIED.** Contains the section "The credit-assignment problem for learning
systems". The essay's line "accessibility is credit assignment across construction
rather than across time" is the essay's own, not Minsky's.

    Richard S. Sutton, Doina Precup, Satinder Singh
    "Between MDPs and semi-MDPs: a framework for temporal abstraction in
     reinforcement learning"
    Artificial Intelligence 112(1-2), 181-211 (August 1999)
    doi:10.1016/S0004-3702(99)00052-1

**VERIFIED.** The options framework. The essay's reading -- that temporal
abstractions shorten the distance credit must travel -- is a standard motivation for
the framework, not a quotation from it.

## What remains uncited because it is genuinely ours

The capability/accessibility distinction as applied to cognitive primitives, the
"invocation without content" fossil and its generalisation, the assembly-geometry
principle, the hidden axis, the six rulers and the accessibility ratio, and the
controlled-pair experiment are the essay's own constructions and should not be given
borrowed authority. The general phenomenon they describe -- fitness valleys, sign
epistasis, deception, credit assignment -- is not ours and the essay says so.
