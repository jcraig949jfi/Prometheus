Prometheus  /  Physics of Intelligence 2026‑09‑23

Possible fundamentals — not AI architecture

Selective Irreversibility

Intelligence is not thermodynamically efficient forgetting. It is thermodynamically efficient selective irreversibility: destroying distinctions that no longer matter while preserving distinctions that predict, control, or generalize.

Working note · filed under Physics of Intelligence · a hypothesis stated to be killed

What Prometheus is

We build agents that build worlds, organisms and fitness functions — substrates to test intelligence primitives in. A central premise is that human cognitive architecture is one of many possible cognitive architectures, not the reference point — so the primitives worth finding are the ones that survive across substrates that share none of our machinery.

This is a working note from that programme, filed under possible fundamentals rather than AI architecture: a hypothesis written to be attacked, not a result.

That distinction turns the analogy into something we may actually be able to formalize.

Landauer’s principle says that logically irreversible operations — many possible logical states being mapped into fewer states — carry a thermodynamic cost. Reversible computation can in principle avoid that cost until information is actually erased or reset. Experiments have directly demonstrated the kBT ln 2 limit for bit erasure,3,4,5 and more general treatments relate the minimum work cost to the information discarded conditional on the output.6,7

The really interesting connection: abstraction is many-to-one

Suppose an intelligent system observes some gigantic state X and constructs an abstraction

Z = f(X)

Imagine millions of distinct X’s all become:

predator

prime number

unstable trajectory

use this subroutine.

That’s a many-to-one map. The abstraction deliberately makes distinctions disappear.

If the physical implementation really disposes of those distinctions rather than retaining them somewhere as reversible bookkeeping, then the amount erased is approximately related to H(X | Z) — the information about the original state that cannot be recovered from the abstraction.

So an idealized cyclic physical learner suggests something like

Werase ≳ kBT ln 2 · H(X | Z)

with important physical assumptions around how the memory and reset are implemented. That is already remarkably close to the proposed principle.

But now introduce another variable:

Y = future / task / action-relevant structure

A good abstraction doesn’t merely maximize H(X | Z). A random shredder could do that. It tries to make Z very small while preserving I(Z; Y).

And that’s essentially the territory of the Information Bottleneck: find a compressed representation of X that retains information relevant to Y.8,9

Combine Landauer and the Information Bottleneck, and suddenly we get something much more interesting than either separately:

The dial Intelligence ~ preserve useful distinctions while irreversibly eliminating useless ones

That might be one of our missing dials. And it changes the four propositions in useful ways.

1Hallucination as the thermodynamic default

I’d weaken the literal claim but keep the deeper insight.

Thermodynamic entropy and semantic incorrectness aren’t the same thing. A system can produce an extremely ordered, low-entropy, completely false story. So Landauer alone doesn’t imply hallucination.

But an intelligent physical system is unquestionably a nonequilibrium system. Maintaining organized internal states against noise requires continued free-energy expenditure. And maintaining truth-correlated organization requires repeated coupling to the environment.

So I’d replace:

intelligence requires energy to prevent thought dissolving into noise

with:

intelligence requires continued expenditure to maintain task-relevant correlations against degradation, perturbation, and irrelevant state proliferation.

That is much more defensible. And notice the important addition: grounding isn’t merely information coming in. It’s entropy exported while useful correlations are maintained.

2Falsification as erasure

Here there’s an important subtlety. Falsifying a hypothesis doesn’t necessarily erase it. I can retain

{ H1 : false, H2 : false, H3 : true }

reversibly forever. Landauer’s bill arrives when finite memory eventually says I no longer need the complete histories of H1 and H2 — and resets those states.

So perhaps falsification has two stages:

distinguish  ⟶  discard

The first creates information. The second destroys distinctions. And bounded intelligence forces the second step eventually.

That’s fascinating, because an unbounded reasoner could theoretically keep its entire search tree. A bounded reasoner cannot. Therefore:

Boundedness may be what makes intelligence thermodynamic

Memory limits force irreversible choices about which distinctions deserve continued physical existence.

That sounds like a serious candidate for the physics-of-intelligence search.

3Steering as work

The intuition survives, but I’d move it from Landauer specifically into nonequilibrium control theory. There is actual work connecting thermodynamic control to geometries over state spaces; within certain regimes, a metric over thermodynamic states determines dissipation along finite-time trajectories.11

So imagine the intelligence manifold again. An intelligent trajectory wanders toward some undesirable region. A steering intervention changes its trajectory: st ⟶ s′t.

The right question becomes not merely did steering work? but:

capability restored control work injected

Now we have another dial: restorative efficiency. Some cognitive architectures might need enormous intervention to return to a useful basin. Others might self-correct after a tiny perturbation. That is measurable.

And it suggests that the geometry of the intelligence region itself matters: basin width, boundary curvature, attractor strength, escape rates, restoration energy.

4Compression has a boundary

Here the boundary likely comes from rate-distortion, not Landauer alone.10 Compression itself can be reversible — I can compress a file and retain every bit. The critical act occurs when we say these distinctions aren’t worth keeping. Then compression becomes lossy.

So imagine moving along

X ⟶ Z1 ⟶ Z2 ⟶ Z3 ⟶ ⋯

with increasingly aggressive abstraction. Initially we erase nuisance variation. Performance improves. Then eventually I(Z; Y) starts collapsing.

That’s the abstraction cliff. And different architectures may have radically different cliffs. That feels exactly like one of the hidden sliders.

An existing result that makes this more compelling

Still, Sivak, Bell and Crooks studied physical systems that retain information about their environments, and separated remembered information into information useful for predicting the future and information merely about the past.1

They found a direct relationship between nonpredictive stored information and thermodynamic inefficiency. Their term for the useless retained past information is particularly memorable: useless nostalgia — and it names a specific quantity, instantaneous memory minus predictive power. Their result is in fact stronger than “a relationship”: summed over the protocol, the total nonpredictive information is a lower bound on total dissipation.

That is strikingly close to what we’re circling. A highly efficient adaptive system should not remember everything. It should preferentially retain what predicts what comes next.

So perhaps the fundamental isn’t compression. It’s:

The fundamental predictive selective retention

Everything else gets progressively released. Worth knowing before claiming novelty: this already has an exact formulation. In computational mechanics the causal states of a process, and the ε-machine built from them, are the minimal representation consistent with accurate prediction.12 And Still later showed that minimizing the dissipation bound yields maximal retention of predictive information — the principle falls out of the physics rather than being imposed on it.2

A candidate “Landauer principle of intelligence”

I wouldn’t state it yet as a law. I’d state it as a hypothesis we can try to kill.

Selective Irreversibility Hypothesis

Any bounded physical intelligence operating cyclically must continually compress its accumulated state. When that compression physically erases distinctions, it incurs an irreducible thermodynamic cost. Intelligence is therefore constrained not simply by computation or memory, but by its ability to minimize irreversible destruction of task-relevant information while efficiently disposing of task-irrelevant information.

That gives us three measurable quantities:

  • D — information discarded
  • R — relevant / predictive information retained
  • E — physical or computational expenditure

And now we can start looking for ratios. An intelligence thermodynamic efficiency:

ηI = I(Z; Y) E / (kBT ln 2)

roughly: predictively useful bits maintained per Landauer-equivalent unit of expenditure. Or, independently of actual joules initially:

ηA = I(Z; Y) I(X; Z)

useful information per bit retained. And perhaps, for falsification efficiency:

ηF = hypothesis-space volume eliminated correctly irreversible state changes

I wouldn’t become attached to any of these equations yet. They are rulers to attack.

The part most consequential for Prometheus

We have been treating constraints as annoyances or experimental parameters. Memory caps. Compute caps. Bandwidth. Energy. Organism size. Communication restrictions.

They may be causal ingredients.

Without pressure to forget, there is little pressure to abstract. Without pressure to abstract, there is less pressure to discover invariants. Without discarded distinctions, everything can remain episodic.

So one possible progression is:

boundedness  ⟶  forced forgetting  ⟶  selective compression  ⟶  invariants  ⟶  abstraction  ⟶  transfer

That is a testable causal chain. And it produces a rather provocative prediction:

Giving an adaptive system more memory should sometimes make it less intelligent, because it removes the pressure to discover cheaper sufficient representations.

Not always. But there should be regimes where capability peaks at an intermediate resource constraint rather than at maximum memory.

This is not unprecedented. In iterated-learning experiments, artificial languages passed down chains of human learners — each seeing only part of the language — evolve compositional structure that no participant intended. The structure is produced by the transmission bottleneck itself; remove the bottleneck and the pressure toward structure goes with it.13 That makes the experiment below a replication attempt with a known positive control, rather than a shot in the dark.

That would be a beautiful thing for some of the Prometheus engines to attack independently. They are not variations on a single system — they disagree with one another about what a world even is, which is precisely what makes running the same question through all of them worth doing:

  • Nestor, driving the Nestor Primordial Engine — large preregistered campaigns: many thousands of cells, a grammar frozen before the run, gates fixed in advance, and findings that count only once they have survived adjudication. This is where a claim goes to be tested at scale rather than anecdotally.
  • Ensorain, the Tensor World Engine — navigable worlds whose geometry, information and puzzles are literally tensors, inhabited by organisms with a hard storage cap. The one substrate where the memory bound this essay is about is a structural property of the organism rather than a knob on the side.
  • BEE, the Worlds Kernel — it makes an experiment description executable while explicitly never deciding what is interesting. Worlds, substrates, players and controls are named by kind, so an identical design can be lowered onto quite different machinery. The instrument for telling a real effect apart from an artefact of one implementation.
  • Cosmos and Aether — the newest seats, charters still being written. Their use here is that they carry no house style yet, and so would come to the question without the habits the others have already formed.

We could dial memory from effectively infinite down through severe constraint and ask whether there is a phase transition where the organisms suddenly begin inventing reusable abstractions.

Then do the same with compute, bandwidth, communication, replay, falsification cost, and irreversible-update cost. If the same transition appears across radically different substrates, we have found something much more interesting than another ML trick.

We may have found one of those hidden axes in the control space:

The hidden axis pressure to eliminate irrelevant distinctions

And that might genuinely sit underneath compression, abstraction, generalization, forgetting, falsification, dreaming, and even reasoning itself.

That is exactly the sort of candidate to put on the wall under Physics of Intelligence — possible fundamentals, rather than under “AI architecture.”

Three arguments against this

A hypothesis written to be attacked should name its own best attackers. Here are the three that worry me most.

The capability curve is not shaped the way this predicts. Deep double descent shows test error falling, then rising, then falling again as capacity grows past the interpolation threshold — and identifies regimes where even quadrupling the training data makes performance worse. Most of modern machine learning operates on the far side of that second descent, where more capacity reliably helps. If pressure to forget were the engine of abstraction, that is not the curve we should be seeing.14

Biology kept the redundancy. The efficient-coding tradition began with precisely this essay’s intuition — that sensory systems exist to reduce redundancy — and it had to be substantially weakened. Real neural codes retain considerable redundancy, and retain it for good reasons: noise tolerance and error correction. “Destroy distinctions that no longer matter” is not the design evolution actually settled on, in the one substrate we can inspect at leisure.15

The thermodynamics may be surplus. Resource-rational analysis already accounts for a great deal of bounded cognition as optimizing performance against time, memory and computational cost — with no physics in it whatsoever. If that framework explains the phenomena, then Landauer is a layer of explanation nothing yet requires. The burden on everything above is to produce one prediction that resource-rationality does not already make.16
