# ASTRA-6.0 / Enceladus: Stage I requirements

## Scope, derivation, and reading contract
These requirements were derived **before salvage analysis**, solely from the shared `docs/phase3/PHASE3_ARCHITECT_PROMPT.md` and the operator's Stage I instructions. No existing engine is presumed useful or defective.
Generic concerns about leakage, weak controls, seeded mechanisms, monoculture, and premature interpretation come from the **operator charter**, not an empirical audit performed here. Historical claims remain unassessed.
The target is an instrument for discriminating developmental explanations, not an implementation already entitled to the name Recursive Sagacity Engine (RSE). Architecture v0 follows this specification in `RSE_ARCHITECTURE.md`.
REQUIRED means a release/claim gate; HIGH VALUE means defer only with a recorded scope reduction; EXPERIMENTAL means a bounded hypothesis, not a prerequisite; REJECTED means incompatible with this design; AVOID means a presumptively harmful default requiring explicit justification.
Every entry provides a reason, a testable acceptance criterion, and an alternative with a discriminator. Acceptance criteria below are **proposed tests, not results**. A requirement may be met by demonstrating a limitation and lowering the claim ceiling, where explicitly stated.
Before execution, each claim must name a task distribution, resource envelope B, practically meaningful effect delta*, independent sampling unit, and uncertainty rule. Missing values block confirmatory execution, rather than silently becoming defaults.
No finite battery certifies general reasoning. Positive evidence is conditional on sampled worlds and interventions; finite failure cannot establish that a substrate lacks all latent reasoning capacity.
Minimal physics means costed persistent writable state, readdressable references, and transition rules. This is the study envelope, not a theorem that every possible intelligence requires this particular implementation.
Named faculties, belief objects, planners, symbol types, abstraction modules, and self-models are not part of that minimum. Their functional analogues are outcomes to test, not mandatory objects to install.

## 1. Scientific requirements
### R-SCI-01 | REQUIRED | Bounded, competing claims
- Why: a score increase alone underdetermines both its cause and its scope.
- Acceptance: each confirmatory manifest names at least two explanations, a signed discriminating contrast, delta*, exclusion rules, a claim ceiling, and a result that would reverse the interpretation; all are frozen before outcome access.
- Alternative/discriminator: exploratory pattern collection is allowed in a separately labeled stream; compare whether its nominated effects survive a fresh preregistered test rather than treating nomination as confirmation.

### R-SCI-02 | REQUIRED | Interpretable negative evidence
- Why: organism, world, developmental path, search, ruler, and sampling can each prevent detection.
- Acceptance: every nonpositive result carries a typed disposition and checked prerequisites; only an interval excluding delta* under qualified controls permits a bounded negative claim. Missing power or capacity witnesses produces uncertainty, not `NO EMERGENCE`.
- Alternative/discriminator: a binary success/failure dashboard is rejected as an evidence interface; inject independent defects and verify that the instrument routes them to distinct dispositions without inventing a cause.

### R-SCI-03 | REJECTED | Universal certification and historical verdicts without evidence
- Why: finite batteries do not establish unrestricted reasoning, and this stage has not audited Prometheus.
- Acceptance: outputs contain neither general-intelligence certification nor a current-program maturity/salvage verdict; every substantive claim includes its task, intervention, budget, and sampling boundary.
- Alternative/discriminator: use a widening envelope of replicated conditional claims; withhold a new family to test extension, explicitly retaining the possibility of failure outside that envelope.

## 2. Organism requirements
### R-ORG-01 | REQUIRED | Minimal, accountable physical affordances
- Why: cumulative construction needs revisitable material and effective transitions, but not a supplied cognitive vocabulary.
- Acceptance: each candidate track demonstrates persistent writable state, readdressability through its own physics, transition closure, read/write/maintenance costs, bounded allocation, and deterministic invalid-reference behavior; ablations can independently disable persistence, writing, or revisitation.
- Alternative/discriminator: a fixed stateless transducer is a cheap control; delayed hidden-state tasks distinguish genuine use of these affordances from a larger reactive mapping. Native pointers are not required.

### R-ORG-02 | HIGH VALUE | Computation and structure on multiple timescales
- Why: useful organization may require delayed internal work, creation, deletion, copying, and consolidation rather than a larger fixed input-output map.
- Acceptance: selected tracks permit costed internal transitions without immediate external action and native structural edits; trace/checkpoint tests distinguish inherited, transient, retained, and newly constructed state with explicit reset semantics.
- Alternative/discriminator: fixed topology with equal memory and compute is a first-class competitor; matched interventions on structural editing versus parameter/state updating test whether construction adds value.

### R-ORG-03 | AVOID | Hard-coded cognitive ontology
- Why: mandatory belief registers, named reasoning operations, or privileged planner instructions can seed the target and exclude unfamiliar organizations.
- Acceptance: the base instruction/interaction inventory exposes no task-semantic reasoning faculty; any such module is labeled a seeded control, its authored information is counted, and unseeded trials cannot inherit it.
- Alternative/discriminator: a seeded planner may calibrate detectability; compare against low-level physics with matched resources and test whether the target effect disappears when its supplied semantic operator is removed.

## 3. Developmental requirements
### R-DEV-01 | REQUIRED | Capacity, reachability, and competence are different estimands
- Why: expressibility, discovery through allowed experience, and current task success answer different questions.
- Acceptance: resource-bounded implanted witnesses establish only capacity lower bounds; unseeded developmental replicates estimate reachability probability/cost; frozen checkpoints measure realized competence without additional learning. Reports never substitute one estimate for another.
- Alternative/discriminator: end-to-end training scores are insufficient; cross prior experience, plasticity, inheritance, and resource allowance to separate supplied solutions from pathways that construct them.

### R-DEV-02 | REQUIRED | History-sensitive construction without teleology
- Why: age, training volume, curriculum order, and architectural change can be confounded.
- Acceptance: counterfactual lineages receive equal interaction/compute budgets with ordered, shuffled, repeated, or irrelevant histories; checkpoint interventions distinguish state accumulation, reusable structure, and changed future learning operations.
- Alternative/discriminator: continued parameter fitting is a valid competitor, not a disqualified mechanism; topology-fixed and structure-editable variants discriminate only under shared task/resource boundaries.

### R-DEV-03 | EXPERIMENTAL | Nested improvement of developmental machinery
- Why: stronger future learners might result from learned update processes, accumulated task answers, or ordinary optimizer state.
- Acceptance: a successful recursive-sagacity claim must pass the nested history/update-process/next-learner intervention protocol in architecture section 5, including lesion, rescue, fresh initialization, resource matching, and nonoverlapping task families.
- Alternative/discriminator: fixed-rule meta-learning, optimizer-state accumulation, and longer training are mandatory competitors. Changing optimizer state alone is not logically outside broad meta-learning; the criterion is causal nesting, not ontological novelty.

## 4. World requirements
### R-WLD-01 | REQUIRED | Minimum sufficient policies and depth profiles
- Why: world size or narrative complexity does not establish cognitive demand.
- Acceptance: each world family reports the cheapest demonstrated solver and lower bounds where provable, plus a depth vector for memory, delay, branching, information acquisition, irreversibility, compositional scope, and law change; tiny instances admit exact enumeration.
- Alternative/discriminator: enlarge a reactive world versus deepen a small hidden-state world; matched policy classes must show which axis raises the minimum demonstrated demand. Unproved minima are labeled upper bounds, not necessities.

### R-WLD-02 | REQUIRED | Qualified diversity and frozen novelty
- Why: changing seeds or textures can leave the same causal problem unchanged.
- Acceptance: training, calibration, discovery, and sealed test partitions are separate by generator lineage and causal family; tests include new parameter settings, new compositions, and changed mechanisms, with novelty level explicitly labeled.
- Alternative/discriminator: same-generator random splits remain interpolation controls; compare them to independently authored generators and hidden law interventions to expose generator-specific shortcuts.

### R-WLD-03 | HIGH VALUE | Revision under costly, ambiguous evidence
- Why: critical thought is observable as selective information use and warranted revision, not verbal self-description.
- Acceptance: worlds combine misleading early evidence, source reliability changes, observation costs, and irreversible decisions; matched truthful, misleading, stable-law, and changed-law conditions measure appropriate revision and inappropriate reversal.
- Alternative/discriminator: reward-following reversal or always-seek-information policies are baselines; counterbalance cues, hold out reliability mechanisms, and lesion retained evidence to distinguish reusable revision from a switch trigger.

## 5. Pressure/search requirements
### R-SRH-01 | REQUIRED | Separate what is inherited from what is learned
- Why: selection can place complete answers into inherited material while appearing to reward development.
- Acceptance: ledger every inherited bit/source and all optimizer-side information; compare fixed solutions, adaptive construction, and no-selection/random-search arms across lifetime length, law variability, and inheritance bottlenecks.
- Alternative/discriminator: evolution is optional; enumeration, stochastic search, and gradient-based baselines receive comparable evaluation budgets. Claim developmental advantage only if it survives direct-solution competitors.

### R-SRH-02 | HIGH VALUE | Multiple pressures without an opaque reward soup
- Why: scalar reward can privilege a cheap proxy, while unconstrained novelty can reward irrelevant noise.
- Acceptance: retain separate axes for competence, adaptation cost, retention, diversity, and resource use; preregister any scalarization and cap novelty allocation independently of holdout results.
- Alternative/discriminator: scalar fitness and Pareto/quality-diversity selection are matched arms; compare held-out useful diversity and qualified scientific yield, not archive size or nominal novelty.

### R-SRH-03 | EXPERIMENTAL | Adaptive worlds and ecological pressure
- Why: interaction may favor construction, but coadaptation can produce moving targets and circular evidence.
- Acceptance: an adaptive-world proposal stays in discovery, with population snapshots, fixed evaluation panels, independent test generators, and a capped spend; it cannot modify an active holdout.
- Alternative/discriminator: static diverse curricula are the cheaper reference; continue coevolution only if it adds replicated transfer or identifies a mechanism unavailable under the matched static schedule.

## 6. Measurement requirements
### R-MSR-01 | REQUIRED | Instrument qualification precedes novel interpretation
- Why: unknown sensitivity/specificity makes both positives and nulls ambiguous.
- Acceptance: each ruler version passes blinded known-positive recovery, known-negative rejection, neutral/sham handling, saturation tests, effect-size/noise sweeps, and explicit false-negative bounds using the confidence gates in architecture section 6.
- Alternative/discriminator: qualification organisms may be hand-built but are labeled seeded; an independently implemented injected-effect harness tests the ruler without borrowing its own score computation as ground truth.

### R-MSR-02 | REQUIRED | Resource-matched factorial evidence
- Why: memory, training, search effort, or inference leakage can mimic developmental gains.
- Acceptance: preregister blocked factorial contrasts and independent lineage/world units; log joint sample/compute/memory/energy budgets, censoring, cluster-aware intervals, family-wise error control, and any unsupported interactions. No task episode is silently treated as an independent organism.
- Alternative/discriminator: show both equal-cap comparisons and cost-performance frontiers; token count, parameter count, FLOPs, or wall time alone cannot establish cross-substrate resource equality.

### R-MSR-03 | REQUIRED | Compression must predict useful reuse
- Why: smaller encodings may discard relevant information, exploit a decoder prior, or merely rename a lookup table.
- Acceptance: jointly measure complete code-plus-decoder length, held-out predictive/causal loss, new-family acquisition cost, retention, and intervention-based reuse; shortening counts as sagacity evidence only with retained or improved future competence.
- Alternative/discriminator: compare memorization, generic lossless compression, and distilled/reactive policies at matched cost; invariance-preserving relabeling versus structure-destroying scrambling separates representation dependence from functional compression.

## 7. Causal-analysis requirements
### R-CAU-01 | REQUIRED | Validated lesion, sham, and rescue
- Why: damaged organisms can fail for nonspecific reasons unrelated to the proposed mechanism.
- Acceptance: interventions preserve specified resource and interface invariants, log off-target changes, include equal-cost sham lesions, and restore original function under rescue; necessity claims remain conditional on the intervention set.
- Alternative/discriminator: correlations and probes nominate candidates only; compare targeted lesions with damage-matched random lesions and a replacement performing equivalent I/O without the proposed organization.

### R-CAU-02 | HIGH VALUE | Transplantation without hidden teaching
- Why: transferring a useful structure could establish reuse, or merely transfer an adapter containing the solution.
- Acceptance: donors, recipients, adapter information, compatibility limits, and edit costs are recorded; scrambled-donor, blank-donor, adapter-only, and native-rescue controls accompany fresh-family recipient tests.
- Alternative/discriminator: within-track transplant precedes cross-track functional reconstruction. Failed cross-track transfer is not evidence of absent cognition unless compatibility and delivery are independently qualified.

### R-CAU-03 | REQUIRED | Independent causal pathways
- Why: one world/ruler implementation can make all apparent replications share a defect.
- Acceptance: any mechanism claim is tested by a second independently authored world/ruler or simulator pair from a behavioral contract, with disjoint decisive scientific code and a disclosed common-dependency ledger.
- Alternative/discriminator: same-code reruns establish repeatability only; perturbation tests and independent derivations decide whether shared protocol code can explain the observed effect.

## 8. Transfer requirements
### R-TRF-01 | REQUIRED | Transfer as reduced total acquisition cost
- Why: higher target accuracy can hide extra pretraining, larger inherited state, or degraded old skills.
- Acceptance: compare developed, naive, scrambled-history, and fixed-meta-learner arms on sealed new causal families; report target cost-to-criterion, full lifecycle/amortized cost, asymptotes, retention, and timeout censoring.
- Alternative/discriminator: warm-start memorization and more target-only training are competitors; vary number of future tasks to expose the crossover where prior development actually pays back.

### R-TRF-02 | HIGH VALUE | Invariance and mechanism-sensitive transfer
- Why: useful abstraction should ignore nuisance changes without becoming blind to causal changes.
- Acceptance: separately vary encodings, task composition, and underlying laws; preserve performance under qualified nuisance relabelings, require adaptation under law shifts, and test mechanistic reuse with ablation/rescue.
- Alternative/discriminator: broad invariance alone may be insensitivity; include cases where the previously invariant feature becomes causally relevant and compare reactive/lookup controls.

### R-TRF-03 | EXPERIMENTAL | Cross-substrate generalization
- Why: a principle that survives different physics is stronger than a representation-specific trick.
- Acceptance: state a functional claim implementable in at least two tracks, independently reconstruct its mechanism, and reproduce a signed causal contrast; disclose information introduced during reconstruction.
- Alternative/discriminator: identical binary transplantation is not required across incompatible physics; failure restricts the architectural-principle claim without erasing a qualified within-track effect.

## 9. Provenance requirements
### R-PRV-01 | REQUIRED | Complete exposure and lineage ledger
- Why: endogenous construction cannot be distinguished from importation without ancestry and exposure records.
- Acceptance: each artifact links immutable source/configuration/world/ruler versions, random streams, parents, training/test access, inherited state, interventions, authorship, and resource receipts; seeded and unseeded branches cannot be conflated.
- Alternative/discriminator: summary-only logs are cheaper but fail audit; inject an undeclared training exposure or donor swap and verify admission is blocked, not merely annotated afterward.

### R-PRV-02 | REQUIRED | Generation, reality, measurement, interpretation separation
- Why: allowing one agent to propose, alter evidence, and judge it creates a self-validating loop.
- Acceptance: generators emit candidates; sandboxed reality emits immutable trajectories; versioned rulers emit measurements; interpreters receive read-only evidence. No interpreter can silently change admissions, world outcomes, or a frozen test.
- Alternative/discriminator: a shared executable may orchestrate these roles but must preserve access boundaries; deliberate forbidden writes/access attempts must fail and leave an audit event.

### R-PRV-03 | HIGH VALUE | Immutable freeze and correction chains
- Why: adapting an original hypothesis after seeing data erases the experiment's evidential meaning.
- Acceptance: Stage I is committed by the parent before salvage; subsequent requirements/design changes append dated deltas with cause and evidence. Corrections invalidate affected claims while retaining original artifacts and dependency links.
- Alternative/discriminator: editable living notes remain discovery aids; reconstruct a prior claim from its pinned versions to test whether a correction can be traced without trusting current prose.

## 10. Reproducibility requirements
### R-REP-01 | REQUIRED | Replayable execution with declared numerical limits
- Why: a seed is insufficient when scheduling, library behavior, or floating-point changes alter trajectories.
- Acceptance: deterministic tracks reproduce event/checkpoint digests from a pinned manifest; numerical tracks specify tolerances and distributions across reruns, explicitly recording hardware, dependencies, thread count, and RNG streams.
- Alternative/discriminator: exact bitwise replay is not forced across all substrates; compare event-level agreement where possible and preregister statistical equivalence elsewhere.

### R-REP-02 | REQUIRED | Leakage-resistant holdout custody
- Why: test feedback can become a training signal even without direct access to test instances.
- Acceptance: sealed generator/configuration hashes and access policy precede search; evaluators expose no per-case feedback until candidate selection is locked; any redesign after unsealing retires that partition from confirmation.
- Alternative/discriminator: rotating development panels are permitted for tuning; a fresh independent family, not relabeled used cases, discriminates selection artifacts from transfer.

### R-REP-03 | HIGH VALUE | External rerun before broad claims
- Why: local repeatability and independent implementation answer different reliability questions.
- Acceptance: a minimal runnable bundle reproduces the primary contrast on a clean machine within a published budget; a second operator reports discrepancies and all negative controls, not only the headline score.
- Alternative/discriminator: an archive without a runnable path supports inspection only; an independent execution attempting the frozen rejection tests is the discriminator for reproducibility claims.

## 11. Anti-prior / anti-gravity requirements
### R-APR-01 | REQUIRED | Prior-transparent physical diversity
- Why: one substrate with different names or curricula is not independent architectural coverage.
- Acceptance: maintain three initial tracks with different locality/addressing/update assumptions; disclose every instruction, topology, parameterization, and generator bias. No track is declared prior-free or the preferred RSE before discrimination.
- Alternative/discriminator: a single substrate may win on yield; equal-budget track retirement requires inability to qualify or repeated redundant results, not unfamiliarity or unattractive internal traces.

### R-APR-02 | HIGH VALUE | Admission without familiar labels
- Why: functioning unfamiliar mechanisms must not depend on recognition by a language model.
- Acceptance: a fixed share of discovery evaluations uses structural/stochastic generators and ontology-blind retention; reproducible causal anomalies can enter a bounded queue even when every interpreter returns unclassified.
- Alternative/discriminator: LLM proposal generation is an explicitly tagged optional arm; blind family labels during triage and compare retained functional diversity at equal evaluation cost.

### R-APR-03 | AVOID | Uninterpretability as novelty evidence
- Why: noise, bugs, and opaque common mechanisms can all be difficult to describe.
- Acceptance: unfamiliarity never raises a claim level; increased investigation priority requires qualified repeatability, baseline survival, and a concrete causal contrast, with a capped audit allocation.
- Alternative/discriminator: novelty-only ranking is a comparator in discovery; blind adversarial noise and known-but-obfuscated mechanisms test whether it displaces scientifically useful candidates.

## 12. Compute requirements
### R-CMP-01 | REQUIRED | Bounded scheduler and complete accounting
- Why: a scientifically ambiguous run should not consume an unbounded resource budget.
- Acceptance: jobs enforce limits on core/device time, memory, storage, transitions, samples, and wall time; checkpoints resume within those limits; breach events are typed budget outcomes with consumed resources retained.
- Alternative/discriminator: open-ended search is deferred; a small capped pilot must first demonstrate increased discriminating evidence per marginal resource unit.

### R-CMP-02 | HIGH VALUE | CPU-first, measured acceleration
- Why: the charter anticipates scarce inference and costly GPU access, not a universal GPU prohibition.
- Acceptance: initial qualification is runnable on CPU; GPU adoption requires measured speed/energy/cost advantage on an equivalent qualified workload, including transfer, idle, and verification overhead.
- Alternative/discriminator: tensor tracks may benefit from acceleration; compare complete experiment cost and ruler equivalence, not kernel throughput alone.

### R-CMP-03 | AVOID | Exhaustive Cartesian campaigns before qualification
- Why: crossing every track, world, pressure, history, and seed can consume the program before checking its assumptions.
- Acceptance: staged contrasts name interactions they can and cannot estimate; new axes open only after preceding controls pass, with preregistered stopping and a reserve for independent replication.
- Alternative/discriminator: full factorials are preferred for the decisive small comparison; fractional/screening designs must disclose aliasing and be followed by an unconfounded confirmatory contrast.

## 13. Inference requirements
### R-INF-01 | REQUIRED | Inference-free experimental inner loop
- Why: model-mediated mutation, scoring, or admission can dominate cost and import untracked human priors.
- Acceptance: routine execution, development, mutation/search, controls, statistics, and gating make zero external model-service calls; disconnected-network runs reproduce the same decisions. Candidate-local numerical computation remains metered organism computation.
- Alternative/discriminator: pretrained foundation-model-assisted arms require separate later authorization and exposure accounting; compare them as seeded controls, never silently mix them into unseeded development.

### R-INF-02 | HIGH VALUE | Inference only at recorded epistemic forks
- Why: interpretation may benefit from models, but neither every event nor every anomaly needs narration.
- Acceptance: offline requests have a written unresolved question, evidence bundle, token/cost cap, and decision impact; cached answers do not authorize changes to frozen experiments.
- Alternative/discriminator: deterministic triage and human review are the baseline; compare accepted discriminators per token/operator minute rather than prose volume or number of model calls.

### R-INF-03 | REJECTED | Model consensus as an admission oracle
- Why: shared training priors can make multiple agreeing models a single source of exclusion bias.
- Acceptance: no scientific eligibility or effect label requires a model's semantic recognition; disagreements are stored as interpretation metadata and cannot override measured controls or claim ceilings.
- Alternative/discriminator: blinded interpretations may generate follow-up hypotheses; their value is tested by new interventions, not by voting on whether a mechanism resembles known cognition.

## 14. Energy requirements
### R-ENE-01 | REQUIRED | Energy with uncertainty and system boundary
- Why: abstract operation counts do not equal physical energy expenditure.
- Acceptance: record measured joules where available and otherwise active/idle host-time power bounds, hardware scope, utilization, and uncertainty; label proxies as estimates and never convert them into exact biological efficiency claims.
- Alternative/discriminator: CPU time is an auxiliary proxy; calibration against a meter on representative jobs tests whether it ranks complete experiments reliably.

### R-ENE-02 | HIGH VALUE | Energy-aware scientific frontier
- Why: a higher score can be an expensive route to the same discriminating information.
- Acceptance: report qualified claims and resolved alternatives alongside total kWh, dollars, and attention, including failed qualification and replication; preserve a frontier rather than a single maximized yield score.
- Alternative/discriminator: reward energy efficiency only after task-quality floors are met; otherwise a nonperforming organism or undersensitive experiment can appear optimal.

### R-ENE-03 | EXPERIMENTAL | Resource pressure on endogenous organization
- Why: costly memory access, maintenance, and internal computation may favor reusable structures, or merely suppress exploration.
- Acceptance: vary these native costs independently with matched total allowances; distinguish fewer operations from lower host energy and monitor the loss of reachable competent policies.
- Alternative/discriminator: zero-maintenance toy physics is a control; retain cost pressure only where it changes a qualified developmental contrast, not just survival rate.

## 15. Human-attention requirements
### R-ATT-01 | REQUIRED | Bounded, fail-closed exception handling
- Why: continuous manual babysitting defeats the charter's resource assumptions and encourages selective intervention.
- Acceptance: scheduler summaries separate safety/resource breaches, qualification failures, and scientific anomalies; repeated signatures deduplicate; exhausted review capacity pauses promotion rather than auto-accepting a result.
- Alternative/discriminator: inspect every trajectory only during tiny qualification cases; replay seeded faults to test triage recall and operator time before autonomous runs.

### R-ATT-02 | HIGH VALUE | Small decision packets
- Why: opaque dashboards and long narratives conceal the actual choice requiring attention.
- Acceptance: each review packet contains one decision, competing explanations, primary contrast/interval, control status, artifact links, cost, and next cheapest discriminator; reviewer minutes and reversals are logged.
- Alternative/discriminator: full raw artifacts remain available without being the default interface; a blinded second reviewer must be able to reconstruct the decision from the packet and its evidence links.

### R-ATT-03 | AVOID | Activity as scientific yield
- Why: commits, runs, agent messages, and interesting flags are easy to produce without reducing uncertainty.
- Acceptance: progress is counted as qualified instruments, bounded exclusions, replicated effects, and independently supported mechanisms; unresolved anomalies and failed controls remain separate categories.
- Alternative/discriminator: compare program allocations by alternatives resolved and claim robustness at equal resources; never pressure a team to turn an inconclusive result into a positive metric.

## 16. Publication/output requirements
### R-OUT-01 | REQUIRED | Auditable positive, null, and correction artifacts
- Why: selective export creates a false picture of both capability and instrument reliability.
- Acceptance: bundles contain preregistration, configurations, controls, seeds/streams, environment versions, raw sufficient traces, analysis, resource ledger, typed dispositions, limitations, and correction links; private data/secrets are excluded.
- Alternative/discriminator: plots or prose alone support no reproducibility claim; an independent reader must reconstruct the primary contrast and its claim ceiling from the bundle.

### R-OUT-02 | HIGH VALUE | Externalization proportional to evidence
- Why: useful instrument qualification or a precise negative can be more valuable than a grand RSE narrative.
- Acceptance: release a methods/benchmark package after independent qualification, a mechanism note after causal replication, and a broader principle only after cross-family/implementation support; label pilots and registered plans as such.
- Alternative/discriminator: a single dramatic demonstration remains an anomaly report; a hostile reproduction attempt determines whether it deserves promotion.

### R-OUT-03 | REQUIRED | Freeze before salvage; retain unknowns
- Why: component familiarity can silently determine the requirements it is supposed to satisfy.
- Acceptance: parent commits this specification, architecture v0, and the process record before any salvage reading; later candidates are mapped to requirement IDs with KEEP/HARDEN/EXTRACT/REBUILD/RETIRE/HISTORICAL CONTROL/UNKNOWN evidence and rebuild comparisons.
- Alternative/discriminator: no reuse fraction is assigned now. A later requirement-to-component audit can overturn implementation choices but cannot rewrite this freeze as though its conclusions preceded exposure.
