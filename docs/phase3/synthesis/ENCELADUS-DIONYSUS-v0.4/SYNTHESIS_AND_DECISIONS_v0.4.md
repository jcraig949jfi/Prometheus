## 5. Fable's unseen-pair proposal: preserve the useful result

Eight of nine table/move pairings precede a ninth. Three linked maps determine
it by composition with an inverse. For state depending on a fixed non-linking
set of shown maps under the stated model, the ninth permutation is uniform;
Fable reports an exact elimination expectation, rounded to 3.38 correct of
16 trials. An all-eight-map cache lies outside that class, even if it does
not use labels. The reported
512-recombination cache scores 13.46/16, while the label-directed composer
scores 16/16. Those are exploratory measurements, not a class bound.

The repeated-key watcher is the decisive custody warning. With the intact
key repeated but the control key fresh, it scores 15.96/16 versus 3.46/16
and is falsely called COMBINED by the arms alone. The delivered key-reuse
check refuses that planted case. Fresh independent key generation, selective
leaks, semantic key equivalence and cross-life state remain review targets.

Before this experiment: specify whether map selection is fixed or adaptive,
include selection metadata in the information budget, randomize the held-out
pair, and independently check the class theorem and control assumptions.
A unique key hash does not itself prove an independently drawn secret key.
Keep legitimate acquired maps across the intended within-life boundary;
erasing them to meet an unrelated content-reset rule would remove the very
capability being tested. NOT_SHOWN remains no positive, not earned absence.

## 6. What we will not claim

No universal gate qualification, native cross-physics observatory, economic
crossover, search discovery, independent scientific replication, or recursive
sagacity. No numerical gate coverage inferred from 110 versus 28 tests. No
claim that our smaller harness is safer because fewer escapes are known.
The missing original harness remains a missing input, not a failed runtime.

The next plan can end in REVISE or STOP. A useful outcome may be a trustworthy
methods result with no reason to build the larger observatory.
