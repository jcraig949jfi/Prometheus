## 1. The benchmark's ground truth was not unique, and that is a real defect

Tested before buying any resolution, because more features cannot fix a benchmark whose correct
answer is not unique. For every development pair, all pool objects satisfying `op(src)` exactly over
≥ 20 overlapping terms were enumerated — the *same* rule the builder used to certify the recorded
pair. Anything in that set is a correct answer by the benchmark's own definition.

    answer-set size        mean    median   max   frac > 1
    diff                   1.080     1        2     0.080
    partsum                1.280     1        2     0.280
    binomial               1.000     1        1     0.000
    moebius                1.200     1        3     0.160
    shift                  4.000     1       27     0.400

Sanity: the recorded target was absent from its own answer set in **0 of 125** cases.

**The hypothesis is confirmed and it is specific to near-identity operators.** `binomial` is
perfectly unique (max 1). `shift` has 40% of queries with multiple valid answers and one query with
**27**. Scoring `shift` against a single recorded target was counting correct retrievals as
collisions.

**Scoring rule changed, and here is the justification.** L1 is now scored as "rank-1 is *any* valid
partner" rather than "rank-1 is the recorded one." This is a **ground-truth correction**: the
benchmark defines a valid pair by exact agreement over ≥ 20 terms, and objects meeting that
definition are correct by the benchmark's own criterion. Both scorings are reported side by side
throughout so the change is auditable:

    L1 top-1 STRICT      114/125 = 0.9120    diff 24/25 · partsum 23/25 · binomial 25/25 · moebius 23/25 · shift 19/25
    L1 top-1 ANY-VALID   118/125 = 0.9440    diff 24/25 · partsum 23/25 · binomial 25/25 · moebius 23/25 · shift 23/25

All 4 rescued pairs are `shift`, exactly where the hypothesis predicted them.

**Stated against myself:** the any-valid rule makes the task genuinely easier where answer sets are
large. Retrieving any of 27 is not the same problem as retrieving one of 27. The rule is correct as
ground truth, but the difficulty reduction is concentrated in `shift`, and the answer-set table is
reported beside it so a reviewer can see exactly where the task got easier.

## 2. The residual deficit was a metric pathology — a *different* one

After the correction, 7 genuine misses remained. The tell: **the same distractor `D017288` was
rank-1 for two unrelated `partsum` queries.** One pool object winning several unrelated queries is
the signature of **hubness** — the concentration-of-distance effect where a few points become
nearest neighbours of disproportionately many others. V2 is 125-dimensional, squarely in that regime.

Measured independently of the gate, k = 10 over all 20,358 pool points:

    k-occurrence   mean 10.00   std 14.04   max 235   SKEWNESS 3.783
    points appearing at > 10× their fair share: 43

A mean of 10 with a maximum of 235 is a 23.5× hub. **Severe, and the reading stands on its own
whatever the gate does.** The script pre-committed `skew > 1.0` as the condition for applying any
correction, checked *before* the corrected score was computed — had the distribution been flat, the
hypothesis would have died there and no correction would exist.

*This is not a retry of pass 1's refuted metric argument.* Pass 1 tested ZCA whitening, which fixes
**correlation** between feature blocks and was worth −0.016. Hubness survives whitening untouched:
decorrelating axes does nothing about a point sitting near the data mean and therefore near
everything. Distinct pathology, distinct evidence, distinct correction.

Correction applied: local scaling, `d'(q,x) = d(q,x) / sqrt(r_k(q)·r_k(x))`. A hub's own `r_k` is
small, which inflates its corrected distance and demotes it. Uses no labels.

    UNCORRECTED    strict 114/125 = 0.9120   any-valid 118/125 = 0.9440
    LOCAL-SCALED   strict 115/125 = 0.9200   any-valid 119/125 = 0.9520
      by op (any-valid): diff 23/25 · partsum 23/25 · binomial 25/25 · moebius 24/25 · shift 24/25

Local scaling is **+1 net, not +1 clean**: it gained `moebius` and `shift` one each and *lost* `diff`
one. Reported because a net figure hides that.

## 3. **ENTRY GATE PASSED at 0.9520** — and it passed by one pair

0.9520 ≥ 0.95. The gate was preregistered as a numeric threshold and it is met, with no stratum
excluded; the `shift` carve-out refused in pass 1 stays refused and was never needed.

**The margin is one pair.** 119/125 = 0.9520 against a granularity of 1/125 = 0.008. Without the
ground-truth correction it fails at 0.912; without the hubness correction it fails at 0.944. A gate
cleared by one pair on development is not evidence that the instrument generalises — it is the
minimum evidence the preregistration demanded, and no more.

**Pre-committed now, before the frozen split is touched:** pass 3 must **first** measure L1
(any-valid, local-scaled) on the NEW frozen split. If frozen L1 top-1 < 0.95, the instrument did not
generalise, **E4 PARK applies, and no L2 number may be read** — a one-pair development margin does
not license a frozen L2 adjudication on its own. Only if frozen L1 ≥ 0.95 may pass 3 proceed to
E1/E2/E3, using the same any-valid rule and reporting answer-set sizes beside it.
