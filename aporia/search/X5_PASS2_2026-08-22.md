## A2 — my verdict rule was wrong, and there IS a second artifact: it is OVERLAP, not length

The script compared binomial's gap-reduction against the other operators and concluded "no second
artifact" because `moebius` reduced by the same 1.000. **That rule tested the wrong thing** — it
assumed binomial was uniquely affected, so an equally-affected second operator masked the finding
instead of confirming it.

    operator    V2 gap, full target   length-matched target   reduction
    diff                     1.3101                  0.1951      +0.851
    partsum               1906.7234               1906.5135      +0.000
    binomial               734.1297                  0.0060      +1.000
    moebius               1951.0567                  0.6092      +1.000
    shift                    1.3040                  0.6896      +0.471

Reading it correctly, with the benchmark's own `exact_terms` field beside it:

    operator    exact_terms  min / median / max     frac < 45
    diff                       21 / 40 / 44           1.000
    partsum                    20 / 38 / 45           0.675
    binomial                   20 / 24 / 24           1.000
    moebius                    20 / 35 / 40           1.000
    shift                      20 / 30 / 44           1.000

**The relation is only guaranteed over `exact_terms` — median 24 to 40 — but the V2 block computes
its features over up to 45 terms.** Every feature therefore incorporates the tail *beyond the window
where the relation is known to hold*, and truncating the target to the image's length collapses the
gap to near zero precisely where that tail is largest. `partsum` shows no reduction because a third
of its pairs run the full 45 terms, so there is nothing to truncate.

**A second artifact exists, and it is more fundamental than the padding bug**: it is not about
capped operators, it is about measuring a representation over a window wider than the relation it is
meant to detect. Note the accident that saved the raw block — `RAW_K = 20` exactly equals the
benchmark's minimum `exact_terms`, so the raw arm never reads past the guaranteed overlap while the
V2 arm always does. That is likely part of why a 20-term log-magnitude vector has stayed competitive
across four campaigns.

**Not fixed here.** X-5's representation is frozen by its own preregistration and frozen-B has not
been read; changing it now would be adaptive. Handed forward as the primary design change for a
successor: compute features over `min(exact_terms)` or per-pair over the guaranteed overlap.

## A3 — my second verdict rule was also wrong: G-pos is fine as written

The script reported "45% of learned derangements exceed the threshold → **G-POS UNRELIABLE**". That
label is an artifact of the rule, not of the gate:

    single-derangement granularity at n = 320:   1 hit = 0.003125
    G-pos threshold (3x adjusted chance)       =         0.002304

**A single draw already exceeds the threshold with one hit**, so per-draw it can only be satisfied by
exactly zero hits — which is why 45% "failed". But **G-pos is defined on the MEAN over 20
derangements**, and the mean is well below the line:

    learned  permuted mean 0.00166   min 0.00000   max 0.00937   p90 0.00313   true 0.3719
    raw      permuted mean 0.00125   min 0.00000   max 0.00625   p90 0.00313   true 0.1719
    threshold 0.002304

On frozen-B the granularity improves anyway: 1 hit = 0.001563 sits *below* the threshold, 2 hits
above. **G-pos as preregistered is resolvable and passes on development.** Pass 3 applies it
unchanged, on the mean, exactly as written.

## The pass's most transferable finding is about my own instruments

**Two of the three verdict rules I wrote in this audit script were wrong** — A2's compared against a
comparator that was equally affected, A3's compared single draws against a threshold designed for a
mean. Both produced confident labels (`NO SECOND ARTIFACT`, `G-POS UNRELIABLE`) that were the
opposite of the truth. The measurements were sound; the automated judgements over them were not.

Four campaigns of doctrine have been about preregistering thresholds and verifying partitions. This
adds a fifth requirement of the same family: **a verdict rule is an instrument and needs the same
scrutiny as the measurement it judges** — state what it would say under the null, and check that its
comparator can actually discriminate the thing it names.
