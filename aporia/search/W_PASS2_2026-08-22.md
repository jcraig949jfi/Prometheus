## 2. The raw arm is structurally zero, and that is the control

`raw@20` sits inside every pair's guaranteed overlap (0.0% of pairs have `exact_terms < 20`), so
windowing cannot change it. **W_raw ≡ 0 by construction** — reported as a structural control rather
than computed. If the machinery ever produced a non-zero W there, it would be a bug.

## 4. The finding that matters: my threshold is ~3x the resolution

The MDE was **restated from the observed within-arm discordance**, not inherited:

    preregistered (P123)    assumed discordance 0.15   ->  MDE 0.0306
    observed (this pass)    discordance 0.0225         ->  MDE 0.0106 at frozen n = 800

Within-arm discordance is tiny — the two variants agree on 97.75% of pairs — because windowing
perturbs features modestly and most pairs flip together. **The campaign can resolve effects roughly
three times smaller than its own branch thresholds assume.**

The consequence is concrete and uncomfortable. The observed effect of 0.0175 is **1.65x the actual
MDE** — comfortably detectable — but sits **well below the 0.0306 W1 bar**. On frozen, a real,
statistically clean effect of that size would fire **W2 REDESIGN**, whose preregistered gloss is
"real but below the powered effect." That gloss would be wrong: the effect is above the *achievable*
resolution and below only a threshold set from a variance assumption that turned out to be off by a
factor of nearly seven.

**The branches are not being changed.** They are final, frozen is unread, and adjusting a threshold
after seeing a development effect is exactly the adaptivity the split exists to prevent. Pass 3
applies W1–W5 as written and reports this mismatch beside whatever fires.

**New doctrine, added to `engine/LOOP_APORIA.md`:** the existing gate-design rule catches thresholds
*finer* than the measurement can resolve. This is the mirror failure — a threshold far *coarser* than
the resolution, which makes a real detectable effect unreportable as an advance. Check the threshold
against the measured variance **in both directions**, and where the variance is unknown in advance,
preregister the threshold **as a multiple of the MDE** rather than as an absolute number.

## 5. Sixth firing of the wrong-population lesson, in a new direction

Five prior firings had a statistic measured on one population misapplied to another and making the
work look better than it was. This one made the campaign look *worse-powered* than it is, by assuming
a cross-arm discordance for a within-arm quantity. The lesson generalises: an assumed variance is a
measurement you have not made, and it can be wrong in the direction that costs you a true finding as
easily as in the direction that flatters one.
