**The line's real output is a replicated negative.** Two independent representation families — one

## Should the line continue

**Not as it stands.** Two representation families have failed the same way; a third would be
momentum. But pass 2 found something that changes the question rather than the representation: **the
relation is guaranteed only over `exact_terms` (medians 24–40) while features are computed over up to
45 terms**, so every measurement in all five campaigns partly reads a window where no relation holds.
`RAW_K = 20` happens to equal the benchmark's minimum `exact_terms` — the raw arm never reads past
the guaranteed overlap, and the elaborate arms always do. That is a specific, cheap, falsifiable
explanation for why the simple baseline keeps winning.

**Recommendation, stated as a judgement and not a certainty:** one further campaign is justified
*only* if the overlap fix is the preregistered hypothesis with a stated directional prediction —
compute every feature over the guaranteed per-pair overlap, and predict that the elaborate arms'
advantage over raw terms increases. If that fails, the line closes. It needs a fresh benchmark build
because no reserve remains, so the cost is roughly four passes.

**The honest competing option:** spending those four passes on a different question entirely is at
least as defensible. The X-line has already produced its transferable result, and a fifth
representation attempt on a sixth benchmark is the kind of continuation the campaign discipline was
written to prevent. I would not argue strongly against a reader who read this tally and closed the
line here.

## GATE_ELI5 — what this park is waiting on

**What is stuck:** a fancy learned way of comparing sequences looked twice as good as the simple one
while we were tuning it, but on fresh data the advantage vanished — and separately, our system still
fails to recognise about one sequence in eight when handed it directly, so we cannot tell "the fancy
method is genuinely no better" apart from "our search is too unreliable to see the difference."

**What would unstick it:** we have been measuring sequences over 45 terms while only the first 20–40
are actually guaranteed to be related, so the comparison includes a stretch where the sequences have
no reason to match. Recompute every feature over just the guaranteed stretch, on a fresh benchmark,
and predict in advance that the elaborate methods improve relative to the simple one — then that
single test settles whether five campaigns of negative results were measuring the wrong window.
