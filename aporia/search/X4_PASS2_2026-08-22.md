# CAMPAIGN X-4 pass 2/3 — the learned metric doubles L2 and destroys L1

Both frozen splits untouched: frozen-A (800) is pass 3's decision split, frozen-B (800) is reserved
for a successor and was not opened. Only the 400 development pairs were used. **No frozen quantity
was computed.** M0–M5 not adjudicated.

## The fit, with `k` fixed by a rule rather than a search

150-dim feature space (raw-25 sign-log ⊕ V2's 125). Generalised eigenproblem `S_neg v = λ S_pos v`,
where `S_pos` is the covariance of `z_src − z_tgt` over development positives and `S_neg` the same
over development negatives.

**Rule for k, stated before running: keep every direction with λ > 1.0.** That is not a knob — λ > 1
says exactly *"along this direction decoy pairs are more spread out than true pairs"*, so it is the
natural boundary between informative and anti-informative directions and it is readable off the
spectrum before any retrieval happens. **100 of 150 directions kept.** No values of k were tried and
compared; doing that and keeping the winner is a hyperparameter search wearing a disguise, and on
400 pairs with no reviewer it would not be caught.

    eigenvalues: top five [304.9, 276.9, 200.8, 136.3, 107.4], median 1.622, min 0.030

## Result: a dissociation, not an improvement

    L1 development (any-valid)   272/400 = 0.6800   Wilson 95% CI (0.6328, 0.7238)
      diff 73/80 · partsum 76/80 · binomial 3/80 · moebius 55/80 · shift 65/80
    L1 development (strict)      246/400 = 0.6150

    L2 development, four real operators (n = 320), like-for-like, neither arm corrected:
      learned   129/320 = 0.4031
      raw        56/320 = 0.1750
      D = +0.2281   paired 95% CI [0.1790, 0.2773]   (b = 77 learned-only, c = 4 raw-only)

**L2 roughly doubles while L1 collapses**, and `binomial` L1 falls to **3/80**. The mechanism is
visible in the objective: the metric was fitted to pull sources and targets *together*, which shrinks
exactly the directions along which a sequence differs from its own relatives — the directions
identity resolution needs. It buys relational proximity by spending identity.

## The control that had to run before believing any of it

A metric that merely **coarsened** the space would produce both symptoms — higher top-10 hit rates
and destroyed identity — without learning anything. Two controls, both fixed before either ran:

    C1 PERMUTATION NULL (score each source's top-10 against a DIFFERENT pair's answer set)

      learned_plain        true 129/320 = 0.4031 | permuted 1/320 = 0.0031 | ratio 129
      learned_localscaled  true 122/320 = 0.3812 | permuted 1/320 = 0.0031 | ratio 122
      raw_plain            true  56/320 = 0.1750 | permuted 1/320 = 0.0031 | ratio  56
      raw_localscaled      true  59/320 = 0.1844 | permuted 1/320 = 0.0031 | ratio  59

**The permuted rate is identical — 1/320 — in both arms.** The learned space is not coarser; a
random answer set is hit exactly as rarely as before. **The coarsened-space explanation is refuted**,
and the gain is relational.

    C2 LIKE-FOR-LIKE PROCESSING. The pre-committed skew > 1.0 rule corrected the learned space
    (skew 1.305) but NOT raw (skew 0.952), so the two arms had received different processing and the
    headline D confounded representation with correction. Re-scored with neither corrected, D rises
    to +0.2281 — the asymmetry was working *against* the learned arm, not for it.

**Development D remains the training objective, not an estimate.** It is biased upward by
construction and means little on its own. The permutation null is not biased that way, and it is
what carries the claim.

## Two problems this pass creates, both reported before frozen is touched

**1. The preregistered power assumption has failed, exactly where P117 said it might.** Observed
development discordance is **0.2250** against the preregistered assumption of 0.1125. That doubles
the variance, so the minimum detectable effect at n = 640 rises from **0.0265 to 0.0375**. Frozen-A
is therefore *not* powered for the 0.03 that X-3 could not exclude. It remains far more than
adequate for an effect of the observed size, but a small true effect would now be missed — and the
P117 weakness note predicted this failure mode in advance.

**2. K0 will fail, and the gate is mis-specified for this case.** Development L1 is 0.680 with an
upper bound of 0.724; frozen-A L1 above 0.90 is not credible. Under the preregistration that is
**M0 PARK with no L2 computed** — a campaign parked while showing the strongest signal in four
campaigns.

The gate's justification, from X-2 and X-3, is that an L2 **null** is uninterpretable if the index
cannot find what it is handed. **That argument covers nulls and does not cover positives.** A gate
built to make nulls interpretable should not veto a positive result.

**I am not amending the preregistration.** Pass 3 reads frozen-A once and applies M0–M5 exactly as
written, including M0 PARK if K0 fails; deviating after seeing development data is precisely what
preregistration exists to prevent, and my prediction that K0 will fail is itself a claim that should
be tested rather than assumed — I have been wrong about transfer four times.

**The amendment belongs to a successor, and the reserve split is why one is possible.** X-5 should
preregister a gate that is *conditional on the direction of the result*: K0 required before a null
may be read as a null, not required before a positive may be read as positive — with the positive
arm instead gated on the permutation null, which is the control that actually protects it. Frozen-B
is untouched and available for exactly this. **The multi-split build paid for itself one pass after
being built.**
