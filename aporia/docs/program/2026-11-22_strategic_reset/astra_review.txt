# **Independent Adversarial Review**

PROJECT PROMETHEUS · STRATEGIC RESET · NOVEMBER 22, 2026

Overall verdict: REJECT AS WRITTEN

**Retain the strategic inversion. Reject the proposed experimental sequence and its scientific gates.**

The proposal is materially better than launching a 26th engine. It correctly identifies architectural inflation, weak experimental demand, and the failure to acquire reusable computational mechanisms as central problems.

But it contains a serious contradiction:

**Prometheus has spent eight months demonstrating that toy substrates do not support reliable compositional discovery. The reset proposes making success in another toy substrate a prerequisite for returning to real computational problems.**

That is not a defensible experimental dependency.

A second problem is more fundamental. Moving a frontier LLM into the abstraction loop and unplugging it during evaluation demonstrates that useful machinery can be *distilled*. It does not demonstrate that the machinery was autonomously discovered, that it is genuinely novel, or that an evolving computational ecology developed it.

Those are three separate scientific claims. The current gates do not adequately distinguish them.

A third problem is that the proposal makes an inadequately validated LLM-based detector responsible for judging whether discoveries are independent of familiar human ideas. The proposed calibration establishes neither that capability nor the claimed inference.

## **1. What the evidence actually establishes**

I accept the reported experimental baseline provisionally, not as independently reproduced findings. The supplied documents establish the intended measurements and prior audit conclusions, but I have not rerun the repository.

AGREE

**The composition wall is real within the tested architectures.**

Eight substrate families failed to reliably discover two-step mechanisms without individually rewarding their components. That is compelling evidence against repeating the same search architecture.

AGREE

**The supplied-stones versus discovered-stones distinction is critical.**

The TFS-1, Aether and Crius results support the hypothesis that the limiting factor is discovering, retaining and exploiting intermediate structure—not simply executing structures once supplied.

AGREE

**The operating system became a research liability.**

The 75-seat organization, 54% of files in agent/role directories, and disproportionate instrumentation effort justify radical simplification.

MODIFY

**The composition wall is not a universal impossibility result.**

The experiments share architectural assumptions, reward structures, evaluation choices and research history. Eight failed substrates are not eight independent proofs that composition requires an LLM, a new representation, or a specific learning algorithm.

DISAGREE

**An LLM-unplugged success is not necessarily Sagacity.**

A conventional compiler, manually engineered library, learned search heuristic or program synthesizer can all exhibit reusable abstraction and strong transfer. The experiment must distinguish the proposed mechanism from these alternatives.

The evidence identifies a problem worth solving. It does not uniquely identify the proposed solution.

The strongest result Prometheus could produce next is not another impressive performance gain. It is evidence that **previously discovered intermediate structures make subsequent discoveries possible that were otherwise unreachable under matched resources**, and that this effect compounds through inheritance rather than increased exposure, compute or injected prior knowledge.

That is the actual scientific target.

### **Phase 1 — KILL as prerequisite**

The Gravity Pilot is scientifically weaker than the proposal acknowledges.

It asks a model to distinguish genuinely unfamiliar computational mechanisms from familiar mechanisms disguised through alternative terminology or structure.

The 12 calibration controls cannot establish that ability.

Recovering disguised PID, Kalman filtering, stigmergy and other familiar mechanisms demonstrates some ability to recognize familiar patterns. It does not demonstrate the ability to recognize a mechanism that lacks a familiar analogue.

This is an asymmetric classification problem:

- Recognizing known concepts can be positively tested.
- Declaring something genuinely unprecedented requires a vastly stronger negative claim.
- Failing to recognize something familiar can easily be mistaken for discovering something alien.

The Hecate results sharpen the concern. The reported 0.98 AUC concerns separation of unfamiliar lawful systems from noise, not recognition of historically unprecedented computation. Its adversarial alien performance was materially weaker.&#x20;

## **3. Attack on the core inversion: compiled human priors**

The inversion is useful engineering. The proposed scientific interpretation is too ambitious.

Suppose a frontier model reviews failures from a program synthesizer and proposes:

- a new bitwise manipulation primitive;
- a rewrite law connecting two representations;
- a search priority favoring a previously successful construction.

The deterministic optimizer subsequently solves many problems without calling the model.

What happened?

Possibility A: The model exposed a familiar mathematical identity that the optimizer did not previously know.

Possibility B: The model inferred a useful domain-specific pattern from experimental evidence.

Possibility C: Evolution and selection subsequently combined the proposed structures into something not explicitly present in the model's proposal.

Only C begins approaching Prometheus's stronger hypothesis. A and B may still have substantial engineering value, but they should not be described as autonomous compositional discovery.

**The proposed Phase 1 + Phase 4 separation does not solve this.** Phase 1 tries to infer the origin of knowledge from model judgments. Phase 4 attempts to establish novelty through further transformations and the same class of judgments.

Neither establishes causal independence from the LLM's inherited knowledge.

### **A stronger causal decomposition**

For a real-domain experiment, require these distinct comparisons:

| Experimental arm                             | What it tests                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Frozen deterministic solver                  | Existing search capability                                                       |
| LLM-supplied library, no learning            | Benefit from compiled model knowledge                                            |
| Deterministically learned library            | Autonomous acquisition                                                           |
| LLM-guided learned library                   | Combined guidance and experience                                                 |
| Learned library, no inherited structure      | Whether inheritance caused the gain                                              |
| Learned library with matched sham structures | Whether gains result from genuine semantics rather than larger search vocabulary |

The key result would be that **learned inheritance produces an additional gain beyond the frozen LLM-supplied library**, under matched offline and online resources.

An even stronger result would be the creation of a useful mechanism whose intermediate parts were not explicitly proposed by the model, with a verified lineage showing how the search process constructed and retained them.

That would still not prove "non-human reasoning." It would provide defensible evidence of autonomous computational organization.

One more distinction matters: discovery is not the same as the appearance of novelty in a printed program. Multiple structurally unusual circuits may compute an ordinary known algorithm. Claims of novelty must operate on semantic and causal structure, not naming or syntax alone.

## **4. The mathematically honest accounting rule**

The proposed `MG-UNPLUGGED` versus `SHADOW-NAKED` comparison is vulnerable to choosing a convenient number of evaluation tasks.

This can be corrected without adding much infrastructure.

### **Use an explicit amortization model**

Let:

- \\(F\\) = total fixed cost of producing the crystallized artifact, including unsuccessful LLM proposals, training searches, verification, compilation and development-time selection.
- \\(v_M\\) = average cost per evaluation task using the frozen machine.
- \\(v_L\\) = average cost per task using direct frontier-model prompting, including retries and verification.
- \\(N\\) = number of future tasks drawn from the declared deployment distribution.

Then:

\\[ C\_{\mathrm{MG}}(N)=F+Nv_M \\]

\\[ C\_{\mathrm{naked}}(N)=Nv_L \\]

Assuming comparable success rates and \\(v_L>v_M\\), the break-even workload is:

\\[ \boxed{N^\*=\frac{F}{v_L-v_M}} \\]

This is more informative than whether the crystallized system happens to win on 30 tasks.

The decisive correction is to **report the entire amortization curve**, not a single conveniently selected point.

Predeclare a deployment workload independently of experimental outcomes. Report the break-even value, confidence intervals, and cost per verified solution. If workload or success rates differ, report a success-versus-total-cost Pareto curve rather than claiming one universal break-even point.

Also report CPU time, wall time and verifier calls separately. API dollars are not a complete measure of computational cost.

### **Four additional accounting requirements**

**First: include failed research expenditure.** If 200 LLM proposals produce three useful laws, the cost of all 200 proposals belongs in \\(F\\).

**Second: compare equal success conditions.** A direct LLM with 90% task success cannot be declared inferior to a crystallized solver with 30% success merely because the latter has lower marginal cost.

**Third: report failures as censored outcomes.** The current G2 alternative of ≥5× lower median hitting cost *on solved families* can reward a system that solves only the easiest tasks. Use a fixed per-task timeout and report coverage, capped cost and verified success jointly.

**Fourth: give direct prompting a fair tool allowance.** Compare naked prompting both without tools and with access to the same external verifier or deterministic execution tools. Otherwise the benchmark may primarily measure tool access rather than crystallization.

This accounting is straightforward and largely automatable. There is no need to build another certification subsystem.

## **5. The hidden foundry trap: qualifying Order-2 tasks**

The requirement for 30 fresh families is insufficient.

Independently authored does not mean independently difficult. A different model family may construct problems using the same familiar algebraic motifs as the Builder.

Worse, a task described as "requiring two stepping stones" may have a simpler one-step solution in the actual search language.

The minimum viable qualification should require four properties.

| Requirement                  | Minimum evidence                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Constructive solvability** | A witness program exists and passes the independent verifier                                                                     |
| **Order-2 necessity**        | Two intermediate structures are causally required by a qualifying witness; removing either breaks that construction              |
| **No cheap shortcut**        | Bounded exhaustive search or a solver-backed lower-bound check excludes shorter solutions within the declared grammar and budget |
| **Non-saturation**           | Baselines do not solve essentially 0% or 100% of the family under the intended resource cap                                      |

The word *bounded* matters. Proving that no smaller program exists in every conceivable representation is not feasible. Proving the absence of a shortcut inside a specifically declared finite search space is an achievable qualification criterion.

Do not send the hidden witness or decomposition to the learner.

The qualification procedure must also reject timeouts and unknown outcomes rather than treating them as proof that a problem is hard.

**My minimum:** Qualify a small collection of independent families first, then freeze the generator, grammar, budgets and holdout seeds. Use family-level sampling and paired runs rather than treating numerous closely related examples as independent evidence.

If qualifying the first ten candidates consumes more engineering than implementing the learning mechanism, the experimental substrate has failed its practicality test.

Do not build another sophisticated World-Demand Foundry to rescue it.

### **Concrete starter slice**

Use SMT-LIB bit-vector expressions with 8-bit programs initially, then 16- and 32-bit programs. Support a small, fixed collection of well-defined integer and bitwise operations: AND, OR, XOR, NOT, shifts, rotates, addition, subtraction and conditionals where needed.

Avoid undefined behavior and ambiguous signedness.

Given a reference program \\(f\\), search for a cheaper equivalent program \\(g\\).

The verifier checks:

\\[ \exists x:\ f(x)\neq g(x) \\]

If the query is `UNSAT`, the expressions are equivalent under the stated semantics. A solver timeout or `UNKNOWN` result is not accepted as a proof.

Optimization cost is independently measured using a frozen operation-count or instruction-cost model. Equivalence alone is insufficient; simply reproducing the input program must not count as an optimization.

For independently verified headline discoveries, use a second SMT solver where feasible.

### **Why this domain beats the alternatives**

It avoids reinventing the execution semantics of another toy world. The verifier is mature, the problem connects to real compiler optimization, and the search space contains meaningful combinations of reusable transformations.

Importantly, no model must decide whether a proposed optimization is correct.

A rewrite law is admitted only when its semantic preconditions are established. An observational-equivalence collision is a candidate discovery, not sufficient proof of a universal rewrite.

The existing Souper work provides relevant architectural precedent for SMT-verified superoptimization. Its original repository was archived in October 2025, so I would use it as a reference and comparator where feasible, not commit to integrating its LLVM toolchain.&#x20;

### **The compounding experiment**

Freeze three artifact snapshots:

\\[ A_1=(L_1,T_1,S_1),\quad A_2=(L_2,T_2,S_2),\quad A_3=(L_3,T_3,S_3) \\]

Evaluate all three on the **same untouched final holdout distribution**, with identical search budgets.

Do not compare Generation 1 on easy tasks with Generation 3 on harder tasks and interpret the difference as compounding. That confounds generation with task difficulty.

Require evidence that:

1. G2 improves on G1, and G3 improves on G2, under matched evaluation resources.
2. Improvements survive independent task-family shifts rather than only new examples of old motifs.
3. Removing inherited abstractions erases the corresponding improvement.
4. A matched sham-library control does not reproduce the effect.
5. Strong conventional synthesis and optimization baselines do not explain away the entire improvement.

The model proposing new laws is allowed during the sleep phases, but its total cost remains attributable to each generation.

**What would be a breakthrough?** A compact, verified library that repeatedly develops new useful abstractions, transfers them to unseen integer-program families and demonstrates a causal dependency on inherited intermediate structures across generations.

Even that would be a strong result in *compositional program synthesis*, not proof of general intelligence or alien computation.

## **7. Did the bake-off triage discard the wrong things?**

### **Fable 5.2 / Mathesis — MODIFY**

The proposal is too quick to designate M0 as the strongest zero-LLM baseline.

M0's rewrite-law mining is useful, but the load-bearing hypothesis may be in the structure around it.

In particular, the proposed **schemas&#x20;**\\(S\\) and inverse rules could matter more for crossing the composition wall than a larger collection of rewrite laws.

Consider the distinction:

- A forward rewrite law simplifies something already discovered.
- An inverse or goal-directed rule can reveal a useful intermediate structure that has not yet been discovered.
- A generalized schema can capture a family of transformations rather than one particular expression.

That is directly relevant to the repeated failures in which component structures have no independent fitness.

Only implementing OE-collision mining and normal-form enumeration risks evaluating the easiest part of Mathesis while discarding the part most capable of changing search connectivity.

The supplied documents do not contain enough of the full Mathesis implementation specification to establish that its schemas would work. This is a mechanistic reason to preserve them as an experimental option, not proof they will.

**Patch:** Keep M0 as a baseline, but allow one bounded extension incorporating schemas/inverse rules if the initial experiment shows that forward rewriting cannot cross the validated composition barrier.

Do not allocate a separate large project to M1 before that evidence.

### **Opus 5.5 / RLL — AGREE with deferment**

I would not build the new self-editing DSL.

Its reflexive self-improvement architecture introduces precisely the meta-level composition problem Prometheus has repeatedly failed to solve at the object level.

However, its causal controls are worth retaining.

The most important reusable ideas are resetting the starting state, testing inheritance against non-inheritance, verifying improvements independently, and attributing an improvement to a particular modification of the search machinery.

Those tests can be applied to a small, conventional artifact containing rewrite rules, a library and search-policy parameters.

Self-modifying execution infrastructure is not a prerequisite for self-improving search behavior.

## **8. Most likely counterfeit positive**

**My highest-probability failure mode is a counterfeit Order-2 result caused by the benchmark's hidden construction assumptions.**

The Cross-Family Adversary authors task families that appear to require two reusable intermediate structures.

The model-guided search discovers an abstraction that exploits a repeated construction motif. The baseline lacks that shortcut, so `MG-UNPLUGGED` scores strongly.

Removing the abstraction causes performance to collapse. The knockout passes.

The Cross-Family Adversary finds no information leakage. RSO controls pass.

The headline becomes: *Prometheus has crossed the composition wall.*

But the machine actually learned a shortcut tied to the test generator. It did not necessarily discover independently useful compositional structure.

This can pass all the proposed G2 requirements.

### **Patch before Day 1**

Preregister the Order-2 qualification procedure and the distinction between *generator-specific reuse* and *cross-family structural transfer*.

Use at least two distinct task construction mechanisms, one never exposed during abstraction learning. Require the gain to survive the second mechanism.

Also include an ordinary cached-template baseline with a comparable library size. If the learned artifact cannot beat an equally sized cache of verified conventional transformations, the result supports library retrieval or compilation, not the stronger composition claim.

This is a much more important defense than another layer of agent-based adversarial review.

### **Three gate defects that must also be fixed**

**G2 has inconsistent branching.** It allows `ZERO-LLM-M0` to clear the BASE-OE improvement condition, but requires `MG-UNPLUGGED` to beat SHADOW-NAKED and pass its knockout. It then separately permits zero-LLM to advance if it matches or beats model-guided learning. These are different hypotheses and need different exit receipts.

**G2's cost threshold is susceptible to selection bias.** Median hitting cost computed only on solved instances can improve even when overall problem-solving capability declines.

**G3 doesn't isolate generational improvement.** `G3 > G1` on progressively harder, separately authored slices does not establish compounding unless all checkpoints receive comparable tests and resources.

None of these are merely documentation issues. Each can change a GO/KILL decision.

## **10. Single highest-ROI alternative**

## **A 28-day Verified Composition Sprint**

Rather than executing the proposed five-phase reset, I would run one deliberately narrow program.

**Days 1–3: Establish experimental viability**

Quiesce the fleet, pin dependencies, restore green tests. Run the tiny TFS-1 feasibility probe while bringing up Z3 equivalence checking and the first real bit-vector targets. If TFS-1 has no valid Order-2 headroom, stop using it; the real-domain experiment continues.

**Days 4–10: Test autonomous abstraction versus model guidance**

Build the smallest equivalent of \\(L,T,S\\) on the verified real domain. Compare frozen search, conventional cached transformations, deterministic abstraction learning and LLM-guided abstraction learning. Freeze the held-out tasks before running the comparison.

**Days 11–21: Test causal inheritance and compounding**

Run three generations with frozen intermediate artifacts. Evaluate all generations on common unseen families, matching compute and controlling for accumulated library size. Use selective knockouts and heredity-removed shadows.

**Days 22–28: Independent falsification**

Let the cross-family adversary attack the strongest claimed improvement. Test unseen family types, frozen cost accounting, conventional solver baselines, re-expression of known solutions and semantic equivalence. Publish the result whether positive, negative or inconclusive.

This schedule is a proposed research budget, not a prediction that the experiments will succeed or that implementation will proceed without interruptions.

### **The one decision this sprint must resolve**

Does preserving and recombining learned computational structure enable future discovery beyond what is achievable using the same resources without inherited structure?

Everything else is secondary.

If the answer is no, Prometheus has learned something important: adding libraries, search heuristics, and frontier-model proposals does not automatically overcome the composition barrier.

If the answer is yes, Prometheus has earned the right to investigate the harder questions of self-modifying representations, autonomous improvement and unfamiliar mechanisms.

### **Final adjudication**

The original reset correctly recognizes that **the next major investment should be in mechanisms of abstraction and composition, not more engines or organizational infrastructure**.

But it risks reproducing a familiar Prometheus pattern: build a sophisticated preliminary framework, spend time validating that framework, then discover that the underlying environment or representation cannot support the scientific question.

I would not authorize the proposed plan as written.

I would authorize the Verified Composition Sprint, with a strict distinction between these increasingly strong claims:

1. **Crystallization:** useful knowledge can be compiled into reusable, LLM-free mechanisms.
2. **Autonomous composition:** learned structures are causally reused to discover additional structures.
3. **Compounding:** this advantage strengthens across generations under matched resources.
4. **Low-prior novelty:** some discovered mechanisms resist reduction to known solution families under substantial independent examination.

Claims 1 and 2 are achievable experimental targets. Claim 3 is a serious scientific result if properly controlled. Claim 4 requires a separate research program and must not be smuggled into the interpretation of the earlier results.

**My single highest-ROI alternative is therefore a direct, exact-verification test of inherited compositional discovery in a real program-synthesis domain.**

Stop attempting to prove that an architecture *should* produce Sagacity. Create a condition in which it must produce causally attributable, transferable compositional gains—or fail cleanly.

That is the shortest defensible path from the existing Prometheus evidence toward the North Star.
