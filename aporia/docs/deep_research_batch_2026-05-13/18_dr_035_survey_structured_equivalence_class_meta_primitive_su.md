## (a) PRIMARY SOURCE CONFIRMATION

The verification of the candidate anti-anchor necessitates anchoring to unconditional primary sources that strictly distinguish Local Unitary (LU) equivalence coordinates from Stochastic Local Operations and Classical Communication (SLOCC) equivalence coordinates, and then precisely establish the mathematical conditions under which these coordinates collapse.

### Initial Falsification of the Finite-Class Conjecture
The assumption that AME states for a given multipartite system share a single SLOCC equivalence class—a gravity well driven by the fact that bipartite states of equal rank share a single class, and $N=3$ genuine entanglement is partitioned cleanly into finite GHZ and W classes—was unconditionally falsified in April 2020 by Burchardt and Raissi [cite: 1]. 

In their UNCONDITIONAL preprint (arXiv:2003.13639, April 02 2020), they state:
> "The conjecture that for a given multipartite quantum system all AME states are SLOCC-equivalent is falsified. We also show that the existence of AME states with minimal support of 6 or more particles results in the existence of infinitely many such non-SLOCC-equivalent states. Moreover, we present AME states which are not SLOCC-equivalent to the existing AME states with minimal support." [cite: 1]

They explicitly construct two families of $AME(5,d)$ states, $|AME(5,d)\rangle$ and $|AME(5,d)'\rangle$, and verify via direct computation of LU invariant polynomials that they are not LU-equivalent for any prime local dimension $d$.

### The Kempf-Ness Topological Closure Coordinate Collapse
The structural foundation linking LU equivalence to SLOCC equivalence relies on the Kempf-Ness theorem from Real Geometric Invariant Theory (R-GIT). This is the critical sub-tactic needed for the `Structured-Equivalence-Class Tier-E meta-primitive`. 

A state is defined as *critical* (or 1-uniform) if all of its single-party reduced density matrices are proportional to the identity [cite: 2]. The Kempf-Ness theorem establishes that a generic SLOCC orbit contains a critical state if and only if the orbit is topologically closed (in the Zariski topology). Furthermore, this critical state is unique up to the action of the maximal compact subgroup, which corresponds to Local Unitary (LU) operations [cite: 2, 3]. 

Because an $AME(N, d)$ state is by definition $\lfloor N/2 \rfloor$-uniform, it is strictly 1-uniform for all $N \ge 2$, making all AME states special classes of critical states. Thus, the substrate must register the following conditional invariant collapse:
**For any two AME states $|\Psi\rangle$ and $|\Phi\rangle$, $|\Psi\rangle \sim_{SLOCC} |\Phi\rangle \iff |\Psi\rangle \sim_{LU} |\Phi\rangle.**

### Definitive 2024 Primary Source Confirmation
The specific parameterization of infinite equivalence classes for $N=5$ AME states was definitively cemented by N. Ramadas and A. Lakshminarayan. The result was ANNOUNCED in November 2024 (arXiv:2411.04096) and PEER-REVIEWED and PUBLISHED in early 2025 in the *Journal of Physics A: Mathematical and Theoretical* (J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. 58, 125301 (2025)) [cite: 4, 5].

The exact theorem/result stated in the primary source:
> "One of our main findings is that the existence of special irredundant orthogonal arrays implies the existence of an infinite number of equivalence classes of AME states constructed from these. In particular, we show that there are infinitely many local unitary inequivalent three-party AME states for local dimension $d > 2$ and five-party AME states for $d \ge 2$." [cite: 2]

And explicitly binding the Kempf-Ness constraint:
> "A consequence of the Kempf-Ness theorem is that a SLOCC class is topologically closed if and only if it contains a critical state, and that this is unique up to LU equivalence. Therefore, two critical states belong to the same SLOCC equivalence class if and only if they are LU equivalent. This implies that SLOCC (and thus LOCC) equivalence is equivalent to LU equivalence for AME states, as these states are special classes of critical states." [cite: 2, 6]

## (b) FOLLOW-ON WORK (2024-2026)

A survey of literature in the 24-month target window reveals substantial follow-on work refining the topological coordinates of AME states, establishing related mathematical invariants, and propagating the Kempf-Ness equivalence. 

### Entanglement Coordinate Refinements
The understanding that LU and SLOCC coordinates collapse for AME states has been robustly cited and utilized in late 2025 literature. Rajchel-Mieldzioć et al., in a comprehensive survey of AME states published on arXiv (arXiv:2508.04777, v1 August 06 2025, v2 December 30 2025), assert:
> "Although, in the general case, for a given state $|\psi\rangle$ its orbit of SLOCC-equivalent states is strictly larger than its LU orbit, these sets are equal when we restrict the orbit to AME states. This results from the application of Kempf-Ness theorem for 1-uniform states. Consequently, since LU/LOCC/SLOCC sets coincide for AME states, for the remainder of this section, we shall mention the LU class alone." [cite: 3]

Similarly, Ramadas (arXiv:2512.02749, December 02 2025) introduces a new distinct coordinate, the **minimal decomposition entropy**, which identifies optimal local product basis representations. In establishing the framework, the author explicitly relies on the equivalence:
> "A coarser classification is provided by stochastic LOCC (SLOCC), which considers conversions achievable with a nonzero probability of success. For AME states, however, the LU, LOCC, and SLOCC classes coincide, so it suffices to consider only LU equivalence in their classification." [cite: 7]

### Distinct Coordinate (HARD-5) Preservation in Analogous Systems
While the LU/SLOCC/LOCC coordinates collapse for pure AME states, recent follow-on work highlights the critical necessity of preserving coordinate distinctions in neighboring domains.

The authors explicitly note that while LOCC and SLOCC yield defined equivalence classes for pure entangled states, "no analogous structure exists for nonlocal correlations," and "no corresponding unique unit has been identified for correlations" [cite: 8]. 
*   **Hypergraph States:** In March 2026, research into quantum hypergraph states (arXiv:2603.10917, March 11 2026) emphasizes that Local Clifford (LC) equivalence, LU equivalence, and SLOCC equivalence must be treated as independent, non-collapsing coordinates [cite: 9].
