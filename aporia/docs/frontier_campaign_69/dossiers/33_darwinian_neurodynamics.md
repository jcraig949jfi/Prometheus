PART 1. THE FIELD IN 2026, AND ITS FRONTIER

Darwinian Neurodynamics in 2026 is a specialized, mathematically rigorous subfield sitting at the intersection of evolutionary computation, cognitive architectures, and Bayesian inference. The field defines itself in strict opposition to older, purely selectionist theories like Edelman's Neural Darwinism. While Edelman proposed that synapses are selectively stabilized by reward without being copied, Darwinian Neurodynamics insists on true evolutionary replication: multiplying units with hereditary variation and selection. The replicators are not physical neurons, but the transient dynamic activity patterns those neurons produce (cite: 19).

What is SETTLED: It is mathematically settled that replicator dynamics can be implemented in neural networks without anatomical reproduction. Activity patterns can be copied from one network to another with high fidelity. It is also settled that to maintain a functioning heredity channel in Hopfield-like attractor networks, palimpsest memory mechanisms such as the Storkey learning rule are mandatory to prevent catastrophic forgetting (cite: 3, 42). Furthermore, Czegel and colleagues have conclusively demonstrated the existence of a sharp error threshold in these systems; if neural noise during pattern transmission exceeds a specific limit, the evolutionary accumulation of information collapses (cite: 26, 27).

What is CONTESTED: The biological plausibility of these models remains a fierce battleground. Szathmary, Fernando, and Czegel argue that the spatial organization of the cortex naturally supports parallel populations of networks exchanging solutions. On the other side, classical neuroscientists argue that the precise transmission fidelity required for Darwinian replication is unlikely to survive the noisy, analog environment of biological tissue, preferring gradient-based or purely selectionist explanations. A secondary contest involves the necessity of explicit fitness functions. Traditional models rely on symbolic, hard-coded fitness evaluators to score patterns, which critics argue breaks biological realism (cite: 4, 18).

What is OPEN: The frontier is currently focused on two open problems. The first is scaling these mechanisms from discrete binary combinatorial search to high-dimensional continuous control spaces. The second is integrating Darwinian Neurodynamics into modern Reinforcement Learning architectures. Recent theoretical work proposes using replicating neural dynamics to evolve multiple intrinsic reward functions simultaneously within a single agent lifetime, but empirical implementations of this remain open (cite: 35, 48).

What CHANGED in the last three years: Between 2023 and 2026, the field experienced a structural shift. Early work relied almost exclusively on Hopfield attractor networks (cite: 1, 3). The frontier has now shifted toward Reservoir Computing, where one recurrent network acts as a teacher to another, allowing for the replication of complex, time-varying dynamic patterns rather than static binary strings (cite: 19). Most significantly, Csillag and colleagues recently proved an algorithmic equivalence between finite population replicator dynamics and Bayesian particle filtering, formalizing evolutionary search not as a random heuristic, but as an exaptation of principled statistical inference (cite: 22, 54).

Expected Result:

The Three Most Common Ways People Get This Wrong:
1. Using a standard Hebbian covariance learning rule instead of the Storkey rule. Standard Hebbian learning will cause catastrophic forgetting after a few generations of retraining, causing the attractor landscape to collapse into spurious noise.
2. Failing to inject noise during the recall phase. The generation of variants relies entirely on noisy recall and spontaneous pattern generation in spurious attractors. If the relaxation is deterministic and noiseless, the mutation rate drops to zero and evolution halts.
3. Using synchronous batch updates for the Hopfield relaxation. Hopfield dynamics require asynchronous, randomized neuron updates to guarantee convergence to an energy minimum. Batching the updates for GPU speedup causes the networks to oscillate indefinitely between states without settling on a candidate solution.

PART 6. WHAT DOES NOT EXIST AND WOULD HAVE TO BE BUILT

If you are entering this field as a competent computational scientist in 2026 with access to compute, the most glaring gap is the total absence of a hardware-accelerated, vectorized framework for evolving populations of dynamic networks.

What you must build: A JAX or PyTorch based library for Darwinian Neurodynamics that natively handles the retraining of network populations as a batched tensor operation.

The Interface:
Inputs: A batched initial state tensor of shape "Population_Size by Network_Size", a fitness function callable, and an environment hyperparameter dictating neural noise levels.
Outputs: The phylogenetic tree of activity patterns, the rolling mean/max fitness, and the updated batched weight matrix of the population.

The Hard Part:
In standard deep learning, the network architecture and the training dataset are fixed, and you compute gradients over batches of data. In Darwinian Neurodynamics, the training data is dynamically generated by the networks themselves in the previous generation, and the weights of the networks are the subjects of the update. You must implement the Storkey learning rule (for Hopfield models) or reservoir teacher-forcing (for Reservoir models) as a vectorized operation across a population dimension. Writing a custom JAX `vmap` that handles parallel asynchronous relaxations for the variant generation phase, followed by a selective gathering of high-fitness states, followed by a batched outer-product weight update for the heredity phase, is highly non-trivial.

Several research groups have quietly rebuilt these exact vectorized operations privately to bypass the slow C-based CPU loops of the 2016 era, but no off-the-shelf tool exists in the open source ecosystem. Building this correctly is roughly one month of work for an expert tensor programmer.

PART 7. NEGATIVE RESULTS, FAILED PROGRAMMES, AND STANDING CRITIQUES

The history of Darwinian Neurodynamics is largely defined by distinguishing itself from failed or incomplete earlier paradigms.

The Failure of Neural Darwinism:
Gerald Edelman's theory of Neuronal Group Selection (Neural Darwinism) dominated theoretical neuroscience in the 1980s and 1990s. It proposed that synapses are selectively stabilized based on value or reward. This framework has been heavily critiqued by Fernando, Szathmary, and Czegel. The standing critique is that Neural Darwinism is purely selectionist, not evolutionary. It lacks a mechanism for true heredity and multiplication (copying information from one group to another). As proven by the baseline benchmark in Part 5, pure selection without heredity fails on complex combinatorial landscapes (cite: 10, 66, 32). This critique has never been refuted by the Edelman camp, leading to the deliberate rebranding of the modern field as Darwinian Neurodynamics.

The Catastrophic Forgetting Artefact:
Early attempts to implement evolutionary search in attractor networks failed because researchers used standard Hopfield dynamics. When a standard Hopfield network is iteratively retrained on highly correlated offspring patterns over generations, its memory capacity is rapidly exceeded, leading to catastrophic forgetting. The method looked promising for five generations and then collapsed. This was eventually shown to be an artefact of the Hebbian covariance rule. The field only bypassed this failure by adopting the Storkey palimpsest memory rule, which gracefully degrades old memories rather than shattering the entire landscape (cite: 3, 12).

The Error Threshold Collapse:
Czegel (2021) documented a critical negative physical limitation of the theory. In their reservoir computing experiments, they discovered a sharp phase transition known as the error threshold. If the neural noise level during the teacher-learner transmission phase exceeds a specific critical value, the evolutionary process entirely breaks down. Information accumulated by the Darwinian process cannot be maintained, and the population's fitness drops to random baselines (cite: 26, 27). Any system operating above this noise threshold is measuring noise artefacts, not evolutionary optimization.

The "Just Another Genetic Algorithm" Critique:
For years, computer scientists critiqued the field by claiming Darwinian Neurodynamics was just a standard genetic algorithm poorly implemented on slow neural network simulators. Why simulate Hopfield networks when you can just manipulate bitstrings directly? This standing methodological critique was finally answered by Csillag and colleagues in 2024/2025 (cite: 54, 55). They proved a formal algorithmic equivalence between replicator-mutator dynamics and Bayesian particle filtering in hidden Markov models. They demonstrated that Darwinian dynamics over neural representations is not a heuristic genetic algorithm, but an exaptation of sampling-based Bayesian inference. The neural implementation allows the collective to infer the statistics of high-dimensional environments in real-time, which static bitstring genetic algorithms cannot dynamically achieve.

Given the computational leverage available in 2026, a well-resourced newcomer should bypass the discrete binary Hopfield networks entirely and aim directly at continuous control and modern Reinforcement Learning integration.

Rank 1 Experiment: Multi-Objective Evolutionary RL (The Hamidi-Czegel Synthesis)
What makes it feasible now: JAX/Flax hardware acceleration allows for the simultaneous forward-passing of thousands of reservoir networks in parallel during an RL agent's training loop.
The Experiment: Embed a population of Reservoir Computers inside the policy network of a standard Deep RL agent (like PPO). Instead of training the agent on a single scalar environment reward, allow the Darwinian Neurodynamics module to output replicating neural dynamics that serve as multiple intrinsic reward functions. The reservoirs that output intrinsic reward signals leading to higher survival in the RL environment are selected and act as teachers to the less fit reservoirs, simulating evolution within a single lifetime (cite: 2, 35, 48).
What it measures: The speed of policy convergence in sparse-reward environments compared to a standard scalar-reward baseline.
Falsification: The idea is falsified if the evolutionary module collapses into outputting a static scalar clone of the environment reward, or if the computational overhead of the reservoir population results in slower wall-clock convergence than simple random exploration.

Rank 2 Experiment: Empirical Error Thresholds in High-Dimensional Continuous Spaces
What makes it feasible now: We can easily trace massive neural phylogenies using modern graph databases.
The Experiment: Replicate the Czegel 2021 TSP experiment, but replace the discrete combinatorial combinatorial paths with high-dimensional continuous robotic joint-angle trajectories. Systematically increase the teacher-forcing noise between reservoirs.
What it measures: The exact mathematical location of the error threshold phase transition in continuous control spaces.
Falsification: The idea is falsified if the error threshold does not appear as a sharp phase transition, but rather as a smooth linear degradation, which would indicate that continuous representations do not behave like discrete replicators.

What will NOT work:
Attempting to scale the foundational Szilagyi 2017 binary Hopfield architecture to millions of neurons to process raw perceptual data like image pixels. Attractor networks, even with the Storkey learning rule, have strict theoretical capacity limits. The Darwinian mechanism thrives as a cognitive combinatorial search engine in compressed, low-dimensional latent spaces. Throwing raw compute at binary attractor networks to solve vision or language problems will hit a catastrophic capacity ceiling and fail immediately.
