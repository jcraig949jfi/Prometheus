# FRONTIER PRACTITIONER DOSSIER: Open-Endedness Metrics and Evolutionary Activity

**Key Points**
*   Research suggests that open-ended evolution (OEE) is best understood as a continuum rather than a binary threshold, requiring continuous measurement rather than simple categorization [cite: 1, 2].
*   It seems likely that classical "shadow runs"—while theoretically elegant—suffer from long-term genetic drift, leading modern practitioners to rely more on persistence filtering and phylogenetic ancestry tracking [cite: 3, 4].
*   The evidence leans toward the conclusion that closed, autonomous AI-to-AI generative loops tend to collapse into high-probability attractors rather than yielding true open-ended novelty, highlighting a significant barrier in modern foundation model-driven ALife [cite: 5].
*   Emerging frameworks attempt to measure open-endedness relative to an "observer," acknowledging the complexity that what appears novel to a simple metric might just be noise to a human, and vice versa [cite: 6, 7].

**Understanding the Frontier**
For the computational scientist entering this space, the pursuit of open-endedness is the attempt to build systems that continually surprise us without simply degenerating into static noise. Historically, this meant building artificial environments and measuring whether new, useful components (like genes or behaviors) kept appearing. To prove these new components were actually useful and not just random junk, researchers compared the system to a "shadow run"—an identical simulation where survival was entirely random. If the real system produced more long-lasting components than the shadow, it was deemed to be evolving in an open-ended way. 

Today, this foundational concept remains relevant, but the tools have shifted. Modern research integrates massive AI models to judge whether a system's output is genuinely novel, and experimental platforms have evolved to test exactly which physical constraints or evolutionary rules are required to keep a system generating new things indefinitely. The field is highly interdisciplinary, balancing theoretical biology, physics, and advanced artificial intelligence, with ongoing debates about whether true open-endedness can ever be achieved in a completely isolated computer program.

PART 1. THE FIELD IN 2026, AND ITS FRONTIER

In 2026, the field of Open-Ended Evolution (OEE) and evolutionary activity has bifurcated into two distinct methodological camps that are presently attempting a synthesis. The first is the classical ALife lineage, anchored in computational substrates, cellular automata, and digital evolution (such as Avida, Tierra, and modern reaction-diffusion platforms like Genesis). This camp focuses on substrate-agnostic mathematical metrics, phylogenetic tracking, and isolating the precise physical or algorithmic constraints required to prevent an evolutionary system from plateauing [cite: 8, 9, 10]. The second, newer camp is driven by Deep Learning and Foundation Models (FMs). This faction treats Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models as mutation operators and "observers," arguing that open-endedness cannot be purely intrinsic to a system but must be defined relative to an observer capable of learning from the generated artifacts [cite: 6, 7, 11].

What is SETTLED: The field has reached consensus that open-endedness is not a binary property but a multidimensional continuum [cite: 1]. The original Bedau-Packard goal of classifying a system definitively into "Class 3" (unbounded) has been largely replaced by continuous measurements of change, novelty, and complexity potential. It is also settled that explicitly defined, static fitness functions actively prevent open-endedness; they inevitably lead to convergence [cite: 12]. True OEE requires endogenous selection pressures, novelty search, or constraint-driven survival [cite: 8, 13]. Finally, it is settled that raw novelty is insufficient; systems can generate unbounded noise. A filtering mechanism—whether a shadow run, a persistence filter, or an algorithmic learnability metric—is strictly mandatory [cite: 1, 3, 7, 14].

What is CONTESTED: The primary live disagreement is the Observer Debate versus the Substrate Debate. DeepMind and the FM-ALife researchers (Hughes, Kumar, Stanley, Clune) argue that foundation models are required to act as the "human-aligned observer" to judge novelty and learnability, asserting that without an observer, open-endedness is meaningless [cite: 6, 7, 11]. On the other side, classical ALife researchers and complexity theorists (Dolson, Channon, Bedau, Sharma, Lopez-Diaz) argue for intrinsic, observer-independent metrics. They contend that relying on an LLM to measure novelty just measures the boundaries of the LLM's training distribution, pointing to recent findings that AI-to-AI loops inevitably collapse into high-probability "visual elevator music" [cite: 5, 15]. 

What is OPEN: Achieving mathematically verifiable, unbounded growth in structural complexity within a closed, fitness-free computational system remains unsolved. Every strictly closed computational system evaluated to date has eventually reached a complexity plateau [cite: 16].

If the field has been absorbed, it is actively fighting absorption into mainstream Generative AI. Classical ALife risks becoming merely a benchmark suite for Foundation Models. What is lost in this merge is the rigorous pursuit of "life as it could be"—systems that generate alien complexities fundamentally outside human training data, which foundation models are structurally incapable of recognizing or rewarding.

You cannot measure OEE on a pre-computed CSV of images. The "benchmarks" in this field are established environmental configurations and archival data used to calibrate new metrics.

PART 5. THE REPRODUCTION RECIPE

The single most reproducible, informative, and structurally rigorous experiment in this field right now is the Genesis Version 3 Sham-Controlled Niche Construction Experiment (2026). It perfectly instantiates the modern application of the "shadow run" (sham control) to test a specific evolutionary mechanism (niche construction/secretion) without fitness functions.

The Specification:
Software and Version: Genesis, Version 3 architecture (Python/Numba implementation from GitHub `gearupsmile/genesis-emergence`, commit from roughly early 2026). [cite: 8, 26]
Dataset/Generator: Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion substrate.
Parameters to set: Diffusion rates D_u = 0.16, D_v = 0.08. Feed rate F = 0.035. Kill rate k = 0.060.
Replicates and Seeding: Exactly 10 independent replicates for the Main Run, and 10 for the Sham Control.
Seeding regime: Fixed integer seeds: 42, 123, 456, 789, 1011, 1213, 1415, 1617, 1819, 2021.
Duration: 1,000,000 generations.
Compute Cost: Approximately 24.7 hours of wall-clock time distributed across 9 workers using Numba JIT acceleration (roughly 220 total CPU hours). No GPU required. [cite: 8]
Expected Result: Secretion physics validation S_real = 0.48, S_sham = 0.00. Expressed Phenotype Complexity (EPC) growth = 0 in both conditions.
Citation for Number: Sharma, A. (2026). Genesis: A Falsifiable Platform for Open-Ended Evolution... arXiv:2607.21631. [cite: 8, 13]

How people get this experiment wrong (The Three Failures):
1. The Lempel-Ziv Artifact: When measuring the behavioural complexity of the agents, action traces must be strictly capped (e.g., at 100 steps). If action traces are allowed to span reproduction events continuously, the Lempel-Ziv compression algorithm yields artificially low values (approx 0.012 instead of the corrected 0.068), ruining the complexity metric [cite: 8].
2. Sham Drift: The sham control must isolate the exact causal variable (the environmental feedback of the secretion). If the sham run is poorly implemented by just randomizing all survival, the population size and spatial distribution will drift away from the real run. The sham must mirror the spatial constraints of the real run identically, only breaking the feedback loop of the secreted chemical.
3. Premature Evaluation: Running for 50,000 generations and observing high novelty. In complex ALife substrates, transient developmental noise looks exactly like open-ended evolution for the first 100,000 generations. The complexity plateau is only mathematically proven by hitting the 1,000,000 generation limit and observing the hard ceiling.

PART 6. WHAT DOES NOT EXIST AND WOULD HAVE TO BE BUILT

If you want to run frontier experiments measuring evolutionary activity across arbitrary systems, you will quickly discover a massive tooling void. 

1. The Substrate-Agnostic Shadow Runner (Middleware)
Currently, if you want to implement a Bedau-Packard shadow run or a Genesis-style sham control, you must hardcode it directly into the memory management and physics loop of your specific simulation. There is no off-the-shelf tool that can take an arbitrary Python or C++ simulation and manage a shadow population. 
What goes in: An API hook that fires on every organism birth, death, and mutation, plus a serialized state of the environment.
What comes out: A parallel, decoupled execution thread where the selection events are forced into an adaptively neutral distribution (randomized), while perfectly maintaining the population size and turnover rate of the main thread.
The hard part: State synchronization. As Channon proved in 2001, shadows drift [cite: 4]. The middleware must implement "shadow-resetting," pausing the shadow thread, deep-copying the main thread's genetic state at interval T, and then running random selection up to T+1. This requires extreme memory bandwidth and seamless serialization. Groups constantly rebuild this privately for every new paper because writing generic deep-copy hooks for custom ALife memory structures is excruciating. 

2. Continuous-Space Topological Persistence Filters
The MODES toolbox (via Empirical) elegantly handles persistence filtering, but it relies on exact phylogenetic trees of discrete genes or programs. In modern continuous-space artificial chemistry or embodied physics simulators, "parent-child" relationships and "genes" are often blurred by horizontal transfer, partial fission, or continuous mutation.
What goes in: A time-series of continuous parameter vectors (e.g., neural network weights or diffusion coefficients).
What comes out: A discrete phylogenetic tree isolating which continuous traits have persisted longer than a noise threshold.
The hard part: Defining the threshold for when a continuous vector becomes a "new component" versus a "slight variation of the old component." This requires dynamic clustering algorithms running over millions of generations. 

PART 7. NEGATIVE RESULTS, FAILED PROGRAMMES, AND STANDING CRITIQUES

The history of this field is a graveyard of systems that claimed to be open-ended, only to be debunked when the measurement apparatus was corrected. You must know these failures to avoid repeating them.

The Shadow Drift Failure (Channon, 2001)
The original Bedau-Packard evolutionary activity metric initialized a shadow run at time zero and let it run forever with random selection. Channon mathematically proved this invalidates the metric. After millions of generations, the genetic landscape of the real run is highly structured, while the shadow run is a randomized soup. Comparing a mutation in the real run to a mutation in the shadow run becomes meaningless, as the shadow is no longer a valid control for the real population's current state [cite: 4]. This standing critique was answered by Channon's "shadow-resetting" method, which forces the shadow to resync to the real population at every snapshot.

The Bounded Diversity Inflation Artifact
A massive flaw in the original definition of "unbounded mean cumulative evolutionary activity." If a system's diversity (population size or niche count) is strictly bounded by hardware, but a single "optimal" component evolves and is retained forever, its individual persistence count grows to infinity. Consequently, the *mean* activity of the whole system grows to infinity [cite: 18]. The system is mathematically classified as unbounded (Class 3), even though absolutely no new novelty is being generated. This critique was answered by shifting from mean activity to median activity, and ultimately led to the creation of the MODES toolbox to track novelty generation separately from persistence.

Spector and Stout's "Unlifelike" Spoofing (2005)
Spector and Stout deliberately built intuitively unlifelike, trivial string-manipulation systems that successfully achieved Class 3 unbounded dynamics under the Bedau-Packard metrics. This was a devastating negative result. It proved that evolutionary activity statistics can be fooled by a system that simply accumulates adaptively neutral "junk" strings that happen to persist due to algorithmic quirks rather than genuine adaptive utility [cite: 19]. This standing critique heavily motivated the modern shift toward phylogenetic persistence filtering (ensuring a component actually spawns a lasting lineage) rather than simple time-in-system counts.

The Passive Niche Construction Null Result (Sharma, 2026)
A major theoretical premise in ALife was that open-endedness requires agents to modify their own environment (niche construction), creating a shifting fitness landscape. Genesis Version 3 tested this by giving agents secretion physics to alter their chemical substrate. The sham-controlled results proved definitively that passive environmental modification is insufficient. Both the real run and the sham run flatlined at the exact same complexity ceiling. Environmental feedback alone does not break the complexity plateau [cite: 8, 13].

AI-to-AI Creative Collapse (2025)
With the rise of Foundation Models, a massive research programme attempted to achieve open-endedness by simply pairing two generative models in an autonomous feedback loop (e.g., an LLM proposing tasks and an LLM solving them, or text-to-image loops). Rigorous empirical testing in late 2025 proved this fails completely. Autonomous AI loops exhibit systematic drift toward high-probability attractors embedded within their training data—a phenomenon dubbed "visual elevator music." Without continuous human curation or explicit anti-convergence mathematical mechanisms, LLM loops are fundamentally bounded by their pre-training distributions and cannot generate temporally open-ended novelty [cite: 5]. 

PART 8. WHERE AN ENTRANT SHOULD ACTUALLY AIM IN 2026

If you are a well-resourced newcomer with compute and engineering capability, avoid building yet another continuous cellular automaton or writing philosophy papers about the definition of open-endedness. The frontier requires rigorous, causal experiments that bridge the gap between foundation models and classical ALife measurement.

1. The Sham-Controlled Foundation Model (Highest Rank)
What to do: Replicate the ASAL (Automated Search for Artificial Life) architecture [cite: 10, 11], which uses a Vision-Language Model to search for novelty in a cellular automaton. However, completely replace their measurement of novelty. Do not just measure cosine distance of embeddings. Build a rigorous shadow-resetting framework around the VLM's selection mechanism. Run the VLM-guided search, and parallel to it, run a shadow thread where the exact same cellular automaton configurations are selected randomly. Extract the OEE-metric (attractor cycle lengths) from both.
What makes it feasible now: ASAL open-sourced the hooks for VLM-to-ALife evaluation, and local, fast embedding models (like SigLIP) can now be run efficiently in parallel.
What it would measure: Whether Foundation Models actually induce mathematically verified structural open-endedness in a substrate, or if they simply cherry-pick visually pleasing but structurally bounded transient states.
Falsification: If the OEE-metric of the VLM-guided run does not statistically exceed the shadow run after 1,000,000 steps, it falsifies the DeepMind hypothesis that foundation models are sufficient observers for generating true open-endedness.

2. Meta-Evolution of Physics via Indirect Encodings (Second Rank)
What to do: Execute a replication and expansion of Genesis Version 4 [cite: 8]. Build a system where not only do the agents evolve via Compositional Pattern Producing Networks (CPPNs), but the physical constants of the simulation grid itself (diffusion, decay, collision physics) are treated as an evolving meta-genome subject to speciation. 
What makes it feasible now: JIT-compiled array operations (Numba/JAX/Taichi) allow millions of parallel reaction-diffusion physics updates on consumer GPUs, making meta-evolution of physical constants computationally tractable.
What it would measure: Whether allowing a system to dynamically rewrite its own physics rules bypasses the hard complexity plateaus observed in fixed-physics environments.
Falsification: If Expressed Phenotype Complexity (EPC) hits a hard ceiling comparable to Genesis V2 (approx 150) despite the physics parameters drifting, the hypothesis that "rigid physical laws bound open-endedness" is falsified.

What will NOT work:
Do not attempt to achieve open-endedness by simply taking an existing framework (like Avida, Lenia, or a standard Gridworld) and scaling it up to massive GPU clusters with billions of parameters. Classical ALife systems are structurally bounded by their genetic encodings and fixed update rules [cite: 9, 13, 16]. Running a bounded system for ten times as long, or on a grid ten times as large, simply results in it reaching its complexity plateau and homogenising faster. Furthermore, relying entirely on autonomous LLM-to-LLM loops without structural persistence filtering will fail; the system will inevitably collapse into interpolating its training data rather than evolving [cite: 5].
