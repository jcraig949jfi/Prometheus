* The Structural Reality of RL Evaluation
Research evaluating the synergy of multiple algorithmic mechanisms in reinforcement learning is inherently complex. While combining methods like curricula generation, retention policy changes, and representation learning seems intuitively promising, the empirical reality of neural network optimization frequently contradicts additive or multiplicative assumptions. The interactions between these mechanisms are often antagonistic, as they compete for the same representational capacity or disrupt the finely tuned optimization landscape of the baseline algorithm.

* The Illusion of the Additive Baseline
When evaluating combinatorial enhancements, the assumption that independent mechanisms should sum linearly is a mathematical convenience, not a property of neural networks. Because neural network training operates through non-linear gradient descent, multiple mechanisms often address the same underlying bottleneck, leading to extreme diminishing returns. A factorial experiment designed to estimate these interactions will reliably output positive or negative interaction terms that reflect the geometry of the metric's bounds and the limits of the network's capacity, completely obscuring whether the mechanisms themselves possess true synergistic properties.

## PART 2. THE STRONGEST PUBLISHED EVIDENCE AGAINST
The strongest published evidence against the viability and reliability of measuring synergistic interactions among reinforcement learning mechanisms stems from attempts to rigorously benchmark combinations of algorithmic improvements. 

The foundational critique of this experimental structure is found in Henderson et al., Deep Reinforcement Learning That Matters, arXiv:1709.06560. This paper demonstrated that apparent algorithmic improvements and their interactions are frequently artefacts of hyperparameter selection, network architecture, and random seed variance. Henderson explicitly showed that using independent seeds as the unit of analysis is statistically dangerous; selecting five or even ten random seeds can produce completely non-overlapping confidence intervals for the exact same algorithm. When testing a factorial design where factors are turned "on or off" without independent tuning, arXiv:1709.06560 serves as verified proof that performance changes are more likely driven by the untuned baseline's incompatibility with the default hyperparameters than by the mechanisms themselves. Furthermore, multiplying the reward by a constant or altering the baseline configuration can completely erase or invert the apparent performance gains.

The Rainbow DQN paper by Hessel et al., arXiv:1710.02298, explicitly tested the combination of multiple mechanisms (prioritized replay, multi-step learning, dueling networks, etc.). While the paper is often cited as a success story for combining mechanisms, the actual ablation studies within arXiv:1710.02298 provide severe evidence against clean, synergistic interactions. The ablation results verified that the effects of these mechanisms are heavily interdependent and display rapidly diminishing returns. As noted in retrospective analyses of Rainbow, combining improvements does not yield cleanly separable interaction terms; instead, the presence of one mechanism often renders another redundant or actively harmful if not meticulously co-tuned. 

Further evidence against the additive or multiplicative interaction assumption on bounded scales is found in recent investigations of performance ceilings, such as Chalumeau et al., arXiv:2505.21236. This research verifies that state-of-the-art reinforcement learning systems rapidly hit performance ceilings in complex tasks, resulting in bounded solve rates. When multiple mechanisms are combined, they collide with this performance ceiling, completely invalidating the linear assumptions required to accurately estimate a factorial interaction term. If mechanism A and mechanism B both push the agent near the ceiling, their combination cannot exceed it, mathematically forcing the ANOVA to report a severe negative interaction that is entirely an artefact of the metric's boundaries.

Additionally, the phenomenon of entropy regularization smoothing the optimization landscape, as discussed in retrospective reviews of policy gradient algorithms by Islam et al., UNCONFIRMED, suggests that many mechanisms intended to alter dynamics or retention simply act as implicit regularizers. If multiple mechanisms all implicitly smooth the landscape, their combination provides no extra benefit, yielding null or negative interactions that merely indicate a redundancy in regularization, not a profound mechanistic insight.

## PART 3. THE CONFOUND THAT MANUFACTURES A FALSE POSITIVE
The proposed experiment is structurally designed to manufacture false positive interactions through several concrete, mechanical confounds. If this experiment returns a positive interaction term, it is most likely measuring one of the following artefacts rather than true mechanistic synergy.

The Bounded Metric Compression (Ceiling and Floor Effects):
The experiment measures outcomes on a "solve-fraction scale," which is strictly bounded between 0.0 and 1.0 (or 0 and 100 percent). In a factorial ANOVA, an interaction is defined as the departure from additivity. Because the scale is compressed at the limits, the interaction term is fundamentally corrupted by the link function of the environment's difficulty. 
If the baseline solves 10 percent of tasks, Mechanism A adds 40 percent (reaching 50 percent), and Mechanism B adds 40 percent (reaching 50 percent), a purely additive expectation demands the combination reach 90 percent. If it only reaches 70 percent because of natural diminishing returns at the top of the sigmoid capability curve, the experiment reports a massive negative interaction. Conversely—and most dangerously for a false positive—if the baseline is at 0 percent, A reaches 5 percent, and B reaches 5 percent, but their combination crosses a critical threshold required to solve the task and jumps to 80 percent, the experiment will report a massive positive interaction. This is a false positive. The interaction is not a property of the mechanisms combining synergistically; it is merely a monotone activation function of the environment's threshold difficulty. The environment requires a minimum amount of "capability" to be solved, and only the combined arm cleared that threshold. 

The Untuned Baseline Confound:
The context explicitly states that "each factor is on or off rather than tuned." In deep reinforcement learning, introducing a new mechanism (such as changing retention policy, learning an encoding, or generating curricula) fundamentally alters the gradient flow, the effective batch size, or the optimization landscape. If the baseline is tuned for the absence of these mechanisms, and the treatment arms simply turn them "on" without retuning the learning rate, entropy coefficient, or network capacity, any measured interaction is an artefact of tuning compatibility. 
For example, learning a new encoding (Mechanism 1) might require a lower learning rate to remain stable. Changing the retention policy (Mechanism 2) might increase the variance of the batch, also requiring a lower learning rate. If both are turned on together, the combined effect might catastrophically crash the network because the default learning rate is now vastly too high. This yields a false negative. Alternatively, if one mechanism accidentally compensates for a poor hyperparameter choice in the baseline, the combination will show a massive false positive interaction, measuring only the correction of a sub-optimal hyperparameter rather than a true theoretical synergy.

Overstated Precision via Seed Variance:
The experiment uses "independent seeds as the unit of analysis." As verified by arXiv:1709.06560, random seed variance in deep reinforcement learning is highly heteroscedastic and non-normally distributed. If the factorial design uses standard OLS regression or traditional ANOVA to estimate the interaction term, it assumes homoscedasticity across the arms. However, combining mechanisms often inherently changes the variance (e.g., generating curricula usually reduces variance, while altering retention policies increases it). Using the seed as the unit of analysis overstates the degrees of freedom and the precision of the estimate, leading to artificially tight confidence intervals. A positive interaction term will easily clear the significance threshold purely because the standard error is artificially deflated by failing to account for the true unit of analysis, which should be a hierarchical model accounting for network initialization and environment sampling.

## PART 5. WHY SIMILAR PROGRAMMES STOPPED
Research programmes focused on meticulously categorizing the combinatorial interactions of architectural mechanisms in reinforcement learning have largely been abandoned. It is vital to distinguish why this occurred: they were not necessarily refuted on theoretical grounds, but they were abandoned because the field moved on to a more robust paradigm that rendered these micro-interactions irrelevant.

During the era of Rainbow DQN (arXiv:1710.02298), there was intense interest in understanding how mechanisms like prioritized replay, n-step returns, and distributional reinforcement learning interacted. However, researchers quickly discovered that these interactions were exceedingly fragile. An interaction that was robustly positive in one environment with one hyperparameter seed would invert to a negative interaction in a different environment or under a different learning rate schedule. The interaction terms were measuring the highly localized topography of the loss landscape, not fundamental laws of learning.

This research line was absorbed into the scaling paradigm. The community found that generalized, high-capacity architectures (like Transformers) combined with massive, diverse data and simple, stable algorithms (like Proximal Policy Optimization) reliably outperformed complex mosaics of handcrafted mechanisms. Programmes stopped testing whether "transporting failures" interacted with "changing retention policy" because the engineering overhead required to maintain these distinct mechanisms was unjustifiable when simply increasing the batch size or the context window provided superior, more predictable returns, as verified by recent scaling laws and studies like arXiv:2505.21236. For someone starting now, pursuing fine-grained interaction effects between discrete, on/off mechanisms is a regression to a highly brittle era of reinforcement learning research, fraught with irreproducibility.
