# Adversarial Review: The Viability of Dynamical Systems as Reusable Computational Components

The proposition that a fixed dynamical system, specifically a discrete cellular automaton driven as a streaming component with a trained readout, can serve as a robust and reusable computational component faces formidable theoretical and empirical challenges. Research strongly suggests that the apparent success of such systems often masks profound methodological artifacts, and the evidence leans toward the conclusion that the encoding mechanisms and the linear readout are manufacturing the computational results rather than the intricate dynamics of the substrate itself. While the concept of emergent computation at the edge of chaos is theoretically elegant, empirical validations frequently fail rigorous ablation testing, indicating that the actual information processing is localized in the periphery of the architecture. The following report extensively details the mechanics of these confounds, the specific published evidence refuting the core hypothesis, and the precise controls required to expose the illusion of computation within the dynamical substrate.

## PART 2. THE STRONGEST PUBLISHED EVIDENCE AGAINST

The specific architectural hypothesis under review—combining a cellular automaton substrate with a linear readout to form a reusable computational component—is known in the literature as Reservoir Computing with Cellular Automata, or ReCA. The strongest published evidence refuting the claim that the cellular automaton is responsible for the computation comes from recent, highly targeted critiques that re-evaluated the foundational benchmarks of this exact field. 

The most devastating evidence is found in the work of Glover, Osipov, and Nichele, specifically in their paper titled "On when is Reservoir Computing with Cellular Automata Beneficial?" published in 2024, identifier arXiv:2407.09501 [cite: 1, 2]. The authors conducted an explicit test of the ReCA architecture on the UCR Time Series Classification Archive, which is a standard benchmark for evaluating time-series classification tasks. The paper reports a complete failure of the cellular automaton to provide computational value. The authors explicitly state that they report a "failed attempt on the UCR Time Series Classification Archive where ReCA seems to work, but only because of the encoding scheme itself, not in any part due to the CA" [cite: 1, 2]. This is a verified, published null result demonstrating that the apparent computational success of the dynamical system was an artifact of how the data was injected into the system. The authors conclude that this highlights the absolute necessity of ablation testing, as the encoding scheme alone was responsible for the positive outcome [cite: 2, 3].

Furthermore, a second paper by the same primary authors, published in Complex Systems under identifier DOI 10.25088/ComplexSystems.32.3.309, systematically dismantles the benchmark most commonly used to justify the ReCA architecture: the 5-bit memory task [cite: 4]. The 5-bit memory benchmark has been the gold standard for proving fading memory and computational capacity in discrete reservoirs since the foundational ReCA papers in 2014 and 2015. However, Glover et al. conclusively expose severe weaknesses in this benchmark. They demonstrate that the benchmark can be solved using random vectors, meaning that the complex dynamics of the cellular automaton are entirely unnecessary for the computation [cite: 4]. They further critique the use of highly celebrated rules, such as Rule 90, showing that its success is merely an artifact of its additive properties rather than profound spatiotemporal computation [cite: 4]. The authors explicitly urge the research community to abandon the 5-bit memory benchmark as it is currently applied [cite: 4, 5].

Beyond the specific ReCA literature, the broader field of Reservoir Computing has faced identical structural critiques. The phenomenon whereby random, untrained networks appear to compute is closely related to the concept of Random Kitchen Sinks or Extreme Learning Machines. In these architectures, feedforward random static projections achieve state-of-the-art results purely through high-dimensional non-linear expansion, a mathematical concept grounded in Cover's Theorem on the separability of patterns [cite: 6, 7]. Theoretical analyses of reservoir computing have demonstrated that the random projection component of the architecture often consumes the vast majority of the algorithm's utility, and the recurrent dynamics of the reservoir add negligible value to the actual classification boundary [cite: 7, 8]. 

Finally, the unique selling point of classic reservoir computing methods, such as Echo State Networks and Liquid State Machines, quietly disappeared from the literature as the field moved on. As explicitly noted in historical reviews of the field, the original unique selling point of these dynamical systems—stable and simple training algorithms bypassing the fading gradient problem—has completely dissolved with the advent of modern deep learning, autodifferentiation, and architectures like Long Short-Term Memory networks and Transformers [cite: 9, 10].

## PART 3. THE CONFOUND THAT MANUFACTURES A FALSE POSITIVE

The most likely way this experiment returns a positive result that is not the claimed effect is through a mechanical confound known as the spatial expansion illusion, driven by the encoding scheme and the parameter budget of the linear readout. 

In ReCA and similar dynamical systems, the input must be injected into the discrete cellular automaton. Because the input is typically low-dimensional continuous or discrete data, and the cellular automaton requires a high-dimensional binary grid to operate, the input undergoes an encoding phase. This encoding phase typically involves projecting the input onto the initial conditions of the automaton cells, often utilizing multiple random mappings or random permutations to create a vast, redundant feature space [cite: 11, 12]. 

This step is the primary confound. By projecting a low-dimensional input into a massively high-dimensional space, the encoding scheme performs a non-linear kernel expansion. According to Cover's Theorem, a complex pattern classification problem cast in a high-dimensional space non-linearly is more likely to be linearly separable than in a low-dimensional space. The treatment arm of the experiment will feed this massively expanded, linearly separable state into the cellular automaton, which will iterate over time, shuffling the bits. The linear readout will then successfully decode the output. The researchers will attribute the success to the dynamical system's "edge of chaos" iterations. In reality, the computation was already completed by the random projection during the encoding step. This is precisely what arXiv:2407.09501 verified on the UCR dataset: the encoding scheme itself did all the work [cite: 1, 2].

A secondary mechanical confound is the unit of analysis and the readout's parameter budget. The proposed experiment assumes a fixed readout class and parameter budget. However, if the cellular automaton iterates over time, it generates a space-time volume of the automaton state space. If the readout concatenates multiple time steps into a single feature vector, the treatment arm is receiving more information and a vastly larger parameter budget for the readout than a control arm that only looks at a single static state [cite: 12, 13]. If the treatment arm concatenates the history of the cellular automaton, the linear readout is effectively performing a high-dimensional finite impulse response filtering operation. The computation is therefore entirely localized in the readout's weight matrix, not in the cellular automaton's local interaction rules.

A third confound applies to the specific choice of task, such as the 5-bit memory task or parity tasks. The measure of success on these tasks is a monotone function of something trivial. For example, Elementary Cellular Automaton Rule 90 is equivalent to a simple XOR between the right and left neighbor [cite: 4]. If the task requires parity or binary memory, Rule 90 simply acts as an additive delay line. The readout easily picks up this delayed parity. The researchers will claim the dynamical system is computing, but the substrate is merely acting as a trivial shift-register that perfectly aligns with the linear readout's biases [cite: 4]. 

UNCONFIRMED: If the experiment relies on a continuous streaming input without explicit memory constraints, there is a risk of selection on the outcome regarding the tuning parameters. The "edge of chaos" is notoriously difficult to pinpoint. If the researchers sweep through hundreds of elementary cellular automata rules or tuning parameters to find the one that does not immediately result in a uniform state or chaotic noise, they are implicitly training the reservoir. This violates the premise of an untrained, reusable component, shifting the baseline to be untuned while the treatment is effectively tuned through hyperparameter search [cite: 14, 15].

## PART 4. THE CONTROL THAT WOULD KILL IT

To isolate the true computational value of the cellular automaton's internal dynamics from the massive confounds of the encoding scheme and the linear readout, a highly specific suite of controls must be executed. A lesioned substrate or a matched-random substrate is insufficient because it does not properly isolate the spatial expansion confound. The following specific control arm must be run for a positive result to mean anything:

The Zero-Iteration Ablation Control: 
In this control arm, the input is passed through the exact same encoding scheme and random projection used by the treatment arm to map it into the high-dimensional binary grid. However, the cellular automaton is immediately halted at time step zero. The initial encoded state is fed directly into the identical trained linear readout. 
Cost: This control costs practically nothing relative to the main experiment; it requires zero additional compute for the substrate and only the standard cost to train the readout. 
Interpretation: If the effect is an artifact of the encoding scheme, as published in arXiv:2407.09501, the Zero-Iteration Ablation Control will achieve a performance statistically indistinguishable from the fully iterated cellular automaton [cite: 1, 2]. This proves the dynamical rules of the substrate added zero computational value.

The Pure Shift-Register Control:
If the task inherently requires memory of past inputs, the Zero-Iteration control will naturally fail, which might yield a false sense of security regarding the treatment's success. Therefore, a temporal control is required. The substrate is replaced with a simple shift-register or delay-line that possesses zero local interaction logic. The input is randomly projected into a high-dimensional vector, and at each time step, the vector is simply shifted down a buffer, dropping the oldest state. 
Cost: Computationally cheaper than the cellular automaton treatment. 
Interpretation: This control completely isolates the "fading memory" requirement from the "dynamical computation" claim. If the shift-register control matches the performance of the cellular automaton, it proves that the cellular automaton is not performing complex spatiotemporal computation. It is merely acting as a random delay matrix, and the linear readout is doing all the heavy lifting by assigning weights to different temporal delays.

If the effect were real, the cellular automaton would significantly and consistently outperform both the Zero-Iteration Control and the Pure Shift-Register Control across a wide variety of non-linear temporal tasks, proving that the specific, deterministic local interactions of the discrete grid are creating linearly separable boundaries that neither static random projection nor simple temporal delays can achieve.

## PART 5. WHY SIMILAR PROGRAMMES STOPPED

Research lines investigating fixed dynamical systems, particularly cellular automata and early recurrent neural networks, as untrained computational reservoirs have largely been abandoned or marginalized for two distinct reasons: one related to refutation of the core mechanism, and one related to the field moving on.

1. Refutation of the Core Mechanism (The Edge of Chaos Illusion):
The early promise of ReCA was heavily predicated on the idea of computation at the "edge of chaos," a concept championed in the late 1980s and 1990s suggesting that phase transitions between order and chaos inherently maximize computational capacity [cite: 15, 16]. Programmes built on this shape stopped because the hypothesis was practically refuted in hardware implementations. Tuning a discrete system perfectly to this edge is exceptionally brittle. If the internal dynamics are too stable, input perturbations decay instantly; if they are too chaotic, the memory of recent inputs is rapidly scrambled and lost [cite: 14, 15]. Studies explicitly simulating reservoir computers across parameter ranges found that the edge of chaos is not necessarily the optimal regime for computation, rendering the theoretical foundation ambiguous and virtually impossible to scale without extreme, task-specific manual tuning [cite: 15]. Furthermore, as demonstrated by Glover et al., the apparent success of these systems on benchmarks like the 5-bit memory task was an artifact, leading to the abandonment of the substrate by researchers seeking genuine computational power [cite: 4, 5].

2. Absorbed and Obsoleted by Deep Learning:
The broader paradigm of classical Reservoir Computing (Echo State Networks, Liquid State Machines) was abandoned because the field moved on. The original motivation for reservoir computing was strictly functional: training recurrent neural networks was mathematically unstable due to vanishing and exploding gradients [cite: 7, 9]. Reservoir computing circumvented this by keeping the recurrent weights fixed and only training the linear readout. Today, with the advent of autodifferentiation, robust optimizers, and architectural breakthroughs like Long Short-Term Memory and Gated Recurrent Units, the problem of training recurrent networks end-to-end is entirely solved [cite: 9, 10]. Consequently, the unique selling point of reservoir computing dissolved [cite: 9]. The field moved toward end-to-end trained models because they are empirically, vastly superior. 

Additionally, the hardware argument for discrete cellular automata collapsed. While a binary cellular automaton is incredibly cheap to run on an FPGA or ASIC, the required linear readout is not. The readout necessitates high-precision, floating-point matrix multiplications to decode the massive feature space generated by the reservoir [cite: 4, 17]. This creates a severe bottleneck, entirely negating the energy efficiency and hardware benefits of the discrete substrate.

## PART 6. WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE

For the claim to hold—that a fixed dynamical system can serve as a reusable computational component where the computation is genuinely attributable to its own dynamics—a very specific and narrow set of theoretical conditions must be met. 

First, the Target Task Must Defy Static Kernel Expansion.
The computational problem cannot merely be a linearly inseparable problem that becomes separable through high-dimensional random projection. It must possess deep, non-linear spatiotemporal dependencies that require conditional routing of information over time. The task must be designed such that a static random projection (Zero-Iteration Control) and a trivial memory buffer (Shift-Register Control) fundamentally cannot extract the required features, regardless of the parameter budget of the linear readout. 

Second, the Encoding Must Be Dimensionally Restrictive.
To attribute computation to the dynamics rather than the encoding, the input injection must not artificially inflate the dimensionality of the data. The input must be injected as a minimal perturbation, ideally a 1-to-1 dimensional mapping, forcing the cellular automaton to be solely responsible for expanding the feature space through its temporal evolution over the grid.

Third, the Substrate Must Reside Robustly in Wolfram's Class IV.
The cellular automaton must be governed by a transition table that guarantees Class IV behavior (complex, localized, propagating structures) irrespective of the specific input data [cite: 16, 18]. It cannot devolve into Class I or II (homogeneous or periodic attractors) which act as a fading memory bottleneck, nor can it devolve into Class III (chaotic noise) which destroys input separability. The substrate must possess a mathematically proven mechanism for maintaining this phase transition robustly across the entirety of the streaming execution, without the need for manual hyperparameter resets or continuous external tuning [cite: 15, 16].

Fourth, the Information Light-Cone Must Match the Task Horizon.
In a discrete cellular automaton, information propagates locally step-by-step. The maximum speed of information transfer (the light-cone) is strictly bound by the neighborhood size. For the computation to be attributable to the dynamics, the temporal distance between correlated events in the streaming input must perfectly align with the spatial distance information can travel across the discrete grid during that same time window. If the task requires correlating inputs that occur further apart in time than the cellular automaton can physically propagate across its grid before being overwritten, the dynamics will structurally fail.

## PART 7. HOW A NULL WOULD BE RECOGNISED

If the claimed effect is entirely artefactual, the experiment will not produce a clean, zero-accuracy null. Instead, it will produce a highly deceptive "small positive that survives to publication." 

Because high-dimensional dynamical systems inherently perform non-linear mixing, they act as a form of high-dimensional reservoir. Even if the specific dynamical rules of the cellular automaton are computationally useless for the specific task, the mere act of shuffling bits and expanding the state space will provide a mathematical boost to the linear readout via Cover's Theorem. The chaotic mixing acts as a regularization mechanism, preventing the linear readout from overfitting to raw input noise, and slightly boosting accuracy above a completely frozen, low-dimensional baseline.

To recognize this noisy, artifactual positive as a true null, the research programme must pre-commit to strict relative baselines. A null is recognized if the fully executing dynamical system fails to out-perform the Zero-Iteration Ablation Control or the Pure Shift-Register Control by a statistically significant margin (e.g., pre-committing to a required margin of >10% relative accuracy improvement). 

If the treatment arm achieves 85% accuracy, the frozen substrate achieves 60% accuracy, but the Zero-Iteration Control also achieves 84% accuracy, the result is a clean null regarding the core hypothesis. The dynamical system did nothing; the encoding and readout manufactured the result. The programme must pre-commit to publishing the control arm results alongside the treatment results, explicitly acknowledging that any performance delta between the frozen substrate and the treatment is fully explained by the spatial expansion of the encoding scheme, rather than the emergent computation of the dynamical component.
