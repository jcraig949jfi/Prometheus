## 1. Introduction and Epistemological Context

The Yang-Mills existence and mass gap problem is widely considered one of the most formidable challenges in modern mathematical physics. Officially formulated by Arthur Jaffe and Edward Witten for the Clay Mathematics Institute (CMI) in 2000, the problem requires a rigorous mathematical proof that for any compact simple gauge group \( G \), a non-trivial quantum Yang-Mills theory exists on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) and exhibits a strictly positive mass gap \(\Delta > 0\) [cite: 1, 2]. 

In physics, the "mass gap" refers to the observation that while classical Yang-Mills gauge fields (such as gluons in quantum chromodynamics, QCD) are massless, the corresponding quantum theory exclusively produces massive particle excitations (glueballs or hadrons) [cite: 6]. The lowest energy state above the vacuum must have a finite, non-zero mass. While extensive empirical evidence from particle accelerators and sophisticated lattice QCD computer simulations robustly confirm this phenomenon, a formal analytical proof utilizing the Wightman axioms or Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction theorem remains elusive [cite: 6].

## 6. Theoretical Analysis: Why Do These Claims Fail?

To fully substantiate the Lethe agent's anti-anchor mapping, it is academically beneficial to understand *why* the modal-LLM space becomes polluted with these specific types of claims and what fundamental mathematical errors typify them.

### 6.1 The Epistemological Vulnerability of the Mass Gap
The Yang-Mills mass gap is particularly vulnerable to false proofs because it straddles the boundary between rigorous functional analysis and heuristic theoretical physics [cite: 2]. The classical Yang-Mills action is scale-invariant. The action for a gauge group \(G\) with field strength \(F_{\mu\nu}\) is given by:
\[ S_{YM} = - \frac{1}{4} \int d^4x \text{Tr}(F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu}) \]
At the classical level, there is no parameter with the dimension of mass. Through quantization and renormalization, a phenomenon known as *dimensional transmutation* occurs, wherein the scale anomaly breaks the scale invariance, generating a fundamental mass scale (\(\Lambda_{QCD}\) in the case of strong interactions) [cite: 6]. 

False proofs universally fail to rigorously construct the measure of the path integral in four dimensions. 

### 6.2 Flaws in Candidate 1: Jacobsen's Orbifold Regulator
Jacobsen's claim utilized a 5D orbifold regulator, attempting to leverage dimensional reduction to control ultraviolet divergences [cite: 7, 8]. The fatal flaw in such AI-generated or heuristic approaches is that while a 5D geometry can regularize certain field behaviors, proving the existence of the Wightman axioms in the \(\mathbb{R}^4\) limit requires bounding the infinite-volume limit of the joint polymer expansion [cite: 8]. Theoretical physics literature has long established that cluster expansions, while effective in highly massive theories or lower dimensions (like the work of Glimm and Jaffe in 2D and 3D) [cite: 2], face insurmountable Gribov ambiguities and non-perturbative infrared divergences when applied naively to pure SU(3) in 4D [cite: 3]. The arXiv administration's swift withdrawal of this paper underscores the superficiality of its mathematical rigor [cite: 7].

### 6.3 Flaws in Candidate 2: Srichan's Tensor-Scalar Equivalence
The Srichan paper represents a profound breakdown of basic differential geometry, which slipped through peer review. The authors attempted to equate the Einstein Field Equations with quantum phase (Berry curvature) to generate mass. Specifically, they proposed a modified Yang-Mills field strength:
\[ \tilde{F}_{\mu\nu} = F_{\mu\nu} + \lambda \Omega_{\mu\nu} \]
where \(\Omega_{\mu\nu}\) is the Berry curvature [cite: 9, 11]. 
However, post-publication independent reviews—including a formal Expression of Concern—highlighted that the paper's central equations frequently equated scalar quantities (single numbers) directly with rank-2 tensors (4x4 matrices) [cite: 14]. For instance, adding an invariant scalar mass to a tensor without proper index contraction violates Lorentz covariance and basic linear algebra [cite: 14]. As physicist Sabine Hossenfelder and others pointed out, equating a 4x4 matrix of numbers to a single number renders the mathematical framework nonsensical [cite: 14, 15].

### 6.4 Flaws in Candidate 3: Cody's Classical Misdirection
Cody's "Latnex" model is an example of attempting to solve a quantum mechanical problem using purely classical or fluid-dynamic analogies. The claim asserts that mass is formed when a "compression threshold" \(C_t\) is exceeded by second-order motion \(\Delta\Delta m\) [cite: 18, 19]. The Yang-Mills mass gap, however, is exclusively a property of the *quantum* spectrum of the Hamiltonian [cite: 2, 6]. Classical fields, no matter how their "directional motion" or "inertia" is phenomenologically modeled, do not possess a discrete spectrum of energy eigenvalues bounded strictly above zero. By ignoring the path integral, the functional measure, and the Hilbert space of the quantum theory entirely, Cody's preprint solves a different, fundamentally non-quantum problem, prompting his own voluntary retraction due to "serious scientific errors" [cite: 17].
