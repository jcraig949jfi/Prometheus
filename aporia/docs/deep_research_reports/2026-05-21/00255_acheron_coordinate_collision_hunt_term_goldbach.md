## 2. Case Study I: Helical vs. Linear Arithmetic Coordinate Spaces 

### 2.1 Literature Context and Citation Identification
**Source:** B. Bahbouhi, "Goldbach’s Conjecture as a Direct Deduction of Dynamic Symmetry and Invariant Prime Windows," / "A Near-Proof of Goldbach’s Conjecture via Symmetric Prime Structures." Preprints.org, January 2026. 
**arXiv ID / DOI:** Preprint DOI: `10.20944/preprints202601.1811.v1` (as indexed in January 2026 systems) [cite: 6].

### 2.2 The Substrate Type A Collision
The author attempts to bypass the apparent randomness of prime distributions by structuring prime deviations around an even integer $E$ into a "helical coordinate system." The goal is to prove that there must always exist a deviation $d$ such that $(E/2 - d)$ and $(E/2 + d)$ are both prime.

**The Exact Quote containing the Collision:**
> "For a shifted even integer, all admissible deviations are embedded in the same helical coordinate system. Deviations are then ranked according to a joint distance combining angular deviation from the predicted lane and vertical deviation from the predicted depth." [cite: 6]

### 2.3 Deconstruction of the Conflated Coordinate Systems
*   **Coordinate System $\alpha$ (Linear Arithmetic Space $\mathbb{Z}$):** The foundational space of the Goldbach problem. Here, distance is measured in standard integer steps, and the location of a prime is dictated by discrete, non-periodic sieving (the removal of multiples of smaller primes). In this space, the "deviation" $d$ is a simple scalar integer. The invariant is the binary primality of $E/2 \pm d$.
*   **Coordinate System $\beta$ (3D Helical Coordinate System):** The author maps the integers onto a three-dimensional helix (cylinder $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{R}$), where the $z$-axis represents magnitude, and the angular coordinate $\theta$ represents residue classes modulo a set of small primes. Distance in this system is computed as a "joint distance combining angular deviation... and vertical deviation."

### 2.4 The Mechanics of Conflation
The collision occurs when the author utilizes the continuous Euclidean metric of the 3D helical space to "rank" and establish the existence of "true Goldbach-successful deviations" [cite: 6]. The author states that "Deviations belonging to these residue classes can be eliminated without performing any primality test," yielding a set of "admissible deviations." 
However, the distance metric in the helical coordinate system does not isomorphically map to the distribution of primes. By combining "angular deviation" and "vertical deviation," the author treats modulo arithmetic (angular) and integer magnitude (vertical) as orthogonal vectors in a metric space. In reality, the prime sieve is a coupled dynamical system where the magnitude inextricably determines the required modulo bounds. 

### 2.5 The Falsification Signal (The Invariant)
**The Invariant:** The cardinality and primality density of the set of *Admissible Deviations*.
**The Change:** In Coordinate System $\alpha$, the density of primes in the set of admissible deviations decays logarithmically according to the Prime Number Theorem. In Coordinate System $\beta$, because the "joint distance" ranks deviations using a decoupled geometric metric, the space artificially projects a non-zero, periodic density of primes at specific "predicted lanes" and "depths." The invariant density of true prime pairs fundamentally differs between the strict arithmetic evaluation and the helical ranking evaluation, generating a false positive for the absolute existence of a Goldbach pair.

### 2.6 Formal Status

## 3. Case Study II: Spectral-Operator Continuous Space vs. Discrete Additive Space

### 3.1 Literature Context and Citation Identification
**Source:** "The Goldbach Conjecture--A Deterministic Spectral-Operator Proof Beyond Sieve Theory Where (i) Herglotz Positivity, (ii) Carleman Uniqueness, and (iii) Paley-Wiener Confinement Demonstrate That Randomness Is Not Intrinsic." ResearchGate Preprint, November 2025.
**arXiv ID / DOI:** Extracted via DOI proxy mapping `10.13140/RG.2.2.XXX...` (indexed as November 28, 2025, manuscript) [cite: 7].

### 3.2 The Substrate Type A Collision
The author translates the additive problem of Goldbach into a quantum mechanical / spectral framework. By treating the distribution of primes as a "signal" generated by a self-adjoint operator, the author attempts to prove that a failure of the Goldbach conjecture would violate foundational principles of operator theory (Herglotz positivity and finite-type Paley-Wiener confinement).

**The Exact Quote containing the Collision:**
> "Weyl separation give a stable coordinate system for the Goldbach signal, Weyl subtraction makes the arithmetic visible in the trace, and Paley–Wiener localization provides the knob we turn to test or forbid mass at a target even integer... A Goldbach counterexample requires the $\Lambda–\Lambda$ signal to behave as if a two-prime representation exists at $2N$ when none does... curvature leakage into geometrically impossible states." [cite: 7]

### 3.3 Deconstruction of the Conflated Coordinate Systems
*   **Coordinate System $\alpha$ (Discrete Arithmetic State Space):** The discrete lattice of even integers $\mathbb{Z}_{\text{even}}$ where the indicator function for a Goldbach sum is either $0$ (counterexample) or $\ge 1$ (conjecture holds). The "mass" at any point $2N$ is an exact, discrete integer count of prime pairs.
*   **Coordinate System $\beta$ (Weyl Operator Coordinate System):** A continuous functional space over $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, where the primes are treated as the spectrum (eigenvalues) of a hypothetical self-adjoint operator. In this system, "mass" is a continuous density measure (a spectral trace), and "curvature" represents the deviation from the expected density.

### 3.4 The Mechanics of Conflation
The author states that "Weyl separation give a stable coordinate system for the Goldbach signal" [cite: 7]. The conflation happens when the author maps a hypothetical Goldbach counterexample (a $0$ in System $\alpha$) into the continuous Weyl space (System $\beta$). The author argues that a missing prime pair at $2N$ would cause "curvature leakage" and "off-shell mass" that violates *Herglotz positivity* (the rule that the imaginary part of a certain complex function must remain positive). 
However, Herglotz positivity and Carleman uniqueness apply to continuous spectra and entire functions on the complex plane. The mapping of the discrete von Mangoldt function $\Lambda(n)$ onto a continuous Weyl manifold is *not* unitary. The author forces an isomorphism by claiming that "any attempt to assign mass where no two-prime representation exists produces an analytically detectable contradiction." But in the discrete space, no mass is "assigned"—it simply evaluates to zero. The continuous coordinate system artificially introduces a requirement for smoothness and bounded curvature that the integers do not inherently possess.

### 3.5 The Falsification Signal (The Invariant)
**The Invariant:** *Herglotz Positivity / Trace Mass Distribution.*
**The Change:** In the discrete Coordinate System $\alpha$, an integer with zero Goldbach partitions simply creates a gap in the sequence; it does not violate any physical or arithmetic "positivity" constraint. In the Weyl Coordinate System $\beta$, the topological requirement for smooth mass distribution implies that a gap must "leak" mass into adjacent coordinates, generating a contradiction. The invariant (the localized existence of the gap) changes from arithmetically permissible in $\alpha$ to topologically impossible in $\beta$.

### 3.6 Formal Status
**Unflagged.** The paper utilizes highly advanced functional analysis terminology, which often deters pure number theorists from executing a rigorous teardown. No formal comment paper has yet debunked the operator-geometry coordinate conflation.

## 4. Case Study III: 2D Nested Area vs. 1D Prime Gap Metric

### 4.1 Literature Context and Citation Identification
**Source:** "We prove that every even integer $2N \ge 8$ is the sum of two distinct primes." Preprints.org, February 10, 2026.
**arXiv ID / DOI:** `10.20944/preprints202511.0701.v4` [cite: 5, 12].

### 4.2 The Substrate Type A Collision
The authors attempt a "novel geometric equivalence" by mapping the arithmetic problem of finding two primes $P$ and $Q$ such that $P + Q = 2N$ into a problem of geometry involving nested squares. They define $M = (Q - P)/2$. The sum and difference become $P+Q = 2N$ and $Q-P = 2M$. They then map this to an area: $N^2 - M^2 = P \cdot Q$.

**The Exact Quote containing the Collision:**
> "Each such pair $(P, Q)$ contributes $M = (Q - P)/2$ to $D_N$. Different pairs can yield the same M (collision), but the key point is that as N grows, new primes Q continually enter the interval $(N,2N)$, each bringing opportunities to populate $D_N$ with new M-values... The area $N^2 - M^2 = P \cdot Q$ is a semiprime (product of exactly two primes) if and only if both P and Q are prime." [cite: 5, 12]

### 4.3 Deconstruction of the Conflated Coordinate Systems
*   **Coordinate System $\alpha$ (1D Prime Gap Coordinate $M$):** A one-dimensional scalar coordinate system representing half the distance between two primes. $M \in \mathbb{Z}^+$. The set $D_N$ is populated by integers.
*   **Coordinate System $\beta$ (2D Area Coordinate Space):** A geometric coordinate space where the entity $N^2 - M^2$ represents the two-dimensional area of a nested square, and the invariant property of the area is its status as a "semiprime" (having exactly two prime factors).

### 4.4 The Mechanics of Conflation
The text explicitly acknowledges a "collision" in the 1D space: "Different pairs can yield the same M (collision)" [cite: 12]. For example, a gap of $M=3$ (distance 6) can be yielded by the prime pairs (5, 11) and (7, 13). 
The conflation occurs when the authors transition from the 1D gap distribution (driven by Dusart’s theorem on prime density) to the 2D area coordinate $N^2 - M^2$. They rely on the assumption that as $N$ grows, the density of available $M$ values naturally forces the *area* $N^2 - M^2$ to land on a semiprime configuration. However, the probability of a number being semiprime in the area coordinate system $\beta$ is multiplicatively dependent, whereas the generation of $M$ in coordinate $\alpha$ is additively dependent. The authors use a geometric area space to claim that the interval density guarantees a target area, bypassing the unique prime factorization constraints of the area itself. 

### 4.5 The Falsification Signal (The Invariant)
**The Invariant:** *Semiprime Density / Unique Factorization Cardinality.*
**The Change:** Under Coordinate System $\alpha$, the occurrence of identical $M$ values (the text's literal use of the word "collision") is a purely additive phenomenon that does not restrict factorization. When mapped to Coordinate System $\beta$ (the area $N^2 - M^2$), the specific requirement that the result is semiprime is governed by the Landau-Ramanujan constants and multiplicative density. The reported value of successful semiprime hits is artificially inflated in the conflated space because it imports additive gap densities (Dusart’s bounds) directly into a multiplicative semiprime metric without the required isomorphic transformation.

### 4.6 Formal Status
**Unflagged.** The paper relies on a combination of computational verification (up to $N=3274$) and Dusart's proven density bounds [cite: 12], which lends it an air of rigorous analytic number theory. The coordinate conflation between additive interval bounds and multiplicative area status has not yet resulted in a formal journal rejection or erratum in the public domain.

## 5. Case Study IV: PCGC-Goldbach Modular vs. Euclidean n-Axis

**arXiv ID / DOI:** Qeios ID: FMA6AW. DOI proxy: `10.32388/FMA6AW` [cite: 2].

### 5.2 The Substrate Type A Collision

To do this, the author maps the sequence of even numbers to a local residue structure defined by $Q_p(n)$.

**The Exact Quote containing the Collision:**
> "If the small–prime contribution were treated as a single factor, these roles would be conflated, leading to incorrect scaling and spurious overestimates of the remainder... The squaring reflects the Goldbach geometry $(n-m)(n+m)=n^2-m^2$, so that changes in the effective modulus $Q_p(n)$ induce corresponding blocks along the n-axis." [cite: 2]

### 5.3 Deconstruction of the Conflated Coordinate Systems
*   **Coordinate System $\alpha$ (Modular Block Coordinate $Q_p(n)$):** A discrete sequence space partitioned by the primorial product of small primes. Distance in this space is measured in discrete "blocks" where local residue structures remain invariant.
*   **Coordinate System $\beta$ (The Geometric n-Axis):** The continuous real number line (or discrete integer line embedded in a continuous geometric envelope), where scaling behavior and "deviation behavior across finite analytic windows and cutoff scales" can be measured using standard calculus. 

### 5.4 The Mechanics of Conflation
The author explicitly attempts to *avoid* conflation ("If the small-prime contribution were treated as a single factor, these roles would be conflated..." [cite: 2]), yet commits a higher-order Substrate Type A error immediately after. The text claims that changes in the effective modulus $Q_p(n)$ "induce corresponding blocks along the n-axis." 
The error lies in mapping the $p$-adic/modular topology of $Q_p(n)$ directly onto the Euclidean geometry of the "n-axis" through a bounding envelope. The author uses this bounding envelope to control the exact remainder uniformly. However, the modular space of small primes wraps periodically, whereas the n-axis scales linearly. An interval in the modular coordinate system does not possess a constant geometric weight on the n-axis, due to the interference patterns of larger primes not included in the "effective modulus." The "curvature constant $\Omega_{\text{prime}}$" is calculated as if the n-axis geometry can perfectly absorb modular periodicities without fractal distortion.

### 5.5 The Falsification Signal (The Invariant)
**The Invariant:** *The Scaling Exponent of the Goldbach Remainder Term.*
**The Change:** In the true arithmetic modular Coordinate System $\alpha$, the remainder term fluctuates wildly, driven by the Moebius inversion and the non-trivial zeroes of the associated L-functions. In the geometric n-axis Coordinate System $\beta$, the conflation forces the remainder into an "explicit bounding envelope... shown to control the exact remainder uniformly" [cite: 2]. The invariant of the remainder's fluctuation amplitude is mathematically squashed; the reported bounding value changes from wildly oscillating (in reality) to uniformly bounded (under the alternative coordinate).

## 6. Case Study V: Cartesian 2D Independence vs. Coupled Divisibility 

### 6.1 Literature Context and Citation Identification
**Source:** "Number of pairs divisible by 3... Methodology for Proving Goldbach's Conjecture." arXiv preprint.
**arXiv ID / DOI:** `arXiv:1811.02415v7` (Updated/Reviewed August 11, 2025) [cite: 4].

### 6.2 The Substrate Type A Collision
The author sets up a proof by defining prime pairs as coordinates $(x,y)$ such that $x+y = n$, and evaluates their divisibility by small primes (like 3 and 5) to sieve out composites and count the remaining prime pairs. 

**The Exact Quote containing the Collision:**
> "There are exactly $\lfloor (|P(n)|-1)/3 \rfloor$ pairs where both the x and y coordinates are evenly divisible by 3... For example, for $n = 994$, of the 196 pairs are divisible by 5, there are 132 pairs (about two-thirds) that are divisible by 3. This is because for every x coordinate divisible by 5 starting with (15,979), every third pair is also divisible by 3... and starting with (25,969), every third pair the y coordinate is divisible by 3... There are no cases where both x and y are divisible by 3." [cite: 4]

### 6.3 Deconstruction of the Conflated Coordinate Systems
*   **Coordinate System $\alpha$ (Coupled Diophantine Line):** The 1D line $y = n - x$ embedded in $\mathbb{Z}^2$. Because $x$ and $y$ sum to a constant $n$, their divisibility by any prime $p$ is strictly coupled. If $n \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, then $x \equiv 0 \pmod 3 \implies y \equiv 1 \pmod 3$. 
*   **Coordinate System $\beta$ (Independent 2D Cartesian Coordinates):** The standard $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ plane, where the divisibility probability of an $x$ coordinate is statistically and algebraically independent of the $y$ coordinate.

### 6.4 The Mechanics of Conflation
The author explicitly oscillates between treating $x$ and $y$ as coupled components of $n$, and treating them as independent coordinates on a plane. In Case 1, the author writes: "There are exactly $\lfloor (|P(n)|-1)/3 \rfloor$ pairs where *both the x and y coordinates* are evenly divisible by 3" (which requires $n \equiv 0 \pmod 3$) [cite: 4]. But immediately following this, the author treats the sequential progression of coordinates as independent streams, stating that "for every x coordinate divisible by 5... every third pair is also divisible by 3... and every third pair the y coordinate is divisible by 3" [cite: 4].
The author calculates the aggregate number of surviving pairs by subtracting densities as if the sieving on the $x$-axis and the sieving on the $y$-axis operate on the set of pairs with the statistical independence found in orthogonal 2D Cartesian space. The linear Diophantine constraint $x+y=n$ destroys orthogonal independence, meaning the sieving fractions (e.g., subtracting $1/3$ for $x$ and $1/3$ for $y$) cannot be combined as they are in the paper.

### 6.5 The Falsification Signal (The Invariant)
**The Invariant:** *The Sieve Survival Cardinality $\Pi_3^*(n)$.*
**The Change:** In the coupled Coordinate System $\alpha$, subtracting pairs divisible by 3 and 5 requires exact inclusion-exclusion logic tailored to the residue of $n \pmod{15}$. In the independent Cartesian Coordinate System $\beta$, the author uses generic fractional subtractions ("about two-thirds"). The invariant quantity of surviving prime pairs diverges massively between the correct Diophantine coupling and the falsely orthogonal Cartesian coordinates.

### 6.6 Formal Status

## 7. Extended Analysis: The Metamathematics of Coordinate Conflation

The recurrent nature of the Substrate Type A collision in the 2024–2026 Goldbach literature points to a systemic psychological and methodological attractor. Why do mathematicians repeatedly force additive number theory into continuous or pseudo-geometric coordinate systems?

### 7.1 The Allure of Topography over Topology
The Goldbach Conjecture is exceptionally difficult because it asks a purely additive question ($x+y = 2n$) about purely multiplicative entities (primes). Standard number theory relies heavily on 1D analytic techniques [cite: 1]. However, algorithms like the Wolfram Cellular Automaton for the Goldbach Conjecture literally visualize primes as "particles" that "collide at points that represent the sum of the prime numbers associated with the particles" [cite: 13]. This spatialization [cite: 14, 15] trains modern researchers to think of the conjecture in terms of physical distance, geometry, and spatial dimensions [cite: 16].

When researchers attempt to formalize this intuition, they build coordinate systems: helices, nested squares, and spectral operator fields. But $\mathbb{Z}$, the set of integers, lacks the topological completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$. Continuous coordinate systems possess intermediate values, smooth limits, and isotropic distance metrics. The discrete primes do not. When an author constructs a "distance metric" in a novel coordinate system to rank prime pairs (as seen in Bahbouhi's helical model [cite: 6]), they unknowingly inject the axioms of continuous geometry into discrete arithmetic. 

### 7.2 The Falsification Crisis in Proof Verification
As Lakatos notes via heuristic falsification, "a formal theory may be said to be refuted if one of its theorems is negated by the corresponding theorem of the informal theory" [cite: 17]. In the context of the Charon swarm, Acheron operates as an automated Lakatosian falsifier. 
In these five cases, the "informal theory" is the author's heuristic geometric coordinate system, while the "formal theory" is the strict Diophantine reality of the integers. Because the authors trust their geometric coordinates, they bypass Popperian falsifiability [cite: 18]. As noted by the literature, "finding an odd number not representable as a sum of fewer than five odd primes... confirms the Goldbach conjecture... We are surprised then to find the latter hypothesis confirmed by one of its negative instances" [cite: 19]. When coordinate systems conflate, the logic of confirmation breaks down. A contradiction in the geometry is assumed to be a proof of the arithmetic, rather than a falsification of the chosen coordinate mapping [cite: 20].
