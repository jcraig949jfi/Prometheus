However, the discovery of Sedna and subsequent extreme Trans-Neptunian Objects (eTNOs)—bodies with semi-major axes $a > 250$ AU and perihelia detached from the gravitational influence of Neptune—revealed a profound dynamical enigma [cite: 1, 2]. The probability of such alignment occurring by pure chance is exceedingly low, leading to the formulation of open problem `ASTRO-0001`. The most prominent astrophysical solution proposed is the "Planet Nine" hypothesis, which posits the existence of a super-Earth or sub-Neptune mass planet residing at hundreds of astronomical units from the Sun [cite: 2, 6]. To investigate this problem, modern astrophysics relies on highly diverse analytical methodologies.

## 2. Lens 1: `STANCE_DYNAMICAL_SYSTEMS@v1`

### 2.1 Attempt 1: Mean-Motion Resonances and Secular Dynamics (Batygin & Morbidelli, 2017)

**Context and Theoretical Framework:**

Distant encounters with Neptune, combined with the secular forcing of a highly eccentric Planet Nine, would induce a chaotic diffusion of the semi-major axes of the eTNOs, eventually leading to their ejection from the Solar System [cite: 4, 5]. To resolve this, they utilized a compact-form approach to perturbation theory to demonstrate that the long-term survival of the clustered population is enabled not by secular interactions alone, but by a complex web of Mean-Motion Resonances (MMRs) driven by Planet Nine [cite: 5, 7]. 

#### (a) The Measurement Projected

The specific measurement entails evaluating the critical resonant angle $\phi$, which librates rather than circulates, shielding the minor bodies from close encounters with both Neptune and Planet Nine [cite: 4, 9]. Furthermore, the lens projects that the secular dynamics *embedded within* these MMRs regulates the apsidal confinement (the clustering of the orbits) and the perihelion detachment (raising the perihelion distance $q$ beyond 30 AU) [cite: 5]. Numerical simulations within this lens also project the generation of highly inclined and even retrograde Centaurs through the Kozai-Lidov mechanism operating inside these resonances [cite: 2, 5].

#### (b) The Verdict Reached
The verdict reached by Batygin and Morbidelli (2017) is that the existence of an undiscovered, massive planet is not merely a viable fit for the data, but an elegant and necessary dynamical architect for the outer Solar System. The study concludes that the observed clustering of distant orbits in physical space, the detachment of their perihelia from Neptune, and the excitation of long-period objects to extreme inclinations are all seamlessly explained by the web of MMRs generated by Planet Nine [cite: 2, 5]. The chaotic diffusion of semi-major axes is halted by resonance capture, meaning that Planet Nine actively maintains the architectural integrity of the distant Kuiper Belt [cite: 4, 7].
