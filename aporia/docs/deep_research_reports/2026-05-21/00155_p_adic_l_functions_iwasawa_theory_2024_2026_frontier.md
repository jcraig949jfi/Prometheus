**Overview of the Field**
Iwasawa theory is a profoundly rich area of algebraic number theory that investigates how arithmetic objects evolve across infinite towers of field extensions [cite: 1, 2]. Traditionally focused on the $p$-primary parts of arithmetic structures—such as ideal class groups and Selmer groups—over extensions generated by $p$-power roots of unity, the subject establishes a powerful bridge between abstract algebra and $p$-adic analysis [cite: 1]. At its core lies the formulation of $p$-adic L-functions, which are $p$-adic analogues of complex L-functions that interpolate critical L-values [cite: 3, 4]. The overarching philosophy, crystallized in the Iwasawa Main Conjecture, posits that the algebraic invariants governing the growth of these modules (such as Selmer groups) are intimately dictated by the analytic properties (the zeros and poles) of the corresponding $p$-adic L-functions [cite: 3, 5]. Between 2024 and 2026, the frontier of this domain has rapidly expanded from classical $\mathrm{GL}_1$ and $\mathrm{GL}_2$ settings to higher-rank automorphic forms, supersingular reduction, and non-equal characteristic extensions [cite: 1, 6].

## 1. Foundations and the Evolution of Iwasawa Theory

### 1.1 Classical Roots: From Class Groups to the Main Conjecture
In 1959, Kenkichi Iwasawa revolutionized algebraic number theory by introducing a novel method for studying the class numbers of specific number fields [cite: 7]. His foundational insight was that the $p$-primary part of the ideal class group in an infinite Galois extension of number fields—specifically, a tower with a pro-$p$ Galois group isomorphic to the additive group of $p$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_p$—could be systematically analyzed as a module over the Iwasawa algebra [cite: 7, 8]. 

Let $F_\infty / F$ be a cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension, and let $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(F_\infty / F) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$. The Iwasawa algebra is formally defined as the completed group ring $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]] \cong \mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$, where $T = \gamma - 1$ for a topological generator $\gamma \in \Gamma$ [cite: 8]. If $X_\infty$ denotes the inverse limit of the $p$-primary parts of the ideal class groups along the tower, Iwasawa demonstrated that $X_\infty$ is a compact, finitely-generated, torsion $\Lambda$-module [cite: 7]. The structure theorem for such modules allows one to associate specific invariants that control the growth of the class group at each layer $F_n$:
*   **$\mu$-invariant:** Measures the exponential growth of the $p$-part [cite: 7, 8].
*   **$\lambda$-invariant:** Measures the linear growth rate [cite: 7, 8].
*   **$\nu$-invariant:** A constant shift parameter [cite: 8].

Iwasawa famously conjectured that for the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension of any number field, the $\mu$-invariant vanishes ($\mu_{F_\infty} = 0$) [cite: 7]. For abelian extensions over $\mathbb{Q}$, this was proven by Ferrero and Washington [cite: 7]. 

### 1.2 The Two Faces of $p$-adic L-functions
The beauty of Iwasawa theory lies in the dichotomy and subsequent unification of algebraic and analytic methods. A $p$-adic L-function generally takes one of two forms:
1.  **Analytic $p$-adic L-functions:** First constructed by Tomio Kubota and Heinrich-Wolfgang Leopoldt, these functions arise via the $p$-adic interpolation of special values of classical complex L-functions [cite: 3, 4]. Utilizing Kummer's congruences for Bernoulli numbers, Kubota and Leopoldt constructed the $p$-adic Riemann zeta function $\zeta_p(s)$, whose values at negative odd integers mirror those of the classical Riemann zeta function up to an explicit Euler factor correction [cite: 3, 9]. 
2.  **Arithmetic (Algebraic) $p$-adic L-functions:** Kenkichi Iwasawa independently discovered that $p$-adic L-functions can be formulated from the arithmetic of cyclotomic fields—specifically, through Galois modules over towers of fields [cite: 3]. The characteristic ideal of the dual of the Selmer group (viewed as a $\Lambda$-module) acts as a generating function, fundamentally encoding the arithmetic data of the module [cite: 8, 10]. 

### 1.3 The Iwasawa Main Conjecture
The Iwasawa Main Conjecture acts as the ultimate bridge between these two perspectives. Formulated initially for cyclotomic fields, it proposes that the analytically defined Kubota-Leopoldt $p$-adic L-function generates the same characteristic ideal in the Iwasawa algebra as the algebraically defined arithmetic $p$-adic L-function derived from ideal class groups [cite: 3, 5]. The conjecture predicts that the zeros of the $p$-adic L-function precisely dictate the growth of the $p$-primary class groups [cite: 8].

This monumental conjecture was proven for the rational numbers by Barry Mazur and Andrew Wiles in 1984, representing a watershed moment in arithmetic geometry [cite: 3, 5]. Since then, the guiding philosophy—that algebraic objects (Selmer groups) are intimately related to analytic objects ($p$-adic L-functions)—has been systematically extended to elliptic curves, modular forms, and broader motives [cite: 2, 7]. 

### 1.4 Generalization to Elliptic Curves and Modular Forms
In the modern era, Iwasawa theory frequently replaces the study of ideal class groups with that of Selmer groups attached to elliptic curves or Galois representations of modular forms [cite: 2, 7]. Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at $p$. The Selmer group $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F_\infty)$ is analyzed over the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension, and its Pontryagin dual $X(E/F_\infty) := \mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/F_\infty), \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)$ is shown to be a finitely generated $\Lambda$-module [cite: 7]. 

Pioneering work by Kato, as well as Skinner and Urban, successfully expanded the Main Conjecture to these settings, demonstrating that appropriate Selmer groups yield $p$-adic distributions that interpolate the L-values of ordinary cusp forms [cite: 11]. Recent findings further refine this by investigating the Kurihara numbers, providing semi-local descriptions of mod $p$ Selmer groups, and yielding deep structural refinements of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture [cite: 12]. Under specific hypotheses, it has been shown that if the Iwasawa Main Conjecture holds, the collection of Kurihara numbers does not vanish identically, which inherently controls the structure of the Selmer groups and restricts the Tate-Shafarevich group [cite: 12].

## 2. The Euler System Frontier

Euler systems are arguably the most powerful theoretical tools available for bounding Selmer groups and proving instances of the Iwasawa Main Conjecture and the Bloch-Kato conjecture. An Euler system is a highly structured, norm-compatible collection of cohomology classes defined over a tower of field extensions [cite: 13, 14]. Due to their rigidity, discovering and constructing new Euler systems is notoriously difficult; historically, they have only existed for a handful of representations (e.g., cyclotomic units for $\mathbb{Q}$, elliptic units for imaginary quadratic fields, and Heegner points) [cite: 13, 15]. The 2024-2026 frontier is heavily defined by breakthroughs in constructing higher-rank Euler systems.

### 2.1 Classical and Degenerate Euler Systems
The theory of Euler systems gained immense traction through Kolyvagin's work, which utilized Heegner points to bound Selmer groups [cite: 15]. The most classical instances of Euler systems linked to $p$-adic L-functions include:
1.  **Leopoldt's Formula:** Relates the value at $s=1$ of the Kubota-Leopoldt $p$-adic L-function to the $p$-adic logarithms of circular units [cite: 15].
2.  **Katz's $p$-adic Kronecker Limit Formula:** Relates the values of the two-variable $p$-adic L-function of an imaginary quadratic field to the $p$-adic logarithms of elliptic units [cite: 15].

In contemporary research, circular and elliptic units are increasingly viewed as "degenerate cases" of the Euler system of Heegner points, obtained by substituting cusp forms with Eisenstein series [cite: 15]. 

### 2.2 Kato's Euler System and Beilinson-Flach Elements
A major paradigm shift occurred with Kato's construction of an Euler system for modular forms using Beilinson elements in the algebraic K-theory (specifically the second K-group) of modular curves [cite: 15]. Their connection to L-values relies heavily on Rankin's method applied to a cusp form and a pair of weight-two Eisenstein series [cite: 15].

Expanding on Kato's work, mathematicians have recently generalized these constructions to "Euler systems of Garrett-Rankin-Selberg type." These include:
*   The Euler system of **Beilinson-Flach elements** in the first K-group of a product of two modular curves [cite: 15].
*   The Euler system of **generalized Gross-Kudla-Schoen diagonal cycles**, whose link to special L-values derives from Garrett's formula for the central critical value of the convolution L-series attached to a triple of newforms [cite: 15].

### 2.3 New Constructions via Eisenstein Classes in Shimura Varieties
A defining advancement in the 2024-2026 period is the development of a systematic methodology to construct Euler systems using Eisenstein classes in the cohomology of Shimura varieties [cite: 13]. Pioneered by Sangiovanni and Skinner, and further advanced by Fernando Trejos Suárez, this framework re-contextualizes the construction of Euler systems [cite: 13]. 

In this approach, the classes forming the Euler system are defined as extensions of Galois representations within the étale cohomology of a modular curve, relative to a specific collection of points (such as cusps or CM points) [cite: 13]. Eisenstein series are deployed to construct distinguished, non-trivial classes in this cohomology. Crucially, these Eisenstein series are naturally entwined with the respective $p$-adic L-functions [cite: 13]. This methodology has not only reconstructed cyclotomic and elliptic units elegantly but is actively being pushed toward the construction of an elusive Euler system for the "triple product" of modular forms [cite: 13].

### 2.4 Asai-Flach Euler Systems and Ultrafilters
At the 2025 Darmonfest at the Université de Montréal, researchers showcased cutting-edge applications of Euler systems. For instance, in the case of Asai motives attached to quadratic Hilbert modular forms, a Rankin-Selberg-type integral simultaneously yields the Asai-Flach Euler system and its corresponding $p$-adic L-function [cite: 14]. Joint work by David Loeffler and Sarah Zerbes has proven the precise relationship between these two objects, directly leading to the verification of new cases of the Bloch-Kato conjecture [cite: 14].

Furthermore, Sarah Zerbes has introduced highly innovative topological methods to the field, delivering a lecture in 2025 titled "Euler systems via ultrafilters." [cite: 14]. Because Euler systems provide bounds on the "strict" Selmer group (cohomology classes whose localization at $p$ vanishes), leveraging ultrafilters allows for a novel level of abstraction in controlling the size of Selmer spaces under local conditions [cite: 14].

## 3. Four-Variable $p$-adic Triple Product L-functions and the Trivial Zero Conjecture

One of the most celebrated recent milestones in Iwasawa theory is the definitive construction and application of the four-variable primitive $p$-adic L-function associated with the triple product of Hida families [cite: 16, 17]. Published by Ming-Lun Hsieh and Shunsuke Yamana, this monumental work directly addresses the trivial zero conjecture for elliptic curves [cite: 17, 18].

### 3.1 Hida Families and the Triple Product
A Hida family represents a $p$-adic analytic variation of ordinary modular forms across varying weights [cite: 19]. Let $\mathcal{F} = (F_1, F_2, F_3)$ be a triplet of primitive Hida families with tame conductors and specified nebentypus characters [cite: 20]. The goal of Hsieh and Yamana was to construct a multi-variable Iwasawa function capable of interpolating the algebraic part of the critical values of the complex triple product L-function attached to $\mathcal{F}$, precisely at all balanced critical specializations twisted by Dirichlet characters [cite: 20].

### 3.2 The Method: Pull-back of Siegel Eisenstein Series on $\mathrm{GSp}(6)$
The construction heavily relies on the $p$-adic Rankin-Selberg convolution method applied to Garrett's integral representation of triple product L-functions [cite: 16, 18]. The main technical novelty introduced by Hsieh and Yamana is the explicit construction and $p$-adic patching of four distinct $p$-adic families of the pull-back of nearly holomorphic Siegel Eisenstein series on the symplectic group $\mathrm{GSp}(6)$ [cite: 17, 18]. 

Historically, Böcherer and Panchishkin attempted to construct one-variable $p$-adic L-functions for three primitive elliptic newforms using this pull-back on $\mathrm{GSp}(6)$ [cite: 17]. However, achieving a full four-variable interpolation (accounting for the weight variations of all three forms plus the cyclotomic variable) represents a staggering technical achievement [cite: 17]. The authors achieved explicit interpolation formulas at all critical points in the balanced range, fully complying with the conjectural frameworks established decades earlier by Coates and Perrin-Riou [cite: 20].

### 3.3 Resolution of the Trivial Zero Conjecture
The application of this four-variable $p$-adic L-function is of paramount importance to arithmetic geometry. When evaluating the $p$-adic L-function $L_p(E, s)$ of an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$, an exceptional phenomenon known as a "trivial zero" arises at $s=1$ if and only if $E$ exhibits split multiplicative reduction at $p$ [cite: 17]. 

The Greenberg-Benois trivial zero conjecture extends this phenomenon to ordinary motives and semistable representations [cite: 17]. By specializing their four-variable function down to the cyclotomic $p$-adic L-function for the motive associated with the triple product of $p$-ordinary elliptic curves, Hsieh and Yamana successfully proved the trivial zero conjecture for this specific motive [cite: 16, 18]. Remarkably, this achievement constitutes the very first proven case of the Greenberg-Benois trivial zero conjecture in scenarios where *multiple* trivial zeros are simultaneously present, and where the associated Galois representation is not merely of $\mathrm{GL}(2)$-type [cite: 18, 20].

## 4. Higher Rank Groups and Automorphic Period Conjectures

Moving beyond $\mathrm{GL}_1$ and $\mathrm{GL}_2$, the construction of $p$-adic L-functions for general reductive groups has seen aggressive advancement. A primary focus is on Regular Algebraic Cuspidal Automorphic Representations (RACARs) of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$ [cite: 6].

### 4.1 General Type RACARs and the Panchishkin Condition
A persistent obstacle in the Iwasawa theory of $\mathrm{GL}_n$ ($n > 2$) was the assumption of self-duality or functorial lifting. Recently, researchers have successfully given the very first constructions of $p$-adic L-functions for RACARs of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$ of "general type"—meaning those that do *not* arise simply as functorial lifts from smaller groups [cite: 6, 21]. 

This advancement required overcoming the strict ordinarity conditions originally proposed by Coates and Perrin-Riou [cite: 6]. Coates and Perrin-Riou had conjectured the existence of a $p$-adic L-function attached to every motive over $\mathbb{Q}$ that is ordinary and has good reduction at $p$, provided its L-function has at least one critical value (relying heavily on Deligne's period conjecture) [cite: 6]. 

Recent developments have bypassed the Coates-Perrin-Riou assumption of full Borel-ordinarity. Instead, mathematicians have verified a broader, more flexible conjecture posited by Panchishkin [cite: 6]. For a RACAR $\Pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_3$, it is sufficient to impose $p$-ordinarity solely along a maximal standard parabolic subgroup (e.g., $P_1$ with Levi factor $\mathrm{GL}_1 \times \mathrm{GL}_2$, or $P_2$ with Levi factor $\mathrm{GL}_2 \times \mathrm{GL}_1$) [cite: 6]. 

### 4.2 Betti Euler Systems and Manin Relations
To achieve this, researchers have utilized the theory of spherical varieties to construct a "Betti Euler system" [cite: 6]. This is a norm-compatible system of classes housed within the Betti cohomology of a locally symmetric space for $\mathrm{GL}_3$ [cite: 6]. Using Beilinson's motivic Eisenstein classes, researchers can $p$-adically interpolate the pushforward of these classes [cite: 6].

The strength of this modern construction is multifold:
*   **Arbitrary Cohomological Weight:** The construction holds without restrictions on weight [cite: 6].
*   **Arbitrary Ramification at $p$:** The theory permits arbitrary ramification along the Levi factor of the standard parabolic [cite: 6].
*   **The Manin Relations:** Rather than constructing a distinct measure for every single critical integer, researchers have proven the Manin relations by constructing a *single* unifying $p$-adic measure that comprehensively "sees" the L-values across all critical integers in a given critical region [cite: 6].

## 5. The Frontier of Inert Primes, Supersingular Reduction, and Non-Equal Characteristics

Historically, the machinery of Iwasawa theory functioned most smoothly at primes $p$ where the arithmetic object exhibited *ordinary* reduction. In the context of elliptic curves with complex multiplication (CM), this meant restricting attention to primes that split in the imaginary quadratic field [cite: 22]. The modern frontier, heavily emphasized in 2024-2026, confronts the "non-ordinary" scenarios: primes of supersingular reduction, primes that are inert in the reflex field, and extensions with differing prime characteristics [cite: 1, 22, 23].

### 5.1 Supersingular Reduction and Inert Primes
If $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is an elliptic curve and $p$ is a prime for which $E$ has good supersingular reduction (i.e., $p$ divides the trace of Frobenius $a_p(E)$), the $\Lambda$-module structure of its Selmer group becomes vastly more complicated [cite: 7]. The classical invariants $\mu$ and $\lambda$ behave erratically, and new points of infinite order can unexpectedly appear in alternating anticyclotomic layers [cite: 24]. As noted by researchers, "new phenomena abound and CM Iwasawa theory is still incipient" for non-split primes [cite: 24].

A landmark achievement in this area is the study of $p$-adic L-functions and rational points on CM elliptic curves at inert primes, primarily driven by Ashay Burungale, Shinichi Kobayashi, and Kazuto Ota [cite: 24, 25]. For an imaginary quadratic field $K$ where a prime $p \geq 5$ is inert, and an elliptic curve $E$ with CM by the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, Karl Rubin had previously introduced a specialized $p$-adic L-function $\mathscr{L}_E$ that interpolates the special values of L-functions of $E$ twisted by anticyclotomic characters [cite: 25, 26].

The Burungale-Kobayashi-Ota collaboration succeeded in proving a formula that explicitly links values of $\mathscr{L}_E$ *outside* its defining range of interpolation with the existence of rational points on $E$ [cite: 25, 26]. A critical tool in this proof was the resolution of Rubin's long-standing conjecture regarding the structure of local units in the anticyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension of the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [cite: 25, 27]. To achieve this, the authors developed a theory of local points over the Lubin-Tate formal group of height 2 [cite: 25, 27].

### 5.2 The $p$-converse to the Gross-Zagier and Kolyvagin Theorem
The resolution of Rubin's conjecture and the behavior of $\mathscr{L}_E$ at inert primes directly yielded profound arithmetic consequences, most notably establishing a $p$-converse to the Gross-Zagier and Kolyvagin theorem for $E$ at inert primes [cite: 25, 26]. 

The classical Gross-Zagier and Kolyvagin theorems assert that if the analytic rank of an elliptic curve is 1, then its algebraic rank is also 1. The $p$-converse seeks to prove the reverse: if the $p$-adic Selmer group indicates a rank of 1, does the complex L-function have a simple zero at $s=1$? By utilizing the Bertolini-Darmon-Prasanna formula and leveraging the Iwasawa theory of the anticyclotomic deformation of a CM elliptic curve, the authors established a $p$-adic criterion proving that an algebraic rank of 1 implies an analytic rank of 1 for these specific non-ordinary cases [cite: 26, 27]. Furthermore, this $p$-adic L-value facilitates the $p$-adic construction of a rational point of infinite order [cite: 24].

### 5.3 Local Sign Decomposition for Symplectic Representations
Expanding on non-semistable primes, research has introduced a functorial decomposition of the Galois cohomology of $p$-adic representations of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ of rank two [cite: 23]. Because the Bloch-Kato conjecture suggests that associated epsilon factors are pivotal, this new decomposition mirrors an underlying symplectic structure and yields wide-ranging global applications [cite: 23]. It establishes compatibility between the Mazur-Rubin arithmetic local constant and the epsilon factor, proves the parity conjecture at primes of non-semistable reduction, and outlines a rigorous framework for integral Iwasawa theory at non-semistable primes strictly in terms of a newly defined $p$-adic L-function [cite: 23]. This directly recovers a 1987 conjecture of Rubin regarding the Galois cohomology of anticyclotomic local units [cite: 23].

### 5.4 Unitary Groups and Locally Analytic Distribution Algebras
Another major frontier involves adapting Iwasawa theory to representations of the unitary group $\mathrm{GU}(2,1)$ at primes that are inert in the respective imaginary quadratic reflex field $E$ [cite: 28]. Because traditional cyclotomic or anticyclotomic Iwasawa theory relies on structural properties of the Galois group that fail in the inert Lubin-Tate setting, an entirely novel approach is required [cite: 28].

Recent advances have bypassed these limitations by utilizing the Schneider-Venjakob regulator map [cite: 28]. Because one cannot simply 'pull back' to a quotient of the standard Iwasawa algebra, researchers must instead work over locally analytic distribution algebras ($\Lambda^\infty_L$) [cite: 28]. The algebra is defined by an $L$-analyticity condition on distributions rather than a topological Galois condition [cite: 28]. Through mapping Kato's Euler system to the $p$-adic L-function via this regulator, exact sequence computations have shown the vanishing of specific Bloch-Kato Selmer groups when the associated $p$-adic distribution is non-vanishing [cite: 28]. These results successfully verify rank-0 cases of the Bloch-Kato conjecture for $\mathrm{GU}(2,1)$ at inert primes [cite: 28].

### 5.5 Non-Equal Characteristic Extensions
At the University of Ottawa, researchers like Antonio Lei are pushing the boundaries by asking a deceptively simple question: what happens in Iwasawa theory when the primes do not match? [cite: 1]. While traditional Iwasawa theory tracks $p$-primary arithmetic objects over extensions generated by $p$-power roots of unity, the "non-equal characteristic" frontier explores the $p$-primary structure of objects (like class groups and Selmer groups) over infinite towers constructed using a *different* prime $q$ [cite: 1]. This seemingly simple twist fundamentally breaks classical descent mechanics, opening up an intriguing, less-explored topology that forms a major topic of colloquia in late 2025 [cite: 1].

## 6. Geometric Generalizations: Iwasawa Theory of Weighted Graphs

A fascinating, highly interdisciplinary development in the 2024-2026 timeframe is the successful translation of Iwasawa-theoretic philosophies into discrete mathematics and combinatorial geometry, specifically regarding the asymptotic properties of graphs [cite: 11].

In classical Iwasawa theory, one studies the asymptotic growth of class numbers in a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-tower of number fields. The combinatorial analogue studies the asymptotic behavior of the number of spanning trees (complexities) in a compatible system of $\mathbb{Z}_p^d$-covers (towers) of graphs [cite: 11, 29, 30]. 

### 6.1 Weighted Complexities and the Artin-Ihara L-function
Recent work by Taiga Adachi, Kosuke Mizuno, and Sohei Tateno has substantially generalized the Iwasawa theory of graphs to the Iwasawa theory of *weighted* graphs [cite: 29, 31]. By introducing an arbitrary weight function to the vertices and edges of a symmetric digraph, the classical count of spanning trees is upgraded to "weighted complexities" [cite: 29]. 

To estimate the growth of these weighted complexities, the researchers utilized the Artin-Ihara L-function for weighted graphs [cite: 29]. In graph theory, the Ihara zeta function is the discrete analogue of the Riemann zeta function, tracking prime cycles in a graph. The Artin-Ihara L-function allows for Galois-twisted interpolations of these cycles. Because the proofs in graph-theoretic Iwasawa theory rely heavily on three-term determinant expressions of these L-functions, establishing these expressions for weighted graphs enabled a full translation of the theory [cite: 29].

### 6.2 Analogues of Kida's and Riemann-Hurwitz Formulas
The crowning achievements of this discrete Iwasawa theory are the strict analogues of deep arithmetic formulas [cite: 11, 29, 30]:
1.  **Analogue of Iwasawa's Class Number Formula:** The researchers proved that the weighted complexity in a $\mathbb{Z}_p^d$-tower of weighted graphs follows a deterministic asymptotic growth pattern parameterized by combinatorial analogues of $\mu$ and $\lambda$ invariants [cite: 11, 29].
2.  **Analogue of the Riemann-Hurwitz Formula:** In algebraic geometry, the Riemann-Hurwitz formula describes the topological balance among genera, ramification indices, and extension degrees in a covering of Riemann surfaces [cite: 29]. In classical Iwasawa theory, *Kida's formula* serves as the exact arithmetic analogue of Riemann-Hurwitz, describing the balance of $\lambda$-invariants, ramification indices, and extension degrees between a pair of $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extensions over number fields [cite: 29]. Adachi, Mizuno, and Tateno successfully proved a precise analogue of both the Riemann-Hurwitz and Kida formulas for $\mathbb{Z}_p^d$-towers of weighted graphs, demonstrating a profound, structural universality of Iwasawa theory that transcends algebraic number theory [cite: 29, 30, 31].
