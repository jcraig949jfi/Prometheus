# Progress on Beilinson Conjectures and Special L-Values (2024-2026)

*   **Function Field Breakthroughs:** The Beilinson-Bloch conjecture has been established for new classes of non-isotrivial varieties over global function fields, including specific cubic threefolds and powers of CM elliptic curves.

**The Landscape of Special L-Values**
The study of $L$-functions is a cornerstone of modern number theory and arithmetic geometry. These complex analytic functions encode the distribution of prime numbers and the fundamental arithmetic properties of algebraic varieties. The Beilinson conjectures propose a sweeping, generalized bridge between the leading Taylor coefficients of these $L$-functions at specific integer points and the dimensions of corresponding motivic cohomology groups. While originally formulated in the 1980s, the conjectures have historically suffered from a lack of unconditional proofs due to the immense difficulty of constructing explicit algebraic cycles.

**Recent Paradigm Shifts**
Over the period of 2024 to 2026, the mathematical community has adopted a highly diversified toolkit to crack open new cases of these conjectures. Rather than attacking the classical formulations head-on with traditional algebraic cycles, mathematicians are circumventing the roadblocks by using noncommutative geometry, relative cohomology in hypergeometric families, and intersection theory over function fields. By mapping these classical geometric invariants onto differential equations and derived categories, researchers have transformed the Beilinson conjectures from isolated analytic hypotheses into a tightly woven tapestry of homological algebra, Hodge theory, and spectral resolution protocols.

## Introduction to the Beilinson and Beilinson-Bloch Conjectures

The conjectures formulated by Alexander Beilinson in the 1980s represent a vast generalization of several pivotal class number formulas in algebraic number theory, including the analytic class number formula, the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture, and Borel's theorem on the $K$-theory of number fields. At its core, the Beilinson conjecture predicts a precise relationship between the non-critical special values of Hasse-Weil $L$-functions attached to algebraic varieties and the regulators of specific elements within motivic cohomology [cite: 1, 2].

For a smooth, projective variety $X$ defined over an algebraic number field $F$, the $L$-function $L(H^i(X), s)$ is defined via an Euler product over the primes of $F$. This complex analytic function converges absolutely in a right half-plane and is conjectured (and in many cases proven) to admit a meromorphic continuation to the entire complex plane, satisfying a functional equation relating $s$ and $d-s$ [cite: 2]. 

The primary object of interest is the behavior of $L(H^i(X), s)$ at integer points $s = n$. Beilinson's insight was to construct a regulator map:
\[ r: H^i_{\mathcal{M}}(X, \mathbb{Q}(n)) \to H^i_{\mathcal{D}}(X, \mathbb{R}(n)) \]
where $H^i_{\mathcal{M}}$ denotes the motivic cohomology of $X$ (often defined via Bloch's higher Chow groups or algebraic $K$-theory) and $H^i_{\mathcal{D}}$ denotes Deligne-Beilinson cohomology [cite: 2, 3]. The conjecture posits two main claims:
1.  **Rank Hypothesis:** The dimension of the $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space generated by the essential part of the motivic cohomology is equal to the order of vanishing of the $L$-function at $s = n$ [cite: 2, 4].
2.  **Special Value Hypothesis:** The leading non-zero coefficient of the Taylor expansion of $L(H^i(X), s)$ at $s = n$ is equal, up to a non-zero rational factor, to the determinant of the regulator matrix formed by evaluating $r$ on a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of the motivic cohomology [cite: 2, 3].

A closely related generalization is the Beilinson-Bloch conjecture, which refines these predictions for the Chow groups of zero cycles on $X$. Over number fields, it connects the rank of the homologically trivial part of the Chow group $CH^i(X)_0$ to the order of vanishing of the associated $L$-function [cite: 5, 6]. More recently, these ideas have been extended via the Beilinson-Bloch-Kato conjecture, which utilizes Galois cohomology, Bloch-Kato Selmer groups, and $p$-adic periods to formulate a universal framework that governs the arithmetic of motives, automorphic forms, and their $L$-functions [cite: 7].

Between 2024 and 2026, research into these conjectures exploded across multiple distinct methodologies. The remainder of this report synthesizes the massive strides made in verifying these conjectures unconditionally across function fields, noncommutative spaces, Calabi-Yau motives, and K3 surfaces.

## Homological Projective Duality and Noncommutative L-Functions

One of the most radical approaches to the Beilinson, Tate, and Riemann conjectures during the 2024-2026 period stems from the realm of noncommutative algebraic geometry. Led by researchers such as Goncalo Tabuada, a major initiative funded by the European Research Council (and later EPSRC) successfully established the invariance of these conjectures under **Homological Projective Duality (HPD)** [cite: 8].

### The Noncommutative Shift
Classical motives are extracted from the category of smooth proper algebraic varieties. The modern noncommutative approach replaces a classical variety $X$ with a smooth proper differential graded (dg) category [cite: 8, 9]. By utilizing the derived category of coherent sheaves $D^b(X)$ and its semi-orthogonal decompositions, researchers can isolate the "interesting" arithmetic components of a variety [cite: 10]. Tabuada and collaborators developed a robust theory of noncommutative $L$-functions that attach Euler products to these dg-categories independently of a traditional geometric space [cite: 8].

### Kuznetsov's HPD-Invariance
Homological Projective Duality, originally introduced by Kuznetsov, relates the derived category of a projective variety to the derived category of its "dual" variety in a highly structured way [cite: 10]. The breakthrough achieved in 2024-2025 was the formal proof that the Tate, Beilinson, and generalized Riemann hypotheses are HPD-invariant [cite: 8, 11]. This means that if the conjectures hold for a specific geometric component (encoded as a dg-category), they automatically hold for its homological projective dual.

This framework allowed researchers to completely bypass the construction of traditional algebraic cycles for highly complex varieties. By proving the Beilinson and Tate conjectures are HPD-invariant over totally imaginary number fields, Tabuada obtained alternative proofs for classic cases (such as Pfaffian cubic fourfolds and complete intersections of quadrics) and established the first proofs for entirely new families of varieties [cite: 11, 12]. Specifically, the conjectures were proven for linear sections of determinantal varieties, low-dimensional root stacks, and twisted orbifolds [cite: 11, 12]. This represented a paradigm shift: instead of tackling the analytic properties of $L$-functions directly, mathematicians could "transport" the truth of the Beilinson conjecture from simple, well-understood spaces to highly complex spaces via categorical equivalences [cite: 9, 13].

## Breakthroughs in the Global Function Field Setting

While the Beilinson-Bloch conjecture was primarily formulated for varieties over number fields, the 2024-2026 period witnessed monumental progress for varieties defined over global function fields of positive characteristic $k = \mathbb{F}_q(t)$. The adaptation of the conjecture to function fields posits that for a smooth projective variety $X/k$, the dimension of the rational Chow group $CH^i(X)_0$ is exactly the order of vanishing of the Hasse-Weil $L$-function $L(H^{2i-1}(\overline{X}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell), s)$ at $s=i$ [cite: 4, 14].

### Broe's Criterion and Geisser's Theorem
In a series of landmark papers published in 2025 and 2026, Matt Broe provided a comprehensive global-to-local criterion for the Beilinson-Bloch conjecture to hold over function fields, significantly extending earlier foundational work by Uwe Jannsen [cite: 4, 5]. By examining the Leray spectral sequence associated with a smooth projective variety fibered over a curve, Broe related the homologically trivial parts of Chow groups to the order of vanishing of automorphic $L$-functions [cite: 7, 14]. 

A direct consequence of this new criterion was a novel, unconditional proof of a theorem by Geisser [cite: 4, 14]. Geisser's theorem establishes a deep equivalence: the Tate conjecture for divisors on a variety over a finite field is equivalent to the Tate conjecture for divisors on its generic fiber, coupled with the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture for the Albanese variety of that generic fiber [cite: 15]. Broe's intersection-theoretic approach over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ solidified this connection, effectively locking the Bloch-Beilinson kernel together with the Artin-Tate and BSD conjectures [cite: 7, 15].

### CM Elliptic Curves and Non-Isotrivial Varieties
The function field framework yielded striking unconditional results for specific geometries. Broe established the full Tate conjecture for the product of a smooth projective curve $C$ with a power of a Complex Multiplication (CM) elliptic curve $E^g$ over any finitely generated field [cite: 4, 14]. Because the motive of a CM elliptic curve admits a highly structured decomposition (utilizing the Deninger-Murre and Moonen refinements), researchers can isolate subrepresentations of the motive to gain extraordinary control over the Chow groups [cite: 7, 14]. 

This allowed for the deduction of the Beilinson-Bloch conjecture for powers of a CM elliptic curve $E^g$ over a global function field [cite: 4, 14]. Furthermore, it provided a conditional answer to a long-standing question posed by Moonen regarding the simplicity and concentration of Chow groups for powers of ordinary CM elliptic curves over arbitrary fields [cite: 5, 14].

In 2026, the methodology was successfully extended to non-isotrivial varieties. The Beilinson-Bloch conjecture was proven for specific classes of smooth cubic threefolds and fivefolds over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ [cite: 16]. By demonstrating an exact relation between the Chow motive of a smooth cubic threefold and that of its intermediate Jacobian, researchers showed that the Beilinson-Bloch conjecture for the threefold is formally equivalent to the BSD conjecture for its intermediate Jacobian. The strong form of BSD was subsequently established for these intermediate Jacobians, yielding the finiteness of the Albanese kernel and resolving the conjecture in these specific dimensions [cite: 15, 16].

## Calabi-Yau Motives, Hypergeometric Families, and Mobile Higher Regulators

One of the most computationally demanding aspects of Beilinson's conjectures is the explicit construction of elements in motivic cohomology and the subsequent evaluation of the regulator map. In late 2024 and 2025, Vasily Golyshev and Matt Kerr achieved a major breakthrough in calculating explicit regulators for certain rank 4, weight 3 Calabi-Yau motives [cite: 17, 18].

### Mobile Higher Regulators
Traditionally, constructing higher rank regulators from a single variety requires an ad-hoc synthesis of algebraic cycles. Golyshev and Kerr introduced the concept of **mobile higher regulators**, an ingenious methodology that relies on varying a motive within a family such that a desired motivic cohomology class is naturally realized by relative cohomology [cite: 17]. 

Their approach combined three core ideas:
1.  Varying the motive in a family to realize $H^4_{\mathcal{M}}(X, \mathbb{Z}(p))$ via relative cohomology.
2.  Constructing higher-rank (e.g., $2 \times 2$) regulators from a single family by evaluating normal functions at different points [cite: 17].
3.  Arranging elements in motivic cohomology with different weight parameters $p$ by intentionally selecting hypergeometric families with differing local exponents [cite: 17].

By utilizing families of generalized algebraic cycles and taking the Hadamard fiber product of families of elliptic curves, they derived explicit expressions for the regulators [cite: 18, 19]. Rather than hunting for static algebraic cycles, the methodology relied on "generalized periods" attached to higher normal functions, bypassing the construction of cycles entirely for the purpose of computing regulators at specific algebraic points in the moduli space [cite: 18].

### Numerical Verifications of the Beilinson Conjectures
Through the use of the Mellin-Barnes trick and exact analytic continuation of Betti periods, Golyshev and Kerr evaluated inhomogeneous differential equations associated with these Calabi-Yau motives [cite: 18, 19]. For fourteen specific hypergeometric cases, they explicitly computed the normal functions and successfully constructed the motivic cohomology cycles realizing the required Hodge classes. 

This monumental effort provided the first unconditional numerical checks of Beilinson's conjectures on special values of $L$-functions for such motives [cite: 17]. Specifically, they verified Beilinson's conjecture for the $L$-values $L''(M_t, 0)$ and $L'(M_t, 2)$ associated with these hypergeometric motives. For the $L'(M_t, 1)$ case, they computed regulators by extending higher Chow cycles to integral models and evaluating the cup-product [cite: 18, 19]. 

In a parallel 2026 study alongside Neil Dummigan and Rob de Jeu, Golyshev and Kerr extended these hypergeometric techniques to investigate the 2-part of the Bloch-Kato conjecture for $L(E_u, 2)$, where $E_u : y^2 = x(x+1)(x+u^2)$ is a twisted Legendre family [cite: 20]. By utilizing the Merkurjev-Suslin norm residue isomorphism and 2-adic regulator maps, they matched the 2-divisibility of global Galois invariants with the 2-Tamagawa factors, successfully confirming the conjecture's predictions for specific values of $u$ where the cycle's pullback matched the Beilinson regulator [cite: 20].

## K3 Surfaces, Involutions, and Mahler Measures

While Calabi-Yau threefolds provided a fertile ground for rank 4 motives, significant progress was also made on K3 surfaces (Calabi-Yau twofolds) and the geometric interpretations of their $L$-values via Mahler measures.

### The Albanese Kernel and Involutions
A persistent obstacle in the theory of algebraic cycles on complex surfaces is Mumford's theorem, which states that the Chow group of zero cycles on a surface with geometric genus $p_g > 0$ is infinite-dimensional. Bloch's conjecture posits the converse: if a surface has geometric genus zero, the Chow group of zero cycles should be isomorphic to the Albanese variety, meaning the Albanese kernel is trivial [cite: 21]. Beilinson generalized this to predict that for any smooth projective surface over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, the Albanese kernel is trivially zero [cite: 21].

In 2025, Kalyan Banerjee proved Beilinson's conjecture for specific families of K3 surfaces defined over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ equipped with an involution. Specifically, Banerjee studied cases where the quotient of the K3 surface by the involution is a projective plane $\mathbb{P}^2$ branched along a sextic curve [cite: 21, 22]. By adapting a technique pioneered by Voisin, Banerjee demonstrated that the symplectic involution acts as the identity on the homologically trivial Chow group $A_0(S)$. Since the rational quotient surface has a trivial $A_0$, it forces the involution to act as $-1$, leading to the conclusion that the Albanese kernel on the K3 surface over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ must be zero [cite: 21]. This marked a rare, unconditional geometric proof of Beilinson's prediction for surfaces of general type.

### Deligne-Beilinson Cohomology and the Mahler Measure
Simultaneously, the analytic side of K3 surfaces saw developments via the logarithmic Mahler measure. Originally introduced in transcendental number theory, the Mahler measure $m(P)$ of a multivariable polynomial $P(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ represents an integral over an $n$-dimensional torus. In the late 1990s, Deninger established that the Mahler measure could be interpreted as a Beilinson regulator evaluated on a specific motivic cohomology class, linking $m(P)$ directly to $L'(0, E)$ up to a rational factor predicted by Beilinson [cite: 23, 24].

In 2025 and 2026, Trieu Thu Ha extended Deninger's work to exact polynomials in arbitrarily many variables, successfully expressing their Mahler measure entirely in terms of Deligne-Beilinson cohomology [cite: 24]. Ha then applied Goncharov's polylogarithmic complexes to four-variable exact polynomials, proving a direct relationship with the special value of the $L$-function of K3 surfaces at $s=4$ [cite: 24]. Assuming standard structural conjectures by Goncharov and Beilinson, Ha explicitly generated the motivic cohomology elements required to match the partial derivative $L$-values, confirming that the Mahler measure evaluates to combinations of $\zeta'(-2)$ and $L'(f, -1)$ for associated modular forms [cite: 23, 24].

## Advancements in the Beilinson-Bloch Height Pairing

The Beilinson-Bloch height pairing is a bilinear pairing on the homologically trivial algebraic cycles of a variety, forming a central component of the generalized Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer and Beilinson conjectures. Between 2024 and 2026, researchers focused intensely on making this pairing explicitly computable and understanding its asymptotic behavior in degenerating families [cite: 25, 26].

### Explicit Computation and Limit Mixed Hodge Structures
The classical definition of the height pairing relies on Arakelov geometry and Gillet-Soulé intersection theory, mapping to $\mathbb{Q}$ via Green currents. However, computing these heights for higher-dimensional varieties has historically been computationally intractable. In recent works (2024-2025), a new methodology emerged: utilizing the equivalence between the Beilinson-Bloch height and the Hodge period of a limit mixed Hodge structure [cite: 25].

For a one-parameter degenerating family of varieties, smoothing a singular fiber encodes the height pairing of cycles supported on the exceptional divisors [cite: 25]. Researchers established a rigorous congruence—up to the rational span of logarithms of primes—between the Beilinson-Bloch heights obtained from odd-dimensional hypersurfaces with a node and the height of specific limit mixed Hodge structures [cite: 26]. This congruence allowed algorithms (implemented in systems like SageMath) to practically compute the relevant limit mixed Hodge structures for nodal quartic curves and nodal cubic threefolds, providing hard numerical data for the pairing [cite: 26]. 

### Asymptotics and Higher-Dimensional Generalizations
Theoretical extensions also advanced rapidly. The refined Beilinson-Bloch pairing was extended to arbitrary smooth projective varieties (higher-dimensional bases) using perverse sheaf techniques and derived intersection products [cite: 25]. In studying the asymptotics of the pairing $\langle Z_t, W_t \rangle_\infty$ in degenerating families, researchers proved that the Archimedean local height is strictly controlled by the non-Archimedean geometric intersection pairing of admissible liftings. Specifically, the pairing exhibits logarithmic growth $\mu_0 \log|t|$ that extends continuously at the singular locus, perfectly aligning with Beilinson's predicted Hodge-theoretic formulation of biextensions in the category of mixed Hodge structures [cite: 25].

## The Beilinson-Bloch-Kato Conjecture: Automorphic Forms and Selmer Groups

The most encompassing modern formulation of these problems is the **Beilinson-Bloch-Kato (BBK) conjecture**. The BBK conjecture acts as a massive unifying framework, linking the order of vanishing of $L$-functions at critical points directly to the dimensions of Bloch-Kato Selmer groups (defined via Galois cohomology) rather than purely algebraic Chow groups [cite: 7].

### Euler Systems and Polarized Motives
During 2025, remarkable progress was made on the BBK conjecture using the machinery of Euler and Kolyvagin systems coupled with Iwasawa theory. For polarized motives—those arising from conjugate self-dual (unitary) or self-dual (orthogonal) automorphic representations—it was shown that the vanishing of central critical $L$-values strictly enforces the vanishing of the associated Bloch-Kato Selmer group for almost all primes $\lambda$ [cite: 7].

This result was achieved by constructing "annihilator classes" in Galois cohomology via geometric and automorphic inputs, such as diagonal cycles on Shimura varieties and explicit reciprocity laws [cite: 7]. For instance, the Asai-Flach Euler system provided crucial cohomology classes for Hilbert modular forms. By bridging the regulator images of these classes to three-variable $p$-adic Asai $L$-functions, researchers unconditionally validated the rank-zero predictions of the BBK conjecture for Asai, Rankin-Selberg, and symmetric power motives [cite: 7]. 

Furthermore, in the self-dual case, the BBK conjecture was successfully reduced to the nonvanishing of orthogonal Gross-Prasad periods. This effectively translated the arithmetic of the motive into explicit period computations on classical groups, representing a triumph of the relative Langlands program's intersection with $L$-value conjectures [cite: 7, 27].

### Categorical Local Langlands and Perverse Sheaves
Looking toward the future, the 2025-2026 literature increasingly speculates that the ultimate conceptual home for the Selmer group and $L$-value comparisons lies within the categorical local Langlands correspondence [cite: 7]. In this advanced framework, nearby cycles, perverse sheaves, and categorical Hecke modules on the moduli stacks of local Langlands parameters encode the passage between Galois cohomology (Euler system classes) and automorphic period integrals. The stratifications and weight filtrations on Shimura varieties are now being interpreted as the sheaf-theoretic avatars of the numerical invariants predicted by Beilinson, Bloch, and Kato [cite: 7].

## Syntomic Realizations and $p$-adic $L$-functions

Parallel to the complex analytic Beilinson conjecture is the pursuit of its $p$-adic analogue. The classical Beilinson conjecture evaluates the non-critical values of Hasse-Weil $L$-functions using the regulator of the cyclotomic element, constructed as the specialization to torsion points of the motivic polylogarithm [cite: 1].

### The Dwork Family and Syntomic Regulators
In 2024 and 2025, researchers focused heavily on $p$-adic extensions. Kenichi Bannai developed the **syntomic realization** of the polylogarithm for the algebraic torus associated to a totally real field. This realization allowed for the direct comparison of the syntomic regulator to the special values of $p$-adic $L$-functions associated with Hecke characters of totally real fields, establishing a rigorous $p$-adic Beilinson conjecture for these cases [cite: 1].

Similarly, Aleksander Horawa and Lambert A'Campo achieved major results in 2024 concerning Beilinson's conjecture for the **Dwork family** of hypersurfaces [cite: 28, 29]. By utilizing automorphic forms and higher algebraic cycles, their work bridged the gap between $p$-adic $L$-functions and the geometric cycles required to compute the regulator, leveraging the equivariant BSD conjecture and triple product $p$-adic $L$-functions [cite: 28]. Massimo Bertolini also presented conjectural formulas directly relating the rational points on these varieties to triple product $p$-adic $L$-functions, expanding the scope of the conjecture beyond $K$-theory into the explicit construction of $p$-adic periods for groups such as $\text{GSp}_4$ [cite: 1]. 

### The Beilinson-Lichtenbaum Connection
Finally, the development of motivic cohomology complexes $Z(i)_{\text{mot}}(X)$ over arbitrary quasi-compact, quasi-separated schemes has enriched the topological understanding of these conjectures. The Beilinson-Lichtenbaum conjecture dictates that motivic cohomology can be expressed as a truncation of syntomic cohomology (or $p$-adic étale cohomology) [cite: 30]. Through the use of trace methods in algebraic $K$-theory, researchers extended this comparison from fields to arbitrary valuation rings, solidifying the foundational map $r_{p,n}: K_{2n+1-j}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \to H^1(F, H^{2n}_{\text{ét}}(\overline{X}, \mathbb{Q}_p(j)))$ as a proven isomorphism in mixed characteristic [cite: 30, 31].

## Conclusion

The years 2024 through 2026 represent a historic inflection point in the study of the Beilinson and Beilinson-Bloch conjectures. The classical roadblock—the impossibility of manually constructing explicit higher algebraic cycles for complex geometries—has been systematically bypassed. By transferring the conjectures to the noncommutative realm of derived categories, utilizing relative cohomology in hypergeometric families, exploiting limit mixed Hodge structures for height pairings, and leaning on Euler systems in Galois cohomology, mathematicians have provided overwhelming theoretical and numerical evidence that Beilinson's grand unified vision of $L$-values is correct. While a universal, unconditional proof over arbitrary number fields remains the holy grail of arithmetic geometry, the boundaries of what can be proven unconditionally have been pushed further than at any point since the conjectures were first published.
