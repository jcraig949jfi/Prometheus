## 2. Transfer I: Topologically Directed Cross-Pollination via LLM Swarm DAGs

### 2.1 The Source-Domain Claim and Technique
**Source:** *Heterogeneous Swarms: Jointly Optimizing Model Roles and Weights for Multi-LLM Systems* by Shangbin Feng, Zifeng Wang, et al. [cite: 4, 5].
**Identifiers:** arXiv:2502.04510 | DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2502.04510
**Publication Date:** February 2025 (Updated October 2025)

The source literature introduces an algorithm known as **Heterogeneous Swarms**, which optimizes multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) systems by modeling them as Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) [cite: 5, 6]. The core technique involves a two-step iterative optimization: a *role-step* and a *weight-step*. In the role-step, the system learns a DAG that explicitly specifies the directional flow of inputs and outputs between heterogeneous agents. The adjacency matrices of these DAGs are optimized using Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) based on a specific utility function (e.g., accuracy on a collaborative task) [cite: 4, 5]. 

Crucially, the authors prove that optimizing the *directional flow* (who passes information to whom) yields substantial collaborative gains over symmetric or unstructured communication models, outperforming 15 baseline methodologies by an average of 18.5% [cite: 4, 5]. The technique demonstrates that heterogeneous nodes generate maximum utility only when their dependencies are strictly directed and acyclic.
