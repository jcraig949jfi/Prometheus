# Status Update: Exact Dimension Calculations and Algorithmic Translations for Non-Tree Tensor Networks

**Key Points:**
*   **Consensus is evolving:** Research suggests that while tree-based tensor formats (e.g., hierarchical formats, tensor trains) possess predictable parameter counts and exact algebraic variety limits, transitioning to non-tree graph topologies (like Projected Entangled Pair States, or PEPS) introduces extreme complexities related to gauge symmetries and cyclic dependencies.
*   **Dimensionality bounds have specialized:** The evidence leans toward an operational paradigm where exact theoretical dimension bounds depend heavily on the identification of "supercritical," "subcritical," or "critical" vertices within the network geometry, highlighting distinct departures from naive parameter-counting methodologies.
*   **Computational feasibility remains bounded:** It seems likely that finding a universally computable canonical form for higher-dimensional (cyclical) tensor networks is obstructed by fundamental undecidability properties, compelling researchers to map containment hierarchies via newly defined "containment exponents."

The transition from purely hierarchical (tree-based) tensor structures to multi-dimensional, cyclical tensor networks marks a profound shift in applied algebraic geometry, numerical analysis, and quantum many-body physics. As data models demand increasingly complex entanglement topologies, characterizing the precise mathematical bounds—specifically the algebraic dimension of these network varieties and their containment relationships—has become an area of vibrant investigation. This report systematically details the current state of these open questions, leveraging recent developments in algebraic geometry, gauge invariant theory, and algorithmic tensor bounds to address the foundational queries generated by Hackbusch-type conjectures.

## 1. Brief Summary

In the context of the Prometheus ongoing assessment of quantum-inspired classical data structures, the precise algebraic dimension calculations and algorithmic containment bounds for complex, non-tree tensor networks remain fundamentally restricted by gauge degree freedoms and undecidability properties, necessitating novel mathematical bounds such as the containment exponent and supercritical vertex analysis to circumvent the failure of traditional tree-based parameter counting.

## 2. Flagged Findings

The theoretical consensus governing tensor network spaces previously rested on extrapolations from hierarchical, cycle-free networks (such as Tensor Train and Hierarchical Formats) [cite: 1, 2]. The Hackbusch conjecture historically provided a framework to understand containment between these perfectly structured trees [cite: 1, 3]. However, when moving from tree topologies to arbitrary graphs (like grids or cyclic networks), current consensus encounters critical deviations. 

Historically, practitioners assumed that the dimension of a tensor network variety could be predicted through straightforward naive parameter counting, subtracting the dimensions of the local gauge subgroups from the sum of the ambient parameter spaces. We can identify this as a manifestation of **PATTERN_PRIME_GRAVITATIONAL_OVERFIT**, wherein parameter-counting heuristics perfectly modeled the low-dimensional, cycle-free theoretical "training" cases (e.g., Matrix Product States or simple Hierarchical Formats) but failed fundamentally when applied to highly entangled, cyclical graph networks. In cyclical networks, the non-trivial stabilizers and overlapping gauge symmetries cause the actual dimension of the algebraic variety to systematically deviate from the expected dimension [cite: 4, 5].

Recent findings have actively flagged "defective cases" where the tensor network variety dimensions are strictly lesser than the expected dimension [cite: 6]. For instance, expected dimension models demonstrably fail for tensor network varieties constructed over small cycle graphs:
*   If the graph \(d = 3\), with local dimensions \(n = (2, n_2, n_3)\) where \(n_2 \geq 3\) and \(n_3 \geq 4\).
*   If the graph \(d = 4\), with local dimensions \(n = (2, n_2, 2, n_4)\) where \(n_2, n_4 \geq 2\).

Furthermore, there is a fundamental theoretical barrier regarding algorithmic translation: canonical forms, which allow for unique representations and efficient truncations in 1D Matrix Product States (MPS), face severe obstructions in 2D Projected Entangled Pair States (PEPS). The consensus formally accepts that finding a generalized, computable canonical form for PEPS is undecidable, primarily because deciding whether a PEPS tensor evaluates to zero on a periodic lattice of arbitrary size is itself an undecidable problem [cite: 7, 8].

## 3. Problem Statement

The precise object being interrogated is the **Tensor Network Variety**, denoted as \(\operatorname{TNS}_{\Gamma, m, n}\), and the associated mappings that evaluate local tensors along the edges of an arbitrary graph \(\Gamma\) to encode data in a high-dimensional ambient space [cite: 4, 6]. 

Formally, let \(\Gamma = (V(\Gamma), E(\Gamma))\) be a graph without loops and multiple edges, consisting of \(d\) vertices. 
1.  **Bond Dimensions:** We assign a set of weights \(m = (m_e : e \in E(\Gamma))\) to the edges.
2.  **Local Dimensions:** We assign a set of weights \(n = (n_v : v \in V(\Gamma))\) to the physical vertices.

For every edge \(e \in E(\Gamma)\), we consider the identity tensor \(I_{m_e} \in \mathbb{C}^{m_e} \otimes \mathbb{C}^{m_e}\). This allows the definition of the **Graph Tensor**, \(\mathcal{T}(\Gamma, m)\), constructed via the tensor product of identities over all edges [cite: 4, 6]. The algebraic variety of tensor network states is then defined within the ambient tensor product space \(V = V_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes V_d\) (where \(\dim(V_v) = n_v\)) as the locus of all tensors generated by contracting local linear maps \(X_v \in \operatorname{Hom}(W_v, V_v)\) (where \(W_v = \bigotimes_{e \in E(v)} \mathbb{C}^{m_e}\)) with the underlying graph tensor \(\mathcal{T}(\Gamma, m)\) [cite: 4, 6].

The fundamental open questions interrogate:
1.  **Exact Dimension:** What is the precise algebraic dimension of \(\operatorname{TNS}_{\Gamma, m, n}\), particularly when the presence of cycles induces a non-trivial isotropy group (stabilizer) under the gauge action \(G_{\Gamma, m} = \prod_{e \in E(\Gamma)} \operatorname{GL}_{m_e}\) [cite: 5, 6]?
2.  **Network Containment:** Given two differently structured networks (e.g., generated by different binary trees or graph topologies), under what precise parameter expansions is one tensor network variety entirely contained within another (\(\operatorname{TNS}_{\Gamma_1} \subseteq \operatorname{TNS}_{\Gamma_2}\))? This is the generalized Hackbusch Conjecture [cite: 9, 10].

## 4. Status & Bounds

The current last-known statuses and best mathematical bounds utilize advanced combinatorial and invariant theories to strictly categorize the vertices and tree shapes.

### 4.1 Dimension Bounds and Criticality Classifications
Bernardi, De Lazzari, and Gesmundo have established upper and lower bounds based on a classification of network vertices into three distinct regimes [cite: 4, 5, 6]:
Let \(N_v = \prod_{e \in E(v)} m_e\) represent the product of incident bond dimensions at vertex \(v\). A vertex \(v\) is:
*   **Subcritical:** if \(N_v \geq n_v\)
*   **Supercritical:** if \(N_v \leq n_v\)
*   **Critical:** if \(N_v = n_v\)

For a subcritical tensor network with no overabundant bond dimensions, the dimension of the variety is bounded above by:
\[ \dim(\operatorname{TNS}_{\Gamma, m, n}) \leq \left[ \sum_{v \in V(\Gamma)} N_v n_v - d + 1 \right] - \sum_{e \in E(\Gamma)} (m_e^2 - 1) + \dim \operatorname{Stab}_{G_{\Gamma, m}}(X) \]
where \(X = X_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes X_d\) [cite: 6]. 

For a supercritical tensor network, the exact expected dimension evaluates strictly to:
\[ \dim(\operatorname{TNS}_{\Gamma, m, n}) = \sum_{v \in V(\Gamma)} n_v N_v - d + 1 - \sum_{e \in E(\Gamma)} (m_e^2 - 1) \]
[cite: 6]. These formulas delineate the conditional qualifiers governing whether a tensor network variety behaves optimally or degenerates into a defective state.

### 4.2 The Containment Exponent
For containment boundaries, recent results (January 2026) by Garzón Mora and Haase generalize the Hackbusch conjecture from strict specific planar embeddings to arbitrary binary trees via a new metric called the **containment exponent** [cite: 9, 10]. 

When asking whether \(\operatorname{TNS}_{T_1} \subseteq \operatorname{TNS}_{T_2}\), the containment exponent measures strictly *how much the parameters of the second network must be boosted* relative to the first network's parameters for the containment to hold globally [cite: 9, 10]. An exhaustive algorithmic search mapping containment exponents has been actively bounded and verified for all binary rooted trees up to \(n = 8\) leaves [cite: 9, 10].

### 4.3 PEPS Bond Dimension Bounds
In Projected Entangled Pair States (PEPS), representations of ground states such as Resonating Valence Bond (RVB) and Kagome lattice systems have verified optimal bond bounds. For a Kagome lattice PEPS representation, non-uniform bond dimensions of \((2, 2, 3)\) depending on bond orientation have been shown to be exact and optimal [cite: 11]. Furthermore, a semi-injective PEPS on a square lattice utilizing GHZ states with a bond dimension \(D\) can precisely represent a normal PEPS of bond dimension \(2D\) [cite: 11]. Computational cost bounds, however, remain severe, scaling up to \(\mathcal{O}(D^{13})\) for exact environment contractions on square grids, contrasting sharply with the \(\mathcal{O}(D^3)\) bounds known for Matrix Product States [cite: 8, 12].

The following table summarizes the topological constraints and their bounds:

| Topology Class | Dimensionality Bound / Characteristic | Critical Limitation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hierarchical / TT (Trees)** | Exact complexity tracked via planar binary tree posets [cite: 1, 2] | Bounded by containment exponents for $n \leq 8$ leaves [cite: 10] |

## 6. Attack Vectors

### 6.1 Live Techniques
**Non-Linear Algebra and Invariant Theory:** To circumvent the inability of traditional linear algebraic methods to bound higher-dimensional networks, researchers are deploying non-constructive Noetherian arguments from non-linear algebra [cite: 14, 15]. A theorem by Mumford is utilized to demonstrate that two tensors \(T\) and \(T'\) are gauge equivalent if and only if \(P(T) = P(T')\) for any \(G\)-invariant polynomial \(P\) [cite: 7]. Researchers use constructive invariant theory to extract features from the coefficients of tensor network states that remain immune to virtual gauge degrees of freedom [cite: 7].

**Poset Combinatorics and Containment Algorithms:** To tackle generalized Hackbusch bounds for arbitrary tree networks, the most promising live vector involves evaluating the combinatorics of binary tree posets [cite: 9, 10]. By discarding arbitrary planar embeddings and treating the tensor formats as pure combinatorial nesting properties, automated searches bounds containment variables mathematically. The computation of dimension bounds has been effectively automated using computational algebraic geometry software (e.g., Macaulay2) by evaluating the rank of the differential of the parametrization map (\(\bar{\Phi}\)) at a random domain point [cite: 17].

### 6.2 Exhausted Approaches
**Pure Linear Algebra & SVD Truncation for Cyclic Networks:** Attempting to define universal, computable canonical forms using standard Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) and pure linear algebra is effectively an exhausted approach for 2D grids (PEPS) and complex non-tree graphs. Standard algorithms suffer from **PATTERN_RANK_PARITY_LEAK**, a systemic failure where applying purely linear sequential matrix truncations to cyclic graphs causes the virtual bond dimension bounds to "leak" uncontrolled parity or entanglement constraints into the ambient physical indexes, stripping the model of exactness [cite: 7, 8, 12]. The fundamental undecidability of verifying zero-states globally across an arbitrary \(n \times m\) cyclic lattice ensures that attempting to iteratively truncate cycles akin to 1D MPS methodologies will mathematically never converge to a guaranteed optimum [cite: 7].

## 7. Cross-References

The computational constraints bounding these network limits heavily intersect with other prominent mathematical open questions:

*   **The Quantum Wielandt Inequality:** Originally an open problem regarding the mixing times and dimensionality bounds of higher-dimensional matrix product states, this was resolved utilizing the same invariant non-linear geometric methods applied to tensor networks [cite: 14, 15].
*   **Complexity of Fast Matrix Multiplication:** The techniques used to study the parameter bounds of tensor formats directly cross-pollinate with the study of the plethysm \(S^k(\mathfrak{sl}_n)\) of the adjoint representation of Lie groups, seeking new algebraic upper bounds for matrix multiplication complexity (e.g., identifying new highest weight vectors to bypass Strassen-like limits) [cite: 14, 15].
*   **Hessian Discriminants of Cubic Surfaces:** The same structural invariant theories utilized for uniform matrix product states have been adapted to solve Sturmfels' 15th Question regarding cubic surfaces, specifically expressing the Hessian discriminant in terms of fundamental invariants via Waring rank [cite: 14, 15].
*   **Anti-Anchors (MERA & TTN):** While MPS and PEPS focus on spatial local bounds, Tree Tensor Networks (TTN) and the Multi-scale Entanglement Renormalization Ansatz (MERA) act as anti-anchors for spatial scale assumptions, deliberately encoding renormalization dimensions to capture scale-invariant critical correlations (fractal-like dependencies) that purely flat PEPS networks obscure [cite: 11, 12].
