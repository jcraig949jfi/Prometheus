## 2. Typology of Mathematical Failure Modes

The gradient archaeology of the 2024–2026 kill ledger reveals four primary categories of mathematical failure, each mapping to a distinct subset of the `f3_active_equal_violated` signature.

### 2.1 Computation Error
Computation errors involve numerical, symbolic, or computer-algebraic mistakes. In pure mathematics, these often manifest as algebraic sign errors, incorrect matrix manipulations, or failures in symbolic calculus derivations. In the context of `f3`, this is a literal violation of equality: the proof asserts \(A = B\), but computational trace reveals \(A \neq B\). 

### 2.2 Gap in Proof
A gap in proof occurs when a critical lemma is quietly assumed, or a deductive step lacks rigorous justification. The author may state "it trivially follows that," but the logical distance between the premises and the conclusion exceeds the acceptable threshold for formal derivation. The `f3` pattern triggers because the formal equivalence between the given state and the proven state cannot be constructed.

### 2.3 Prior Art Collision
A prior art collision does not imply a false mathematical statement; rather, it represents a failure of novelty. The author successfully proves a theorem, but the result was already known in the literature. In the Charon swarm taxonomy, this triggers `f3_active_equal_violated` because a semantic hash of the proven claim yields an exact equivalence (an equality) with a pre-existing theorem in the global knowledge graph, violating the assertion of novelty.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Failure
Hypothesis failure occurs when the main result might be partially true, but the proof's hypotheses do not hold in the claimed generality. This is most dramatically revealed by the discovery of a **counterexample**. The `f3` pattern identifies this when the universal quantifier is violated by a specific instantiation that breaks the claimed logical equivalence.

### 3.1 Prior Art Collision: The Zarhin Case

**Case Identifier:** arXiv:2407.16703
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2407.16703
**Original Title:** Odd quadratic orders and real j-invariants
**Author:** Yuri G. Zarhin
**Date of Withdrawal:** July 28, 2024

**Context and Mathematics:**
The paper investigated orders of odd discriminant \(D\) in an imaginary quadratic field \(K\). The author sought to explicitly describe the group \(Cl(\mathcal{O})[cite: 2]\), which is the kernel of multiplication by 2 in the group of proper \(\mathcal{O}\)-ideals. The paper claimed to prove that the order of this group is \(2^{s_D-1}\), where \(s_D\) is the number of prime divisors of \(D\) [cite: 3]. The author subsequently withdrew the paper after another mathematician pointed out that these results were already established in the historical literature.

**Retraction Notice:** 
"The results of the paper were already known. I am grateful to Yuri Bilu for pointing it out." [cite: 3, 4].

**Failure-Mode Classification:** 
Prior art collision (the result was already known).

**Projected v10-Class Battery Signature:** 
`f3_active_equal_violated::semantic_isomorphism_detected(confidence=0.99, target_node="known_theorems_DB")`

**Distinguishing Signal for Taxonomy Refinement:**
Unlike a mathematical error, the logical trace of this paper would compile perfectly in a formal verification system like Lean or Coq. The `f3` violation occurs at the meta-level of the knowledge graph. A v10-class battery must run a concurrent semantic hashing algorithm during proof ingestion. If the derived logical skeleton of the theorem exactly matches a pre-existing skeleton in the database, the battery should emit a prior art collision signal. The distinguishing signal is **"proof validation SUCCESS" coupled with "novelty assertion FAIL"**.

### 3.2 Gap in Proof: The Nyberg-Brodda Case

**Case Identifier:** arXiv:2603.29558
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2603.29558
**Original Title:** A note on right-angled Artin subgroups of one-relator groups
**Author:** Carl-Fredrik Nyberg-Brodda
**Date of Withdrawal:** April 17, 2026

**Context and Mathematics:**
This paper attempted to provide a short proof of a known result by Howie: if a right-angled Artin group \(A(\Gamma)\) embeds into some one-relator group, then \(\Gamma\) must be a finite forest [cite: 5]. The author attempted to rely entirely on elementary Bass-Serre theory and classical properties of one-relator groups to simplify the proof [cite: 5, 6].

**Retraction Notice:**
"Paper has been withdrawn due to a gap, pointed out by Ashot Minasyan, in the proof of the theorem." [cite: 5].

**Failure-Mode Classification:** 
Gap in proof (lemma quietly assumed or deductive jump unsupported).

**Projected v10-Class Battery Signature:** 
`f3_active_equal_violated::deductive_chain_severed(sub_graph="Bass-Serre_action", constraint_unresolved=TRUE)`

**Distinguishing Signal for Taxonomy Refinement:**
In this instance, the overarching theorem is known to be true (it was previously proven by Howie using deeper cohomological methods), but the specific deductive path taken by Nyberg-Brodda contained an insurmountable gap. A v10-class battery evaluating this substrate would reach a state where it attempts to unify the premises of Bass-Serre tree actions with the structural requirements of the Artin group embedding. The battery would fail to automatically synthesize the missing intermediate logical steps. The distinguishing signal here is **"theorem known TRUE" coupled with "local tactic synthesis TIMEOUT/FAIL"**. This refines the kill_pattern by highlighting that `f3` violations in known-true theorems explicitly indicate broken human logic paths, not necessarily false mathematics.

### 3.5 Hypothesis Failure: The Herrmann Case

**Case Identifier:** arXiv:2408.16437
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2408.16437
**Original Title:** Direct finiteness of representable regular rings with involution: A counterexample
**Author:** Christian Herrmann
**Date of Withdrawal:** August 31, 2024

**Context and Mathematics:**
The paper attempted to construct a \(*\)-regular \(*\)-ring \(R\) of endomorphisms of an inner product space (such as the Hilbert space \(\ell^2\)) to demonstrate that direct finiteness fails for \(R\). This was intended to serve as a counterexample to ongoing questions regarding regular rings with involution [cite: 11]. 

**Retraction Notice:**
"As observed by Wehrung, the identity minus shift has no quasi-inverse in the ring of row and column finite matrices. Thus, the claimed example does not work." [cite: 11].

**Failure-Mode Classification:** 
Hypothesis failure / Counterexample invalidation (the mathematical object constructed to serve as a counterexample failed to possess the required properties).

**Distinguishing Signal for Taxonomy Refinement:**
This is a highly valuable signal for the Hecate agent. The paper itself *is* an attempt at providing a counterexample, but the counterexample itself is mathematically flawed. The author claimed that a specific algebraic object ("identity minus shift matrix") possessed a specific property ("has a quasi-inverse"). Another mathematician (Wehrung) proved this equality/property false [cite: 11, 12]. 

For the v10-class battery, this requires a specific module for **constructive object verification**. ## 4. Synthesis: Refining the `kill_pattern` Taxonomy

The gradient archaeology over these Substrate Type A cases allows us to formally refine the `f3_active_equal_violated` taxonomy for the Charon swarm. ### 4.1 Taxonomy Refinements

1.  **The Meta-Equality Violation (`f3_prior_art`):**
    Equality violations are not strictly mathematical. If the structural isomorphism between a proposed theorem and a verified theorem in the historical ledger reaches \(1.0\), an `f3` violation must be triggered on the axis of *novelty*. (Ref: Zarhin 2024) [cite: 3].

2.  **The Orphaned Branch Violation (`f3_gap_deductive`):**
    If the prover engine hits a gap in the proof, it is attempting to evaluate an equality between the hypothesized state \(S_n\) and the required state \(S_{n+1}\). If the transitional lemma is unproven, the equality is actively violated by the absence of a logical bridge. The battery must classify this not as a falsified theorem, but as an *unsubstantiated transition*. (Ref: Nyberg-Brodda 2026; Angelini 2025; Karp 2025) [cite: 5, 7, 10].

3.  **The Constructive Property Violation (`f3_hypothesis_failure`):**
    When a counterexample is proposed, it acts as a constructive witness. If the witness itself fails to hold the properties claimed by the author (e.g., lack of a quasi-inverse), the `f3` violation occurs at the instantiation level. The battery must test constructive objects for all claimed properties *before* evaluating their impact on the broader theorem. (Ref: Herrmann 2024) [cite: 11].
