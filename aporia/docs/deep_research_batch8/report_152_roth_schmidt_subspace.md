# Deep Research Report #152: Roth-Schmidt Subspace Theorem Empirics

**Target Agent:** Ergon
**Date:** 2026-04-25
**Front:** Diophantine approximation

## 1. Problem Statement

Roth's theorem (1955, Fields Medal) states that for any algebraic irrational α and any ε > 0, there exists C(α, ε) > 0 with |α − p/q| > C(α, ε) / q^(2+ε) for all rationals p/q. The exponent 2 is sharp (Liouville gives ≥ d for degree d, Thue-Siegel improved to roughly d/2 + 1, Roth pinned it at 2). Schmidt's subspace theorem (1972, Acta Math.) generalizes to simultaneous approximation in n variables: the set of integer vectors x ∈ Z^n satisfying a system of linear-form inequalities ∏|L_i(x)| < |x|^(−ε) lies in a finite union of proper rational subspaces of Q^n.

Empirical question: for a sample of algebraic α drawn from LMFDB, how close do best-rational approximations actually come to the Roth bound? Is convergence to exponent μ(α) = 2 fast or slow? For triples of algebraic numbers, do best simultaneous approximations cluster on a small number of subspaces (consistent with Schmidt) or spread broadly (suggesting the bound is loose at scale)?

## 2. Literature

- **Roth 1955** (*Mathematika*): exponent-2 theorem, Fields Medal 1958.
- **Schmidt 1972** (*Acta Math.*): subspace theorem for simultaneous approximation.
- **Schmidt 1980** book *Diophantine Approximation* (LNM 785): canonical exposition.
- **Bombieri-Vaaler 1983**: effective subspace bounds via height theory and geometry of numbers.
- **Evertse-Schlickewei-Schmidt 2002** (*Annals*): explicit upper bound on number of subspaces, polynomial in n and the rank of the multiplicative group.
- **Corvaja-Zannier 2002**: integral-point applications of subspace to S-unit equations and curves.
- **Adamczewski-Bugeaud 2007**: irrationality measures of automatic numbers via subspace.

## 3. LMFDB Data

- **`nf_fields`**: number-field generators α as roots of `coeffs` polynomial; columns `degree`, `disc_abs`, `coeffs`, `signature`. ~1.2M fields.
- **`mf_newforms` / `mf_hecke_cc`**: a_n sequences for modular forms; Hecke quotients a_p / p^((k−1)/2) lie in [−2, 2] and their Diophantine properties (rational vs algebraic vs Sato-Tate distributed) provide a secondary test set.
- **`nf_fields_subfields`**: lets us avoid trivial subfield duplication when sampling distinct α.
- Sage workhorse: `RealField(2000)(alpha).continued_fraction(partial_convergents=True)` extracts (p_k, q_k) at 2000-bit precision; LLL via `pari.qflll` for the Schmidt simultaneous-approximation lattice.

## 4. Test Design

**Step 1.** Sample 500 algebraic α from `nf_fields` with `degree` ∈ {2, 3, 4, 5} and `disc_abs` < 10^4, stratified ~125 per degree. Pick a real embedding via `signature` (require r1 ≥ 1).

**Step 2.** Compute α to 2000 bits. Extract first 50 continued-fraction convergents p_k / q_k. For each k, compute log|α − p_k/q_k| and log q_k.

**Step 3.** Estimate exponent μ(α) = limsup_k (−log|α − p_k/q_k|) / log q_k via robust regression on the upper envelope of the (log q_k, −log|α − p_k/q_k|) cloud. Roth predicts μ = 2 + o(1).

**Step 4.** Schmidt block: pick 100 triples (α_1, α_2, α_3) of bounded height H ≤ 50. For each, run LLL on the lattice generated by (1, α_1, α_2, α_3) scaled to precision N; record short vectors v with ∏|L_i(v)| < |v|^(−0.1). Schmidt predicts they lie in O(1) proper subspaces; record subspace count and density.

**Step 5.** Null: for each α, generate a transcendental control β = α + 10^(−100) · (random Liouville tail); confirm μ(β) explodes (Liouville numbers have μ = ∞).

## 5. Falsification

- **Roth confirmed:** μ(α) ∈ [1.95, 2.10] for ≥ 95% of α tested. Expected and uninteresting per se, but calibrates the pipeline.
- **Anomaly candidate:** any specific α with μ > 2.3 from finite convergent data is publishable — almost certainly an irrationality-measure record analogous to Apéry's μ(ζ(3)) ≤ 5.5 or recent improvements on log 2, π. Cross-check against Bugeaud's tables before claiming novelty.
- **Strong kill:** Schmidt subspace count grows linearly in sample size rather than saturating → bug in LLL setup or misreading of the theorem; test is invalid until repaired.
- **Null sanity:** transcendental Liouville-tail controls must show μ → ∞; otherwise precision is insufficient and convergent extraction is truncated too early.
