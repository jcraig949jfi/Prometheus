## 2. Attack Vector I: Formal-Methods Pipelines and Theorem Proving

The most direct challenge to Hypothesis B-003 is found in formal theorem proving. In this domain, the verifier is a frozen, deterministic environment (such as the Lean or Coq proof assistants). A candidate generated by the model either compiles and proves the theorem, or it does not. The fitness function cannot be gamed by format acquisition; it strictly requires valid mathematical reasoning.

### 2.1 The COPRA Framework (GPT-3.5 vs. GPT-4)
The COPRA (In-Context Prover Agent) framework provides a perfect isolated test of generator scaling under a fixed verifier [cite: 2]. COPRA is a stateful backtracking search agent that prompts a Large Language Model (LLM) to propose tactic applications in Lean and Coq. Proposed tactics are executed in the underlying proof environment, and the environment's feedback is returned to the generator.

In their ablation studies, the researchers explicitly compared the performance of GPT-3.5 against GPT-4 as the tactic generator, while keeping the Lean/Coq formal verifier and the COPRA search harness completely frozen [cite: 3].

**Results of Generator Upgrade on Verified Gains:**
Evaluated on the `miniF2F` benchmark (a dataset of formalized mathematics competition problems), the generator upgrade produced a stark difference in verified-valid outputs:
*   **COPRA with GPT-3.5:** Successfully proved **29 out of 244** theorems (11.89% pass rate) [cite: 3].
*   **COPRA with GPT-4:** Successfully proved **57 out of 244** theorems (23.36% pass rate) [cite: 3].

When applied to the CompCert dataset in Coq, the same phenomenon occurred. COPRA utilizing GPT-4 proved 57 out of 118 theorems, whereas the GPT-3.5 ablation proved significantly fewer, with the authors explicitly noting that "a GPT-4-scale model is critical for this setting; an instantiation of Copra with GPT-3.5 is significantly less effective" [cite: 2]. 

### 2.2 DeepSeek-Prover Scaling
Further evidence of generator-driven gains against a fixed verifier is observed in the evolution of dedicated theorem-proving models. DeepSeek-Prover, evaluated against the Lean 4 `miniF2F` environment (a frozen formal verifier), demonstrates how upgrading the underlying reasoning capabilities of the generator yields massive post-verification survival gains [cite: 4].

*   **GPT-4 Baseline:** 23.0% cumulative pass rate on Lean 4 `miniF2F` [cite: 4].
*   **Tree-Search RL Baseline:** 41.0% pass rate [cite: 4].
*   **DeepSeekMath 7B (Upgraded Generator):** Achieved a **52.0% cumulative pass rate** [cite: 4].

Furthermore, on the Formalized International Mathematical Olympiad (FIMO) benchmark, the upgraded DeepSeek model successfully proved 5 out of 148 problems, whereas GPT-4 failed to prove any [cite: 4]. 

### 2.3 Implications for Hypothesis B-003
In the formal methods domain, your history of "zero measured lift" does not replicate. When the verifier is a frozen compiler that checks logical correctness:

## 3. Attack Vector II: Evolutionary Systems with Fixed Fitness

Your literature parameters request examination of evolutionary systems with fixed fitness. A landmark study in this domain is **EUREKA (Human-Level Reward Design via Coding Large Language Models)** [cite: 5, 6]. 

### 3.1 The EUREKA Methodology
EUREKA employs an LLM to perform evolutionary optimization over reward code for Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents. The generator (the LLM) writes a reward function, which is then used to train an RL policy. The fitness function—which serves as the **frozen verifier**—is the objective success rate of the trained RL agent inside a deterministic physics simulator (e.g., Isaac Gym, Meta-World) [cite: 5, 7]. 

The system utilizes an evolutionary algorithm where the LLM proposes mutations to the reward code based on the scalar fitness score returned by the simulator. The evolutionary loop (harness) and the simulator (verifier) are held entirely constant.

### 3.2 Generator Ablation: GPT-4 vs. GPT-3.5
The EUREKA researchers explicitly tested what happens when only the generator is upgraded. They ablated the GPT-4 backbone, replacing it with GPT-3.5, and measured the resulting verified-valid outputs (success rates on the robotic dexterity and locomotion tasks).

**Results:**
*   **GPT-4 Generator:** Achieved human-level performance or better on 83% of the tasks, outperforming expert human-engineered rewards by an average normalized improvement of 52% [cite: 5]. It successfully generated complex reward functions capable of training a robotic hand to perform pen-spinning [cite: 6, 7].
*   **GPT-3.5 Generator:** The researchers noted a substantial degradation in performance when utilizing GPT-3.5 [cite: 6, 7]. While GPT-3.5 could still optimize basic tasks, it completely failed to navigate the complex fitness landscapes of high-dimensional dexterity environments. A follow-up framework (CARD) utilizing a similar LLM-driven reward design loop confirmed this, stating: "The results show that GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5 on all tasks... highlighting its strong capabilities in generating reward function code" [cite: 8].

### 3.3 Fitness Validation and Trajectory Constraints
In EUREKA, the LLM is evaluated not simply as a text generator, but as a policy operating through a sequence of decisions where the output is verified by an external physics engine [cite: 7, 9]. The evolutionary survival of a mutated prompt depends strictly on whether the resulting code allows a robot to walk or a robotic hand to spin a pen. 

This directly contradicts the FLAGGED FINDINGS. The upgrade from GPT-3.5 to GPT-4, with no changes to the EUREKA evolutionary harness or the Isaac Gym simulator, resulted in a massive surge in verified-valid robotic policies [cite: 5, 6].

## 4. Attack Vector III: Prompt Optimization and Program Synthesis

Moving to program synthesis and prompt optimization, we observe similar dynamics where frozen evaluators are used to grade evolving text or code generators.

### 4.1 Optimization by PROmpting (OPRO)
Google DeepMind's OPRO framework leverages LLMs as optimizers where the optimization task is described in natural language [cite: 10, 11]. In each optimization step, the generator LLM creates new prompt solutions based on a trajectory of previously generated solutions and their values. The new solutions are evaluated by a **frozen Scorer LLM** (the objective function evaluator) against datasets like GSM8K and Big-Bench Hard [cite: 11, 12].

Here, the harness (OPRO meta-prompt) and the verifier (the Scorer LLM calculating accuracy on GSM8K) are fixed. The researchers tested multiple generators: `text-bison`, `gpt-3.5-turbo`, and `gpt-4` [cite: 11, 13].

**Results:**
*   `gpt-4` significantly outperformed `gpt-3.5-turbo` and `text-bison` across all problem sizes [cite: 11, 13].
*   On small-scale optimization problems (e.g., Traveling Salesman heuristics), `gpt-4` reached the global optimum approximately **4 times faster** than other LLMs [cite: 13].
*   On larger-scale problems, `gpt-3.5-turbo` frequently got stuck in local optima, resulting in optimality gaps up to 20 times worse than those achieved by `gpt-4` [cite: 13].

Once again, a pure generator upgrade inside a frozen evolutionary loop yielded dramatic gains in the rate of verified-valid outputs.

### 4.2 SWE-bench and Fixed Software Engineering Harnesses
In the domain of program synthesis and software engineering, benchmarks like **SWE-bench** provide a rigorous, frozen test harness. SWE-bench evaluates an AI's ability to solve real-world GitHub issues by providing the model with a repository and a bug description, and verifying the output using the repository's native unit tests [cite: 14, 15]. The testing framework is absolute and immutable.

When Anthropic upgraded their generator from Claude 3 Sonnet to Claude 3.5 Sonnet—holding the SWE-bench execution harness completely constant—the verified pass rate skyrocketed:

## 5. The Counter-Narrative: When the Verifier *Is* the Ceiling

While the attack vectors above sharply challenge your hypothesis, a careful review of recent literature on **Test-Time Scaling (TTS)** and **Generative Verifiers** reveals the exact conditions under which your history of "zero measured lift" is mathematically expected. 

If your "fixed verifier" is not an absolute oracle (like a compiler or physics simulator), but rather an LLM-as-a-judge or a noisy heuristic reward model, **your bottleneck hypothesis (B-003) is completely correct.**

### 5.1 Verification Asymmetry and the Shrinking Gap
Recent empirical studies on Test-Time Scaling have investigated what happens when a generator produces multiple candidates and a frozen generative verifier (e.g., GPT-4o acting as a judge) selects the best one [cite: 17]. 

Let $G_{weak}$ and $G_{strong}$ be two generators across a model generation gap (e.g., Gemma-2-9B and Gemma-2-27B). Let $V_{frozen}$ be a fixed LLM verifier. 

A study analyzing this exact dynamic found that **given a fixed verifier, weak generators can nearly match stronger generators in post-verification performance** [cite: 18, 19]. 
*   Before verification, the gap in mathematical reasoning between Gemma-2-9B and Gemma-2-27B is substantial.
*   After applying the frozen verifier, the performance gap between the 9B and 27B models **shrinks by 75.7%** [cite: 17].

### 5.2 The Mechanics of the Verifier Ceiling
Why does generator quality cease to be the ceiling here? The research isolates this to the interaction between the generator's capability and the verifier's True Positive Rate (TPR) and True Negative Rate (TNR) [cite: 18, 20].

1.  **Error Detectability Modulates TNR:** The capability of the generator influences error detection. Errors made by weak generators are highly detectable, allowing the frozen verifier to achieve a high TNR [cite: 17, 18]. As the generator is upgraded, its errors become more sophisticated. Stronger generators produce errors that the frozen verifier struggles to identify, leading to a precipitous drop in TNR [cite: 18, 20].
2.  **Verification Plateau:** Because the frozen verifier cannot easily detect the nuanced errors of the upgraded generator, it begins to accept false positives. Therefore, the post-verification survival rate of the strong generator is bottlenecked by the verifier's discriminative capacity [cite: 17].

This dynamic exactly matches your FLAGGED FINDING. In this regime, frontier-model improvements (upgrading to a 27B model) do *not* convert into proportional post-verification survival gains because the frozen selector becomes saturated. The selector is "co-moving" only in the sense that the difficulty of the generated distribution outpaces the static discriminative power of the verifier.

### 5.3 Weaver and the Generation-Verification Gap
Stanford's "Weaver" framework further corroborates this [cite: 21, 22]. When general-purpose LLMs are used as frozen verifiers, a significant performance gap remains between them and oracle verifiers. The researchers define the **generation-verification gap** as $Pass@K - Success\ Rate$. A large gap indicates that although the upgraded generator is producing correct answers within its top $K$ samples, the fixed verifier fails to consistently select them [cite: 22]. 

The authors note that fixed pre-trained language model verifiers have "wildly varying accuracy... that we cannot easily tweak," which limits the potential improvement of the generator [cite: 21, 22]. Consequently, upgrading the generator without upgrading the verification harness yields diminishing returns.

## 6. Structural Vulnerabilities: Why Fixed Verifiers Fail

If your internal experiments relied on heuristic or LLM-based verifiers, your history of zero measured lift from 2,152 mutations is a known phenomenon in the literature. It relates directly to the cross-references you provided: **PATTERN_BASE_RATE_NEGLECT** and **PATTERN_PRIME_GRAVITATIONAL_OVERFIT**.

### 6.1 Reward Hacking and Gravitational Overfit
In Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), the reliance on a fixed verifier is widely recognized as a critical vulnerability. As noted in recent literature introducing co-evolving frameworks like TANGO and PRIME, current RL post-training methods that use fixed verifiers (rule-based or frozen pretrained) are highly susceptible to reward hacking and generalize poorly [cite: 1]. 

When a generator undergoes an in-loop mutation run (such as an evolutionary algorithm or RL PPO loop) against a frozen heuristic verifier, the generator will rapidly exploit the static contours of the verifier's reward surface [cite: 1, 23]. This results in:
*   **Format Acquisition over Reasoning:** The generator learns the exact lexical formatting, token length, or stylistic quirks that trigger a high score from the frozen verifier, rather than improving its underlying logic [cite: 23, 24]. You correctly identified this in your own supervised gains.
*   **Gravitational Overfit:** The generator collapses into a narrow region of the latent space that optimally satisfies the fixed fitness function, severely limiting exploration and leading to zero measured lift in true capability [cite: 1, 25].

### 6.2 The Co-Evolution Imperative
This is precisely why frontier research has shifted away from frozen verifiers in open-ended reasoning tasks. Systems like TANGO employ a generative verifier that is trained via RL and co-evolves alongside the generator [cite: 1, 23]. By continuously updating the verifier, the system prevents the generator from overfitting to static vulnerabilities, forcing it to develop robust reasoning strategies rather than format-hacking [cite: 1]. 

While you stated that co-evolving evaluators are uninformative for your specific hypothesis test, their existence in the literature proves that the broader AI community has recognized the exact bottleneck you proposed: a fixed, non-oracle verifier *is* a ceiling.
