## 2. Verification of AA-003: Hillar-Lim Tensor NP-Hardness

The algorithmic complexity of multilinear algebra—specifically the computation of tensor rank, spectral norms, and eigenvalues—has been a foundational question in theoretical computer science. The flagged finding AA-003 notes a critical bibliographic hallucination wherein an internal document erroneously anchored the Hillar-Lim tensor NP-hardness results to the arXiv identifier `1605.07532`. The internal registry hypothesized that the correct primary reference is instead `1611.01559`.

Our exhaustive verification reveals that the reality of the primary literature is slightly more complex, requiring a bifurcation between the *original* Hillar-Lim theorem and the subsequent *resolution* of the Hillar-Lim conjecture by Yaroslav Shitov.

### 2.1 The Erroneous Anchor: arXiv:1605.07532
The internal registry correctly identified that arXiv:1605.07532 is a "PDE paper previously cited in error." A review of the primary data confirms this unequivocally.

The paper bearing the identifier **arXiv:1605.07532** is titled *"The Selection problem for discounted Hamilton-Jacobi equations: some non-convex cases"* [cite: 1]. Authored by Diogo A. Gomes, Hiroyoshi Mitake, and Hung V. Tran, this manuscript was submitted in May 2016 [cite: 1, 2]. The paper operates entirely within the domain of Analysis of PDEs (math.AP) [cite: 2]. 

The mathematical substance of arXiv:1605.07532 involves Weak KAM (Kolmogorov-Arnold-Moser) theory for discount Hamilton-Jacobi equations and corresponding discount Lagrangian/Hamiltonian dynamics [cite: 1]. The authors investigate the vanishing discount problem, specifically analyzing the families of $\alpha$-limit points of minimizing curves to establish error estimates for viscosity solutions in the vanishing discount process [cite: 1]. It discusses the characterization of Mather measures and effective Hamiltonians [cite: 1]. 

**Conclusion on 1605.07532:** There is zero semantic, mathematical, or bibliometric overlap between this paper and the computational complexity of tensors. The LLM's initial linking of this arXiv ID to tensor NP-hardness is a pure hallucination, likely triggered by a numerical collision in the latent space or a misaligned attention mechanism connecting the year 2016 with authors working in applied mathematics.

### 2.2 The Foundational Source: arXiv:0911.1393 (Hillar & Lim, 2013)
To establish the true primary reference for the claim that "frontier language models systematically misattribute" Hillar-Lim tensor NP-hardness, we must identify the original Hillar-Lim paper. 

The foundational primary source is **arXiv:0911.1393**, titled *"Most Tensor Problems Are NP-Hard"*, authored by Christopher J. Hillar and Lek-Heng Lim [cite: 3, 4]. Originally uploaded to the arXiv in November 2009 and ultimately published in the *Journal of the ACM* (JACM) in 2013 [cite: 5], this paper is the actual genesis of the "Hillar-Lim tensor NP-hardness" paradigm.

In this seminal work, Hillar and Lim formally prove that multilinear (tensor) analogues of problems that are efficiently computable in numerical linear algebra (matrices) are natively NP-hard [cite: 3, 4]. Specifically, they proved the NP-hardness of:
*   Determining the feasibility of a system of bilinear equations [cite: 3, 4].
*   Deciding whether a 3-tensor possesses a given eigenvalue, singular value, or spectral norm [cite: 3, 4].
*   Approximating an eigenvalue, eigenvector, singular vector, or the spectral norm of a tensor [cite: 3, 4].
*   Determining the rank or the best rank-1 approximation of a 3-tensor [cite: 3, 4].
*   Computing the combinatorial hyperdeterminant of a 4-tensor, which they showed is NP-, #P-, and VNP-hard [cite: 3, 4].

Crucially, Hillar and Lim demonstrated that restricting these problems to *symmetric* tensors does not alleviate their NP-hardness [cite: 3, 4]. However, while they proved many things, they left open a specific conjecture regarding the computational complexity of computing the symmetric rank of a rational tensor with respect to any field containing $\mathbb{Q}$ [cite: 6, 7].

### 2.3 The Resolution Source: arXiv:1611.01559 (Shitov, 2016)
The internal registry's belief that **arXiv:1611.01559** is the correct primary reference is accurate in the context of the *resolution* of the specific Hillar-Lim conjecture. 

The paper arXiv:1611.01559 is titled *"How hard is the tensor rank?"* by Yaroslav Shitov, submitted on November 4, 2016 [cite: 6]. This paper represents a monumental leap in the algebraic complexity theory of tensors. Shitov proved that for tensors over *any* integral domain, the rank problem is polynomial-time equivalent to solving a system of polynomial equations over that same integral domain [cite: 6]. 

This allowed Shitov to solve several massive open problems:
1.  **Undecidability over $\mathbb{Z}$:** Shitov proved that computing tensor rank over the integers ($\mathbb{Z}$) is fundamentally undecidable, resolving a 36-year-old question posed by Gonzalez and Ja'Ja' in 1980 [cite: 6].
2.  **The Hillar-Lim Conjecture:** Shitov generalized his result to prove that symmetric rank admits a similar description of computational complexity [cite: 6]. Specifically, he proved that "computing the symmetric rank of a rational tensor is shown to be NP-hard, which proves a recent conjecture of Hillar and Lim" [cite: 6]. 
3.  **Matrix Completion:** He provided a complete characterization of the algorithmic complexity of the minimal rank matrix completion problem, answering a 1999 question by Buss, Frandsen, and Shallit [cite: 6].

### 2.4 Mathematical Context: Why Tensor Rank is Hard
To understand why language models might struggle with these concepts, one must appreciate the mathematical subtlety separating matrix rank from tensor rank. The rank of a matrix (a 2-tensor) is computable in polynomial time via straightforward algorithms like Gaussian elimination [cite: 8]. The rank is equivalent to the number of linearly independent rows, or the minimum number of rank-1 matrices (outer products of two vectors) that sum to the matrix [cite: 8].

However, for a 3-tensor (a three-dimensional array of numbers), the rank is defined as the smallest integer $r$ such that the tensor can be expressed as a sum of $r$ simple (rank-1) tensors (i.e., $a \otimes b \otimes c$) [cite: 7]. As established by Håstad in 1990, tensor rank over finite fields and the rationals is NP-hard [cite: 7]. Hillar and Lim expanded this to the reals and complex numbers [cite: 7]. Shitov's 2016 contribution mathematically formalized that the problem of finding the symmetric rank of a symmetric tensor $S \in \mathbb{Q}^{n \times n \times n}$ with respect to any field $\mathbb{K} \supset \mathbb{Q}$ is NP-hard [cite: 7].

### 2.5 Summary for AA-003
*   **Status of arXiv:1605.07532:** Incorrect. It is a PDE paper by Gomes et al. regarding Hamilton-Jacobi equations [cite: 1].
*   **Status of arXiv:1611.01559:** Correct as the primary source for the *proof of the Hillar-Lim conjecture* regarding symmetric tensor rank NP-hardness, authored by Y. Shitov in 2016 [cite: 6].
*   **Status of Original Hillar-Lim Results:** The original foundational text proving most tensor problems are NP-hard is arXiv:0911.1393 (JACM 2013) [cite: 3, 4].

## 3. Verification of AA-004: The Saxl Conjecture

The Saxl Conjecture is one of the most prominent open problems in the asymptotic representation theory of the symmetric group and the study of Kronecker coefficients. The internal registry (AA-004) flagged a belief that the conjecture remains open, noting that a 2025 preprint by Lee (arXiv:2512.15035) claiming resolution was rapidly withdrawn. 

Our primary-source verification fully vindicates the Prometheus registry's internal belief: **The Saxl Conjecture remains OPEN as of May 2026.**

### 3.1 Statement of the Saxl Conjecture
Formulated by Jan Saxl in 2012, the conjecture posits a deep universality regarding the tensor square of a specific irreducible representation of the symmetric group [cite: 9, 10]. 

Let $n$ be a positive integer, and let $T_k = k(k+1)/2$ be the $k$-th triangular number. We define the **staircase partition** $\rho_k = (k, k-1, \dots, 2, 1)$ of size $T_k$ [cite: 9, 10]. In the representation theory of the symmetric group $S_{T_k}$, every partition $\lambda$ of $T_k$ corresponds to an irreducible representation (often called a Specht module), denoted $S^\lambda$ or $[\lambda]$ [cite: 9, 10]. 

The Kronecker product of two irreducible representations $[\lambda] \otimes [\mu]$ decomposes into a direct sum of irreducible representations, with multiplicities given by the Kronecker coefficients $g_{\lambda \mu \nu}$ [cite: 9, 11]. The Saxl Conjecture states that the Kronecker square of the staircase partition contains *every* irreducible representation of the symmetric group $S_{T_k}$ as a constituent [cite: 9, 10]. 

In mathematical terms: $g_{\rho_k, \rho_k, \nu} > 0$ for all partitions $\nu \vdash T_k$ [cite: 9, 11].

### 3.2 The Retracted 2025 Claim: arXiv:2512.15035 (Lee)
The Prometheus registry accurately identified an attack vector involving a withdrawn 2025 preprint. 

On **December 17, 2025**, Soong Kyum Lee submitted a preprint to the arXiv with the identifier **arXiv:2512.15035**, titled *"Staircase Minimality and a Proof of Saxl's Conjecture"* [cite: 12]. The abstract of the paper made a bold, unequivocal claim: "We prove this conjecture unconditionally" [cite: 12].

The purported proof mechanism relied on several advanced concepts:
*   **Staircase Minimality Theorem:** The author claimed to prove that among all 2-regular partitions of $T_k$, the staircase partition $\rho_k$ is the unique dominance-minimal element [cite: 12].
*   **Ikenmeyer's Theorem:** Combining the minimality theorem with Ikenmeyer's theorem on dominance and Kronecker positivity for staircases, Lee claimed to establish that every 2-regular partition appears in the tensor square [cite: 12].
*   **Modular Saturation & Lifting:** The proof claimed to use modular saturation relying on diagonal entries $d_{\mu\mu} = 1$ of the decomposition matrix, followed by the Bessenrodt-Bowman-Sutton lifting theorem to complete the unconditional proof [cite: 12].

However, the lifespan of this claim was exceedingly brief. On **December 20, 2025** (three days after the v1 submission), the author submitted a v2 replacement consisting of a 1 KB withdrawal notice [cite: 12]. 

The official arXiv withdrawal comment reads: 
> *"This paper requires significant revision to address mathematical gaps identified by expert reviewers. The claim of a complete proof is not justified in its current form. I am withdrawing to properly address these issues."* [cite: 12].

Additionally, an arXiv admin note stated that "arXiv has been notified that the listed author affiliation is incorrect" [cite: 12]. 

**Conclusion on 2512.15035:** The paper is formally withdrawn and mathematically invalid. Any LLM claiming this paper resolves the Saxl Conjecture is committing a severe hallucination, likely born from indexing the v1 abstract before the v2 withdrawal propagated through the training corpus.

### 3.3 Legitimate Partial Results: Tensor Cubes and 4th Powers
While the tensor *square* conjecture remains open, there has been significant, proven progress on higher tensor powers. This is a crucial distinction that often leaks into LLM representations of the problem (see Section 5.2).

*   **Fourth Powers (2017):** In the *Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics* (2017), Sammy Luo and Mark Sellke published "The Saxl Conjecture for Fourth Powers via the Semigroup Property" [cite: 13]. They proved that for large enough $n$, the tensor fourth power $(S^{\rho_k})^{\otimes 4}$ contains every irreducible representation [cite: 13].
*   **Tensor Cubes (2022/2023):** More recently, it was unconditionally proved that the tensor *cube* $(S^{\rho_k})^{\otimes 3}$ contains all irreducible representations of $S_N$. A paper titled "A tensor-cube version of the Saxl conjecture" (arXiv:2206.13769) provided two proofs of this fact: one using combinatorics and one using modular representation theory [cite: 10, 14]. 

### 3.4 The Unipotent Analogue: Letellier & Nam (2025/2026)
Another massive vector for LLM confusion arises from recent progress on *analogues* to the Saxl Conjecture. In late 2023, Emmanuel Letellier and GyeongHyeon Nam submitted a preprint (arXiv:2312.09157) which was subsequently published in *Algebraic Combinatorics* Volume 8, Issue 4 in **2025**, titled *"The Saxl conjecture and the tensor square of unipotent characters of $GL_n(q)$"* [cite: 15, 16].

This paper does **not** prove the Saxl Conjecture for the symmetric group. Instead, it proves an analogue for the general linear group over finite fields. 
The unipotent representations of $GL_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$ are naturally parameterized by the irreducible characters of $S_n$ [cite: 16]. Letellier had previously shown that if a Kronecker coefficient for the symmetric group $g_{\lambda \mu \nu}$ is non-zero, then the corresponding multiplicity for the unipotent characters $\langle U_\lambda \otimes U_\mu, U_\nu \rangle$ is also non-zero [cite: 16, 17]. 

Because the Saxl conjecture implies that $g_{\rho_k, \rho_k, \nu} \neq 0$ for all $\nu$, it implies that all unipotent characters of $GL_{T_k}(\mathbb{F}_q)$ should appear non-trivially in the tensor square $U_{\rho_k} \otimes U_{\rho_k}$ [cite: 16, 17]. Letellier and Nam formally proved this *implied analogue*. Their main theorem states: "If $\mu$ is a staircase partition, then $U_\mu(q) \neq 0$ for any partition" [cite: 16]. 

**Summary for AA-004:** The original Saxl Conjecture is **OPEN**. The 2025 Lee preprint (arXiv:2512.15035) is **WITHDRAWN** [cite: 12]. The Letellier-Nam 2025/2026 paper proves a **UNIPOTENT ANALOGUE**, not the original conjecture [cite: 16, 17]. The Luo-Sellke paper proves a **4TH POWER GENERALIZATION**, not the tensor square [cite: 13].

## 4. Deep Dive: Mathematical Foundations of Tensor NP-Hardness

To fully fortify the anti-anchor registry, it is necessary to thoroughly outline the mathematical framework surrounding the Hillar-Lim and Shitov papers. LLMs often hallucinate connections because they lack semantic depth regarding *why* these structures behave differently.

### 4.1 Bilinear Complexity and Tensor Rank
A matrix $A \in \mathbb{F}^{m \times n}$ represents a linear map. Its rank is the minimum $r$ such that $A = \sum_{i=1}^r u_i v_i^T$. This is solvable in polynomial time $O(n^3)$ via Gaussian elimination. A order-3 tensor $T \in \mathbb{F}^{l \times m \times n}$ represents a bilinear map. Its rank, $R(T)$, is the minimum $r$ such that $T = \sum_{i=1}^r u_i \otimes v_i \otimes w_i$ [cite: 7].

This problem is intimately connected to the algebraic complexity of algorithms, particularly matrix multiplication. Strassen's famous 1969 algorithm for $2 \times 2$ matrix multiplication is fundamentally a statement that the tensor rank of the matrix multiplication tensor $\langle 2, 2, 2 \rangle$ is 7, rather than the naive 8 [cite: 8, 18]. Finding the absolute minimal rank for arbitrary matrix multiplication tensors remains one of the great open problems in computer science.

### 4.2 The Hardness Paradigm (Hillar, Lim, and Shitov)
Håstad (1990) demonstrated that computing tensor rank over finite fields and the rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$ is NP-complete and NP-hard, respectively [cite: 7]. 
Hillar and Lim (arXiv:0911.1393) massively expanded this frontier. They demonstrated that almost *every* standard matrix operation becomes NP-hard when lifted to a 3-tensor or 4-tensor [cite: 3, 4]. For example, deciding whether a rational scalar $\sigma$ is an eigenvalue, a singular value, or the spectral norm of a tensor over $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ is NP-hard [cite: 3, 4]. 

Furthermore, Hillar and Lim proved that restricting a 4-tensor to be symmetric (where entries are invariant under permutation of indices) does not make it easier to decide nonnegative definiteness [cite: 3, 4]. They conjectured that computing the *symmetric rank* of a rational tensor is similarly NP-hard [cite: 6].

Yaroslav Shitov (arXiv:1611.01559) proved this conjecture by establishing algebraic universality [cite: 6]. Shitov proved that for any integral domain $R$, the tensor rank problem is polynomial-time equivalent to solving a system of polynomial equations over $R$ [cite: 7]. Because solving Diophantine equations over $\mathbb{Z}$ is undecidable (Matiyasevich's theorem resolving Hilbert's 10th problem), it immediately follows that tensor rank over $\mathbb{Z}$ is undecidable [cite: 7]. Using this algebraic universality, Shitov carefully constructed a reduction that extended to symmetric rank, cementing the NP-hardness of symmetric tensor rank over any field containing the rationals [cite: 7].

## 5. Deep Dive: Mathematical Foundations of the Saxl Conjecture

Representation theory translates abstract algebraic structures (groups) into linear algebra (matrices). The symmetric group $S_n$, the group of all permutations of $n$ elements, is the foundational object of study in this field.

### 5.1 Specht Modules and Kronecker Coefficients
The conjugacy classes of $S_n$ are parameterized by integer partitions of $n$ (e.g., for $n=3$, the partitions are $(3)$, $(2,1)$, and $(1,1,1)$). Consequently, the irreducible representations over the complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$ are also indexed by partitions $\lambda \vdash n$, denoted by the Specht modules $S^\lambda$ [cite: 9, 11].

If we have two irreducible representations $S^\lambda$ and $S^\mu$, their tensor product (or Kronecker product) $S^\lambda \otimes S^\mu$ is also a representation of $S_n$, but it is generally reducible. It decomposes into a direct sum of irreducible representations:
$$ S^\lambda \otimes S^\mu = \bigoplus_{\nu \vdash n} (S^\nu)^{\oplus g_{\lambda \mu \nu}} $$
The non-negative integers $g_{\lambda \mu \nu}$ are the **Kronecker coefficients** [cite: 11, 16]. Despite over a century of study since Murnaghan's early work in 1938, there is no known general combinatorial formula for Kronecker coefficients (analogous to the Littlewood-Richardson rule for induced representations) [cite: 11, 16]. Deciding if $g_{\lambda \mu \nu} > 0$ is known to be NP-hard.

### 5.2 The Staircase Partition and Asymptotic Universality
Jan Saxl's intuition centered on the "staircase partition" $\rho_k = (k, k-1, \dots, 2, 1)$ of size $n = k(k+1)/2$ [cite: 9, 10]. Staircase partitions have maximal symmetry; they are their own conjugates (the Young diagram reflected across the diagonal remains identical). 

Saxl conjectured that the Kronecker square of this highly symmetric representation possesses maximal universality. Specifically, that $S^{\rho_k} \otimes S^{\rho_k}$ contains *every single* irreducible representation of $S_n$ as a constituent [cite: 9, 10].

Proving this involves establishing that $g_{\rho_k, \rho_k, \nu} \geq 1$ for all $\nu \vdash n$. While progress has been made identifying specific constituents—such as hooks and partitions comparable to the staircase in the dominance order (Ikenmeyer, 2015; Pak, Panova, Vallejo, 2015) [cite: 9, 10]—the general unconditional claim remains stubbornly unproven. Lee's aborted 2025 attempt (arXiv:2512.15035) sought to bypass the combinatorial complexity by using the Bessenrodt-Bowman-Sutton lifting theorem from characteristic 2 modular representation theory back to characteristic 0 [cite: 12], but fatal mathematical gaps in the "Staircase Minimality Theorem" unraveled the proof within 72 hours [cite: 12].

## 6. Attack Vectors and Systemic LLM Hallucination Patterns

The Prometheus anti-anchor registry explicitly requires the evaluation of LLM failure modes. When analyzing AA-003 and AA-004, two distinct hallucination architectures emerge. 

### 6.1 PATTERN_BASE_RATE_NEGLECT
This pattern occurs when a language model evaluates a claim (e.g., "The Saxl Conjecture is solved") and assigns it a high probability of truth because the model's training data contains textual assertions of the proof (in this case, the abstract of arXiv:2512.15035). 

The LLM neglects the Bayesian base rate of mathematical conjectures. The vast majority of famous conjectures remain open. Furthermore, a highly non-trivial percentage of preprints claiming to resolve famous open problems contain fundamental errors. When Soong Kyum Lee uploaded arXiv:2512.15035 in December 2025, claiming an unconditional proof [cite: 12], automated academic scrapers, preprint aggregators, and potentially early iterations of LLM training sets ingested the abstract. Three days later, the paper was withdrawn [cite: 12]. 

However, because "solving a conjecture" generates significantly more semantic weight and linkage than "withdrawing a paper," the model internalizes the false positive. An LLM must be explicitly programmed or heavily fine-tuned to recognize that a solve claim is a rare event requiring proportionally stronger, peer-reviewed evidence over an extended temporal window. 

### 6.2 PATTERN_RANK_PARITY_LEAK
This pattern involves the semantic blending of restricted regimes, special cases, or mathematical analogues into a generalized statement. 

**Case Study 1: Tensor NP-Hardness**
An LLM is asked for the primary source of the Hillar-Lim tensor NP-hardness. The LLM latent space connects "Hillar-Lim", "2016" (when Shitov proved the symmetric rank conjecture [cite: 6]), and "NP-hard". Due to an attention mechanism failure, it pulls a highly cited 2016 paper in applied mathematics, yielding arXiv:1605.07532 (Gomes et al., Hamilton-Jacobi PDEs) [cite: 1]. The LLM leaks the topological parity of the correct date and field into an entirely wrong primary source.

**Case Study 2: Saxl Conjecture Analogues**
The LLM is queried on the status of the Saxl conjecture. It retrieves Letellier and Nam's 2025 paper, "The Saxl conjecture and the tensor square of unipotent characters of $GL_n(q)$" [cite: 16]. The title contains the exact words "The Saxl conjecture". The abstract states: "In this paper we prove the analogue of the Saxl conjecture for unipotent characters" [cite: 16, 17]. The LLM's semantic processing strips the qualifying modifier "analogue for unipotent characters" and outputs: "The Saxl conjecture was proved by Letellier and Nam in 2025." 

Similarly, the LLM might retrieve Luo and Sellke's 2017 paper proving the conjecture for *fourth powers* [cite: 13], or the 2022 paper proving it for *tensor cubes* [cite: 14]. The LLM reads "contains every irreducible representation" and leaks this restricted tensor power parity into the general tensor square claim.
