# Problem #180 — Beauville–Voisin Algebraic Chow Ring Structure on K3 Surfaces

**Domain:** higher-dimensional algebraic geometry / motivic theory
**Substrate region:** operator behavior in K3-Picard-rank-≥8 structural region of motivic-cohomology space
**Doctrine anchors:** feedback_tensor_first, feedback_calibration_anchors_in_depth
**Pattern guards:** PATTERN_BASE_RATE_NEGLECT, PATTERN_CONDUCTOR_CONFOUND
**Date:** 2026-04-28

## 1. Problem Statement

Beauville and Voisin (2004) proved that for any projective K3 surface X, the cycle classes c_1(L_i)·c_1(L_j) for line bundles L_i, L_j and the second Chern class c_2(T_X) all lie in a single one-dimensional subgroup of the Chow group CH_0(X) of zero-cycles, generated by the class of any point lying on a rational curve. This "distinguished" zero-cycle class o_X is conjectured to organize the entire algebraic Chow ring CH^*(X)_Q multiplicatively. Beauville's splitting conjecture extends this to hyperkähler varieties: CH^*(X)_Q should admit a multiplicative bigrading compatible with the Bloch–Beilinson filtration, with graded pieces controlled by deformation type. The conjecture is verified in special cases (Hilbert schemes of K3s by Voisin and Vial; generalized Kummer varieties by Pavic–Shen–Yin; some Lehn–Lehn–Sorger–van Straten 8-folds), but the general statement, and even the K3 multiplicativity for Picard rank > 1 with subtle lattice structure, remains open.

## 2. Literature

- Beauville–Voisin, *On the Chow ring of a K3 surface* (J. Alg. Geom. 13, 2004) — the original o_X result.
- Beauville, *On the splitting of the Bloch–Beilinson filtration* (2007) — splitting conjecture for hyperkähler varieties.
- Voisin, *Chow rings and decomposition theorems for K3 surfaces and Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces* (Geom. Topol. 16, 2012).
- Voisin, *Chow rings, decomposition of the diagonal, and the topology of families* (Princeton, 2014) — multiplicative Chow–Künneth decomposition.
- Vial, *Pure motives with representable Chow groups* (2013), and *On the motive of some hyperKähler varieties* (J. reine angew. Math., 2017).
- Shen–Vial, *The Fourier transform for certain hyperkähler fourfolds* (Mem. AMS, 2016) — splitting verified for LLSvS 8-folds and Fano varieties of lines on cubic 4-folds.
- Pavic–Shen–Yin, *On O'Grady's generalized Franchetta conjecture* (2018) and follow-ups — splitting for relative cases.
- Huybrechts, *Lectures on K3 Surfaces* (Cambridge, 2016) — Chapter 12 Chow ring exposition.

## 4. Test Design

1. **Population pull.** Extract every K3 in `bhfs_*` (and any K3 transcendental-lattice table Mnemosyne surfaces) with Picard rank ρ ≥ 8. Record the Néron–Severi lattice Gram matrix, discriminant, and signature (1, ρ−1).
2. **Distinguished-class candidate count.** For each surface, enumerate primitive classes h with h^2 ≥ −2 up to a bounded degree window; compute the symmetric pairings {h_i · h_j} mod the lattice. Tally how many products c_1(L_i)·c_1(L_j) collapse onto the same zero-cycle class predicted by Beauville–Voisin (numerical proxy for the symbolic CH_0 collapse).
3. **Mukai-vector clustering.** Compute Mukai vectors v = (rank, c_1, ch_2 + rk·[pt]) for the polarization classes; cluster surfaces by Mukai-lattice orbit. Test whether the candidate-count distribution stratifies cleanly across orbits.
4. **Family stratification.** Tag each surface by family: Kummer, Shioda–Inose, elliptic with section, Mukai-model (degree 2g−2 for g ≤ 10), generic. Compute per-family means and variances of the candidate count.
5. **Operator profile export.** Push the (ρ, disc, signature, candidate-count, Mukai-orbit) tuples into the substrate's tensor as a new region; check whether known substrate operators (intersection-form curvature, lattice-discriminant gradient) align with the empirical Chow-collapse signal.

## 5. Falsification

- **Calibration anchors.** (a) Kummer K3 from a generic abelian surface — splitting is known; substrate must reproduce it. (b) Fermat quartic K3 — singular K3 with explicit transcendental lattice, splitting verified. (c) Generic projective K3 of degree 4 with ρ = 1 — reduces to Beauville–Voisin's theorem in its simplest form. Failure on any anchor invalidates the pipeline.
- **PATTERN_BASE_RATE_NEGLECT.** The candidate count must be normalized against a permutation null where line bundle pairings are randomized within the lattice. Without this null, large ρ inflates raw counts mechanically and the result is uninterpretable.
- **PATTERN_CONDUCTOR_CONFOUND.** Discriminant of the Néron–Severi lattice is the K3 analogue of conductor and correlates with both Picard rank and Mukai orbit. All cluster claims must be conditioned on disc(NS) bins, not pooled.
