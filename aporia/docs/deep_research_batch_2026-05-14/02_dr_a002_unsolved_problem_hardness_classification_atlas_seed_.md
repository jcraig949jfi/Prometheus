# PROJECT PROMETHEUS: PROBLEM-ATLAS PILOT
## Substrate Initialization Briefing

*   **Riemann Hypothesis (RH):** Research suggests the most promising modern pathways avoid traditional zero-finding, focusing instead on how often "Dirichlet polynomials" take unexpectedly large values. This shifts the problem from pure complex analysis to localized frequency tracking. 
*   **Goldbach Conjecture:** Evidence leans toward tackling "exceptional sets" (numbers that fail the conjecture) using "Chen primes" (numbers that are either prime or the product of two primes). By proving these exceptions are vanishingly rare, mathematics edges closer to a complete solution without hitting classical barriers.
*   **P vs NP:** It seems likely that traditional attempts to prove computational hardness (like analyzing individual logic gates) are permanently stalled by logical obstacles. New approaches like "hardness magnification" attempt to take tiny, known computational limits and mathematically amplify them to solve the larger problem, sidestepping old roadblocks.
*   **Hodge Conjecture:** Recent breakthroughs show that for highly specific, multi-dimensional geometric shapes (like "hyper-Kähler varieties"), the complex interplay between algebra and calculus perfectly aligns. Expanding these specialized cases to general geometry remains profoundly difficult.
*   **Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) Conjecture:** The evidence suggests that "p-adic" analysis—a parallel universe of arithmetic where numbers behave completely differently based on prime divisibility—offers the most rigorous way to connect a curve's algebraic points with its analytic equations.

## Substrate Doctrine and Hardness Taxonomy

Project Prometheus classifies unsolved mathematical problems not by their historical prestige, but by their topological obstruction signatures. To process these five pilot problems, the substrate utilizes an 8-dimensional Hardness Signature vector, evaluated continuously on a scale of [0.0, 1.0]. 

The dimensions are registered as follows:
1. **METHOD_GAP:** The distance between current analytic/algebraic machinery and the machinery theoretically required to resolve the problem.
2. **REPRESENTATION_GAP:** The degree to which the problem is currently formulated in a suboptimal mathematical language (e.g., attempting to solve an operator-theoretic problem using pure number theory).
3. **GLOBAL_OBSTRUCTION:** The presence of proven, formalized barriers to large classes of approaches (e.g., Relativization, Natural Proofs, the Parity Problem in sieve theory).
4. **EXACTNESS_BARRIER:** The difficulty in transitioning from asymptotic, "almost all" bounds to absolute, exact equalities for all integers/spaces.
5. **HIDDEN_PATHOLOGY:** The likelihood that counterexamples exist at scales computationally inaccessible to current brute-force verification (e.g., Skewes' number phenomena).
6. **COUPLED_DIFFICULTY:** The extent to which solving the problem requires simultaneously solving mathematically distinct parallel conjectures.
7. **NON_HEREDITARY_STRUCTURE:** The failure of the problem's invariants to pass cleanly from global spaces to local sub-spaces, or from infinite limits to finite truncations.
8. **CONCEPTUAL_ABSENCE:** The lack of a fundamental defining object or functor necessary to anchor the proof (e.g., the absence of a verified cohomology theory over $\mathbb{F}_1$).

Pursuant to HARD-5 doctrine, we refuse to collapse mathematically distinct invariants. The substrate registers "rank", "density", and "complexity" as fragmented, highly specific geometric coordinates.
