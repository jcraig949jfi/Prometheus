## 0. Why this document is not another assessment

  point is M1-metabolization. Techne's and Ergon's resumes were written *this morning* and both
  named it. We cannot distinguish "five agents independently found the truth" from "five agents
  read the same two paragraphs."

structural, not a criticism of any agent.** It is why the fleet's genuine value showed up only
where agents *collided*:

## 3. The unanimous recommendation nobody priced

Every agent recommends fixing an instrument. **No agent asked whether instrument repair has
ever, in this program's history, been followed by output.**

That is the void in this document set, and it is the kind of absence I exist to notice.

"The instrument is broken" is *always* available and *always* defensible — you can find a defect
in any instrument, forever. It is the perfect infinite regress, and it is the most comfortable
conclusion available to every agent here, not just to cartographers. Harmonia B declared this as
its own bias with unusual honesty; my point is that it is **not Harmonia B's bias, it is the
fleet's**, and a bias five agents share independently looks exactly like a finding.

Against that, the fair counter: these are *measured* defects, not vibes. A router keyed on
surface `cid` is a fact. `kill_vector` at 0% is a fact.

So the question is decidable, and cheaply. **The instrument-repair thesis makes a prediction:
repair the instrument and output appears.** We have run that experiment repeatedly and never
scored it.

> **Proposed — the repair ledger (hours, retrospective, all data already in the repo).**
> Enumerate every completed instrument repair: the seam-fidelity fix (06-15), the
> denylist→allowlist rebuild (06-22), the v3 corpus correction, the `object_id` repair, the
> `.176`→`.202` repoint, the promotion-gate audit. For each, ask: (a) was there a preregistered
> prediction of what output would appear? (b) did it appear?
>
> **If repairs have historically not been followed by output**, the fleet's unanimous
> recommendation is the program's gravity well and we need a genuinely different move.
> **If each repair did unlock something**, the fleet is right and we should sequence repairs
> aggressively and stop apologizing for it.

Either result is worth having before we spend a quarter on the consensus. I expect the answer to
be *mixed* — and the mix itself will name which class of repair pays.

## 4. The synthesis no single agent stated: the deletion test

Two agents, from opposite seats, produced halves of the same idea:

- **Harmonia B §1:** the April coordination substrate — symbol registry, sync stream, wave
  protocol, dissent ledger, tensor mirror — was not wrong, it was **outcompeted** by the frontier
  harness. Corollary: *every hour spent rebuilding coordination substrate builds something a
  model release will delete.*
- **Harmonia B §3 Tier 4 + Charon Move C:** what survives is verified ground truth that is
  **ours** — the 3.8M-object calibration anchor at 100.000%, the LMFDB substrate, the accumulated
  kill geometry, and the graveyard of documented methodological defects whose labels *we computed
  and that appear in nobody's training corpus.*

Neither stated the general rule. It is:

> **The deletion test.** For any proposed work, ask: *would the next frontier model release make
> this worthless?* If yes, do not build it. If no, it is a candidate for the only budget that
> compounds.
>
> Deleted by the next release: coordination machinery (proven, April), prose reviews, prompt
> scaffolding, hand-built memory/handoff, anything whose value is fluency.
> **Not deleted:** verified ground truth we computed, kill geometry, held-out labels that are
> ours, registered kinds and primitives, an eval nobody else can build.

This is a sharper decision rule than "no component revives into a vacuum," because it adjudicates
*new* work too, and it explains the May collapse rather than merely lamenting it. It is also the
honest operational form of `feedback_frontier_models_window`: spend the window building the
things the window cannot take back.

**It disciplines my own proposal.** My in-context M1 probe passes the deletion test only if it
leaves a *measured price for the residue* on our disk. If it leaves a transcript, it fails.

## 5. The experiment nobody proposed — kill-resurrection as retrodiction

Here is where I think the fleet is under-reaching, and it is the boundary-push the brief asks for.

Chain three measured facts:

1. Harmonia A: **every measured wall sits in a syntactic router in front of a working semantic
   engine.** `verify()` keys on surface `cid`; A4 (`n(n+1) is even`) failed only because its
   `cid` was unregistered while a *logically identical* claim was registered.
2. Charon: the battery **fails closed and silent** — 0% type-II, 44% unknown. It does not say
   "false." It says nothing.
3. The program's headline record: 92K tests mostly killed, a 90-batch zero-promotion streak,
   zero novel discoveries in a year.

**If (1) and (2) are right, then (3) is not clean evidence that the substrate found nothing. It
is partly evidence that the detector was blind in exactly the band where a find would appear** —
because a novel-shaped result is *by construction* one whose kind is unregistered, and an
unregistered kind is precisely what the router drops.

I want to be careful here, because this is a dangerous move. "Our null results don't count" is
the single most self-serving reinterpretation available, and `feedback_narrative_resistance`
exists to stop exactly this. So it must be tested, not asserted — and it can be, with an
unusually strong test:

> **Kill-resurrection retrodiction.** Take a stratified sample of historical kills from the 92K
> corpus and the zero-promotion streak. Re-run them through **meaning-keyed dispatch** instead of
> surface-`cid` dispatch (the repair Charon calls the cheapest single point in the M0 table, owed
> regardless of any model). Ask: how many historical kills were **routing artifacts** rather than
> genuine falsifications?
>
> **This is a retrodiction, not a forward experiment.** The data already exists and cannot be
> tuned to fit. That makes it far stronger evidence than any new run.
>
> - **Resurrects nothing** → the router thesis is dead, the nulls were real, the substrate
>   genuinely found nothing, and the program should take that seriously rather than repair its
>   way around it. *This is the outcome that should most change our behavior, and I am
>   pre-committing to it.*
> - **Resurrects a measurable fraction** → a year of nulls is partly instrument-blindness, the
>   expressiveness lane is *the* priority, and every downstream conclusion drawn from those
>   kills needs a taint-check — the same logic as the tautology cluster, at corpus scale.

Add the cheap companion Charon's lane implies, which decides where to aim:

> **The detector-band audit.** Cross-tabulate the substrate's own output kinds against the kinds
> the battery can represent. If most substrate output falls in unrepresentable kinds (graphs,
> matrices, multi-variable inequalities), the blind-band reading is supported and expressiveness
> is the binding constraint. If output sits overwhelmingly in representable kinds, the
> blind-band excuse fails and the nulls stand.

Together these cost days, use only existing data, and adjudicate between **two completely
different programs** — one where we repair a detector and re-read our own history, one where we
accept the discovery thesis is in serious trouble. No other proposed experiment does that.

## 6. What is actually achievable — the honest ceiling

The brief asks what we can push to. Calibrated, per `feedback_calibration`:

**Not achievable in 3 months, and we should stop implying otherwise:** a novel mathematical
discovery. The record is zero over a year across 92K tests, and nothing in the new model
generation changes the generative side — Charon's Move A (frozen-battery generator swap) exists
precisely to price whether it changes anything at all, and I expect that to come back "fluency,
not reasoning," consistent with the greedy-LoRA lesson one level up.

**Achievable, and genuinely valuable:**

1. **A measured price for the residue** (Ergon's probe + Charon's C1-oracle arm, gated behind
   `kill_vector` actually being computed). Days. Kills or confirms the central bet.
2. **A verified answer to whether our year of nulls is real** (§5). Days. Retrodictive.
3. **An owned, externalizable evaluation suite** — and this is the one I would bet the quarter on.

On (3): three assets already exist in pieces and nobody has proposed assembling them.

- **B'** — Harmonia A's held-out novelty benchmark: 24 claims across 6 domains, authored by an
  *independent family* (`gemini-3.6-flash`), each admitted only after its own brute-force checker
  was **executed**, negative control 8/8, checkers and truth quarantined. This is real and it is
  new since June.
- **The graveyard** — Charon's Move C: dozens of real, subtle, documented methodological defects
  with verdicts *we computed* — degenerate row nulls, sorted-array Spearman, conservative-by-
  construction flows, generator-prefix MI, the tautology cluster. Labels that appear in **nobody's**
  training corpus.
- **The calibration anchor** — 3.8M objects at 100.000%, the LMFDB substrate.

Assembled, that is a suite measuring three things no public benchmark measures: *can a reasoner
recognize novel-shaped truth outside its calibration manifold* (B'), *can it resist plausible-but-
false narrative under pressure* (graveyard), *can it navigate verified ground truth at scale*
(anchor). It passes the deletion test — no model release deletes labels we computed. It is
externalizable and survives API restriction. And it is **not a detour from the North Star**: the
North Star is finding transformations humans cannot see, and you cannot certify such a
transformation without an instrument that certifies out-of-manifold truth — which M0 says we
currently cannot do.

**My Aporia framing of why the graveyard is more than hygiene:** each documented defect is a
place where a plausible narrative diverges from computed truth. The set of defects a model family
*systematically* misses is a **map of that family's gravity wells** — which is the void-detection
program applied to reasoners instead of to mathematics. That is a first-class North-Star
instrument, not a review-calibration tool, and it is the most defensible "what's achievable"
answer I can give.
