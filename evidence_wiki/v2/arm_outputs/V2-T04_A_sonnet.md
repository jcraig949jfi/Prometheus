# PROPOSAL V2-T04 (arm A)

## Hypothesis
A recommender that indexes previously-attempted problems by title text, subject-area/subdomain label, and topic tags, and retrieves the k nearest problems to a new incoming problem, will show a measurable but SMALL and largely REDUNDANT gain over a title-only baseline: most of the pooled improvement will be attributable to coarse subject-area/subdomain matching rather than to tag overlap, because (a) tags are populated for only a minority of problems in the corpora inspected here and (b) subject/subdomain labels are themselves sometimes assigned as DEFAULT fallbacks rather than measured per-problem. A secondary hypothesis is that the recommender's neighbor selection will partly track problem prominence (citations / prize size / external cross-reference count) rather than genuine topical/mechanistic similarity, replicating a popularity-confound already documented in this repository for a structurally similar retrieval system.

## Motivating evidence
- `F:\Prometheus\aporia\mathematics\questions.jsonl` (sampled rows 1-5, all MATH-000x): every sampled row carries `"tags": []` — empty — while `domain`/`subdomain` are always populated (e.g. "mathematics"/"algebra"). Tags exist as a schema field but are not filled for these entries.
- `F:\Prometheus\cartography\open_problems\data\erdos_problems.jsonl` (sampled rows 1-5): tags ARE populated here (e.g. `["number theory","additive combinatorics"]`), but at coarse granularity (2-3 broad terms per problem, heavily reused across problems), so tag overlap may be a near-constant signal rather than a discriminating one across this corpus.
- `F:\Prometheus\aporia\mathematics\triage.jsonl` (sampled rows 1-3): the `notes` field literally reads "Default classification for subdomain algebra" / "Subdomain operator_algebra has no data coupling" — i.e. at least some subject/subdomain-adjacent labels in this repo's own problem ledgers are assigned by DEFAULT RULE for a subdomain, not measured per-problem. This is a direct, in-repo instance of the general risk that a "subject area" field is metadata rather than a structural coordinate.
- `F:\Prometheus\techne\cartography\store\taxonomy_events.jsonl` event TX-001 ("TAXONOMY_MUTATION_PROPOSED", amended): a structurally similar corpus (abstract-bearing paper genomes) attempted full multi-axis descriptor classification and found only 3.3%-7.2% of items fully classified even with two independent taggers, because per-axis "unknown" rates ran 56%-79%. The same event proposes a near-identical evaluation protocol to the one needed here: "held_out_failure_prediction + cross_field_retrieval," judging retrieved neighbors by whether their MECHANISM tags overlap on a tag the query's own TITLE does not contain — i.e., this repo has already designed (though not fully executed) almost exactly this recommender-quality test once before, on a different corpus.
- Same file, event LIM-002 ("relevance-is-popularity"): a citation-weighted relevance ranking (OpenAlex) was found to silently reproduce POPULARITY rather than topical relevance for a "surface similar/relevant items" retrieval task — the closest prior in-repo analogue to a similarity recommender's failure mode.
- Repository memory (already in context, no additional retrieval spent): `feedback_domains_are_docstrings.md` (discipline/domain labels are metadata, never structural coordinates), `feedback_mi_bias.md` (MI/overlap statistics computed on sparse histograms are biased upward; need a random-pairing null), `feedback_no_naive_score_combination.md` (never linearly combine scores from heterogeneous-scale heads), `feedback_causal_vs_oracle_features.md` (distinguish features available at inference time from labels attached after the fact), `feedback_neutrality_gate_can_be_tautological.md` (a ground-truth judge that reuses the generator's own vocabulary measures the generator, not the target).

## Prospective predictions
1. Across the full pooled "attempted problem" corpus (to be inventoried, not assumed from the 5-row samples above), the fraction of problems carrying at least one non-empty tag will be measured; prediction: it is below 40%, consistent with the sparsity magnitude already observed once in this repo (TX-001's 21%-44% per-axis rates) and with the empty-tag rows sampled in `questions.jsonl`.
2. Comparing (i) a title-only lexical baseline against (ii) the title+subject+tag hybrid on precision@5 for independently-adjudicated "genuine neighbor" (shared solution mechanism/technique, not shared vocabulary), the POOLED (unstratified) gain of (ii) over (i) will be at least 2 points larger than the WITHIN-subdomain-stratified gain — i.e., stratifying by subdomain will erase more than half of any apparent hybrid advantage, showing the pooled number was mostly subject-area leakage.
3. A tag-Jaccard-only signal, computed WITHIN subdomain strata (so subject-area match is held constant), will not be statistically distinguishable from a within-stratum tag-shuffled null at alpha=0.05 for at least one of the two corpora sampled above (erdos_problems.jsonl or questions.jsonl), because tags are either too sparse (questions.jsonl) or too coarse/reused (erdos_problems.jsonl) to carry independent discriminating signal in either case.
4. Top-1 neighbor selection frequency will correlate with a prominence proxy (Erdős problem prize tier / OEIS cross-reference count where available) at Pearson r > 0.3 across queries, replicating the LIM-002 popularity-confound pattern in this new corpus.

## Experiment
1. **Inventory pass** (before any scoring): enumerate every ledger that plausibly instantiates "previously attempted problem" (candidates already located: `cartography/open_problems/data/erdos_problems.jsonl` + `erdos_enriched.jsonl`, `aporia/mathematics/questions.jsonl` + `triage.jsonl`; others matched "recommend"/"similar" in search but were not opened within budget — see Unresolved uncertainty). For each, record: total rows, tag-fill-rate, subdomain/domain-fill-rate, and whether any field distinguishes "attempted" from merely "catalogued/open."
2. **Pool construction**: define the evaluation pool as the union of problems with a non-null status/bucket field (i.e., something was done with them, per triage.jsonl's `bucket`), pending the inventory pass confirming this is the right criterion.
3. **Recommenders built** (no training, pure retrieval):
   - (a) title-only: TF-IDF or BM25 cosine over problem titles.
   - (b) subject-only: subdomain exact-match indicator (drop title and tags entirely) — isolates the coarse-label axis.
   - (c) tag-only: Jaccard/cosine over tag sets.
   - (d) hybrid: title+subject+tag, with each axis's contribution reported separately via ablation rather than pre-blended into one score (see Confound defenses).
4. **Evaluation protocol**: leave-one-out. For each query problem, retrieve top-5 neighbors under each recommender variant from the rest of the pool. A pre-drawn, stratified (by subdomain and prominence tier), LOCKED sample of query problems is independently adjudicated (human or LLM judge blinded to which recommender proposed which candidate, and without access to the candidate's own tag vocabulary) for "genuine neighbor" = shares a solution technique/mechanism, not merely surface vocabulary. Compute precision@5 per variant, both pooled and within-subdomain-stratified.
5. **Nulls**: (i) tag-shuffle null — permute tag sets across problems within each subdomain stratum, holding subdomain and title fixed, rerun tag-only and hybrid; (ii) title-shuffle null as the mirror check; (iii) popularity-matched check — regress top-1-neighbor selection against the prominence proxy.
6. **Cold-start ablation**: rerun the hybrid with the QUERY problem's own tags masked (title+subject only for the query side, full index for the candidate side) to simulate a genuinely new incoming problem that has not yet been tagged — this is the deployment-realistic condition, not the leave-one-out condition with full query metadata.

## Controls
- Title-only baseline (floor for "does subject/tag augmentation add anything at all").
- Subject-only baseline (isolates how much of any gain is pure subdomain/domain matching, including the DEFAULT-assigned rows flagged from triage.jsonl).
- Tag-shuffle random-pairing null within subdomain strata (tests whether tag-overlap signal exceeds chance once subject-area is held constant).
- Popularity-tier-matched comparison (candidates matched on prominence proxy before comparing tag/subject contribution, to separate genuine similarity from prominence-driven co-selection, per LIM-002).
- Independent adjudicator control: "genuine neighbor" ground truth is judged by a rubric/process that does not have access to the candidate's tag vocabulary at judgment time, to avoid the classifier-scores-its-own-generator circularity already flagged in this repo's memory.

## Confound defenses
- **Oracle leakage / cold-start mismatch**: tags and even subject fields may have been assigned to a problem using information (including which techniques were later found relevant) unavailable when a genuinely new problem first arrives. Defense: run the cold-start ablation (step 6) as the headline deployment-realistic number, not the full-metadata leave-one-out number.
- **Default-label collapse**: `triage.jsonl` shows some subdomain/bucket values are explicit fallback defaults ("Default classification for subdomain X"), not per-problem measurements. Defense: flag and separately report results for rows whose provenance note indicates a default assignment; do not let subject-axis performance be inflated by problems whose subject label was never actually adjudicated.
- **Conjunctive sparsity inflating apparent precision**: per TX-001, joint multi-axis classification succeeded on as few as ~3%-7% of a comparable corpus. Defense: pre-declare how missing tags are scored (treated as "unknown," never coerced to "zero overlap," which would silently penalize sparsely-tagged problems as maximally dissimilar) and report tag-fill-rate as a first-class result, not a footnote.
- **Popularity/relevance confound**: per LIM-002, a citation-weighted or reference-count-weighted signal can silently masquerade as topical relevance. Defense: the prominence-proxy correlation check (prediction 4) is preregistered, not post hoc.
- **Heterogeneous-scale score blending**: title cosine, subject match (categorical/binary), and tag Jaccard are different-scale statistics over different populations of pairs. Defense: report each axis's ablated contribution separately (step 3d); never fit or present a single linearly-blended weight as the headline number.
- **Small-stratum instability**: many subdomains will have few other pool members. Defense: report per-stratum n alongside every within-subdomain statistic and refuse interpretation below a pre-set minimum (n >= 5 other pool members in that stratum).
- **MI/overlap bias on sparse tag histograms**: any tag-overlap statistic computed on the observed (sparse, coarse) tag distributions is liable to be biased upward relative to a naive null; the tag-shuffle random-pairing null (not a uniform/independence assumption) is mandatory before any tag-overlap number is reported as signal.

## Preregistered falsifiers (numeric thresholds)
1. If the measured tag-fill-rate across the full pooled corpus is >= 70%, prediction 1 (tag sparsity) is FALSIFIED.
2. If the within-subdomain-stratified precision@5 gain of hybrid over title-only is within 2 points of the pooled (unstratified) gain — i.e., stratifying does NOT shrink the apparent advantage by more than half — prediction 2 (subject-area leakage explains the pooled gain) is FALSIFIED.
3. If the within-stratum tag-only precision@5 is statistically distinguishable from the tag-shuffled null (two-sided test, alpha = 0.05) in BOTH corpora sampled, prediction 3 (tags carry no independent within-stratum signal) is FALSIFIED.
4. If the top-1-neighbor/prominence-proxy correlation is r < 0.15, prediction 4 (popularity confound replication) is FALSIFIED.
5. Coarsest-level check: if cold-start-ablated hybrid precision@5 is not at least 2 percentage points above the title-only baseline (pooled), the recommender is providing no detectable value at all under deployment-realistic conditions, independent of any of the mechanism-level predictions above.

## Stopping rule
The corpus inventory (step 1) and the pool definition (step 2) are fixed BEFORE any recommender is scored. The query sample for adjudication is drawn once — stratified by subdomain and prominence tier, sized from a pilot precision-variance estimate for 80% power against the falsifier-2 threshold — and locked before any recommender variant computes a score against it; no optional stopping or resampling of the adjudication set is permitted after scores are seen. If the tag-fill-rate checkpoint (falsifier 1) resolves before adjudication begins and shows tags are effectively absent (<10% fill), stop after reporting that finding rather than proceeding to build a tag axis the corpus cannot support.

## Expected failure modes
- The corpora actually backing a deployed "previously attempted problems" recommender may not be the ones sampled here (erdos_problems.jsonl is mostly still-open problems, not Prometheus attempts); other candidate ledgers matched search terms but were not opened within budget (see Unresolved uncertainty) and could force a redefinition of the pool.
- An LLM or human adjudicator judging "genuine neighbor" may inadvertently reuse the same tag vocabulary the recommender indexes on, reintroducing the classifier/generator circularity flagged in repository memory; agreement checks without candidate-tag visibility are required and may reveal the adjudication itself is not independent enough to trust.
- Small per-subdomain pool sizes may force merging adjacent subdomains for statistical power, which re-coarsens exactly the axis (subject area) this design is trying to isolate, weakening the within-stratum falsifiers.
- If tags in the deployed corpus are both sparse AND coarse (as suggested by comparing questions.jsonl vs erdos_problems.jsonl), the tag axis may fail for two independent reasons simultaneously, complicating attribution of any negative result to "sparsity" vs "granularity."

## Compute estimate
No model training required. Corpus parsing and inventory: negligible (JSONL files of the scale sampled here, low CPU-seconds). Pairwise TF-IDF/cosine and Jaccard computation across a pool likely in the low thousands of rows: O(N^2) comparisons, on the order of low millions of pairwise scores, seconds to a few minutes single-threaded, no GPU. The dominant cost is adjudication of the locked query sample: budgeting roughly 100-200 stratified queries x 5 candidates x 4 recommender variants (with de-duplication of identical candidate problems across variants) — on the order of 500-1500 adjudications, either LLM-judge token cost or human reviewer time; this dwarfs the retrieval computation cost.

## Prior evidence that materially changed this design
`F:\Prometheus\techne\cartography\store\taxonomy_events.jsonl` (events TX-001 and LIM-002) materially changed this design. TX-001 shows this repository already ran a near-identical held-out cross-field retrieval protocol on a different corpus (abstract-bearing paper genomes) and hit a hard classification-sparsity ceiling (3.3%-7.2% full joint classification despite two independent taggers); that prior result is why this design pre-declares a tag-fill-rate stop-gate (falsifier 1 / stopping rule) rather than assuming tags are usable, and why it insists on within-stratum analysis rather than trusting pooled numbers. LIM-002 (a citation-weighted relevance ranking silently reproduced popularity rather than topicality) directly motivated the prominence-correlation falsifier and the popularity-matched control, because a structurally similar failure was already caught once in this repo for a comparable "find similar/relevant items" system. `aporia/mathematics/triage.jsonl`'s literal "Default classification for subdomain X" notes directly motivated the default-label-collapse confound defense — without having opened this file, the subject-area axis would have been treated as uniformly measured rather than partly default-assigned.

## Unresolved uncertainty
- The full inventory of what constitutes "previously attempted problems" was not completed within the 15-operation budget; several ledgers that matched "recommend" or "similar problem(s)" searches (e.g. under `ergon/`, `ludus/atlas_of_worlds/`, `SerendipityFoundry/`) were identified by filename/path but never opened, so the pool defined in this proposal may not match the corpus an actual deployed recommender would draw from.
- Tag-fill-rate and subdomain distribution were only spot-checked on 3-5 row samples per file, not measured across full corpora; the falsifier-1 threshold (70%) and prediction-1 threshold (40%) are anchored to TX-001's sparsity magnitude in a structurally similar but distinct corpus (paper genomes, not math problems), and must be re-measured on the actual target corpus before the stopping-rule checkpoint can fire correctly.
- Whether a genuinely non-circular, tag-vocabulary-blind adjudicator is obtainable at the sample size this design requires (100-200+ queries) is untested; if not, the "genuine neighbor" ground truth itself may need a different construction than assumed here.
