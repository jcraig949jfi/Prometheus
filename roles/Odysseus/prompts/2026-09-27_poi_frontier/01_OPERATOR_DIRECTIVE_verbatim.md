ODYSSEUS — PHYSICS OF INTELLIGENCE FRONTIER PROGRAM

This is a multi-hour research program.

Do not return after one result or one research spike.

Your purpose is to explore, organize, and expand a major Prometheus frontier:

THE PHYSICS OF INTELLIGENCE

Treat this phrase broadly.

We are interested in the physical, computational, dynamical, informational and evolutionary conditions under which systems can:

* preserve useful state;
* propagate influence;
* transform information;
* build structure;
* adapt;
* learn;
* coordinate;
* reproduce;
* modify their own machinery;
* accumulate capabilities;
* discover abstractions;
* become increasingly capable without those capabilities being explicitly installed by the experimenter.

Do not assume intelligence has to resemble neural networks, brains, digital computers, biological organisms, or current AI architectures.

Prometheus is explicitly interested in mechanisms outside familiar modalities.

Your job is not to settle “the physics of intelligence.”

Your job is to build and cultivate a deep research frontier around it.

There should be substantially more worthwhile work in the backlog when you finish than Prometheus can immediately execute.

That is desirable.

⸻

OPERATING MODEL

Work autonomously through this program.

Make ordinary research decisions yourself.

Use bounded research delegates when useful.

Do not return to the operator for routine choices such as:

* which paper to read next;
* whether to follow an interesting citation;
* whether a null result deserves another small check;
* how to split a research question;
* whether two Threads should be merged;
* whether a bounded analysis should be rerun.

The HITL should be reserved for consequential direction.

Surface something to the operator when it involves matters such as:

* a major change to Prometheus’s scientific direction;
* significant irreversible/destructive action;
* meaningful spend beyond already authorized bounds;
* a surprising result with broad program implications;
* two genuinely different high-level paths whose choice depends on operator goals;
* evidence that the operator’s premise is materially wrong.

The operator has decision authority.

The operator does not have epistemic supremacy.

Challenge operator assumptions, Prometheus assumptions, and your own assumptions when evidence warrants it.

BLOCK B — EXTERNAL RESEARCH RAID

External research is a primary input, not an afterthought.

Search broadly across relevant fields.

Potential territories include:

* artificial life;
* origins of life;
* complex systems;
* statistical physics;
* dynamical systems;
* information theory;

* non-equilibrium thermodynamics;
* evolutionary computation;

* self-reproducing systems;
* cellular automata;
* reservoir computing;
* unconventional computing;
* morphogenesis;
* collective intelligence;
* active matter;
* causal inference;
* algorithmic information theory;
* learning theory;
* developmental robotics;
* self-organizing systems;
* automated reasoning;
* current AI/agent research.

Do not stay inside these categories if citations lead elsewhere.

Pay particular attention to recent work.

AI and synthetic reasoning are moving quickly; Prometheus must not build its world model from stale assumptions.

For each useful external idea, ask:

1. What did they actually demonstrate?
2. What assumptions did they build in?
3. What failed?
4. What measurement made the result visible?
5. Is there code/data we could reuse or fossilize?
6. Does Prometheus already have an analogous result under different language?
7. What question remains genuinely open?
8. What would an anti-gravity version of the experiment look like?

Prior art should prevent us from wasting years rediscovering known results.

It should not force Prometheus into the field’s preferred representations.

BLOCK C — CONNECT EXTERNAL RESEARCH TO PROMETHEUS

Map useful external concepts against existing Prometheus lenses.

Consider, where relevant:

* SFE / historical pipeline;
* NPE;
* BEE;
* Aether;
* Cosmos;
* Ensorain;
* Ananke;
* Aphrodite;
* other documented engines and harnesses.

For an external mechanism or open problem, ask:

* Which existing engine can express it?
* Which engine would distort it?
* Which observables already exist?
* What instrumentation is missing?
* Have we accidentally tested it already?
* Do multiple engines provide independent lenses on it?
* Is there no suitable engine at all?

Do not force an external theory into an existing engine just to avoid creating a new lens.

A finding that “none of our current engines can represent this cleanly” is valuable.

These examples are not conclusions.

Mine the evidence.

BLOCK E — BUILD A LARGE THREAD BACKLOG

Create a substantial set of candidate research Threads.

Do not be afraid of backlog size.

There may be decades of legitimate work here.

The purpose is not to finish it.

The purpose is to preserve and continually improve it.

Each worthwhile Thread should become progressively clearer about:

QUESTION

What are we trying to learn?

WHY IT MATTERS

How could the answer alter the North-Star search?

WHAT WE ALREADY KNOW

Internal evidence and external prior art.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY UNKNOWN

Separate genuine uncertainty from missing documentation.

CHEAPEST DISCRIMINATOR

What small research spike or experiment could materially change our belief?

SUITABLE LENSES

Which engines/tools/research modes fit?

NEW-LENS SIGNAL

Would answering this require a fundamentally different world or organism model?

RELATIONSHIPS

What other Threads does it sharpen, depend upon, contradict, or subsume?

Do not over-formalize the schema.

Good research questions matter more than perfect metadata.

⸻

BLOCK F — CHOP THE BACKLOG

The backlog must not remain a pile of grand questions.

Take promising mature Threads and decompose them into research-sized pieces.

Possible progression:

Thread
  -> research spike
  -> Campaign
  -> Experiment
  -> Task
  -> Attempt

Not every Thread needs every layer.

For several promising Threads, prepare enough material that a fresh Claude researcher on another machine could receive:

“Here. Go mine this for several hours.”

and begin without needing an oral history from you.

Do not execute everything you prepare.

The goal is to create research-ready inventory.

⸻

BLOCK G — EXECUTE A FEW CHEAP DISCRIMINATORS

Select a small number of high-information, low-risk questions from the backlog.

Run bounded work where it improves the frontier.

Suitable work may include:

* repository mining;
* literature synthesis;
* tiny simulations;
* deterministic replays;
* synthetic counterexamples;
* cross-engine comparison;
* mathematical reasoning;
* instrumentation feasibility checks.

Avoid major campaigns.

The purpose is to test whether backlog cultivation becomes stronger when the researcher periodically consumes a few of its own Threads.

If a result changes the backlog, update it.

This should be a living frontier, not a static list.

BLOCK H — DELEGATE

Use lightweight workers when useful.

ubu001 is the controlling host, but work need not execute there.

Delegate bounded independent questions to available workers/nodes where practical.

Good delegation targets include:

* literature raids;
* one-engine historical reconstruction;
* checking an external claim;
* searching a corpus for a mechanism;
* reproducing a known result;
* attacking a proposed distinction.

Give delegates questions, not conclusions.

Where possible, isolate disposable Claude context from persistent seat memory.

If context isolation is imperfect, record that fact rather than stopping the science.

Record meaningful environmental changes made by workers.

⸻

BLOCK I — LOOK FOR CROSS-THREAD STRUCTURE

Periodically stop and ask whether apparently separate Threads are manifestations of something deeper.

Look for:

* repeated failure modes;
* common mechanisms;
* incompatible assumptions;
* missing primitives;
* recurring causal structures;
* questions that become simpler when reframed.

Do not force unification.

But do not leave twenty Threads separate if evidence says they are one underlying question.

Likewise, split a broad Thread when it contains several independently testable uncertainties.

Backlog structure should evolve with understanding.

⸻

BLOCK J — TEST THE NORTH-STAR RELATIONSHIP

For the strongest areas of the backlog, ask:

If Prometheus resolved this question, would it actually move us toward the North Star?

Some scientifically interesting problems may not.

Some mundane instrumentation problems may unlock ten North-Star questions.

Some familiar research areas may be intellectually rich but gravitationally pull Prometheus toward conventional architectures.

Identify those distinctions.

Do not rank the entire backlog numerically.

Instead identify a small number of qualitatively different high-value territories.

PROGRAM PRINCIPLE

Prometheus should continuously maintain more high-quality research opportunity than it has capacity to execute.

As new Linux nodes, agents, models, engines and external discoveries arrive, the frontier should be ready to absorb them.

The backlog is not debt.

It is the program’s reservoir of future scientific possibility.

Continuously chop it, test it, merge it, attack it, reprioritize it, and replenish it.

The human should spend less time feeding individual workers and more time deciding what kind of future Prometheus should explore.
