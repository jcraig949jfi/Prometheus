CURRENT SCIENTIFIC BASELINE

Treat these as evidence, not conclusions to defend.

OPTIMIZER / MEMORY CONFOUND

Early design work showed that batch lossless refitting could beat selective online systems because of fitting procedure rather than memory representation.

Once fitting is controlled:

performance generally rises with the quantity of exact records retained.

That means LM01 must distinguish memory effects from optimization effects.

LOSSLESS READOUT STILL COMPRESSES

A raw exact store does not automatically generalize.

Generalization requires some readout/operator that turns retained experience into a prediction.

Therefore a “lossless” system may preserve all history while still performing substantial compression at query time.

Do not equate:

lossless storage

with

absence of compression.

STRICT LOSSLESS FALSIFIER

Its power is weak in several latent families.

A null there is unresolved.

Do not turn low power into evidence.

SELECTIVITY

Whether selective forgetting is causally required remains untested.

That is an important future question.

Do not assume selective systems win because selectivity itself matters; capacity, fitting, optimization, regularization, or query computation may explain the effect.

ARC3 CENTRAL QUESTION

The central scientific question is:

When is discarding information necessary for generalization, rather than merely one way of implementing a bounded learner?

Related questions include:

* Can exact history plus a bounded readout generalize just as well?
* Does generalization depend on compression in storage, compression at readout, or both?
* What kinds of nuisance information actually hurt?
* Can irrelevant distinctions remain stored but simply be ignored?
* When does finite capacity itself create useful inductive bias?
* What is the minimal retained state sufficient for future prediction?

This territory connects directly to Prometheus’s North Star around symbolic compression and reusable abstractions.

BLOCK E — STORAGE COMPRESSION VS READOUT COMPRESSION

Separate:

STORAGE

How much historical information remains recoverable?

READOUT

How much of that information is actually used to predict?

A system can retain everything and still compute a highly compressed sufficient statistic at query time.

Design analysis that distinguishes:

* exact retained history + simple readout;
* exact history + refitted complex readout;
* compressed state + simple readout;
* compressed state + expensive refit.

Ask whether LM01’s interesting phenomenon is really about where compression happens.

This may be more important than “lossless versus lossy.”

BLOCK F — CAUSAL SELECTIVITY

Design the clean successor experiment while LM01 runs.

Do not launch it before LM01 results are integrated unless a very cheap prerequisite probe is needed.

Central question:

Does discarding particular distinctions cause better future generalization?

Use a restore-the-discarded-information intervention.

A strong design should compare states with equivalent useful information while manipulating whether nuisance distinctions remain available.

Possible structure:

1. selective learner discards distinctions;
2. reconstruct or preserve those discarded distinctions without removing the useful compressed state;
3. determine whether their mere availability harms prediction.

Distinguish:

* storage burden;
* optimization burden;
* retrieval interference;
* causal nuisance;
* capacity competition.

If restored information sits unused and performance remains unchanged, selectivity itself was not causal.

If restoring irrelevant distinctions predictably hurts despite matched capacity/readout, that is much stronger evidence.

Avoid building the answer into the intervention.

⸻

BLOCK G — NUISANCE AS A CONTROLLED AXIS

The nuisance worlds are potentially central.

Ask:

What kinds of extra information are harmful?

Separate at least conceptually:

* independent noise;
* spurious correlation;
* obsolete historical structure;
* regime-specific structure;
* misleading high-frequency detail;
* exact episodic identity.

Do not assume all nuisance is equivalent.

A useful future experiment may gradually vary how predictive a formerly useful distinction becomes.

This can turn “forgetting” into a causal adaptation question rather than a memory-budget question.

⸻

BLOCK H — SWITCHING WORLDS

Study switching as its own mechanism problem.

Exact history creates a special challenge when yesterday’s regularity becomes today’s nuisance.

Ask whether successful systems:

* overwrite old state;
* discount it;
* preserve it but gate retrieval;
* infer regime;
* split representations;
* maintain multiple models.

A lossless store may look bad only because the readout cannot identify which history is relevant.

Do not attribute that automatically to too much memory.

Look for mechanisms of context selection.

⸻

BLOCK I — TRANSFER

Use transfer strata to ask:

Which retained information survives a change of task/world?

A compressed representation may help because it captures invariants.

But exact memory plus the right readout might recover the same invariants.

Separate:

* retention of examples;
* retention of latent structure;
* retention of reusable abstractions.

This is especially relevant to Prometheus’s symbolic-compression North Star.

⸻

BLOCK J — LOSSLESS AS AN UPPER BOUND?

Challenge the assumption that more retained information should monotonically help.

In principle, exact history cannot reduce Bayes-optimal information.

In practice, bounded compute/readout/search may make excess information harmful.

Distinguish:

INFORMATION-THEORETIC AVAILABILITY

More information cannot make the optimal predictor worse.

from

COMPUTATIONALLY ACCESSIBLE GENERALIZATION

More information may make the useful structure harder to extract under finite computation.

That distinction may be the core scientific result.

Develop it carefully.

BLOCK M — RESERVOIR AS A BRIDGE

The bounded exact reservoir is scientifically valuable.

* exactness of retained records;
* bounded capacity;
* deliberate semantic compression.

Analyze it carefully.

If a random reservoir performs similarly to sophisticated selective learners at matched capacity, then much of the effect may be capacity rather than intelligent selection.

If semantic selection outperforms random retention robustly, that is evidence for selective representation.
