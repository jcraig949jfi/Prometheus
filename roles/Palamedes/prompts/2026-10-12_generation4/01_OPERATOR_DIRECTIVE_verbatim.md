B. Falsifiability of controls

Across our first three engines, and in the D1 reachability campaign, we repeatedly encountered controls that could not fail meaningfully.

Turn that lesson into an explicit instrument-design requirement.

For every claim-critical baseline or control, identify:

1. What scientific alternative it represents.
2. What observation would make it pass.
3. What observation would make it fail.
4. Whether the experimental construction makes either outcome impossible.
5. Whether it has privileged access to the answer.
6. Whether its failure actually distinguishes the target mechanism from a simpler explanation.

A baseline that cannot produce a required classification is not an adequate discriminator of that classification.

A constant answer that passes every admissible case does not establish origin attribution.

A recipe reader given the answer table does not establish mechanism sensitivity.

4. The scientific question for Generation 4

Proposed question:

Can the Observatory independently distinguish structural causal propagation from the transmission and transformation of usable information in a native spatial medium?

Separate at least four phenomena:

P1 — Spatial influence

A perturbation at one site changes another site within the native causal light cone.

P2 — Causal ancestry

An intervention on an intermediate site changes later descendants, while a suitable non-ancestor intervention does not.

P3 — Information transmission

A source value remains recoverable from a later spatial state under a registered decoder.

P4 — Information transformation

Later state depends causally on multiple independently varied inputs in a way not explained by copying, simple routing, or single-source selection.

These form a family of candidate claims, not an assumed ladder of increasing intelligence.

A runtime may satisfy P1 and P2 without satisfying P3 or P4.

That is a valid result.

In particular:

A causal tree is not automatically an information-processing circuit.

Do not use an ancestry detector as a substitute for an information detector.
