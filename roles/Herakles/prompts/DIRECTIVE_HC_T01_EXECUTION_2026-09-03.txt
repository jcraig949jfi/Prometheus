HERAKLES — HC-T01 EXECUTION DIRECTIVE

TOUSSAINT MISSING-CELL EXPERIMENT

Separate the direct mutation-operator effect from evolved changes in future accessibility

Herakles,

T0 is accepted.

The historical claim survives:

Toussaint’s programme contained both a longitudinal exploration-distribution detector and a genuine mechanism ablation with adaptive acquisition, but the detector was not run inside the ablation.

HC-T01 is therefore authorized with one required methodological upgrade before execution.

The generation-zero mechanical-effect null is necessary but insufficient.

The central confound is:

β directly parameterizes the variation operator that induces the detector.

Therefore a raw difference in exploration distribution between β arms is partly true by construction.

The experiment becomes scientifically meaningful only if we distinguish:

1. the direct mechanical effect of applying β;
2. the representational structure evolution produced under different β histories;
3. the interaction between evolved representation and probe operator;
4. later acquisition.

The target particle is not:

β changes the offspring distribution.

The target particle is:

Evolution under different variation regimes changes the organism/population so that the SAME subsequently applied variation operator produces a different distribution of reachable future phenotypes.

That is the non-tautological quantity.

1. CORE CAUSAL DECOMPOSITION

Treat the observed detector trajectory as containing at least:

direct operator effect
+
evolved population / representation effect
+
operator × representation interaction
+
estimator noise

Generation-zero probing estimates the direct operator effect only at the initial population.

That is not enough because the mechanical effect of β may itself depend on the representation distribution of the population.

At generation t, the effect of β on a population evolved under β may be different from its effect on a population evolved without β.

That difference is potentially the phenomenon we care about.

⸻

2. REQUIRED FROZEN-POPULATION PROBE

At preregistered checkpoints t:

1. freeze the current population;
2. do NOT allow offspring to enter the population;
3. do NOT apply selection;
4. do NOT alter evolutionary history;
5. evaluate the historical exploration-distribution detector under both probe settings.

For the exact same frozen population P_t measure:

D(P_t, beta_on)
D(P_t, beta_off)

where D is Toussaint’s recovered detector and beta_on/beta_off are the historically defined operator settings.

This is an observational counterfactual probe.

The probe must not feed information back into the evolutionary run.

⸻

3. PRIMARY MECHANICAL CONTRAST

For any frozen population P_t define:

M(P_t) = D(P_t, beta_on) - D(P_t, beta_off)

or the historically appropriate multivariate equivalent if D contains:

* neutral degree;
* mutual-information structure;
* modular degree;
* other recovered detector statistics.

Do not collapse a vector detector into one scalar unless the historical detector already did so or the preregistration explicitly defines one primary quantity.

M(P_t) estimates:

how much the current population’s representation changes the consequences of turning second-type mutation on versus off.

⸻

4. EVOLUTIONARY-HISTORY CONTRAST

The first non-tautological result compares populations with different evolutionary histories under the SAME probe.

Let:

P_on,t  = population evolved historically under beta > 0
P_off,t = population evolved historically under beta = 0

Then compare:

D(P_on,t, beta_probe)
versus
D(P_off,t, beta_probe)

for a fixed probe operator.

The cleanest primary probe should be selected before execution.

Candidate primary:

beta_probe = historical beta_on

Rationale:

Both populations are exposed to exactly the same variation operator at measurement time.

Any detector difference then reflects differences in the populations/representations themselves, not the immediate fact that one detector was evaluated under β and the other was not.

⸻

5. DIFFERENCE-IN-DIFFERENCES

Also compute the interaction:

E_t =
    [D(P_on,t, beta_on) - D(P_on,t, beta_off)]
    -
    [D(P_off,t, beta_on) - D(P_off,t, beta_off)]

This is the key operator × evolved-representation interaction.

Interpretation:

E_t ≈ 0

means the direct effect of β is similar regardless of evolutionary history.

E_t != 0

means evolution has changed the representation so that the same operator now induces a different exploration distribution.

This is a candidate microscopic evolvability effect.

Do not assume the sign.

⸻

Expected:

This is calibration only.

It is NOT a scientific positive result.

3. after evolution, populations with different histories respond differently to the SAME β probe;
4. the interaction E_t grows beyond generation-zero baseline;

Evolution under a variation mechanism changed internal representation in a way that altered the distribution of future phenotypic variation beyond the mechanism’s direct mechanical effect, and that change was associated with subsequent adaptive acquisition.

Nothing stronger.

FINAL PRINCIPLE

The direct effect of β is not the particle.

That is merely the knob turning.

The candidate particle is:

Evolution changes the machine such that the same knob now opens a different future.

Two systems may look equally capable today.

They may even have the same phenotype.

But if identical variation applied to them produces systematically different reachable futures, then historical evolution has changed something deeper than current performance.

That is what HC-T01 is now allowed to test.

Measure the knob.

Subtract the knob.

Then see whether the machine changed.
