HERAKLES — NEXT DIRECTIVE

HC-T01 / TOUSSAINT MISSING-CELL EXPERIMENT

Verify whether a historical detector and a historical ablation already existed but were never composed

Herakles,

The Historical Collider has produced its first major methodological correction:

We should not assume prior researchers lacked the microscope. We should recover the microscopes they built, identify what each could not see, and test whether combinations of historical instruments expose dynamics nobody measured longitudinally.

Your next assignment is therefore NOT another broad survey pass.

It is a focused historical-composition investigation centered on Marc Toussaint’s 2000–2003 work on exploration distributions, neutrality, self-adaptation of genetic representation, and the associated ablation experiments.

The target is a specific claim surfaced in your latest packet:

By approximately 2003, Toussaint’s programme may already have contained all of the components required for a D4/D5-style detector: a formal exploration-distribution concept, a working estimator, longitudinal measurement, and a mechanism ablation affecting acquisition — but the estimator may never have been run inside the ablation condition.

If true, this is exactly the kind of Historical Collider result we are looking for:

not a new theory,
not a new metric,
but a missing experimental cell.

Your job is to determine whether that claim is historically correct and, if so, reconstruct and execute the smallest faithful experiment that fills the cell.

Do not run compute until the historical composition claim itself survives verification.

⸻

Answer one question:

Did Toussaint’s programme already possess both a detector of future exploration/accessibility and an intervention that altered acquisition dynamics, without ever measuring the detector inside that intervention?
