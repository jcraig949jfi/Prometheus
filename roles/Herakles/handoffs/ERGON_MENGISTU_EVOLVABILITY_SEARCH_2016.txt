WHY THIS MATTERS TO PROMETHEUS -- TWO USES, AND THE SECOND IS THE IMPORTANT ONE

USE 1, historical detector. This is the closest thing the field has to the
instrument Herakles was about to invent. It goes into
Q_DETECTOR_PARTS_REGISTRY.md as det-part-02.

USE 2, POSITIVE CONTROL. This is the ruling's sharpest instrument-design
observation and the reason this brief exists:

    They directly reward evolvability. We don't ultimately want to. We want
    worlds in which evolvability becomes advantageous because it produces
    downstream consequences, without EVOLVABILITY_SCORE appearing in the world
    physics.

That makes the 2016 design a manufacturable positive control. The three-step
programme it enables, which eventually belongs to Daedalus:

    1. Build a treatment where evolvability is deliberately rewarded,
       reproducing something of the 2016 shape.
    2. Verify Prometheus instruments DETECT the machinery that arises. A
       detector that misses it under direct reward is broken and must be fixed
       or killed before anything subtler is attempted.
    3. Remove the direct reward and ask whether comparable machinery arises
       endogenously.

Step 2 is a stronger calibration than any particle currently in
H_CALIBRATION_PARTICLES.md, because the phenomenon is produced on demand
rather than hoped for.

WHAT IS ALREADY ESTABLISHED (verify and extend, do not re-derive)

Confirmed 3-0 from the proceedings PDF:
  - The fitness function IS the evolvability estimate. 200 offspring are
    generated per individual, evaluated, then DISCARDED; evolvability is the
    count of behaviourally distinct offspring above a distance threshold of
    0.01.
  - Transfer test: the most evolvable organism from each run is moved WITHOUT
    FURTHER EVOLUTION into an unseen 450x450 "mega maze"; 200 offspring are
    generated; the endpoint is the percentage of a 450x450 grid visited at
    least once by any transferred offspring. Evolvability Search beats Novelty
    Search and objective-based search at p<0.001.
  - Scale: maze treatment population 250, 1000 generations, 50 runs,
    evolvability of Novelty/objective populations tracked every 100
    generations; biped population 500, 2000 generations, tracked every 200.
  - The authors hedge: the measure "captures some aspect of generalizable
    evolvability".

Limits already logged by verifiers, which this deep-dive must sharpen:
  - It is a SCALAR summary (count at a threshold), not the full distribution of
    mutational effects.
  - It measures reachable-region SIZE at a point, i.e. a snapshot, rather than
    change between two points -- though Fig 4b tracks it over time across
    treatments.
  - The mega-maze metric is a UNION over 200 offspring, so a single
    far-wandering offspring inflates coverage.
  - Offspring are ONE mutation step only.

WHAT THE DEEP-DIVE MUST PRODUCE

1. THE EXACT COMPUTATION. Write down the estimator precisely: the mutation
   distribution used to generate offspring, the behavioural characterisation,
   the distance metric, the threshold and its sensitivity, whether the count is
   of distinct behaviours or of occupied bins, and how ties and duplicates are
   handled. Enough that it can be reimplemented without the paper.

2. THE ESTIMATOR'S STATISTICAL PROPERTIES. 200 offspring is a sample. What is
   the sampling error of the count? How does the estimate scale with the number
   of offspring, and where does it saturate? Is the threshold of 0.01 doing
   most of the work? A threshold-count estimator can be dominated by its
   threshold, and if so that is a defect Prometheus must not inherit silently.
   Where the paper does not answer this, say so and state what would.

3. WHAT IT DETECTS AND WHAT IT FAILS TO DETECT. Be concrete and adversarial.
   Construct or describe a case where two organisms have identical scores but
   materially different mutational-effect distributions. Construct a case where
   the union-over-offspring coverage metric is misleading. These become the
   "cannot see" clause of the registry entry, which is mandatory.

4. THE UNION-METRIC PROBLEM, QUANTIFIED. Determine whether the transfer result
   survives a metric that is not a union -- for example median per-offspring
   coverage, or coverage by the offspring cloud minus its single largest
   contributor. If the paper does not report this, state it as an open
   vulnerability of the headline claim rather than asserting the claim fails.

5. THE ANTECEDENT TRACE. Establish the genuine earliest instance of
   offspring-neighbourhood measurement in this lineage. Read Lehman & Stanley
   2013 (which ENUMERATES reachable genomes rather than sampling them, and is
   therefore a different and exact instrument -- det-part-03) and Reisinger &
   Miikkulainen 2006. Push back before 2006 if the trail continues. Report the
   earliest instance you can source, with its locator.

6. D-LEVELS. Assign a D-level (defined in Q_DETECTOR_PARTS_REGISTRY.md) to the
   2016 paper and to every antecedent, distinguishing what the measure COULD
   support from what the source ACTUALLY REPORTS. Where those differ, say both.
   Pay attention to whether any of them is longitudinal per LINEAGE as opposed
   to per TREATMENT -- that distinction is where the seat's residual hypothesis
   now lives, and it should be attacked, not protected.

7. THE POSITIVE-CONTROL SPECIFICATION. Draft the minimal specification for
   step 1 of the three-step programme: the smallest world in which evolvability
   can be directly rewarded and the resulting machinery is recognisable. State
   what "the machinery" would look like concretely, so step 2 has a target.
   Design only. Do not run it.

8. THE ENDOGENOUS QUESTION. State precisely what would distinguish
   "evolvability machinery arose because it was rewarded" from "evolvability
   machinery arose because of downstream consequences". If you cannot state a
   discriminating observation, say so -- that would be a serious problem for
   step 3 and better discovered now than after the compute is spent.
