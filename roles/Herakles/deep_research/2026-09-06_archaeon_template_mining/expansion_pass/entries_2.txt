Analyst note on ground truth used below. Section 1 of the brief was taken as
given. Two facts about the existing bench were additionally confirmed by
reading source, because five of the reductions turn on them, and both are
recorded here so that no entry has to assert them from memory:
  1. vivarium/viv/executors.py delegates evaluate_bitstring to the engine's
     reference executor, and SerendipityFoundry/SerendipityFoundryEngine/
     sfe/executors.py computes score as the fraction of positions matching a
     hidden target, described in its own docstring as an onemax-style
     landscape. The target is sha256 of the string target:<seed_root>:<length>
     truncated to length. The landscape is therefore SINGLE-PEAKED, additive
     and noiseless.
  2. The kind contract in vivarium/viv/kinds.py declares params exactly bits
     and length, and does NOT require that the length of bits equals length.
     The scorer counts matches over the shorter of the two lengths but divides
     by the target length.
Where a reduction depends on either fact, it says so.

TEMPLATE_ID: ada_thin_film.v0
FIELD: Automated Experimentation
KIND: synthesize_thin_film_v0
QUESTION: Which combination of dopant concentration and annealing time gives a
  thin film the highest charge-carrier mobility, and can a machine find it by
  choosing its own next experiment rather than following a human's plan?
MECHANISM: A robot runs a closed loop. Each iteration mixes an ink from a
  hole-transport material, a dopant and an additive at a chosen ratio; spin
  coats it onto a substrate; anneals it on a hotplate for a chosen time;
  photographs the film; takes an optical spectrum; and measures a
  current-voltage curve. A pseudomobility is computed from the conductivity and
  the film thickness. A model-based optimiser fits a surrogate to every
  measurement made so far and picks the next ratio and time. The loop repeats
  until the budget of physical experiments is spent.
CANDIDATE: A processing recipe, that is, one point in a two-dimensional
  continuous space of dopant ratio and annealing time. It is judged by the
  measured pseudomobility of the film it produces.
WORLD: A physical laboratory. It supplies the chemistry, the spin coater, the
  hotplate, the camera, the spectrometer and the probe station, and it also
  supplies the noise floor of each of those instruments. The response surface is
  unknown in advance and is only ever sampled, never revealed.
WHAT_CHANGES: The two recipe parameters from iteration to iteration, and,
  underneath that, genuine run-to-run physical variation between nominally
  identical recipes.
MEASURED: Pseudomobility, a positive continuous quantity, one value per film,
  plus the count of physical experiments consumed, an integer.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: A failed campaign still leaves the sampled response
  surface, which is the expensive part. Even with no recipe over threshold you
  keep every measured point, the instrument repeatability at replicated points,
  and the location of the best region reached. The most useful failure is a
  campaign where replicates disagree by more than the surface varies across the
  whole region: that says the measurement, not the chemistry, is the
  bottleneck, and it hands you the number that says so.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. MacLeod et al., Science Advances 6, eaaz8867 (2020),
  the Ada self-driving laboratory, maximised hole mobility of organic hole
  transport materials by iterating ink mixing, spin coating, thermal annealing
  for variable time, imaging, spectroscopy and current-voltage measurement.
  Dopant ratio and annealing time are the paper's own axes and pseudomobility
  is its own reported quantity.
SHARED_MECHANISM: search_over_candidates
ROUTES: R-BACKEND, R-COMPOSE, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-BACKEND is missing an execution-backend capability, namely an
  adapter that hands a recipe to an instrument stack or a physics simulator and
  records its reading as a spec result, together with a provenance record,
  because a physical reading is not reproducible from seed_root and the fossil
  must record the observation rather than a recipe for recomputing it;
  R-COMPOSE is missing an adjudication capability, because the campaign-level
  question, did the optimiser cross threshold in fewer experiments than random
  sampling, compares two campaigns while the outcome rule is one field, one
  operator, one value; R-EXECUTOR is missing a measurement capability, because
  no existing payload can even express a pair of real-valued parameters, let
  alone return a continuous response.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs an external backend the engine calls, either
  a real instrument stack or a physics-based film model, and a provenance record
  binding each reading to the recipe, the instrument state and the operator. The
  bench would keep the loop, the budget and the fossil, and would have to accept
  results that are recorded rather than recomputable. Nothing about the
  sealed-spec architecture is wrong for this method; only the backend is absent,
  which is why this is cheap rather than deep.
REDUCTION: Encode a recipe as a single bitstring of length 16, eight bits of
  dopant ratio and eight bits of annealing time, in GRAY CODE (NEW DESIGN
  CHOICE). The Gray code is load-bearing rather than decorative: bench score is
  the fraction of matching positions, so Hamming-adjacent words are
  score-adjacent, and plain binary would place 0111 and 1000 far apart in the
  quantity the proposer sees while they are adjacent in the parameter they mean.
  Fix one world seed so every iteration faces one landscape, and run a
  producer-side closed loop of 24 single-repeat specs (NEW DESIGN CHOICE),
  proposing a word, reading the score, proposing the next. Pre-register the
  threshold at score at least 0.8125, which is 13 of 16, so it lies on the
  attainable 1/16 grid rather than between two reachable values.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: the closed loop itself, a hard experiment budget, one
  hidden response surface learnable only by sampling, and per-experiment
  fossilisation of the exact recipe and the exact reading.
  WHAT IT LOSES: all physics; all instrument noise, because the bench score is
  bit-deterministic, so replicates are exactly equal and the single most
  important real failure mode, irreproducible measurement, cannot occur at all;
  and the surrogate's real advantage, because the hidden target is a
  single-peaked additive landscape rather than a materials response surface.
  WHICH CLAIM: only that the orchestration is sound, that is, that a budgeted
  closed loop can be driven, fossilised and adjudicated end to end and that a
  proposer reaches a pre-registered attainable threshold within budget. Because
  the landscape is unimodal and noiseless this doubles as a CEILING check: a
  proposer that fails here is broken. It licenses nothing whatever about
  materials and must not be reported as an optimisation result.

TEMPLATE_ID: alife.tierra.soup.v0
FIELD: ALife-Inspired AI
KIND: tierra_soup_v0
QUESTION: If you put one hand-written self-copying program into a block of
  computer memory and let copying make occasional mistakes, does an ecology
  appear on its own, including creatures that live off other creatures?
MECHANISM: A region of virtual memory, the soup, is seeded with a single
  ancestor written in a purpose-built machine code, roughly eighty instructions
  long, whose whole job is to measure itself, allocate space and copy itself
  into it. A scheduler gives each running creature slices of CPU time. Errors
  are injected in two places, as background bit flips in the soup and as
  mistakes during copying. When memory fills, a reaper queue kills the oldest
  and the most error-prone creatures to make room. Nothing selects for anything
  in particular: the only currencies are memory space and CPU time, so whatever
  copies itself faster in the space available takes over. What is observed is
  that shorter creatures appear which have lost the copy loop and instead
  execute a neighbour's copy code, that is, parasites; then hosts that resist
  them; then hyper-parasites; then creatures that cheat cooperative
  arrangements.
CANDIDATE: A creature, that is, a contiguous block of executable instructions
  with its own instruction pointer. What varies is its length and its exact
  instruction sequence. It is judged by nothing external: it is judged by
  whether copies of it are still in memory later.
WORLD: A shared, mutable memory arena with a CPU scheduler and a reaper. The
  world supplies exactly two scarce resources, space and time, and one
  genotype-phenotype map, the instruction set, which decides what a mutated
  instruction sequence actually does.
WHAT_CHANGES: Soup size and mutation rate across runs; within a run, the
  contents of memory, the length distribution of creatures and the lineage
  tree, all of which change continuously.
MEASURED: A time series rather than a scalar: the histogram of creature lengths
  over time, the number of distinct genotypes, the lineage tree, and the
  appearance time of the first creature that cannot replicate in isolation,
  which is the operational definition of a parasite.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: A run in which nothing but the ancestor ever persists is a
  measurement of the INSTRUCTION SET rather than of evolution: it says the
  ancestor sits in a mutational neighbourhood that is almost entirely lethal,
  and the run hands you the lethal fraction, the neutral fraction, and the
  specific instructions whose mutation kills. That is a reusable property of the
  substrate and it is arguably the more valuable of the two outcomes, because it
  is the number that predicts whether any related design will work.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Ray, T. S., An Approach to the Synthesis of Life, in
  Artificial Life II, Addison-Wesley, 1991, is the correct and canonical
  reference for Tierra, the memory soup, the roughly eighty-instruction
  ancestor, the reaper, and the emergence of parasites and hyper-parasites. This
  one was not re-checked online because the session's web-search budget was
  exhausted; the attribution is standard and unambiguous in the ALife
  literature.
SHARED_MECHANISM: evolve_population
ROUTES: R-BACKEND, R-SUBSTRATE, R-WORLD
BLOCKER: R-BACKEND is missing an execution-backend capability, an adapter that
  runs an external simulator binary under a declared seed and captures its
  output stream as a fossilised result; this is the cheap route and it is not
  hypothetical, since the repository already carries an ergon/avida2003 tree and
  previously recovered mid-1990s digital-evolution genomes verified by
  execution. R-SUBSTRATE is missing a state capability, specifically a shared
  mutable arena persisting across a whole run and, harder, inter-candidate
  access, since one creature must be able to execute another's memory. R-WORLD
  is missing a world capability: one integer seed_root is the entire world, and
  there is no population, no arena, no scheduler and no reaper.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs a persistent memory arena, a time-slicing
  scheduler over many concurrently executing programs, an instruction set with a
  real genotype-phenotype map, mutation applied both to stored memory and to
  copy operations, and a reaper. The observation is a time series and a lineage
  tree rather than one number, so the outcome rule would have to adjudicate a
  derived scalar computed downstream, for instance the appearance time of the
  first non-self-sufficient replicator.
REDUCTION: NONE, and the reason is specific rather than general. Tierra's whole
  content lives in the genotype-phenotype map: a bit flip changes an
  instruction, which changes what a program DOES, which is why mutations can be
  lethal, neutral or parasitic. The bench's only landscape has no
  genotype-phenotype map at all, because score is the fraction of positions
  matching a hidden target, so every position contributes independently and
  additively. Consequently the obvious reduction, submit an ancestor word plus
  all of its single-bit mutants and profile the neighbourhood, measures a
  constant that is known before the run: exactly L minus m of the L flips
  improve the score by 1/L and m of them worsen it by 1/L, where m is the count
  of already-matching positions, and the neutral fraction is identically zero.
  It cannot detect lethality, neutrality, robustness or evolvability, because
  none of those exist on an additive landscape. Separately, no reduction on this
  bench can produce a parasite at all, because a parasite is one candidate
  executing another candidate's code and a spec contains exactly one candidate
  with no means of referring to a second.
  WHAT SURVIVES SHORT OF THE METHOD, and it is worth building: the bench is the
  correct NULL ARM for an external run. If Tierra or Avida is run under
  R-BACKEND, the matching-fraction landscape answers the question what does
  mutation plus selection achieve when the genotype-phenotype map is the
  identity, which is the baseline any claim of emergent complexity has to beat.
  That is a control for this template, not an instance of it, and it must be
  labelled as such.

TEMPLATE_ID: bayesian_utility.v0
FIELD: Bayesian Experimental Design
KIND: bayesian_utility_v0
QUESTION: Before collecting any data at all, how much would a planned study of a
  given size be expected to teach us, and is that enough to be worth doing?
MECHANISM: Write down what you already believe as a prior distribution over the
  unknown parameters, and write down how data would be generated from those
  parameters as a likelihood. For a candidate design, such as a sample size or a
  set of measurement points, imagine every dataset the design could produce,
  weighted by how likely each is under your current beliefs. For each imagined
  dataset compute the posterior you would end up with and measure how far it has
  moved from the prior, in the Shannon sense. Average that movement over all
  imagined datasets. The average is the expected information gain, which is the
  design's utility. Under a conjugate normal model this integral is closed form;
  otherwise it is estimated by nested Monte Carlo. No data is ever collected.
CANDIDATE: A proposed design, here parameterised by sample size, judged by its
  expected utility. Nothing is fitted and nothing is observed.
WORLD: An assumed statistical model. It supplies the prior, the prior variance,
  the likelihood and the measurement noise level. The world here is entirely
  stipulated, which is at once the method's strength and its exposure.
WHAT_CHANGES: Sample size and prior variance across runs.
MEASURED: Expected information gain, a non-negative continuous quantity in nats
  or bits, one number per design.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: If utility stays under threshold across the whole grid of
  sample sizes, the failure is diagnostic and not merely negative: it says the
  design family cannot be rescued by spending more, because the bottleneck is
  the model or the noise rather than the budget. What is left behind is the
  utility-versus-sample-size curve, which can be inverted to give the smallest n
  that would ever suffice, or to prove that no n does.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Chaloner and Verdinelli, Bayesian Experimental Design: A
  Review, Statistical Science 10(3), 1995, is the standard review and it covers
  Shannon-information utility and the normal-model closed forms in which prior
  variance and sample size are the natural axes. The template's axes match the
  source's own framing.
SHARED_MECHANISM: compute_closed_form_criterion
ROUTES: R-EXECUTOR, R-BACKEND, R-COMPOSE
BLOCKER: R-EXECUTOR is missing a measurement capability and nothing else: no
  executor accepts a model description and returns a scalar, and no payload
  schema can carry a prior and a likelihood. Note what is NOT blocking: no
  state, no control flow, no population, no chaining, and the outcome rule
  already fits, since expected information gain at least some threshold is
  exactly one field, one operator, one value. This is among the cheapest
  templates in the chunk. R-BACKEND is missing an execution-backend capability,
  an adapter to an existing estimator library. R-COMPOSE is missing an
  adjudication capability, but only for the robustness question, since comparing
  utility across several priors is an aggregation across specs.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs a payload that can carry a model, that is, a
  prior with its hyperparameters, a likelihood family and a design, and an
  executor returning expected information gain either in closed form for the
  conjugate case or by nested Monte Carlo otherwise, with the estimator's own
  bias reported beside the estimate. It is stateless and single-scalar, so it
  requires no substrate change at all.
REDUCTION: A real information-gain measurement is available today with no new
  executor, because the bench already contains a hidden quantity and a query
  that partially reveals it. The hidden target is exactly L bits of unknown
  information under a uniform prior. Treat the DESIGN as the pair of query
  length L and query count R. Fix one candidate bitstring, set repeat count to R
  with seed_derivation sha256_index so that every repeat faces an independently
  drawn target, and record the score distribution. Under this design the number
  of matching positions is Binomial with parameters L and one half, so the
  mutual information between one observed score and the hidden target is
  computable directly from the observed distribution and can be compared against
  its analytic value, which is the entropy of that binomial, of order one half
  the logarithm of L, against a prior entropy of L bits. Sweep L over 8, 16, 32
  and 64 with R equal to 200 (NEW DESIGN CHOICES).
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: information gain as the utility; a design, namely query
  length and query count, as the object judged; and the method's central
  distinction between the pre-experimental expectation, computed analytically,
  and the realised distribution, measured. It also preserves the saturation
  phenomenon, since information per query grows only logarithmically in L while
  the unknown grows linearly in L.
  WHAT IT LOSES: the prior is not user-supplied but fixed by construction to
  uniform over two-to-the-L targets; the likelihood is noiseless and therefore
  degenerate; and there is no way to misspecify the prior, so the robustness
  question, which is exactly the one the template already disclaims, remains
  untouchable.
  WHICH CLAIM: expected information gain per query for this one design family,
  and its saturation with L. It supports a calibration of the bench's own
  information geometry and a check that analytic and measured utility agree,
  which is a genuine instrument test. It supports no claim about any real study
  design.

TEMPLATE_ID: comp_phil_sci.echo.v0
FIELD: Computational Philosophy of Science
KIND: echo_coherence_network.v0
QUESTION: When two scientific theories compete over the same body of evidence,
  can the choice between them be reproduced by a simple mechanical process that
  only balances which theory explains more and contradicts less?
MECHANISM: Every proposition, whether hypothesis or evidence, becomes a unit in
  a network. If one hypothesis, possibly together with others, explains a
  proposition, symmetric excitatory links are created among all of them, with
  the weight divided among co-hypotheses so that explaining something with fewer
  assumptions pays better. If two propositions contradict, an inhibitory link is
  created. Evidence units also receive an excitatory link from a special
  always-on data unit, which is how observation gets its privileged pull.
  Activation is then updated in parallel over many cycles by a standard
  connectionist rule until activations stop changing. Units settling positive
  are accepted, units settling negative are rejected. The whole thing is
  parallel constraint satisfaction with no search and no explicit inference
  rules.
CANDIDATE: A hypothesis unit. What varies is its final activation, and what is
  judged is whether it is accepted, which depends on the whole network rather
  than on the unit alone.
WORLD: A hand-encoded corpus of propositions and explanatory relations, for
  example Lavoisier's oxygen case against phlogiston, or Darwin against special
  creation. The world supplies the network topology, and it is worth stating
  plainly that the encoding is the analyst's, which is where most of the
  method's leverage and most of its criticism both live.
WHAT_CHANGES: The number of hypothesis units, the number of evidence units and
  the number of update cycles allowed.
MEASURED: A settled activation per unit, continuous and bounded between minus
  one and one, plus whether the network settled at all within the cycle budget.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: A network that does not settle, or that accepts both ends
  of an inhibitory link, is a concrete artefact: you keep the exact link
  structure that produced the degeneracy and it becomes a permanent hard case
  for any future coherence model. Better still, a run whose accepted set FLIPS
  when the cycle budget is raised proves the reported result was never a fixed
  point, which falsifies that run's use as an adjudicator without any
  philosophical argument at all.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Thagard, Explanatory Coherence, Behavioral and Brain
  Sciences 12 (1989), pages 435 to 467, presents the seven principles and the
  connectionist implementation named ECHO, encoding coherence and incoherence as
  excitatory and inhibitory links and treating hypothesis evaluation as
  constraint satisfaction, applied to Lavoisier and to Darwin. The template's
  three axes, hypotheses, evidence and cycles, correspond to real dimensions of
  the model.
SHARED_MECHANISM: relax_constraint_network
ROUTES: R-EXECUTOR, R-BACKEND, R-COMPOSE
BLOCKER: R-EXECUTOR is missing both a measurement capability and a control-flow
  capability: the payload schema cannot carry a graph, and there is no
  fixed-point iteration anywhere in the bench, the only loop being the fixed
  step count of the random walk, which has no attractor. R-COMPOSE is missing an
  adjudication capability, because the interesting verdict is an ORDERING over
  hypothesis activations and the outcome rule forbids any ordering or two-field
  test. R-BACKEND is missing an execution-backend capability, though the
  executor here is small enough that the backend route is probably the wrong
  one.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version is a small executor, on the order of a hundred
  lines, taking a proposition list and a relation list as payload, building the
  weight matrix, running the update rule to convergence or to a declared cycle
  cap, and returning per-unit activations plus a settled flag. It needs no
  persistent state and no population. What it does need that the bench lacks is
  a payload able to carry structured input, and an outcome rule able to compare
  two activations, since accepted-over-its-rival is intrinsically a two-field
  question.
REDUCTION: Marginal, and it must be labelled a control rather than an instance.
  The one component of ECHO the current bench can genuinely exercise is the
  BUDGET-TRUNCATION HAZARD, the risk of reading a verdict off an iterative
  process that has not converged. Use random_walk_v0 with repeat state persist
  and seed_derivation linear_index, and run the same configuration at a small
  cycle budget and a large one, for instance steps of 50 against steps of 5000
  at fixed step_scale, over 5 seeds (NEW DESIGN CHOICES; 5 seeds because a
  single seed cannot support a variance claim). Apply the same displacement
  threshold at both budgets and record how often the two budgets disagree.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: only that a verdict read from an unconverged iterative
  process is a function of the budget, and that the fossil must therefore record
  the budget beside the verdict.
  WHAT IT LOSES: coherence, explanation, inhibition, competition between rival
  hypotheses, and, decisively, convergence itself, since ECHO settles to a fixed
  point whereas a random walk is a martingale that never settles. The reduction
  therefore yields an UPPER BOUND on truncation instability, not a model of
  ECHO.
  WHICH CLAIM: a methodological one only, that verdicts read at cycle budget C
  are budget-sensitive and here is the magnitude for a maximally unstable
  process. It supports nothing in philosophy of science. Anyone wanting the
  actual template should write the hundred-line executor instead; this reduction
  is honest but thin, and calling it an ECHO experiment would be a gesture.

TEMPLATE_ID: d_optimal_design.v0
FIELD: Optimal Experimental Design
KIND: d_optimal_design_v0
QUESTION: Given that you can only afford a fixed number of measurements, where
  should you put them so that the model you intend to fit comes out as sharply
  determined as possible?
MECHANISM: Fix a model, for example a polynomial of a chosen degree, and fix the
  region over which measurements may be taken. A design is a specific list of
  measurement locations. Build the model matrix whose rows are the model's basis
  functions evaluated at those locations, and form the information matrix as its
  transpose times itself. The volume of the resulting confidence ellipsoid for
  the fitted coefficients shrinks as the determinant of that matrix grows, so
  the determinant, usually scaled by the number of parameters and expressed as
  an efficiency between zero and one, is the design's score. Exchange algorithms
  improve a design by repeatedly swapping one measurement location for another
  and keeping the swap if the determinant rises.
CANDIDATE: An exact design, that is, a particular set of n measurement
  locations. It is judged by the determinant of its information matrix, or
  equivalently by its D-efficiency relative to the theoretical continuous
  optimum.
WORLD: The assumed model and the design region. The world supplies the
  regression basis and the boundaries within which measurements may be placed.
  No data is generated and no responses are observed; the criterion depends only
  on where you would measure, not on what you would find.
WHAT_CHANGES: The number of design points and the polynomial degree.
MEASURED: The determinant of the information matrix, or the D-efficiency in the
  range zero to one; a single continuous number per design.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: A design whose determinant is zero is not a null result, it
  is a proof: the design is singular and at least one model coefficient is not
  estimable from it at all. The failure leaves the rank deficiency and hence
  exactly which parameter combination is inestimable and why, whether because
  there are fewer support points than parameters or because support points
  coincide. That is reusable and it also protects downstream work, since fitting
  a model on a singular design produces confident nonsense.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Atkinson and Donev, Optimum Experimental Designs,
  Clarendon Press, Oxford, 1992, Oxford Statistical Science Series volume 8, is
  the standard monograph; it contains chapters on D-optimum designs and on
  algorithms for constructing exact D-optimum designs, and design size and
  polynomial degree are its own working axes.
SHARED_MECHANISM: compute_closed_form_criterion
ROUTES: R-EXECUTOR, R-BACKEND
BLOCKER: R-EXECUTOR is missing a measurement capability and nothing else: no
  executor accepts a list of design points and a model degree as payload, and
  none returns a determinant. There is no state gap, no control-flow gap and no
  adjudication gap whatever, because d_efficiency at least 0.95 is already
  precisely one field, one operator, one value. This is the single cheapest
  template in the chunk, blocked by one missing measurement. R-BACKEND is
  missing an execution-backend capability, an adapter to an existing optimal
  design library, but the calculation is small enough that the adapter would
  cost more than the executor.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version is roughly thirty lines: accept a point list and a
  degree, build the model matrix, compute the scaled determinant, return
  d_efficiency together with the matrix rank so that the singular case is
  reported rather than silently returning zero. Because the criterion is purely
  deterministic and depends on no observed response, the sealed-spec model fits
  it exactly, and repeat count above one under a constant seed would be
  correctly flagged as degenerate by construction.
REDUCTION: NONE for the criterion itself, with a precise reason. A design over a
  discretised region can indeed be written as an indicator bitstring, one bit
  per candidate support point, which is how exchange algorithms are commonly
  coded, so the ENCODING reduces cleanly. What does not reduce is the SCORE.
  evaluate_bitstring returns the fraction of positions matching a hash-derived
  target, which is a fixed additive function of the bits; D-efficiency is a
  determinant of a matrix built from the selected points, which is
  irreducibly non-additive because it is precisely a measure of how far the
  chosen points are from being collinear. No choice of seed or length makes an
  additive function equal a determinant, and the seed cannot be steered because
  the target is a hash. The nearest useful thing is not a bitstring caricature
  but the thirty-line executor named above.
  WHAT DOES SURVIVE, and it is worth running: the bench can supply the NULL for
  a design search. Run a producer-side exchange search over indicator bitstrings
  on the matching-fraction landscape at the same budget the real exchange
  algorithm would use, and record the best-of-n distribution. That is the
  correct null for the claim that an exchange algorithm exploited structure
  rather than merely sampling a lot, and because the bench landscape is additive
  and single-peaked it gives the strongest possible version of that null, in the
  sense that any search doing worse than it is doing worse than a search on a
  landscape with no interaction terms at all.
  WHICH CLAIM: the surviving fragment supports only a statement about the search
  procedure's budget efficiency, never about optimality of any design.
END_ENTRY

TEMPLATE_ID: evo_epistemology.bvsr.v0
FIELD: Evolutionary Epistemology
KIND: cumulative_bitstring.v0
QUESTION: Can new knowledge be produced by a process that generates its guesses
  blindly, with no foresight about which will work, provided that the successful
  guesses are kept and built on?
MECHANISM: The claim being tested is that all genuine increases in knowledge
  require three things and only those three: variation that is blind, meaning
  uncorrelated with which variant will turn out to succeed; a selection step
  that culls variants against a criterion the variant-generator cannot see; and
  retention, so that surviving variants become the starting point for the next
  round of variation rather than being discarded. Foresight, if it exists, is
  then explained as the compressed residue of earlier rounds of the same
  process, not as an extra ingredient. Operationally the model is run by
  perturbing a current best guess, testing it, keeping the perturbation only if
  the test improved, and repeating under a fixed budget; the comparison of
  interest is against the same process with retention switched off.
CANDIDATE: A candidate idea, encoded as a bitstring. What varies is its bits and
  what is judged is its score against a criterion the generator has no access
  to.
WORLD: The selection criterion, that is, the environment that decides which
  guesses survive. It supplies exactly one thing, a verdict on a candidate, and
  crucially it never supplies a gradient or a hint about the next guess.
WHAT_CHANGES: The mutation rate and the string length across runs, and, as the
  central manipulated variable, whether retention is switched on, giving
  cumulative selection, or off, giving independent resampling.
MEASURED: Best score reached at a fixed evaluation budget, continuous between
  zero and one, or equivalently the number of evaluations to first reach the
  target.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: A run that never reaches the target still leaves the
  best-so-far curve against evaluation count, which localises exactly where
  blind variation stalls. Sweeping the mutation rate leaves the more valuable

TEMPLATE_ID: illumination.grid.v0
FIELD: Illumination Algorithms
KIND: illuminate_space.v0
QUESTION: Instead of asking what is the single best solution, can a search
  return a whole map showing the best solution of every distinct kind, and how
  much of that map can it actually fill?
MECHANISM: Choose a small number of descriptive features that characterise how a
  solution behaves rather than how well it scores, and cut that behaviour space
  into a grid of bins. Keep an archive holding at most one solution per bin, the
  best found so far for that bin. Repeat: draw a solution at random from the
  archive, mutate it, evaluate it to get both a performance score and a
  behaviour descriptor, find the bin the descriptor falls in, and place the new
  solution there if the bin is empty or if it beats the incumbent. The output is
  the entire archive, summarised as coverage, the fraction of bins filled, and
  as a quality-diversity score summing performance over filled bins. Because
  elites in one bin are the parents of solutions in other bins, an archive acts
  as a set of stepping stones and can reach places that direct optimisation
  cannot.
CANDIDATE: A genome. It is judged on two channels at once, a scalar performance
  and a behaviour descriptor vector, and the second channel decides where it is
  allowed to compete.
WORLD: A domain rich enough to supply both channels, classically a walking robot
  where performance is distance travelled and behaviour is the fraction of time
  each foot spends on the ground. The world must supply a behaviour
  characterisation that is not simply a function of performance, or the grid
  collapses.
WHAT_CHANGES: Grid resolution, at 10, 20 or 50 bins per axis, and the evaluation
  budget between 1000 and 10000. Note that these two values are present and real
  in this template rather than lost to source markers.
MEASURED: Coverage, the fraction of bins filled, between zero and one; the
  quality-diversity score; and the maximum performance found. Coverage is the
  one the outcome rule would use.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Coverage that saturates well below one is not a failed run,
  it is the result. The empty bins localise which combinations of behaviour are
  never realised, and the follow-up question, whether they are structurally
  impossible or merely unfound, is exactly the distinction that matters. A run
  at two resolutions separates the two: bins empty at coarse resolution are
  candidates for impossibility, whereas bins empty only at fine resolution are
  candidates for budget starvation. This maps directly onto the standing
  Prometheus posture that a gate must be shown reachable before a null is read.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Mouret and Clune, Illuminating Search Spaces by Mapping
  Elites, 2015, introduces MAP-Elites with exactly this archive, binning,
  coverage and quality-diversity structure, and grid resolution and evaluation
  budget are its own axes.
SHARED_MECHANISM: maintain_archive
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-SUBSTRATE, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE is missing an adjudication capability, because coverage is an
  aggregation over many specs and the outcome rule permits no aggregation.
  R-SUBSTRATE is missing a state capability, an archive persisting across specs
  and readable by the next spec's proposer. R-EXECUTOR is missing a measurement
  capability, namely a second output channel: evaluate_bitstring returns

FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs an executor that returns a behaviour

TEMPLATE_ID: lm_guided_proof.v0
FIELD: Proof Search
KIND: formal_prover.v0
QUESTION: Can a language model trained on mathematical proofs suggest the next
  step well enough that a machine, checking every suggestion against a strict
  logical kernel, closes proofs it could not close by guessing?
MECHANISM: A formal proof assistant holds a goal and a library of previously
  proved statements, and can check whether a proposed step is legal. A
  transformer, trained on a corpus of formal proofs, is shown an open goal and
  samples candidate next steps. A best-first search keeps a frontier of open
  goals, repeatedly picks the most promising one according to a learned value
  estimate, samples proposals for it, submits each to the kernel, discards the
  illegal ones and adds the resulting subgoals to the frontier. The search stops
  when the goal is closed or when a fixed number of node expansions is spent.
  Sampling temperature controls how varied the proposals are. The kernel is
  never trusted to the model: nothing the model says is accepted without a
  check, which is why a wrong model wastes budget but cannot produce a wrong
  proof.
CANDIDATE: A proof attempt, that is, the tree of tactic applications built
  during the search. It is judged on one thing, whether the kernel accepts a
  complete proof within the expansion budget.
WORLD: The formal library and its kernel. The world supplies the goal, the set
  of legal moves, and, decisively, an incorruptible verdict on every proposed
  step.
WHAT_CHANGES: The node expansion budget and the sampling strategy, here three
  temperature settings.
MEASURED: Closed or not closed, a binary fact, plus the number of expansions
  consumed and the length of the resulting proof, both integers.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: A failed proof leaves the entire search tree, which is the
  valuable artefact. Every expanded goal is a labelled example, every rejected
  step is a labelled negative, and together they map where the model's
  proposals are illegal versus legal-but-useless, which are different diseases
  needing different fixes. The frontier of goals still open at exhaustion often
  names the missing lemma directly, and that is a concrete, actionable output of
  a run that proved nothing.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Polu and Sutskever, Generative Language Modeling for
  Automated Theorem Proving, 2020, the GPT-f system, uses a transformer to
  propose proof steps inside a formal environment under a best-first search with
  an expansion budget and a sampling temperature, so the template's two axes are
  the paper's own. The specific strategy labels in the template's choices list
  are the miner's naming convention, not the paper's.
SHARED_MECHANISM: search_over_tree
ROUTES: R-BACKEND, R-SUBSTRATE, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-BACKEND is missing an execution-backend capability, an adapter that
  invokes an external prover and an external model, captures structured output,
  and fossilises it; and it additionally needs a provenance capability, because
  at temperature above zero the run is not reproducible from seed_root alone, so
  the fossil must record the sampled outputs rather than a recipe for
  regenerating them. R-SUBSTRATE is missing a state capability, since best-first
  search is output-to-input chaining over a persistent frontier. R-EXECUTOR is
  missing a measurement capability, since no payload schema can carry a formal
  goal or a library reference.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version orchestrates two external services, a proof
  assistant and a model endpoint, records every proposal and every kernel
  verdict, and reports closed-or-not with expansions consumed. The bench's
  sealed-spec architecture is not wrong here at all, but its determinism
  assumption is: the fossil contract has to be able to say this observation was
  recorded and cannot be recomputed, which is the same requirement the physical
  laboratory template raises.
REDUCTION: A real but strictly limited reduction exists, and its honest label is
  harness validation. The transferable kernel is a budgeted search against a
  verifier that cannot be fooled, and the bench has exactly that: the target is

  reaching solved, meaning score at least 1.0, at length 24 under a fixed seed,
  let one expansion be one spec, cap the budget at 200 specs, and run two arms,
  a model-guided proposer that sees the score history and proposes the next
  word, and a uniform proposer that does not (NEW DESIGN CHOICES).
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: budgeted search against a sound and independent verifier,
  the guided-versus-unguided contrast at matched budget, per-expansion
  fossilisation, and the exact success metric, closed within budget. It also
  preserves the property that makes the method safe, that a wrong proposal costs
  budget and nothing else.

  lemma reuse, no compositional subgoals, so there is nothing to be

  win for a reason that has nothing to do with proof guidance, and reporting
  that win as evidence about theorem proving would be a claim inflation of

  WHICH CLAIM: only that the bench can drive, fossilise and adjudicate a
  budgeted proposer-verifier loop, and that it detects a guidance advantage
  where one exists. It licenses nothing about mathematics or about language
  models. The honest route for this template is R-BACKEND, which is cheap, and
  the reduction should be run only as a harness test before that backend is

TEMPLATE_ID: mt.relation.eval.v0
FIELD: Metamorphic Testing
KIND: evaluate_metamorphic_v0
QUESTION: How do you test a program when nobody knows what the right answer is,
  and you therefore have nothing to compare its output against?
MECHANISM: Give up on knowing the correct output and instead state a relation
  that must hold between the outputs of two related runs. Pick an input, run the
  program, then transform the input in a way whose effect on the output is known
  by construction, run the program again, and check the relation. Classic
  examples are that the sine of an angle equals the sine of its supplement, or
  that adding a constant to every element of a dataset must shift the reported
  mean by exactly that constant, or that shuffling the input rows of a
  classifier's training set should leave a deterministic classifier's output
  unchanged. The relation is asserted between two executions; neither execution
  is ever independently checked. When the relation is violated, the pair of
  inputs is a failing witness with no ambiguity about whether a bug exists.
CANDIDATE: The pair of inputs, an original and its transform. What is being
  judged is not the pair but the PROGRAM, through its consistency across the
  pair. This inversion is the method's distinguishing feature.
WORLD: The program under test together with the chosen relation. The world
  supplies the transformation and the semantics that make the relation
  necessary; it supplies no ground truth at all, which is the whole point.
WHAT_CHANGES: The base input, here one of two eight-bit words, and the mutation
  mask deciding which single position is flipped.
MEASURED: Whether the relation held, a binary fact, and usually also the
  magnitude of the violation, which is continuous and is what tells you whether
  you have found a rounding artefact or a real defect.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: This template has the strongest informative-failure story
  in the chunk, because the failure IS the product. A violated relation hands
  you a minimal failing witness, a concrete input pair, that can be pinned as a
  permanent regression test and that localises the defect without any oracle
  ever being constructed. A run in which the relation always holds leaves much
  less, and, per the standing posture that an invariance null is one-sided, it
  proves only equivariance under that transformation and never correctness.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Chen, T. Y., Cheung, S. C. and Yiu, S. M., Metamorphic
  Testing: A New Approach for Generating Next Test Cases, Technical Report
  HKUST-CS98-01, Department of Computer Science, Hong Kong University of Science
  and Technology, 1998. The attribution and the oracle-problem framing are
  correct; note only that the source is a technical report rather than a
  conference paper, which is how it is usually cited.
SHARED_MECHANISM: compare_paired_outputs
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE is missing an adjudication capability and only that. Both
  halves of the experiment run today on evaluate_bitstring; what cannot happen
  inside the bench is the comparison, because the relation is a function of two
  result fields drawn from two different specs while the outcome rule is one
  field, one operator, one value. R-EXECUTOR is missing a measurement
  capability, in the narrow sense that an evaluate_metamorphic kind performing
  both evaluations internally and returning a single scalar relation_violation
  would make the existing one-scalar outcome rule legal again; that is a small
  wrapper rather than new machinery.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs either a two-run executor returning a
  violation magnitude, or a downstream comparator over paired fossils, plus a
  registry of pre-registered relations so that the relation is fixed before the
  data is seen. It needs no state, no population and no new world. The
  discipline that matters is not technical: the relation must be committed
  before the runs, or the method degenerates into hunting for whatever happens
  to hold.
REDUCTION: This template barely needs a reduction, because the method can be
  applied AS ITSELF, today, against a real program: the bench's own
  evaluate_bitstring. That makes it the closest thing in the chunk to R-NOW.
  Since the target is the hidden word and the score is the fraction of matching
  positions, the following relations are necessary and can be pre-registered
  before any run. First, SINGLE FLIP: for two specs with the same seed and the
  same length whose bits differ in exactly one position, the absolute score
  difference must equal exactly 1/length. Second, COMPLEMENT: complementing
  every position must give score_B equal to 1 minus score_A. Third,
  DETERMINISM: identical bits, length and derived seed must give bit-identical
  scores across specs. Fourth, and this one is a genuine defect hunt rather than
  a formality, LENGTH MISMATCH: the kind contract declares params exactly bits
  and length and does not require the two to agree, while the engine's scorer
  counts matches over the shorter of the candidate and the target but divides by
  the target length. Two relations follow that a correct implementation should
  satisfy and that a careless one will not: appending a position to bits can
  never decrease the score, and a candidate shorter than length can never reach
  solved, since its score is capped at its own length over the target length.
  Run these over 64 sampled pairs at lengths 8, 16 and 24, over 5 seeds, and
  compare downstream (NEW DESIGN CHOICES).
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: the entire method, oracle-free testing, a relation over an
  input pair, a minimal failing witness, and a real program under test. This is
  an instance, not a caricature.
  WHAT IT LOSES: only generality. The relations available are bit-level rather
  than semantic, and metamorphic testing draws most of its power from rich
  transformations of structured inputs, which this program has none of.
  WHICH CLAIM: that evaluate_bitstring satisfies, or does not satisfy, the four
  pre-registered relations, and specifically whether the unconstrained bits and
  length pairing is a live contract gap. That is a useful and immediately
  actionable quality result about the bench's own instrument. Consistent with
  the one-sided-null posture, passing licenses only equivariance under these
  transformations; failing is a proof of defect.

TEMPLATE_ID: pbt_hyperparam_schedule.v0
FIELD: Population-Based Training
KIND: pbt_optimize_v0
QUESTION: Instead of choosing one learning rate and living with it, can a group
  of models being trained side by side discover, on the fly, a whole SCHEDULE of
  settings that changes as training proceeds?
MECHANISM: Train a population of models in parallel, each with its own
  hyperparameters. Every fixed number of steps, evaluate all of them. In the
  exploit step, a poorly performing worker abandons its own run and copies both
  the weights and the hyperparameters of a well-performing worker. In the
  explore step it then perturbs the copied hyperparameters. Training resumes
  from the copied weights, not from scratch, which is what makes the method
  cheap and what makes its product a schedule: following one surviving worker's
  ancestry backwards yields a sequence of hyperparameter values indexed by
  training step, and that sequence typically beats any constant setting. Total
  compute is the same as running the population independently.
CANDIDATE: A worker, that is, the pair of a weight vector and a hyperparameter
  vector. It is judged by its validation performance at each checkpoint, and a
  poor judgement causes it to be overwritten rather than merely down-weighted.
WORLD: A training task with a dataset, a loss and a validation metric, plus a
  compute budget shared across the whole population. The world must be
  expensive enough for the copy-weights trick to matter, which is why the method
  exists at all.
WHAT_CHANGES: Population size, the interval between exploit and explore rounds,
  and the perturbation applied to hyperparameters; within a run, the
  hyperparameter values themselves change over time, which is the object of
  interest.
MEASURED: Best validation metric against step or wall-clock budget, continuous,
  and the discovered schedule, which is a time series rather than a number.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: If the population fails to beat random search at matched
  total compute, the fossilised lineage tree says why, and this is a genuinely
  useful residue: counting distinct surviving ancestors over time shows whether
  the exploit step destroyed diversity, which is the standard failure mode. A
  collapse to a single ancestor early in training is a concrete, dated event
  that identifies the exploit threshold as the thing to change, rather than
  leaving you with an undifferentiated negative result.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Jaderberg et al., Population Based Training of Neural
  Networks, 2017, introduces exactly this exploit-and-explore population scheme
  producing hyperparameter schedules, and compares against random search and
  hand tuning; population size and the exploit-explore interval are the paper's
  own axes.
SHARED_MECHANISM: evolve_population
ROUTES: R-SUBSTRATE, R-WORLD, R-BACKEND, R-COMPOSE
BLOCKER: R-SUBSTRATE is missing a state capability, and specifically a stronger
  one than persistence: it needs inter-run state COPY, since exploit means one
  worker adopting another worker's carried state, and nothing in the bench lets
  one execution read another's. R-WORLD is missing a world capability, since one
  spec has one integer world and no population. R-BACKEND is missing an
  execution-backend capability, an adapter to a real trainer. R-COMPOSE is
  missing an adjudication capability, since faster than random search compares
  two campaigns.
  A precise secondary blocker is worth recording, because it is the reason the
  reduction below is limited. The only stateful kind is random_walk_v0, whose
  payload is exactly steps and step_scale. Within one spec, step_scale is fixed
  for every repeat, so no schedule can be expressed inside a spec. Across specs,
  start_position is a RETURNED field and not a payload parameter, so a walk
  cannot be resumed from where another walk ended. A hyperparameter schedule is
  therefore inexpressible in both directions at once, and that is a control-flow
  and state gap rather than a measurement gap.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs a population inside one world, per-worker
  persistent state, a copy operation between workers, periodic evaluation, and a
  fossil that records the lineage tree so that the discovered schedule can be
  read off it. The verdict can still be a single scalar computed downstream, so
  the outcome rule is not the obstacle; the substrate is.
REDUCTION: A partial reduction exists, and it is honest only if described as
  testing PBT's PREMISE rather than PBT. The premise is that no single constant
  hyperparameter value is best at every stage of training; if that is false there
  is nothing for a schedule to exploit. That premise is testable now. Run a grid
  of random_walk_v0 specs over step budgets of 50, 200, 1000 and 5000 crossed
  with step_scale values of 0.01, 0.1, 1.0 and 10.0, with seed_derivation
  linear_index and repeat count 20, and for each step budget find the step_scale
  that most often crosses a fixed absolute-displacement threshold (NEW DESIGN
  CHOICES). If the winning step_scale moves as the budget grows, the precondition
  for a schedule holds in this world; if it does not, a schedule has nothing to
  find here.
  One thing must be pinned before freezing, per the standing rule that a
  preregistered threshold needs its attainable range computed first: whether a
  persisted walk under a constant seed redraws the same increments each repeat.
  The design above sidesteps it by using linear_index, but the ambiguity should
  be resolved in the spec rather than left to the reader.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: the question of whether a time-varying setting can beat the
  best constant one; budget accounting; and the discipline of comparing against
  the best constant baseline rather than against a bad one.
  WHAT IT LOSES: the population, the exploit-and-copy operation, the weights,
  and the schedule itself, which is to say the entire PBT mechanism. It tests
  whether PBT would have anything to do, not whether PBT does it.
  WHICH CLAIM: only that the best constant step_scale for crossing displacement
  threshold D depends on the step budget. That is a precondition result, and
  labelling it a PBT result would be exactly the sort of claim inflation that

TEMPLATE_ID: sbse.hillclimb.search.v0
FIELD: Search-Based Software Engineering
KIND: sbse_hill_climb_v0
QUESTION: If you can write down what makes one version of a piece of software
  better than another as a single number, can a dumb local search find a good
  version for you?
MECHANISM: Reformulate a software engineering problem as optimisation. Encode
  the artefact as a string, for example one bit per test case included in a
  suite, or per module assigned to a cluster, or per requirement selected for a
  release. Define a fitness function from measurable properties such as coverage
  achieved, coupling reduced or cost incurred. Start from some candidate.
  Repeatedly evaluate the neighbours reachable by a single small change,
  typically all single-position flips; move to the best improving neighbour;
  stop when no neighbour improves, which certifies a local optimum; restart from
  a new starting point and repeat until the evaluation budget is exhausted. The
  method's real content is that the fitness function encodes a genuine
  engineering objective, and the search is deliberately unsophisticated.
CANDIDATE: A bitstring encoding of a software artefact, judged by the fitness
  function. What varies is the bits, and, across restarts, the starting point.
WORLD: The program or requirement set being optimised, together with whatever
  machinery evaluates fitness, which is often an actual test run. The world
  supplies the evaluation and, importantly, its cost, since fitness evaluations
  in this field are usually expensive.
WHAT_CHANGES: The initial candidate, here all-zeros or all-ones, and the maximum
  number of evaluations.
MEASURED: Best fitness found, continuous, and the evaluations consumed, an
  integer; across restarts, also the number of distinct local optima certified.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: A hill climb that stalls is not a wasted run: it leaves a
  certified local optimum together with the fitness of every neighbour that
  failed to beat it, which is a proof rather than a guess. Accumulated over
  restarts, the set of certified local optima and their basins is a direct
  measurement of the landscape's ruggedness, and that measurement is what
  decides whether a metaheuristic was ever the right tool. A field that reports
  only best-found values throws this away.

  Marked PLAUSIBLE on that distinction, not on any doubt about the citation. The
  citation could not be re-checked online because the session's search budget

  is an aggregation across specs that the one-scalar outcome rule forbids.
  R-REPAIR is listed because the template is also damaged, with max_evaluations
  null and no length declared while initial_candidate_bits is eight characters
  wide; choosing those is necessary but not sufficient, since the named kind
  still does not exist. R-SUBSTRATE is missing a state capability, since repeat
  N plus one cannot take repeat N's bits. R-EXECUTOR is missing measurement and
  control flow bundled, an sbse_hill_climb kind that runs the loop and returns
  best_score.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs a fitness function computed from a real
  artefact, which means an external backend running tests or static analysis; a
  producer or executor carrying the incumbent between evaluations; and a fossil
  that records every evaluation, not merely the best, because the discarded
  evaluations are where the ruggedness measurement lives.
REDUCTION: A reduction exists and runs today, but its honest verdict is that it
  is TOO EASY, and saying so is the useful finding. Fix one world seed and set
  length to 32 (NEW DESIGN CHOICE). Take an incumbent, submit all 32
  single-position flips as 32 single-repeat specs, move to the best improving
  neighbour or else certify a local optimum, and repeat from both starting
  points the template prescribes, all-zeros and all-ones, plus 8 random restarts
  at a budget of 640 evaluations each (NEW DESIGN CHOICES).
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: exactly steepest-ascent hill climbing, including full
  neighbourhood enumeration, the local-optimum certificate, restarts, the
  evaluation budget, and the two prescribed starting points.
  WHAT IT LOSES: the software artefact entirely, and with it the only thing that
  made the fitness function interesting. Worse, and this is the point: because
  the bench score is the fraction of positions matching a hidden target, the
  landscape is single-peaked and additive, so it has NO local optima other than
  the global one and steepest ascent reaches the optimum in at most 32 improving
  moves from any start. The restart machinery has nothing to find, the ruggedness
  measurement returns a constant, and the informative failure described above
  cannot occur.
  WHICH CLAIM: only that the producer-side hill-climb harness is correct, that
  is, it reaches the known optimum within the theoretical bound; a run that
  takes longer indicates a broken neighbourhood enumerator. That is worth having
  as a harness test and nothing more. To get real search-based content the bench
  needs a rugged landscape, which means either an NK-landscape or similar
  executor under R-EXECUTOR, or a real artefact under R-BACKEND. This is also a
  chunk-wide warning: several templates here, including ada_thin_film,
  illumination.grid and lm_guided_proof, would all appear to succeed on this
  landscape for the same reason, and success on a single-peaked additive
  landscape is a shared confound that must be disclosed wherever it applies.

TEMPLATE_ID: universal_darwinism.weasel.v0
FIELD: Universal Darwinism
KIND: cumulative_bitstring.v0
QUESTION: Is the difference between something being astronomically improbable
  and being easy just the difference between guessing it all at once and
  building it up in small steps that are each kept?
MECHANISM: Fix a target string. Start from a random string of the same length.
  Each generation, make a number of copies of the current string, mutate each
  copy at some per-position rate, score every copy by how many positions match
  the target, and keep the single best copy as the parent of the next
  generation. Repeat. The comparison the demonstration exists to make is against
  single-step selection, where the whole string is redrawn at random each time
  and nothing is retained: single-step selection needs on the order of the size
  of the whole space, while cumulative selection needs on the order of tens of
  generations. The gap between the two numbers, not the absolute time, is the
  entire result.
CANDIDATE: A string. What varies is its positions; it is judged by the count of
  positions matching the target.
WORLD: The target string plus the scoring rule. The world supplies one number
  per candidate and nothing else, in particular no indication of which positions
  are the matching ones.
WHAT_CHANGES: String length and per-position mutation rate across runs, and, as
  the manipulated variable of the demonstration, whether the best copy is
  retained between generations.
MEASURED: Generations to reach the target, an integer, or best match fraction at
  a fixed budget, continuous between zero and one.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: If cumulative selection also fails to reach the target, the
  run leaves the mutation rate at which it began failing, which is an error
  threshold: too high a rate destroys retained matches faster than selection
  restores them. That number is a real property of the length-and-rate pair and
  is reusable. The failure of the single-step arm, by contrast, leaves almost
  nothing beyond confirming a combinatorial count that could have been computed
  on paper, and it is worth saying that plainly, since half of this experiment
  has a genuinely uninformative failure mode.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. The weasel program appears in Richard Dawkins, The Blind
  Watchmaker, 1986, in the chapter contrasting single-step with cumulative
  selection, with the target phrase drawn from Hamlet, exactly the mechanism
  described. Not re-checked online because the session's search budget was
  exhausted; the attribution is unambiguous and universally cited.
SHARED_MECHANISM: search_over_candidates
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-SUBSTRATE, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE is missing a state capability in the bench, since retention
  between generations is output-to-input chaining that must be carried by the
  producer, and an adjudication capability at the verdict, since generations to
  target is an aggregation over specs. R-SUBSTRATE is missing a state
  capability, output-to-input chaining inside one spec. R-EXECUTOR is missing
  measurement and control flow bundled, a cumulative_bitstring kind that runs
  generations internally and returns generations_to_target.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs either a producer carrying the incumbent
  between specs, which exists today, or an executor running the generational
  loop and returning one scalar. It also needs both arms at matched evaluation
  budget, since the result is a ratio between two arms and a cumulative arm
  reported alone demonstrates nothing.
REDUCTION: This is the most faithfully reducible template in the chunk, and the
  reason is worth stating precisely: the bench's landscape IS the weasel
  landscape. The engine scores a candidate by the fraction of positions matching
  a fixed hidden target, which is exactly the weasel scoring rule, so no
  encoding, surrogate or analogy is required at all. Fix one world seed, set
  length to 64 and run two arms at a matched budget of 200 evaluations (NEW
  DESIGN CHOICES). Cumulative arm: a chain of single-repeat specs where each
  spec submits the incumbent with a per-position mutation applied, and the
  producer retains the new word only if the score improved. Single-step arm: 200
  independent random words against the same seed. Sweep the per-position
  mutation rate over 1/64, 2/64, 4/64 and 8/64 and repeat over 5 seeds. Compare
  best score at budget, and generations to solved where reached.
  A bench-specific caution applies: each rung must be its own spec with repeat
  count 1, because a constant seed meeting a stateless kind with state reset and
  count above one is flagged degenerate by construction, and the single-step arm
  must vary the BITS under one fixed seed rather than varying the seed, since
  varying the seed changes the target and would silently convert a search
  experiment into a landscape-sampling experiment.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: the mechanism in full, cumulative retention against
  single-step resampling, matched budget, the mutation-rate sweep, and the exact
  scoring rule.
  WHAT IT LOSES: the population within a generation, since only one candidate
  per spec exists, so the reduction runs a one-plus-one variant rather than a
  one-plus-N variant; that changes the constant in front of the generation count
  but not the qualitative gap the demonstration exists to show. It also loses
  nothing else of consequence.
  WHICH CLAIM: that cumulative selection reaches the target in a number of
  evaluations that is small in the string length while single-step selection
  does not, on a real hidden target. Notably the reduction is STRICTER than the
  original in one respect: Dawkins' experimenter knows the target, which is the
  standard criticism of the demonstration, whereas here the target is hidden by
  construction and the producer can only ever see a scalar. That removes the
  smuggled-answer objection rather than inheriting it, which makes this reduction
  worth running on its own merits and not only as an illustration.
END_ENTRY
