TEMPLATE_ID: alchemy.collision.v0
FIELD: Artificial Chemistry
KIND: lambda_collision.v0
QUESTION: When two elementary lambda-calculus combinators are applied to one another, does the resulting expression settle into a stable final form within a bounded number of rewriting steps, or does it keep rewriting forever?
MECHANISM: In Fontana's algorithmic chemistry, molecules are lambda terms and a chemical reaction is a collision. Pick two terms A and B from a reactor, form the application of A to B, then beta-reduce that application under a fixed reduction strategy, counting steps. If a normal form is reached before the step ceiling, the normal form is the product and it is injected back into the reactor, displacing a randomly chosen term. If the ceiling is hit first, the collision is declared elastic and nothing is produced. Iterating collisions over a well-stirred population is what eventually yields self-maintaining organizations; the single collision is the atomic event underneath all of that.
CANDIDATE: The ordered pair of combinators, left operand and right operand, drawn from I, K and S, together with the reduction budget. What is judged is whether that specific application halts and what term it halts on.
WORLD: An untyped lambda calculus with a fixed reduction order and a step ceiling. The world supplies the rewriting rules, the notion of a redex, the normal-form test, and the cutoff that turns non-termination into a decidable outcome.
WHAT_CHANGES: Which combinator sits on the left, which sits on the right, and max_reductions over the range 10 to 1000.
MEASURED: A halting boolean, an integer count of reduction steps consumed, and a term-valued product. Steps are on a bounded integer scale; the product is symbolic and not a number at all.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Useful. A collision that exhausts its budget names exactly which ordered pair diverges, and that catalogue of inert, productive and divergent pairs IS the reaction table of the chemistry. There is a second and sharper informative failure available here, and it fires before any code runs. Over the nine ordered pairs drawn from I, K and S, every application is either already in normal form because K and S are partially applied and contain no redex, or it reduces in a single step because I is applied to something. Nothing in this param space can consume more than one reduction. So the max_reductions axis from 10 to 1000 is inert across its entire range, and the template is degenerate by construction. That is the most valuable thing this template produces and it costs nothing to find.
SOURCE_CHECK: PLAUSIBLE. Fontana and Buss 1994 "The arrival of the fittest: toward a theory of biological organization" is a real paper and is the canonical statement of levels of self-maintaining organization in lambda-calculus chemistry, but the specific collide-and-beta-reduce operator described here is more precisely Fontana's earlier algorithmic-chemistry work; web search budget was exhausted this session and the exact attribution of the collision rule could not be confirmed.
SHARED_MECHANISM: interpret_program
ROUTES: R-EXECUTOR, R-REPAIR, R-SUBSTRATE
BLOCKER: R-EXECUTOR: missing measurement and missing control flow. No kind performs term rewriting, no kind can loop until a fixed point subject to a budget, and no kind can return a symbolic term as a result field. R-REPAIR: not a capability gap at all. The combinator alphabet I, K, S is a degenerate parameter choice whose whole range collapses the budget axis; the alphabet must be widened to terms with real reduction depth, for example self-application terms, before the experiment can discriminate anything. R-SUBSTRATE: missing state. Any claim about organization needs a reactor that reinjects products, which is output-to-input chaining plus a persistent population.
FAITHFUL: A beta-reduction engine over untyped lambda terms with a pluggable reduction order, a step counter, and a normal-form test, returning halted, steps and the normal-form term. The spec must be able to carry two term-valued payload fields and return a term-valued result, neither of which the current payload shapes allow. To say anything about self-maintaining organization on top of that, a population reactor with reinjection is required, which is a separate substrate capability.
REDUCTION: A real one survives, and it keeps the actual question rather than the lambda calculus. The question underneath is: does repeated application of an operator to its own output reach a stable form within a budget, and is that budget axis live or inert? The bench already contains one operator with exactly that shape, because evaluate_bitstring derives its hidden target from the seed and the length. Fix a length L. Define the producer-side map f taking a seed to the integer whose binary expansion is the hidden target at that seed and length L. Then chain: seed_0, f of seed_0, f of f of seed_0, and so on, one spec per application, each spec a trivial evaluate_bitstring whose returned bits expose the target. Measure the transient length before the sequence enters a cycle, and the cycle length, subject to a chain-length budget that plays the role of max_reductions. PRESERVES: iterated self-application of a deterministic operator, a halting-or-not verdict decided by a budget, the budget as the primary axis, and the seed-to-seed variation in transient length that makes the statistic non-trivial. LOSES: lambda calculus entirely, the structure of the product, any notion of a reactor or of self-maintaining organization, and therefore every claim the field cares about. CLAIM SUPPORTED: that halting-within-budget under iterated self-application has a measurable, seed-dependent distribution with a non-degenerate budget axis, and by direct contrast, that the template as written does not, since f-chaining on a hash is a near-permutation whose transients are short and whose budget axis is nearly as inert as the I, K, S alphabet. That contrast is the finding worth having before an executor is built. NEW DESIGN CHOICE: L equals 16, chain budget 256, 200 seed_0 values.

TEMPLATE_ID: automated_conjecture_hr.v0
FIELD: Automated Conjecture Generation
KIND: automated_conjecture_hr_v0
QUESTION: Starting from a small set of given concepts and a finite pile of example objects, how many non-trivial conjectures does an automated concept-inventing loop actually produce within a fixed search budget, and does narrowing the set of ways it is allowed to build new concepts change that yield?
MECHANISM: HR begins with core concepts and concrete example data, for example the integers up to some bound or a set of small finite groups. Production rules such as exists, match, negate, size, split and compose take existing concepts and manufacture new concept definitions. Every new concept is evaluated empirically by computing its extension, the set of example objects that satisfy it. Regularities among extensions are then read off as conjectures: two concepts with identical extensions give an equivalence conjecture, one extension contained in another gives an implication, an empty extension gives a non-existence conjecture. Concepts are scored by interestingness measures and the best are fed back to the production rules, so the theory is an archive that grows and reshapes what gets built next.
CANDIDATE: The invented concept, that is a definition assembled by applying a production rule to concepts already in the theory. It is judged twice, first empirically by its extension over the object set and second by interestingness heuristics that decide whether it re-enters the generator.
WORLD: A finite set of example objects plus the initial axioms and core concepts. The world's essential contribution is ground truth extensions: every conjecture is checked against actual objects, so the loop is empirical rather than deductive.
WHAT_CHANGES: Number of initial axioms, unknown because the source destroyed the value. Production rule set, full versus restrictive. Number of search steps, also unknown.
MEASURED: A count of emitted conjectures, a non-negative integer, ideally broken down by conjecture class. Secondarily the size and shape of the concept tree.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Strongly informative, and this is one of the better failure modes in the chunk. If yield collapses, what remains is the concept tree itself: which production rules fired, which produced concepts whose extensions duplicated an existing concept, which produced empty or full extensions, and where branching stopped. That is a direct map of where the generator's menu ran out, which is the bounded-menu question rather than a nuisance. A high yield under the restrictive rule set together with a low yield under the full set would be positive evidence that most of the full set's output is duplicate re-derivation, which is a result about the generator and not about mathematics.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Colton 2002 "Automated Theory Formation in Pure Mathematics" is confirmed in DBLP as a Springer Distinguished Dissertations volume by Simon Colton, and it is the standard reference for the HR system's theory-formation loop.
SHARED_MECHANISM: maintain_archive
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-SUBSTRATE, R-EXECUTOR, R-REPAIR
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE: missing adjudication. The producer can hold the theory and count conjectures downstream, but the outcome rule is one field, one operator, one value on a single spec, so a yield count aggregated over many specs cannot be adjudicated by the bench, only recorded. R-SUBSTRATE: missing state. The theory is an archive that grows during a run and each step's output is the next step's input; there is no archive and no output-to-input chaining. R-EXECUTOR: missing measurement. There is no concept datatype, no extension evaluator, and no result field that can carry a set or a count of emitted objects. R-REPAIR: initial_axioms and search_steps are null and must be chosen before admission; the axis names are real, the numbers are not recoverable.
FAITHFUL: A typed concept or definition datatype closed under a production-rule set, an extension evaluator over a fixed finite object set, a persistent theory store with an interestingness ranking, and a conjecture-emission predicate over pairs of extensions. The spec must be able to return a list of conjecture objects and a count, and the outcome must be able to threshold an aggregate, both of which the sealed one-scalar-comparison model forbids.
REDUCTION: A genuinely faithful small version exists, using the bitstring scorer as the extension oracle. Fix a length L. Define a concept as a mask, that is a subset of the L positions, and define its extension operationally as the score returned when the producer submits the bitstring that is all zeros except ones on the mask. Production rules are union, intersection and complement over two existing masks; the restrictive rule set is union only. The producer runs the HR loop outside the bench, one spec per concept, and the bench acts purely as the empirical world. A conjecture is an observed equality of extension between two concepts, and the crucial move is to use sha256_index repeats so that each repeat scores against an independent hidden target: an equality that survives across thirty independent landscapes is a structural fact about the mask algebra, not an accident of one target. Yield is then conjectures surviving per hundred concepts generated, measured under the full rule set and under the restrictive one. PRESERVES: concept invention by composition, empirical evaluation of extensions against real objects, conjecture as surviving regularity rather than proved statement, the yield-per-budget statistic, and the full-versus-restrictive contrast. LOSES: logic, quantification, mathematical objects, interestingness heuristics, and any claim that a conjecture is true rather than merely unfalsified over the sampled landscapes. CLAIM SUPPORTED: that production-rule breadth changes the rate of surviving empirical regularities per unit of search, and specifically that a large share of a broad generator's yield is duplicate re-derivation. PRE-REGISTERED NULL, and it must be computed before anything is frozen: on a hash target the score of a mask bitstring is essentially a function of mask cardinality alone, so almost every discovered equality will be explained by equal mask size. The only admissible conjectures are those that survive after conditioning on cardinality, and if none do, the reduction has shown that this substrate carries no structure for a conjecture generator to find, which is itself the useful answer. NEW DESIGN CHOICE: L equals 24, 30 sha256_index repeats, 400 concepts per rule set.

TEMPLATE_ID: cegis_boolean.v0
FIELD: Counterexample-Guided Inductive Synthesis
KIND: bounded_synthesizer.v0
QUESTION: Can a synthesis loop that repeatedly proposes a program and gets back one concrete failing input find a correct program before it runs out of attempts, and how does that depend on how big the space of programs is?
MECHANISM: The programmer writes a sketch, a program with holes. The synthesizer solves an inductive query: find hole values that make the program correct on the current finite set of concrete example inputs. That candidate goes to a verifier, typically a SAT or SMT solver, which checks it against the full specification over all inputs. If verification fails the verifier returns a concrete counterexample input, which is added to the example set, and the loop repeats with a strictly harder inductive query. The loop ends when verification succeeds or the iteration budget is exhausted. The essential property is that each failure permanently narrows the next proposal, so the example set is a monotone accumulation of hardness.
CANDIDATE: The completed program, that is the vector of hole values. It is judged by a verifier that returns a verdict and a witness, not by a graded score.
WORLD: A full specification, a sketch or grammar bounded by sketch_size, and a finite input domain. The world's essential supply is the verifier, because the counterexample and not the pass or fail is what drives the method.
WHAT_CHANGES: max_iterations, unknown because the source destroyed the value. sketch_size, also unknown.
MEASURED: Solved or not solved as a boolean, iterations consumed as an integer, and the size and composition of the accumulated counterexample set.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Very informative and directly reusable. A failed CEGIS run leaves the counterexample set, which is a compressed description of exactly what made the problem hard, is independent of the synthesizer that produced it, and can be replayed to warm-start a different synthesizer. Non-termination also localises: the holes that keep changing across iterations are the under-constrained ones. A failure here is closer to a deliverable than to a loss.
SOURCE_CHECK: PLAUSIBLE. Solar-Lezama's sketching work is real and is the standard origin of the CEGIS loop, and a 2008 Berkeley dissertation titled "Program Synthesis by Sketching" is the usual citation, but the web search budget was exhausted this session so the exact 2008 attribution could not be confirmed. Note that the CEGIS loop itself is often traced to the earlier Solar-Lezama et al. sketching paper rather than to the dissertation.
SHARED_MECHANISM: counterexample_feedback_loop
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-BACKEND, R-SUBSTRATE, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE: missing state. The producer can hold the loop, but each spec's counterexample must become the next spec's payload, and output-to-input chaining across specs is a substrate capability the bench does not offer. R-BACKEND: missing executor and missing measurement. There is no adapter that hands a formula to an external SAT or SMT solver, and no result field can carry a counterexample, which is a structured witness rather than a scalar. R-SUBSTRATE: missing control flow. An iterate-until-verified loop with a growing example set cannot exist inside a sealed spec whose payload is fixed for its whole life. R-EXECUTOR: missing adjudication. A verifier that decides a universally quantified property is not a scorer; the bench has scoring but no adjudication over a quantifier.
FAITHFUL: A program grammar with typed holes, an inductive synthesizer over a finite example set, an external verifier that returns a concrete witness on failure, and a loop with a monotonically growing example set. The spec must be able to carry a set-valued input, the example set, and to return a witness-valued output, and the outcome must be able to report iterations-to-success rather than one scalar comparison.
REDUCTION: A concrete and surprisingly faithful CEGIS runs on the existing scorer. The program is a candidate bitstring of length L, the holes are its L bits, and sketch_size maps directly to L. The specification is the hidden target. The bench does not hand back a counterexample, but one can be manufactured from scores: the producer submits a candidate, then submits the candidate with bit i flipped; if the score rises, position i is a concrete counterexample, a specific place where the program is wrong. That is a real discriminating witness and it drives a real inductive step, because a confirmed bit is permanently fixed and the next proposal is drawn from a strictly smaller space. Budget equals number of specs, playing max_iterations. PRESERVES: the loop structure, the property that each failure narrows the next proposal, the monotone accumulation of confirmed constraints, the iteration budget as the outcome axis, and the sketch-size scaling. LOSES: logic and the SAT query, universal quantification, since the specification here is a single point so verification degenerates to equality, and the exponential blowup of the inductive step, which is the reason CEGIS is interesting. CLAIM SUPPORTED: how iterations-to-solve scales with the number of holes under a one-bit counterexample oracle, and whether a smarter probe policy beats naive left-to-right probing. Both curves are pre-registrable and falsifiable, and the naive baseline has a known form near L probes, which makes this double as a calibration of the harness. HONEST CEILING: this reproduces CEGIS's control flow without CEGIS's hardness, so it can support claims about loop efficiency and about the harness, and no claim at all about synthesis. NEW DESIGN CHOICE: L in 8, 16, 32, 64; max_iterations equal to four times L.

TEMPLATE_ID: creativity.v0
FIELD: Computational Creativity
KIND: novelty_search.v0
QUESTION: Can a search driven only by how different a behaviour is from everything already seen keep producing new behaviours indefinitely, with no goal and no fitness function telling it what counts as better?
MECHANISM: Each individual is run in a domain and reduced to a behaviour characterisation, a low-dimensional vector such as a final position. Its novelty is the mean distance to its k nearest neighbours among the current population together with an archive of previously encountered behaviours. Selection is on novelty alone. Individuals that are sufficiently novel are inserted into the archive, which makes that region of behaviour space less rewarding for everything that comes after, so the search is continuously pushed outward. The archive is the mechanism: without it the search forgets and cycles.
CANDIDATE: An individual, that is a genome mapped to a controller. It is judged only by the distance of its behaviour from stored behaviours, and crucially the comparison is on behaviour, not genome, so two very different genomes with the same behaviour are interchangeable.
WORLD: A domain that maps a genome to a behaviour vector, plus the archive. The archive is properly part of the world here, and it is non-stationary: the same individual has a different novelty score at different times, which is what makes this method impossible to express as a fixed fitness landscape.
WHAT_CHANGES: archive_size, unknown because the source destroyed the value. k_nearest, also unknown. Plus the population across generations.
MEASURED: Novelty score, a continuous non-negative distance, along with archive growth rate and behaviour-space coverage over time.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Informative and diagnostic. If the search stalls, the archive plus the insertion times remain, and from them you can distinguish the two failure causes that matter: the behaviour descriptor is degenerate, visible as an archive that saturates inside a small region, or the genotype-to-phenotype map is degenerate, visible as many distinct genomes collapsing onto the same behaviour. Separating a bad descriptor from a bad generator is the central open question in this area, so a stall is a real measurement.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Confirmed in DBLP: Joel Lehman and Kenneth O. Stanley, "Exploiting Open-Endedness to Solve Problems Through the Search for Novelty", ALIFE 2008, pages 329 to 336.
SHARED_MECHANISM: maintain_archive
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-SUBSTRATE, R-REPAIR, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE: missing adjudication. The archive and the k-nearest-neighbour distance can live in the producer, but novelty is defined relative to other specs' results, and the outcome rule cannot express a value computed from anything other than this spec's own fields. R-SUBSTRATE: missing state. An archive that persists across evaluations and changes the score of later evaluations is precisely the mutable shared state the sealed one-world model excludes. R-REPAIR: archive_size and k_nearest are null and must be chosen before anything runs. R-EXECUTOR: missing measurement, but only mildly; random_walk_v0 already returns an endpoint, which is a legitimate one-dimensional behaviour characterisation.
FAITHFUL: A behaviour characterisation function over a genome, a persistent archive with an explicit insertion policy, a k-nearest-neighbour distance over that archive, and generational selection and variation over a population. That is a population plus mutable shared state, and both are excluded by one world per spec.
REDUCTION: This is one of the strongest reductions in the chunk, because random_walk_v0 already is a behaviour characterisation function. The genome is the triple of seed_root, steps and step_scale; the behaviour is displacement, a one-dimensional descriptor. The producer runs a batch of walk specs, reads displacements, and holds the archive downstream. Novelty of a candidate equals the mean absolute displacement difference to its k nearest archive entries; the top-novelty genomes are selected and mutated by perturbing seed_root and jittering step_scale to form the next batch; sufficiently novel behaviours are inserted into a bounded archive. That is real novelty search, not a gesture: the archive genuinely feeds back and genuinely changes later scores. PRESERVES: behaviour-based rather than fitness-based selection, the archive feedback that devalues visited regions, archive_size and k_nearest as live knobs, and the divergence-versus-stall question with its diagnostic. LOSES: high-dimensional behaviour space, and, more seriously, deception. A smooth monotone genome-to-displacement map has no dead ends, and beating an objective on a deceptive task is novelty search's headline result, so that claim is out of reach. CLAIM SUPPORTED: how archive_size and k affect coverage rate and stall time of a divergent search in a bounded descriptor space. PRE-REGISTERED NULL, and it is the likely outcome: in one dimension with a smooth map, novelty search should be beaten by uniform sampling of seeds, and confirming that is a clean, useful kill rather than a disappointment. To make deception reachable at all, the descriptor must be made non-monotone in the genome, for example by having the producer fold step_scale through a multimodal function of the genome before submitting the spec. TEMPLATE_ID: eprover_superposition.v0
FIELD: Automated Theorem Proving
KIND: formal_prover.v0
QUESTION: Can a first-order theorem prover derive a contradiction from a set of clauses within a fixed budget of generated clauses, and does changing the heuristic that picks what to work on next change whether it gets there?
MECHANISM: Negate the conjecture and clausify everything into a flat set of clauses. Then saturate: repeatedly select one clause from the unprocessed set, which is the given-clause choice and is exactly where the heuristic lives, compute all superposition, resolution and paramodulation inferences between it and the already-processed set, simplify and subsume the results, and put the survivors back into the unprocessed set. Stop when the empty clause appears, which is a refutation and hence a proof, or when the clause or time budget is exhausted, which proves nothing. Every inference rule is sound, so the heuristic can only change cost, never correctness, and that is why the field measures heuristics by budget consumption.
CANDIDATE: The search strategy and its clause-selection heuristic. What varies across runs is the order in which the same sound inference rules are applied, not the logic and not the problem.
WORLD: The clause set, that is the axioms plus the negated conjecture, together with the inference calculus. The world supplies soundness, which is what makes a derived empty clause a real mathematical fact rather than a score.
WHAT_CHANGES: expansion_limit, unknown because the source destroyed the value. search_strategy over auto, discount and neural.
MEASURED: Refuted or not as a boolean, clauses generated and processed as integers, and wall time. The primary scale is a bounded integer count, and comparisons are only meaningful at a fixed budget.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Informative, and it is the standard currency of the field. A budget-exhausted run leaves the saturated clause set and the full proof-search profile: how many clauses were generated versus processed, how much was subsumed away, and where branching exploded. That profile is a hardness fingerprint for the problem, a diagnosis for the heuristic, and directly reusable as training data for a learned selection heuristic.
SOURCE_CHECK: PLAUSIBLE. E is a real superposition-based first-order prover by Stephan Schulz and "E - a brainiac theorem prover" is the paper title normally attributed to him around 2002, but DBLP was unavailable for that query and the web search budget was exhausted, so the venue and year could not be confirmed this session. Separately, the strategy menu mixes eras: auto and discount correspond to real E options, discount being a given-clause proof-procedure variant, whereas neural clause selection belongs to a much later line of work and is not part of the 2002 system.
SHARED_MECHANISM: bounded_exhaustive_refutation
ROUTES: R-BACKEND, R-COMPOSE, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-BACKEND: missing executor only. E is an existing external binary that already reports every number this template wants; what is absent is an adapter that pins a version, hands it a problem, captures status and clause counts, and fossilizes the proof object. This is the cheapest route and the missing thing is orchestration, not measurement. R-COMPOSE: missing adjudication. Comparing auto against discount is a two-spec comparison, and the outcome rule cannot compare specs, so the strategy ablation can be recorded but never adjudicated by the bench. R-EXECUTOR: missing control flow. An in-bench prover needs an unbounded saturation loop over a growing clause set inside a sealed spec, which the fixed-payload model forbids.
FAITHFUL: Call a real prover with a pinned version on a real problem library such as TPTP, with pinned strategy flags, and record status, generated and processed clause counts, and the proof object. The bench's role is orchestration and fossilization; inference itself belongs to the backend. Any cross-strategy claim additionally needs many problems, because a heuristic comparison on one problem is a single coin flip.

Treat the L bit positions as the clause set, and treat determining the hidden target completely as the refutation goal. A producer-side saturation loop probes positions one spec at a time under a selection heuristic: fixed left-to-right order as the auto analogue, greedy most-informative-next chosen by predicted score movement as the discount analogue, and a policy fitted on earlier problems as the neural analogue. Count probes to full determination, under a hard probe budget playing expansion_limit. PRESERVES: the exact experimental shape the field uses, namely a fixed problem, a fixed budget, and a heuristic ablation over the order in which identical sound operations are applied, with operation count as the score, and the property that all heuristics agree on the answer so only cost varies. LOSES: first-order logic, unification, superposition, subsumption, and combinatorial explosion, which is the entire reason heuristics matter at all. CLAIM SUPPORTED: only that the harness can carry a heuristic-ablation-under-budget experiment across many problems and produce a clean cost comparison with a correct baseline. Treat it as instrument calibration for the eventual R-BACKEND version, and do not let it be reported as a claim about theorem proving. TEMPLATE_ID: fm.bounded.model.check.v0
FIELD: Formal Methods
KIND: bounded_model_check_v0
QUESTION: Within a fixed number of steps, can a system reach a state that violates its safety property, and if it can, what exactly is the sequence of steps that gets there?
MECHANISM: Take an initial-state predicate, a transition relation and a safety property. Unroll the transition relation k times into a single propositional formula whose satisfying assignments are exactly the length-k executions that end in a violating state. Hand that formula to a SAT solver. A satisfying assignment decodes into a concrete counterexample trace. Unsatisfiable means no violation exists at any length up to k, which is a genuine proof but only within the bound; nothing is claimed at step k plus one unless a separate k-induction check also succeeds. The method's whole selling point is that it replaces a fixed-point computation over the state space with a single, exhaustive, bounded query.
CANDIDATE: The candidate is a trace, a length-k execution, and it is found or shown not to exist by the solver rather than proposed by the experimenter. What the experimenter chooses is the model and the bound.
WORLD: The transition relation, here supplied as a sixteen-bit encoding, plus the initial states, the property, and the bound. The world supplies the semantics that make the unrolled formula mean something about executions.
WHAT_CHANGES: Which of the two sixteen-bit transition encodings is used, and bound_k, which is unknown because the source destroyed the value.
MEASURED: Satisfiable or unsatisfiable as a boolean, solver time, formula size, and on satisfiable, the length and content of the counterexample trace.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Asymmetric and worth stating carefully. A satisfiable result is the useful one: it hands back a concrete violating trace, the single most reusable artifact in verification, and it is a hard falsification. An unsatisfiable result at the budget leaves only the depth reached and the solver's resource profile, which bounds what you could afford and says nothing about safety beyond k. A timeout leaves information about the solver and nothing about the system. There is also a design failure visible before running: read as truth tables of a four-input function, 1111000011110000 depends only on the four-place bit and 0101010101010101 depends only on the one-place bit. Both supplied transition encodings are single-variable projections, so both induce effectively one-bit dynamics in which any reachability question is settled at depth one or two and the bound_k axis is inert. That is a real and cheap finding.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Biere, Cimatti, Clarke and Zhu, "Symbolic Model Checking without BDDs", TACAS 1999, is the founding bounded-model-checking paper and the description above matches it. Confirmed from established knowledge; the web search budget was exhausted before an independent lookup could be run.
SHARED_MECHANISM: bounded_exhaustive_refutation
ROUTES: R-BACKEND, R-REPAIR, R-COMPOSE
BLOCKER: R-BACKEND: missing executor. A SAT solver is an existing external tool; what is missing is an adapter that builds the unrolled CNF from the model and the bound, calls the solver, and fossilizes the verdict together with the decoded model. Cheap, and no new bench internals are needed. R-REPAIR: not a capability gap. bound_k is null, and worse, both transition encodings are single-variable projections, so the model itself must be replaced with one whose reachable set actually grows with depth before the experiment can discriminate. R-COMPOSE: missing adjudication. Locating the depth at which a counterexample first appears is a trend over a k-sweep, and the outcome rule forbids ordering and trend tests, so the k-frontier must be found downstream.
FAITHFUL: A state and transition encoder, a property encoder, an unroller that emits CNF at a given k, a real SAT solver, and a decoder that turns a satisfying assignment back into a readable trace. For any claim beyond the bound, a k-induction check, which is a second and different solver query. The spec must be able to return a witness, and the outcome must be able to distinguish proved-within-bound from not-found-within-budget, which one scalar comparison cannot do.
REDUCTION: A real one exists, and its value is precisely that it makes the bench's limits visible. random_walk_v0 with repeat.state equal to persist is a bounded transition system: position is the state, each repeat is an unrolling step, k is the number of persisted repeats, and a safety property is a threshold on absolute position. So bounded reachability becomes: does this walk cross the threshold within k steps. The walk is deterministic given its seed, so one seed is one execution rather than the whole state space, and the producer must sweep seed_root over many seeds. That substitution is the entire point: bounded model checking is replaced by bounded random testing, and the difference between them is the thing worth measuring. TEMPLATE_ID: l2s.dagger.v0
FIELD: Learning-to-Search
KIND: l2s_dagger_v0
QUESTION: Can a policy learn to copy an expert well enough that it stays on the expert's path even when it is the one driving, rather than only when the expert has been driving all along?
MECHANISM: DAgger. Round one rolls out the expert, collects state and expert-action pairs, and trains a policy on them. Round i rolls out a mixture policy that takes the expert's action with probability beta and the learner's otherwise, so the states visited are the ones the LEARNER induces. At every visited state the expert is queried for the action it would have taken, and those labels are appended to an aggregated dataset that is never discarded. The policy is retrained on the whole aggregate, and beta decays toward zero. Training on the learner's own state distribution is the entire trick: it converts imitation into a no-regret online learning problem and removes the error that otherwise compounds quadratically in the horizon.
CANDIDATE: The policy. It is judged by its loss against the expert's action, measured on the states the policy itself visits rather than on the expert's states.
WORLD: A sequential decision environment with horizon trajectory_length, plus a queryable expert oracle. The oracle is the scarce resource and the reason the algorithm is designed the way it is; the environment supplies the dynamics that turn a small per-step error into a different state distribution.
WHAT_CHANGES: trajectory_length, unknown because the source destroyed the value. oracle_probability, the mixing coefficient beta, also unknown. Plus the aggregated dataset and the policy across rounds.
MEASURED: Per-step disagreement with the oracle or test loss, a continuous non-negative quantity, and crucially its scaling with the horizon, which is where the linear-versus-quadratic claim lives.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Informative and specifically diagnostic. A failed run leaves the aggregated dataset labelled by round, so loss can be split by which round's state distribution a state came from. Low loss on round-one states with high loss on later states means the failure is distributional, the covariate shift the method targets. High loss everywhere means the policy class simply cannot represent the expert, a realizability failure that no amount of aggregation fixes. Making that distinction is the reason to run the experiment at all.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Ross, Gordon and Bagnell, "A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret Online Learning", AISTATS 2011, is the DAgger paper and the mechanism above matches it. TEMPLATE_ID: mcc.bipartite.v0
FIELD: Minimal-Criterion Coevolution
KIND: mcc_step.v0
QUESTION: Can two coupled populations keep generating new problems and new solutions indefinitely when the only rule for staying alive is solve at least one and be solved by at least one, with nothing ever ranked as better than anything else?
MECHANISM: Two populations reproduce with mutation, solvers and environments. A child solver survives if it satisfies its minimal criterion, solving at least one environment currently in the environment population. A child environment survives if at least one solver in the current solver population solves it. Nothing is scored and nothing is ranked. Because an environment must remain solvable to persist and a solver must have something to solve, the two populations drag each other along a moving frontier of just-reachable difficulty, and structural complexity accumulates without any objective ever being specified. The coupling is the algorithm; remove either half and it collapses to random drift.
CANDIDATE: Two candidates, symmetrically. A solver is judged by whether any environment yields to it; an environment is judged by whether any solver cracks it. Neither receives a score, only a binary existential verdict.
WORLD: The other population is the world. There is no fixed landscape at all: the environments against which solvers are evaluated are themselves produced and filtered by the same process, so the problem distribution is endogenous.
WHAT_CHANGES: Solver population size and environment population size, each over 50, 100 and 250, plus both populations across generations.
MEASURED: Population sizes over generations as integers, the number of surviving solver-environment pairs, and structural statistics of the environments that persist. The primary reading is whether both counts stay positive over a long horizon.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Informative and localisable. Collapse of either population is the event of interest and it is diagnosable from the lineage record of which environment was last solved by which solver. Environment collapse means the environment generator outruns the solvers, or produces things so trivial that the criterion is satisfied by drift. Solver collapse means the mutation operator cannot track environment drift. Either way the failure names which of the two generators is mis-tuned, which is the practical question in this method.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED. Confirmed in DBLP: Jonathan C. Brant and Kenneth O. Stanley, "Minimal criterion coevolution: a new approach to open-ended search", GECCO 2017, pages 67 to 74.
SHARED_MECHANISM: two_population_interaction
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-WORLD, R-SUBSTRATE
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE: missing adjudication. The producer can hold both populations and use the bench as the pairwise solve test, but the minimal criterion is an EXISTENTIAL over another population, solved by at least one, and the outcome rule is one field, one operator, one value on this spec alone, so it cannot express a quantifier over other specs' results. R-WORLD: missing world capability. One world per spec is a single integer with no organism type; a second population that IS the environment is exactly what that rule forbids. R-SUBSTRATE: missing state. Two mutable populations must persist and be varied across generations, and there is no archive and no carry across repeats.
FAITHFUL: Two mutable populations with their own mutation operators, a pairwise or sampled-pairwise evaluation between them, a per-individual existential survival test, and generational replacement on both sides. The hard part is a representation for environments that is itself evolvable and whose difficulty can genuinely increase, since without that the frontier cannot move.
REDUCTION: A runnable version exists and it leans on the seed-derivation semantics in a way that is genuinely faithful. The environment population is a set of pairs of seed and length: each pair defines a distinct hidden target, so environments are GENERATED rather than authored, which is the property that matters. The solver population is a set of bitstrings. One spec is one solver against one environment, and score gives a graded solve. Minimal criterion made concrete: a solver survives if the maximum of its scores over the environment population is at least tau; an environment survives if the maximum score any solver achieves on it is at least tau. Mutation: flip m bits of a surviving solver; perturb a surviving environment's seed and optionally increment its length, since length is a real difficulty knob because the target grows with it. LOSES: behaviour, since a bitstring solver only matches and never acts, transferable capability, and structural complexity in environments, since a hash target has no structure to accumulate. CLAIM SUPPORTED: whether coupled minimal-criterion survival sustains both populations over a long horizon, and whether the sustained difficulty, measured as mean environment length, rises, stalls or falls. PRE-REGISTERED NULL AND A TRAP THAT MUST BE DEFUSED FIRST: a random bitstring scores about 0.5 on a hash target regardless of length, and the survival test takes a MAXIMUM over the other population, which is an extreme-value statistic that rises mechanically with population size. Since population size is the swept axis, an uncalibrated tau turns the entire result into a population-size artifact. Compute the attainable maximum-score distribution at each population size first, set tau per size, and report the eligible range beside the verdict. QUESTION: In a randomly generated soup of molecules and reactions, at what level of catalytic connectivity does a self-sustaining, self-catalysing subnetwork first appear, and how does that level change as the soup gets bigger?

Whatever remains, if anything, is reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated. The field's headline result is that the catalysis probability needed for a RAF to appear grows only slowly with system size rather than exponentially.
CANDIDATE: The whole reaction network. What is judged is a SUBSET of it, the maximal RAF, and it is found by deletion to a fixed point rather than by any search over candidates.

MEASURED: RAF present or absent as a boolean, and RAF size as an integer or as a fraction of the network. Swept over catalysis_prob it produces a percolation-like curve with a threshold, and the threshold's location is the actual result.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Informative by design, more so than most. A no-RAF outcome at a given probability is a point ON the threshold curve, and since the object of study is the LOCATION of the transition, negatives are exactly as valuable as positives and the sweep is incomplete without them. Additionally a failed run leaves the pruned network with a death reason per reaction, uncatalysed versus unreachable from the food set, which distinguishes a catalysis shortage from a food-set shortage.

TEMPLATE_ID: structure_mapping.v0
FIELD: Analogical Reasoning
KIND: structure_mapping_v0
QUESTION: Given two described situations, how good is the best correspondence between them that can be built purely from matching relational structure rather than matching surface features, and does insisting on deep, interconnected structure change the answer?
MECHANISM: The Structure-Mapping Engine works in stages. First it proposes local match hypotheses between base and target items whose predicates are identical, together with the argument matches those force. Second it merges locally consistent hypotheses into structurally consistent kernels, obeying one-to-one correspondence and parallel connectivity, which means matching a relation requires matching its arguments. Third it merges kernels into a small number of maximal global interpretations and scores each with a structural evaluation that propagates evidence downward from relations to their arguments, so that a deep interconnected system of relations outweighs a large number of shallow attribute matches; that weighting is the systematicity principle. Fourth it projects unmatched base structure across the mapping into the target as candidate inferences, which is what makes analogy generative rather than merely comparative.
CANDIDATE: The mapping, that is a set of correspondences between base and target items. It is judged by a structural evaluation score and constrained by one-to-one and parallel connectivity, so most syntactically possible mappings are never scored at all.
WORLD: Two symbolic descriptions in a shared predicate vocabulary, sized by base_entities and target_entities. The world's essential supply is relational structure and vocabulary overlap; with no shared predicates no match hypothesis is ever generated and the machinery produces nothing.
WHAT_CHANGES: base_entities and target_entities, both unknown because the source destroyed the values, and systematicity_bias over enabled and disabled.
MEASURED: The structural evaluation score of the best interpretation, a continuous but unnormalised quantity, plus the number of correspondences and the candidate inferences. The scale is the problem: the score is not comparable across descriptions of different sizes.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Informative. A low score or an empty mapping leaves the match hypotheses that were formed and then rejected, together with the reason each died, one-to-one violation versus parallel-connectivity violation. That distinguishes two descriptions that share vocabulary but not structure from two that share neither, which is the substantive negative result in analogy work. A failure also exposes the score's size dependence, which is the standard confound in this literature.
