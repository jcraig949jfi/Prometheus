TEMPLATE_ID: adam_yeast_growth.v0
FIELD: Robot Scientists
KIND: simulate_yeast_knockout_v0
QUESTION: If you delete one gene from a yeast strain and then add one
  nutrient back into its food, does the yeast grow again? The answer is
  meant to hint at what the deleted gene was doing.
MECHANISM: Take a yeast strain in which exactly one open reading frame has
  been deleted. Grow it in a chemically defined medium that lacks the
  nutrient of interest, and in parallel in the same medium with that one
  nutrient added back. Incubate, and read optical density of the culture
  over time as the growth readout. A strain that fails to grow without the
  supplement and grows with it is auxotrophic for that nutrient, which
  places the deleted gene somewhere upstream in the pathway that makes it.
  In the real Robot Scientist loop this assay is not the whole method: a
  logical model of the metabolic pathway proposes which gene-enzyme pairing
  would predict which growth outcome, the machine picks the assay that best
  discriminates among live hypotheses, the robot physically runs it, and
  the outcome kills the hypotheses it contradicts. The growth assay is the
  single measurement at the bottom of that loop.
CANDIDATE: The knocked-out strain. What varies is which single gene is
  absent and which single nutrient is present.
WORLD: A defined liquid growth medium in a plate well at a set temperature.
  The environment supplies energy, every nutrient except the ones being
  withheld, and time. Crucially it supplies the SELECTIVE CONTEXT: the
  deletion is only visible because the medium refuses to give the cell what
  the missing gene would have made.
WHAT_CHANGES: Across runs, the identity of the deleted ORF and the identity
  of the added supplement. Everything else is held fixed by design, which
  is the whole point of a defined medium.
MEASURED: Final or maximum optical density, a continuous positive real, in
  practice compared against a no-growth control. Richer versions read the
  whole growth curve and extract lag time and doubling rate.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Very informative. A knockout that grows anyway when
  you expected auxotrophy is the single most useful outcome in this method:
  it says a redundant or alternative route to the nutrient exists, which is
  a positive structural claim about the pathway. A knockout that fails to
  grow even WITH the supplement says the gene does something other than, or
  in addition to, making that nutrient. Both failures narrow the pathway.
  This is a method whose failures are its product.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED - King et al. 2009, The Automation of Science,
  Science, is real and describes exactly this: yeast deletion mutants,
  auxotrophic growth experiments, and hypothesis generation over orphan
  enzymes in the aromatic amino acid biosynthesis pathway. Caveat worth
  recording: the three ORFs named in param_space are not aromatic amino
  acid pathway genes as far as I can establish, and the listed supplements
  uracil, histidine and leucine are the standard laboratory auxotrophic
  MARKER nutrients rather than Adam's aromatic targets, so the parameter
  values look like generic yeast-genetics furniture rather than values
  drawn from the cited work. The method and the attribution are right; the
  numbers were not checked against the paper by whoever mined this.
SHARED_MECHANISM: score_fixed_candidate
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-BACKEND, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE - the scientific content is an INTERACTION between two
  factors, does the supplement help more when the gene is missing, and the
  outcome rule is one field, one operator, one value, so the interaction
  cannot be adjudicated inside a spec. This is an ADJUDICATION blocker and
  it is the only one on this route, since the four cells can each be their
  own spec. R-BACKEND - no metabolic simulator is wired in; this is a
  MISSING MEASUREMENT rather than a substrate limit, because an external
  flux-balance model takes a gene deletion and a medium and returns a
  growth rate with no bench internals changed. R-EXECUTOR - if the reduced
  bitstring version below is wanted with the masking done inside the
  executor rather than by the producer, evaluate_bitstring would need a
  mask parameter, and no executor may default a parameter, so it would have
  to be a new kind. MEASUREMENT capability.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs a genotype that is a structured object
  with removable parts, an environment that is a set of supplied resources,
  and a metabolism connecting them, so that removing a part can be
  compensated by supplying a resource. That is a genome-scale model, not a
  parameter. It also needs the surrounding hypothesis loop, since a single
  growth assay in isolation is a measurement and not the Robot Scientist
  method; the method is the loop that chooses which assay to run next.
REDUCTION: A real one survives, as a two-by-two factorial run entirely on
  evaluate_bitstring. Fix one world seed and one length L so that all four
  cells score against the SAME hidden target. Pick an arbitrary candidate
  bitstring C. Designate a contiguous block K of positions as the gene and
  a disjoint block S as the supplement. Run four specs: C intact; C with
  block K forced to all zeros, the knockout; C with block S forced to a
  fixed pattern, the supplement alone; and C with both, the rescue cell.
  Each spec carries an ordinary one-scalar outcome rule on score. The
  interaction, does setting S recover more score when K is zeroed than when
  it is not, is computed downstream across the four fossils.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: the factorial rescue logic; the requirement that all
  four cells share one environment, which repeat.seed_derivation = constant
  enforces literally rather than by assumption; and, unexpectedly, the
  epistemic caveat in the template's own rationale. Because the target is a
  hash, a block S that raises the score does so for reasons that are
  genuinely opaque, so a positive rescue in this toy CANNOT be read as
  saying S is the direct product of K. That turns the alternative-pathway
  caveat from rhetoric into structure.
  WHAT IT LOSES: all biology; the pathway graph; dose response; the growth
  curve and therefore lag and doubling rate; and the entire hypothesis-
  selection loop that is the actual contribution of the Robot Scientist.
  WHICH CLAIM IT SUPPORTS: a methodological one about the bench itself,
  that a bench restricted to one scalar comparison per spec can still
  detect a two-factor rescue interaction, but only across specs and only
  with a downstream adjudicator, and that the resulting verdict cannot
  distinguish direct from indirect rescue. Worth measuring before any real
  assay backend is wired in, because it fixes what the fossil must carry.

TEMPLATE_ID: alife.tierra.v0
FIELD: Artificial Life (ALife)
KIND: tierra_core_v0
QUESTION: Can a hand-written program, running in a shared block of computer
  memory, copy itself successfully? This is the sanity check you run before
  turning mutation on and seeing what evolves.
MECHANISM: Reserve a large block of memory, the soup. Write one ancestor
  program in a machine language designed so that most random edits still
  produce something that runs: no numeric operands, and addressing by
  matching templates of no-operation instructions rather than by absolute
  address, so the code survives being moved. The ancestor's body is a loop
  that measures its own length by locating its start and end templates,
  requests a fresh block of memory from the operating system, copies itself
  instruction by instruction into that block, and issues a divide
  instruction that hands the daughter block its own instruction pointer and
  slice of CPU time. A scheduler round-robins CPU time across all living
  creatures. A reaper kills creatures when memory fills, preferring the old
  and the error-prone. That combination, self-copy plus finite memory plus
  a reaper, is what turns replication rate into a selection pressure. In
  the full experiment a mutation operator flips bits in the soup and during
  copying, and parasites appear that lack a copy loop and execute their
  neighbours' instead.
CANDIDATE: The ancestor program, a sequence of instructions. What varies is
  the instruction sequence and the machine language it is written in.
WORLD: The soup: a finite, addressable, MUTABLE shared memory, plus a
  scheduler granting execution slices and a reaper removing creatures. The
  environment supplies memory as the limiting resource, CPU time as the
  currency, and death. Other creatures are part of the world, which is why
  the world is also an ecology.
WHAT_CHANGES: Across runs, the ancestor's instruction set and length, the
  soup size, and the number of cycles the simulation is allowed to run.
MEASURED: For the baseline question, a fact: did the ancestor produce at
  least one viable daughter, and does the population sustain itself. Scalar
  proxies are population count at cycle N, generations elapsed, or fraction
  of soup occupied. All are continuous or count-valued and all require the
  simulation to have run to completion.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Concretely informative, though it is a debugging
  signal more than a scientific one. If the ancestor fails to replicate you
  learn that the instruction set as specified is not closed under the
  operations the copy loop needs, or that template addressing does not
  resolve, or that the allocator refuses the daughter. Each points at a
  specific missing primitive. The stronger informative failure comes later:
  if replication works but the population collapses, you have learned that
  the reaper and scheduler parameters, not the genome, set viability, which
  is a claim about the world rather than the organism.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED - Thomas S. Ray, An Approach to the Synthesis of
  Life, in Artificial Life II, 1991, is the correct primary reference for
  Tierra and covers the ancestor, the soup, the reaper, template addressing
  and the emergence of parasites. Caveat on the parameter axis: the choices
  are named 80_tierran_opcodes and 82_tierran_opcodes, which appears to
  conflate two different quantities. The canonical ancestor is roughly
  eighty INSTRUCTIONS LONG, whereas the Tierran instruction SET is far
  smaller, on the order of thirty-two opcodes. If the axis means ancestor
  length it is misnamed; if it means opcode-set size the values are wrong.
  Flag for the operator. I could not re-verify the exact counts against the
  paper because the search budget was spent.
SHARED_MECHANISM: interpret_program
ROUTES: R-BACKEND, R-EXECUTOR, R-SUBSTRATE
BLOCKER: R-BACKEND - nothing missing in principle; Tierra and its
  descendants are real, runnable, published programs, and the bench would
  only have to invoke one, pass the seed and parameters, and fossilize a
  scalar from its log. MEASUREMENT capability only, and cheap. R-EXECUTOR -
  for the reduced self-copy version the bench needs a kind that executes an
  instruction sequence over a writable tape and returns a fidelity scalar.
  MEASUREMENT capability. R-SUBSTRATE - for the real thing the bench lacks
  mutable shared memory persisting across creatures, a scheduler, creature
  birth and death, and a population at all. STATE AND CONTROL-FLOW
  capability, and this is the substantive blocker: one world is one
  integer, and there is nowhere to put a soup.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs a mutable addressable memory that
  outlives any single evaluation, an interpreter that can write into it, a
  scheduler interleaving many instruction pointers, and a death rule. It
  needs the world to hold a population rather than a seed, so the world
  object itself changes type. And it needs long runs, millions of cycles,
  so the fossil must summarise a trajectory rather than a returned value.
REDUCTION: Two layers, one honest and small, one a gesture I recommend
  against.
  The gesture to avoid: mapping soup occupancy onto random_walk_v0's
  position and calling the walk a population. Nothing about replication
  survives that. The walk has no copying, no memory to copy into, and its
  increments do not depend on its state. It would produce a number that
  looks like a population curve and means nothing.
  The reduction that does survive addresses exactly the question this
  template actually asks, which is pre-mutation viability of one written
  ancestor, not evolution. Define a tiny copy machine: a fixed-length tape,
  a read head and a write head, and six opcodes - advance-read,
  advance-write, copy-byte, jump-if-template, allocate, divide. Supply the
  instruction sequence as the payload. Run it for a bounded number of
  cycles against a fresh daughter region and return ONE scalar, the
  fraction of the daughter tape matching the parent. That is continuous in
  the range zero to one and drops straight into a single-comparison outcome
  rule with no change to the rule engine. Under repeat.seed_derivation =
  sha256_index each repeat can randomise the daughter's start offset, which
  tests the template-addressing property that is the actual insight of
  Tierran machine code: whether the copy loop still works when the code is
  not where it expects to be. That property is what makes the language
  robust to mutation, and it can be measured with no mutation, no
  population and no ecology at all.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: self-copying as an executed act rather than an
  asserted one, copy fidelity as a continuous measurement, and position
  independence, which is the real design claim of the substrate.
  WHAT IT LOSES: the soup, the reaper, the scheduler, competition for CPU
  time, mutation, parasites, and open-endedness. Everything ecological.
  WHICH CLAIM IT SUPPORTS: only that a given instruction set is or is not
  closed under self-copy and is or is not position independent. That is a
  substrate-viability claim, and it is the correct first claim, but it must
  never be reported as an evolution result.

TEMPLATE_ID: causal_discovery_pc.v0
FIELD: Causal Discovery
KIND: pc_algorithm_skeleton_v0
QUESTION: Given only observational measurements of several variables, and
  no experiments, which pairs of variables could possibly be directly
  connected? The method rules connections out rather than establishing
  them.
MECHANISM: Start with the complete undirected graph, every pair joined.
  Then delete edges. For each remaining pair, test whether the two
  variables are independent given some set of the others, and test with
  conditioning sets in increasing order of size: first the empty set, which
  is plain marginal independence, then all single conditioning variables
  drawn from the neighbours of either endpoint, then all pairs, and so on
  up to the allowed maximum. The moment a conditioning set is found that
  makes the pair independent at the chosen significance level, delete that
  edge and record the separating set. When no more edges can be removed,
  the surviving graph is the skeleton. Orientation is a separate later
  phase that uses the recorded separating sets to find colliders and then
  propagates; this template stops before that. The whole method rests on
  assumptions the data cannot check: causal sufficiency, meaning no
  unmeasured common causes, plus faithfulness and the Markov condition.
CANDIDATE: The edge set, which starts complete and shrinks. What is judged
  is which edges survive.
WORLD: A dataset. The environment supplies a finite sample from some joint
  distribution, and its finiteness is the whole difficulty: every
  independence decision is a hypothesis test with error in both directions,
  and errors propagate, because the neighbour sets used for later tests
  depend on earlier deletions.
WHAT_CHANGES: The significance level alpha, the maximum conditioning set
  size, and, in any honest study, the sample and its seed.
MEASURED: The template asks for skeleton density, edges retained over edges
  possible, continuous between zero and one. Better statistics exist when a
  ground truth graph is available: structural Hamming distance to the true
  skeleton, or precision and recall over edges. All scalar.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Informative in a specific and slightly awkward way. A
  skeleton that stays dense tells you the independence tests lacked power,
  which is a statement about sample size and not about causality. A
  skeleton that empties out tells you alpha was too permissive, or that
  many variables are near-deterministic functions of each other, which
  breaks faithfulness. Either way the failure diagnoses the INSTRUMENT
  rather than the world, and that is genuinely useful, because it forces
  the sample-size question to be settled before any causal claim is made.
  What a failure does not leave behind is any information about direction.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED - Spirtes, Glymour and Scheines, Causation,
  Prediction, and Search, MIT Press, is the standard reference for the PC
  algorithm and the second edition is 2000. The described adjacency-pruning
  phase, the increasing conditioning-set order, the separating sets and the
  causal sufficiency assumption are all as stated.
SHARED_MECHANISM: prune_hypothesis_space
ROUTES: R-BACKEND, R-EXECUTOR, R-REPAIR
BLOCKER: R-BACKEND - mature implementations exist, causal-learn, pcalg and
  Tetrad among them, and the bench would only have to hand over a seed and
  parameters and fossilize one returned scalar. MEASUREMENT capability, and
  cheap. R-EXECUTOR - a self-contained kind that plants a graph, samples
  from a linear Gaussian structural equation model, runs the pruning phase
  and returns density or structural Hamming distance. Also MEASUREMENT.
  R-REPAIR - both parameters are null in param_space, so the template
  cannot be admitted as it stands on any route; that is a missing
  specification rather than a capability gap.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs a data object in the world, since PC
  consumes a sample and not a seed alone, and it needs the conditional
  independence test to be a real hypothesis test with a stated null. It
  also needs a planted ground-truth graph if the result is to be scored at
  all, because skeleton density on its own is uninterpretable without
  knowing the density of the truth.
REDUCTION: Plant the graph, run the real algorithm, return one number. Use
  a small planted directed acyclic graph, six nodes and seven edges as a
  NEW DESIGN CHOICE since the source values were destroyed, generate n
  samples from a linear Gaussian structural equation model seeded from the
  world seed, run the standard PC adjacency phase with Fisher-z tests, and
  return structural Hamming distance to the true skeleton as the single
  scalar. Propose alpha in the set 0.01, 0.05, 0.1 and maximum conditioning
  set size 2, both marked NEW DESIGN CHOICE.
  The part worth doing even before that executor exists: this template is
  an unusually good probe of the bench's own repeat semantics, and the
  design can be settled now. PC on a fixed sample is deterministic.
  Therefore under repeat.seed_derivation = constant with state = reset and
  count above one, every repeat is provably the identical computation and
  the bench should raise degenerate_by_construction. Under sha256_index
  each repeat is a DIFFERENT planted graph and a different sample, so the
  repeats measure a population of graphs rather than the sampling
  variability of one graph. Those are two different scientific questions
  and the template as written does not say which it wants. What a causal
  discovery researcher almost certainly wants is the third thing, the
  sampling distribution with the graph held fixed and only the sample
  advancing, and the current seed derivation CANNOT express it, because one
  integer seeds both the graph and the sample. That is a concrete and small
  substrate finding: the bench needs a way to hold one part of a world
  fixed while varying another.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: the pruning mechanism intact, the alpha-versus-density
  tradeoff, and the finite-sample dependence that is the real content.
  WHAT IT LOSES: orientation, the completed partially directed acyclic
  graph, latent confounders and selection bias, all of which the template's
  own rationale already disclaims.
  WHICH CLAIM IT SUPPORTS: that skeleton recovery error is a function of
  sample size and alpha, measured rather than asserted, on a graph whose
  truth is known by construction.

TEMPLATE_ID: comp_sci_discovery.bacon.v0
FIELD: Computational Models of Scientific Discovery
KIND: bacon_heuristic_search.v0
QUESTION: Can a small set of mechanical rules, given nothing but a table of
  numbers, rediscover a quantitative law of nature such as the relation
  between a planet's distance from the sun and the time it takes to orbit?
MECHANISM: The system holds a table of observations with one column per
  measured variable and applies a very small set of data-driven heuristics
  in a loop. If a term is constant across the rows within tolerance, record
  it as a law and stop. If two terms increase together, define a new column
  that is their ratio. If one increases as the other decreases, define a
  new column that is their product. Then look again at the enlarged table
  for a constant term. Applied to distance D and period P, the recursion
  builds D over P, then that over P again, then a ratio of those, and
  arrives at D cubed over P squared being constant, which is Kepler's third
  law. Later versions add symmetry and common-divisor heuristics and
  intrinsic properties, and handle several independent variables by holding
  all but one fixed and sweeping. The essential point is that this is a
  search over DERIVED TERMS, not a fit of a pre-chosen functional form.
CANDIDATE: A derived term, an expression built from the measured columns.
  What is judged is whether that term is constant across rows.
WORLD: A clean data table. In the classical demonstrations the data are
  noiseless historical measurements and the world supplies exactly the
  variables the law needs and no distractors. That is the great weakness of
  the demonstration and everyone in the field knows it.
WHAT_CHANGES: The number of variables in the table and the noise level
  added to them. Both are null in this template and must be set.
MEASURED: Whether a constant term was found; the coefficient of variation
  of the best term, a continuous non-negative number; and the number of
  search steps taken to reach it, a count.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Yes, and this is the most scientifically interesting
  part of the template. BACON's failure modes are why the field moved on.
  Under noise the constancy test either never fires, so nothing is found,
  or fires on a spurious term, so a false law is reported. The
  false-positive case is the valuable one: it measures how much noise the
  constancy tolerance will absorb before the method starts manufacturing
  laws, and that number is a property of the method, transferable to every
  descendant symbolic-regression system. A failure here leaves behind a
  calibrated false-discovery rate, which is more useful than one more
  successful rediscovery of Kepler.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED - the BACON series by Pat Langley with Herbert Simon
  and colleagues, running through the late 1970s and the 1980s and
  consolidated in Scientific Discovery: Computational Explorations of the
  Creative Processes, is real, and the rediscovery of Kepler's third law is
  one of its canonical demonstrations. The stated span 1978 to 1987 is
  consistent with the BACON.1 through BACON.5 sequence. Caveat on the noise
  axis: the early BACON systems were notably NOT noise tolerant, and noise
  handling is a later and partial addition, so an axis that sweeps noise is
  sweeping the system's known weak point rather than a designed parameter.
SHARED_MECHANISM: search_over_candidates
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-EXECUTOR, R-REPAIR
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE - the bench forbids search inside a spec and BACON is
  nothing but search, so the recursion must live in the producer with each
  spec supplying one data point. CONTROL FLOW, specifically that repeat N
  plus one cannot depend on the result of repeat N. R-EXECUTOR - a
  self-contained kind that plants a law, generates a table, runs the
  heuristic recursion and returns one scalar. MEASUREMENT capability.
  R-REPAIR - variables and noise are both null and must be set before
  admission; a specification gap, not a capability gap.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs a data table in the world rather than a
  seed, a symbolic term representation the executor can extend, and an
  unbounded inner loop, since the number of recursion steps is not known in
  advance. It also needs the planted law to be known, so that a discovered
  term can be scored as right or wrong rather than merely constant.
REDUCTION: Two, and the second is the one I would actually run.
  First, the obvious one: a bacon_terms_v0 executor. Plant P equals k times
  D raised to the power 1.5, generate a table of rows with Gaussian noise
  at sigma, run the ratio-and-product recursion to a bounded depth, and
  return the coefficient of variation of the best constant term found. One
  scalar, one comparison, fits the outcome rule exactly. As NEW DESIGN
  CHOICES propose two variables and noise sigma in the set 0, 0.01, 0.05.
  Second, and this needs no new executor at all. evaluate_bitstring exposes
  a deterministic black-box function from a candidate to a continuous score
  between zero and one. A producer can run BACON's own recursion ACROSS
  specs: encode an integer as a bitstring and call that the independent
  variable, take the returned score as the dependent variable, build the
  table across many specs, and apply the ratio-product-constancy heuristics
  downstream. Here is why that is not a toy. The hidden target is derived
  by hashing, so it is KNOWN BY CONSTRUCTION that no smooth law relates the
  encoded integer to the score. Running BACON there therefore measures
  exactly one thing: how often BACON reports a law where none can exist, as
  a function of table size and constancy tolerance. That is a calibrated
  false-positive rate for the method, obtained for free, on a null that is
  not merely shuffled but structurally guaranteed. It answers directly the
  caveat the template's own rationale raises about what a SURVIVED verdict
  would and would not license.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: the exact search heuristics, the constancy tolerance,
  and the dependence of discovery on table size.
  WHAT IT LOSES: the planted law, and therefore any true-positive rate. It
  is a one-sided measurement, and one-sided is the correct posture here: it
  can only ever produce evidence that the method over-reports.
  WHICH CLAIM IT SUPPORTS: a false-discovery-rate claim about heuristic
  induction, pinned to a landscape where the true answer is provably no
  law. Pair it later with the planted-law executor to get the other side.

TEMPLATE_ID: digitevol.avida.v0
FIELD: Digital Evolution
KIND: avida_cpu_v0
QUESTION: Does a particular sequence of machine instructions, run on a
  small virtual computer, actually compute a specified logic function of
  its inputs?
MECHANISM: An Avidian is a circular sequence of instructions executed by a
  virtual CPU with a handful of registers, two stacks, and instruction
  pointers called heads. Some instructions do arithmetic and bit
  operations; others move heads, allocate memory and divide; a set of
  no-operation instructions act as modifiers that change which register the
  preceding instruction touches, which is what makes the language robust to
  point mutation. Input instructions pull numbers from the environment and
  output instructions push a result back. The environment watches the
  outputs, and when an output equals a designated logic function of recent
  inputs, such as NOT of one input or the EQUALS of two, it credits the
  organism with a merit multiplier, which buys more CPU cycles per update,
  which lets it replicate faster. That is the entire coupling between
  computation and fitness. In the 2003 experiment the point was that EQU,
  which needs five NAND operations to build, only ever appeared in
  populations where the simpler intermediate functions were also rewarded:
  the stepping stones were required.
CANDIDATE: The instruction sequence, the genome. What is judged is the
  outputs it emits given the inputs the environment supplies.
WORLD: A grid of cells with a resource and reward schedule. The environment
  supplies the input numbers, the list of which logic functions are
  rewarded and by how much, and the CPU cycles that reward buys. The reward
  schedule is the environment's entire content, and changing it changes
  what evolves.
WHAT_CHANGES: The genome sequence and which logic function the environment
  rewards.
MEASURED: For the single-genotype question, whether the target function was
  performed, and on how many input cases it was performed correctly. The
  natural continuous version is the fraction of input pairs answered
  correctly, between zero and one.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Moderately informative and easy to over-read. A genome
  that fails to emit the target function tells you the sequence lacks a
  required primitive, or that the heads never reach the output instruction.
  That is a debugging fact. The genuinely informative failure lives at the
  population level and this template cannot reach it: the finding that
  matters is that EQU never appears when the intermediate rewards are
  switched off, and that is a failure of a POPULATION under a reward
  schedule, not of a genotype. A single-genotype failure leaves behind
  almost nothing about evolvability.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED - Lenski, Ofria, Pennock and Adami, The Evolutionary
  Origin of Complex Features, Nature 2003, is the correct reference and it
  is the EQU experiment with the stepping-stone reward schedule. The two
  named environmental resources, logic NOT and logic EQU, correspond to the
  simplest and the hardest of the rewarded functions in that work.
SHARED_MECHANISM: interpret_program
ROUTES: R-NOW, R-COMPOSE, R-BACKEND, R-EXECUTOR
BLOCKER: R-NOW - for the degraded truth-table version below nothing is
  missing; the constraint is INTERPRETIVE, in that the hidden target is a
  random Boolean function rather than a named one, so the result is about
  arbitrary functions and not about EQU. R-COMPOSE - to compare genotypes
  the producer must run many specs, because no search is allowed inside
  one. CONTROL FLOW. R-BACKEND - Avida is real, runnable and open, and
  invoking it and fossilizing one scalar needs no new bench internals.
  MEASUREMENT. R-EXECUTOR - a small stack-machine kind that runs a supplied
  opcode sequence over all input pairs and returns the fraction correct.
  MEASUREMENT.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs an instruction-set interpreter with
  registers and heads, an environment object holding a reward schedule, and
  the ability to run one genome against many input cases and aggregate. For
  the result that made the paper matter it additionally needs a population,
  mutation, and differential replication rate, none of which the bench has.
REDUCTION: There is a reduction that runs TODAY with no new code, and it is
  better than it first looks. Read a bitstring of length two to the power k
  as the complete truth table of a k-input Boolean function.
  evaluate_bitstring already returns the fraction of positions matching a
  hidden target, so it is already computing the agreement of your candidate
  function with a hidden Boolean function over all inputs. Set length to

FIELD: Evolutionary Developmental Systems (Evo-Devo)
KIND: boolean_network.v0
QUESTION: When a developmental process turns a genotype into a body plan,
  are all body plans equally easy to produce, or does the process itself,
  before any selection happens, make some outcomes far more common than
  others?
MECHANISM: Build a network of N nodes, each holding an on or off state.
  Wire each node to exactly K inputs drawn from the other nodes and give it
  a Boolean function of those inputs, in the classical construction by
  drawing the truth table at random. Set an initial state, the genotype.
  Update every node synchronously according to its function and its inputs'
  current states. Because the state space is finite and the update
  deterministic, the trajectory must eventually repeat, so it falls into a
  cyclic attractor. That attractor is the phenotype. Now the measurement:
  sample many genotypes, develop each one, and tabulate how often each
  attractor comes up. The basin sizes are wildly unequal, so a few
  phenotypes absorb most genotypes. That inequality is developmental bias,
  and it exists with no selection anywhere in the procedure. At K equal to
  2 the ensemble sits at the critical boundary between the frozen and
  chaotic regimes, which is why the template pins K to 2.
CANDIDATE: The genotype, here the initial state or the wiring; the
  phenotype is the attractor it reaches. What is judged is the frequency
  with which each phenotype appears.
WORLD: There is no environment in the usual sense, and that is the point.
  The world supplies only the update rule and the topology. No fitness
  function exists. Any structure in the output comes from the map itself.
WHAT_CHANGES: Number of nodes, in-degree K, the number of update steps
  allowed before an attractor is declared, and the sampled genotypes.
MEASURED: The frequency of the most common phenotype, a proportion between
  zero and one, or equivalently the entropy of the phenotype distribution.
  Both are scalars, but both require MANY developmental runs to estimate,
  which matters for the routes below.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Yes, and unusually so, because here the null result is
  the scientifically loaded one. If the phenotype distribution comes out
  flat you have found a developmental map with no bias, which contradicts
  the general expectation and is a genuine result about that map. If the
  network never settles within the allowed update steps you have learned
  the parameters put you in the chaotic regime, which relocates the
  boundary rather than wasting the run. Neither failure is empty.
SOURCE_CHECK: PLAUSIBLE - the cited paper is real and I confirmed it:

  global bias intrinsic to the developmental system. It is not a Boolean
  network study. The Boolean network with K equal to 2 and random truth

TEMPLATE_ID: intrinsic.v0
FIELD: Intrinsic Motivation
KIND: iac_exploration.v0
QUESTION: If a learning agent is given no goal at all, and instead simply
  chooses whatever activity it is currently getting better at fastest, will
  it teach itself a sensible sequence of skills, starting easy and moving
  to hard entirely on its own?
MECHANISM: The agent acts, and after each action tries to predict what its
  sensors will read next. It keeps the prediction errors. It carves its
  sensorimotor space into regions, splitting a region in two whenever the
  data inside become heterogeneous enough that two child regions predict
  better than the parent. For each region it keeps a recent history of
  prediction error and computes LEARNING PROGRESS, the rate at which that
  error is falling, which is a derivative over time and not the error
  itself. It then chooses its next action mostly from whichever region has
  the highest learning progress, with some random exploration mixed in. The
  consequence is a self-organised curriculum: a region already mastered has
  near-zero error and therefore near-zero progress, so the agent leaves it;
  a region that is pure noise has high error but also near-zero progress,
  because the error never falls, so the agent leaves that too. Using
  progress rather than error is precisely what stops the agent being
  captured by unpredictable noise.
CANDIDATE: The agent's behaviour policy, which changes continuously. What
  is judged is the sequence of activities the agent chose, not any single
  action.
WORLD: A sensorimotor environment offering activities of differing
  learnability, canonically including at least one that is easy, one that
  is hard but learnable, and one that is unlearnable noise. That last one
  is not decoration: without it the experiment cannot show what it exists
  to show.
WHAT_CHANGES: The sensor dimension, null in this template, and the learning
  rate of the prediction model.
MEASURED: In the real work, the time-ordered sequence of which region the
  agent selected, compared against the regions' true difficulty. Scalar
  reductions exist but are lossy: rank correlation between selection time
  and difficulty, or fraction of a run spent in the unlearnable region.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Yes, and sharply. If the agent parks itself in the
  unlearnable region, the failure has isolated the noisy-television
  pathology and shows the progress estimator's window or smoothing is
  wrong. If the agent never leaves the easy region, the split criterion is
  too conservative. Both failures name a specific component. The failure
  that leaves nothing behind is a run reported only as a final scalar
  score, because the entire claim of this method is about the ORDER of
  activities and a final scalar destroys order.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED - Oudeyer, Kaplan and Hafner, Intrinsic Motivation
  Systems for Autonomous Mental Development, IEEE Transactions on
  Evolutionary Computation, 2007, is the correct reference for Intelligent
  Adaptive Curiosity, and the region-splitting plus learning-progress
  mechanism and the developmental-trajectory claim are as described.
SHARED_MECHANISM: self_directed_curriculum
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-SUBSTRATE, R-BACKEND
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE - for the stripped diagnostic below the pieces can be
  collected as separate specs and the statistic computed downstream; the
  blocker is ADJUDICATION, since comparing an error slope across two
  conditions is a two-field expression and the outcome rule permits one.
  R-SUBSTRATE - for the method itself the bench lacks the ability for one
  step to choose the next, which is output-to-input chaining, and lacks any
  persistent model accumulating across repeats. STATE AND CONTROL-FLOW
  capability, and it is the fundamental blocker: a sealed spec with a fixed
  payload cannot, by construction, select its next action. R-BACKEND -
  existing intrinsic-motivation codebases could be invoked and their
  trajectory fossilized. MEASUREMENT capability.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs an agent whose action at step t depends
  on results from steps before t, a model that persists and improves, and a
  fossil format recording an ordered trajectory rather than a scalar,
  because the claim is about order. It also needs a world containing an
  unlearnable region, since without one the central discrimination cannot
  be tested at all.
REDUCTION: The agent cannot be reduced, but the SIGNAL it runs on can, and
  the bench supplies the two required conditions for free through its own
  repeat semantics. Learning progress exists to separate a learnable region
  from an unlearnable one, and the bench already has exactly that pair.
  Take one fixed bitstring and one length. Condition A:
  repeat.seed_derivation = constant, so every repeat scores against the
  SAME hidden target. That is a learnable region: a learner watching this
  stream sees a stable target and its error can fall. Condition B:
  sha256_index, so every repeat scores against a DIFFERENT target. That is
  an unlearnable region: the target is redrawn each time and no amount of
  observation reduces future error. Collect both streams, fit the
  error-slope statistic you intend to use as learning progress, and check

  exactly the case the bench flags as degenerate_by_construction, and that
  is correct and is the point: zero within-world variance is what perfect
  learnability looks like from the outside. The degenerate flag and the
  maximum-learning-progress condition are the same fact seen from two
  sides, which suggests the flag is carrying information the bench
  currently throws away as an error.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: the learnable-versus-noise discrimination, the
  progress statistic itself, its window length, and its standard error,
  measured on a case where the ground truth is known by construction.
  WHAT IT LOSES: the agent, action selection, region splitting, the
  developmental trajectory and the self-organised curriculum. In other
  words it loses everything that makes Intelligent Adaptive Curiosity a
  method rather than a statistic.
  WHICH CLAIM IT SUPPORTS: only that the chosen learning-progress estimator
  can tell a fixed target from a redrawn one at a given sample size. Label
  it an instrument test. It is not an intrinsic-motivation result and must
  never be fossilized as one.

  typically how long the system was kept within bounds. The wider method
  around this evaluation is where NEAT lives: start every network minimal
  and fully connected input to output with no hidden nodes, mutate weights
  and occasionally add a node or a connection, use the historical markings

  meaningful, and protect new structure inside species so that a mutation

FIELD: Neural Architecture Search
KIND: nas_search_v0
QUESTION: Given a fixed number of tries, does a smarter search strategy
  find a better neural network design than simply guessing at random?
MECHANISM: Training neural networks to compare architectures is
  prohibitively expensive, so the benchmark pre-computes the answer once.
  A constrained space of small architectures is enumerated exhaustively,
  each one is trained several times to fixed epoch budgets, and the
  resulting accuracies and training times are stored in a table. After
  that, evaluating an architecture is a table lookup, so a search algorithm
  can be run thousands of times and its variability measured honestly.
  The search itself is the object of study. Random search draws
  architectures uniformly. Regularized evolution keeps a fixed-size
  population, repeatedly samples a small tournament, mutates the best
  member of the tournament, adds the child, and removes the OLDEST member
  rather than the worst, which is the regularization: it prevents a lucky
  early architecture from dominating forever and keeps the population
  turning over. Both algorithms are charged the same query budget, and the
  comparison is between best-found accuracy at equal budget, over many
  independent repetitions.
CANDIDATE: An architecture, an encoded cell graph. What is judged is the
  best accuracy the search found within its budget, not any single
  architecture.

  the search space, which is what makes the search problem easy or hard.
WHAT_CHANGES: The search algorithm and the query budget.
MEASURED: Best validation accuracy found within budget, continuous, and
  properly the regret against the table's known global optimum, since the
  optimum is known by exhaustive enumeration.

  smarter algorithm fails to beat random search, that is the field's single
  most-cited cautionary result and it is a strong finding, not a null: it

  of each algorithm, because the between-run variance of these searches is
  large enough to swamp the between-algorithm difference; a single-run
  comparison is a coin flip dressed as a result.

REDUCTION: Strip out generation and open-endedness entirely and keep only
  the transfer claim, which is the one part that is a scalar comparison.
  Call a hidden target an environment, and a bitstring a solution. Have the
  producer optimise a bitstring against target A across specs, then, in one
  further spec, score that same bitstring against target B. Compare with
  the best bitstring found for B under the same query budget.
  Here is the finding, and it arrives before the experiment runs. Targets
  are derived by hashing the seed with the length, so targets from
  different seeds share nothing by construction, and targets at different
  lengths are not even comparable, since a candidate of length L cannot be
  scored at length L prime. Therefore transfer between any two distinct
  environments on this bench is EXACTLY zero in expectation, and the
  measurement would be a confirmation of the hash, not of anything about
  learning. There is no relatedness axis anywhere in the current world
  definition.
  So the reduction that survives is not the transfer experiment but the
  requirement it exposes, and it is worth stating as a deliverable in its
  own right: the bench cannot host any transfer, curriculum, stepping-stone
  or generalisation experiment until it has a family of environments with a
  DISTANCE between them. The minimal such capability is small and concrete:
  a target derived as the seed target with d positions flipped, so that
  environments sit at a controllable Hamming distance d, with d equal to
  zero recovering the current behaviour. That single parameter would turn
  this template, and several others in this chunk, from impossible into
  routine.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: nothing of POET as run. WHAT IT LOSES: everything.
  WHICH CLAIM IT SUPPORTS: a measured zero-transfer baseline, which is
  worth one cheap spec because it converts an assumption about the bench
  into a recorded fact, plus a named substrate requirement with a proposed
  implementation. I would not call this an instance of the method, and it
  should not be fossilized as one.
END_ENTRY

TEMPLATE_ID: science_of_science.v0
FIELD: Science of Science
KIND: network_cascade.v0
QUESTION: Why are citations so unequally distributed, with a few papers
  collecting enormous numbers and most collecting almost none? Can a simple
  growth rule, with no notion of quality, reproduce that inequality on its
  own?
MECHANISM: Grow a graph one node at a time. Each arriving node attaches a
  fixed number of edges to nodes already present, choosing each target with
  probability proportional to how many edges that target already has,
  usually plus a small constant so that a node with none can still be
  chosen. That is the entire rule, and it is enough: because attachment
  probability rises with current degree, early and lucky nodes accumulate
  advantage, and the degree distribution develops a heavy tail. Richer
  variants in this literature multiply the degree term by a per-node
  fitness, so that quality and accumulated advantage compete, and add an
  ageing term so that old papers stop attracting citations, which together
  reproduce the observed pattern in which most papers peak and decline
  while a few remain live for decades. The measurement at the end is a
  structural statistic over the finished graph.
CANDIDATE: The graph. What is judged is a summary statistic of its shape,
  not any individual node.
WORLD: The graph as it stands at each step. This is the important point:
  the environment for an arriving node IS the accumulated state, which is
  why the process cannot be decomposed into independent draws.
WHAT_CHANGES: Initial node count and attachment rate, both null here, plus
  in serious work the fitness and ageing terms.
MEASURED: A structural statistic of the final graph: Gini coefficient of
  the degree distribution, share of edges held by the top one percent,
  maximum degree over node count, or a fitted power-law exponent. All
  scalar, but they differ enormously in how safely they can be gated on.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Moderately informative. If the generated inequality
  falls short of the empirical inequality, the failure says pure
  accumulated advantage is insufficient and some additional term, fitness
  or ageing or a citation-copying mechanism, is required, which is a
  positive result about mechanism. The failure that leaves nothing behind
  is a mismatch reported against a badly estimated power-law exponent,
  because that is a failure of the fitting procedure and says nothing about
  the generative model at all.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED - Fortunato et al., Science of Science, Science,
  2018, is real and is a broad review covering citation dynamics and the
  complex-network perspective, so the description in origin is accurate as
  a description of that review. One attribution caveat for the operator:
  the preferential-attachment mechanism itself is not that paper's
  contribution and belongs to the earlier cumulative-advantage and
  scale-free network literature. Citing the review for the mechanism is
  Tier-2 sourcing and should be pinned to a primary source before the
  template is admitted.
SHARED_MECHANISM: accumulate_trajectory
ROUTES: R-EXECUTOR, R-NOW, R-REPAIR
BLOCKER: R-EXECUTOR - the bench cannot build a graph or compute a
  structural statistic; that is a MEASUREMENT capability and a genuinely
  small one, since a preferential-attachment generator returning one scalar
  is a few dozen lines. R-NOW - the existing walk gives only the
  state-INDEPENDENT null, not the model; the constraint is INTERPRETIVE.
  R-REPAIR - both axes are null; a specification gap.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs an accumulating structure whose next
  step depends on its current state, which means real persistent state
  inside the run rather than across repeats, and a statistic computed over
  the whole finished object. It also needs the statistic chosen in advance
  and defensible under a single-comparison gate, since the outcome rule
  cannot carry a goodness-of-fit test alongside a fitted exponent.
REDUCTION: The bench already has half of this, and naming which half is the
  useful contribution. random_walk_v0 is a stateful accumulation process,
  which is the same broad mechanism, but its increments do NOT depend on
  its current state: the step is uniform and fixed-scale, while
  preferential attachment's step probability is proportional to current
  degree. So the walk is precisely the state-independent null against which
  the rich-get-richer model has to be judged. Run it with persist state and
  sha256_index repeats to obtain, measured rather than assumed, what the
  chosen inequality statistic looks like under accumulation WITHOUT
  cumulative advantage. Then a small pref_attach_v0 executor returning the
  same statistic makes the comparison a real contrast with a real null, and
  the difference between the two is exactly the contribution of the
  state-dependence, which is the whole scientific content.
  One concrete design instruction that follows from the outcome rule. Do
  NOT gate on a fitted power-law exponent. Estimating a power-law exponent
  requires a lower cutoff choice and a goodness-of-fit test reported
  alongside it, and the bench permits one field, one operator, one value,
  so a gate on an exponent would silently drop the part of the procedure
  that makes it valid. Gate instead on the Gini coefficient of the degree
  distribution or the top-one-percent edge share: both are single numbers
  that are meaningful without an accompanying test, and both have a
  computable attainable range, so the gate can be shown reachable before
  it is frozen.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: state-dependent accumulation as the mechanism under
  test, a single defensible inequality statistic, and a measured rather
  than assumed null.
  WHAT IT LOSES: fitness, ageing, the distinction between citing and cited,
  time-resolved citation trajectories, and any contact with real
  bibliometric data. It is a mechanism-sufficiency study, not a fit to
  observation.
  WHICH CLAIM IT SUPPORTS: that state-dependent attachment produces
  inequality exceeding the state-independent accumulation null by a stated
  margin. Nothing about real science, and certainly nothing about policy,
  which the template's own rationale is right to disclaim.

TEMPLATE_ID: version_space_search.v0
FIELD: Inductive Reasoning
KIND: version_space_search_v0
QUESTION: As you show a learner labelled examples one at a time, how fast
  does the set of rules still consistent with everything it has seen shrink
  toward a single answer?
MECHANISM: Fix a language of hypotheses in advance, classically
  conjunctions over attributes where each attribute is either pinned to a
  value, or a wildcard matching anything, or the empty symbol matching
  nothing. The version space is the set of hypotheses consistent with every
  example seen so far. Candidate Elimination represents that set implicitly
  by only its two boundaries: S, the most specific consistent hypotheses,
  and G, the most general. On a POSITIVE example, drop any member of G that
  fails to cover it, and minimally generalise each member of S until it
  covers it. On a NEGATIVE example, drop any member of S that covers it,
  and minimally specialise each member of G until it no longer does. The
  version space is everything lying between the two boundaries in the
  generality ordering, so its size can be huge while S and G stay small.
  Learning is complete when S and G coincide on a single hypothesis. The
  method is exact and non-statistical: it never scores a hypothesis, it
  only eliminates the inconsistent, and one contradictory example collapses
  the space to empty.
CANDIDATE: The whole surviving hypothesis set, not any single hypothesis.
  What varies is which examples arrive and in what order.
WORLD: A hypothesis language plus a stream of labelled examples. The
  environment supplies both the language, which is the inductive bias and
  is chosen not learned, and a target concept that must lie inside that
  language for convergence to be possible at all.
WHAT_CHANGES: The number of attributes defining the concept and the number
  of training examples. Both null here.
MEASURED: The size of the version space after m examples, a positive
  integer that falls monotonically, and convergence when it reaches one.
  Log of the size is the better scale, since it falls roughly linearly with
  informative examples.
INFORMATIVE_FAILURE: Strongly informative and cleanly interpretable, which
  is rare. Failure comes in exactly two shapes and they mean opposite
  things. A version space that COLLAPSES TO EMPTY proves the target concept
  is not expressible in the chosen hypothesis language, or that an example
  was mislabelled: it is a proof about the language, not a shortfall of the
  learner. A version space that stalls above one after all examples proves
  the examples were insufficient to discriminate, and the surviving set
  names exactly which distinctions were never tested, which tells you which
  example to obtain next. That second case is the seed of active learning
  and links this template directly to the Robot Scientist and intrinsic
  motivation templates in this same chunk.
SOURCE_CHECK: VERIFIED - Tom Mitchell's version spaces and the Candidate
  Elimination algorithm are real and foundational, introduced in the
  mid-to-late 1970s and developed in his later work on generalisation as
  search. The 1977 date and the description of the S and G boundaries are
  consistent with the standard account. Both parameter axes are null.
SHARED_MECHANISM: prune_hypothesis_space
ROUTES: R-COMPOSE, R-EXECUTOR, R-REPAIR
BLOCKER: R-COMPOSE - each observation must be applied to the state left by
  the previous one, and the bench cannot chain repeat outputs into repeat
  inputs, so the elimination must be carried by the producer across specs.
  CONTROL FLOW, specifically output-to-input chaining. R-EXECUTOR - a
  self-contained kind that plants a concept, draws m examples and returns
  the final version space size. MEASUREMENT capability, and the cheapest
  executor in this chunk. R-REPAIR - both axes null; specification gap.
FAITHFUL: A faithful version needs a hypothesis language object, a
  generality partial order over it so that minimal generalisation and
  specialisation are defined, and the two boundary sets carried across
  examples. It also needs the example stream to be part of the world,
  because convergence rate depends on which examples arrive and in what
  order, so a template that fixes only their COUNT under-specifies the
  experiment.
REDUCTION: This is the best reduction in the chunk and it needs no new
  executor, because evaluate_bitstring is already a version space and
  nobody has said so. Fix a world seed and a length L, so there is one
  hidden target T, which is an unknown point in a space of two to the power
  L possibilities. That set of possibilities IS the initial version space.
  Submit a candidate bitstring C in one spec. The returned score is the
  fraction of positions matching, so it tells you exactly how many
  positions of C agree with T, call it k. The set of targets consistent
  with that single observation is precisely the set of strings at Hamming
  distance L minus k from C, whose size is L choose k, computable in closed
  form. Submit a second candidate in a second spec at the SAME seed and
  length, and the surviving set is the intersection of two Hamming spheres,
  again exactly countable. Repeat. The producer maintains the surviving set
  size and watches it collapse toward one. That is candidate elimination,
  run for real, with the bench supplying observations and the producer
  supplying the elimination. It is the game of Mastermind, which is what
  evaluate_bitstring has been all along.
  It also makes the active-learning question immediately measurable, at no
  extra cost: compare the collapse rate under RANDOM candidate choice
  against candidates chosen to maximise expected information, and you have
  a real, quantitative result about how much choosing your experiments is
  worth, on a space where the exact answer is computable rather than
  estimated. That result is portable to the Robot Scientist template's
  hypothesis loop and to the intrinsic-motivation template's curriculum,
  which is a genuine cross-discipline payoff from a single cheap run.
  WHAT IT PRESERVES: exact elimination rather than scoring, exact
  computable version space size, monotone collapse, the dependence of
  convergence on which examples were chosen, and the empty-space failure
  mode, which appears here if two observations are mutually inconsistent,
  proving a bug rather than a hard problem.
  WHAT IT LOSES: the S and G boundary representation, since a Hamming
  constraint set is not a conjunctive lattice and has no most-specific and
  most-general pair; the inductive bias question, since the language is
  fixed and complete rather than chosen; and generalisation to unseen
  instances, since there are no unseen instances.
  WHICH CLAIM IT SUPPORTS: a quantitative claim about hypothesis-space
  collapse rate under random versus chosen queries, with the exact
  remaining size known at every step. That is a real result and it is
  available today.
END_ENTRY
