COSMOS C3 — EXTERNAL SEAT REQUIREMENTS

This directive clarifies how other Prometheus seats participate in C3.

The core rule is simple:

Cosmos owns C3. Other seats exist only to provide independent holdouts or independent audits that Cosmos must not author itself.

Do not distribute ordinary C3 development across multiple seats.

Do not turn C3 into a committee project.

Do not wait for another seat before beginning the parts Cosmos can build independently.

⸻

1. COSMOS OWNS C3

Cosmos is responsible for:

* the P1 persistence certificate;
* the P2 causal-utility certificate;
* planted calibration systems;
* the three visible substrate families A/B/C;
* substrate adapters;
* native instrumentation;
* substitution attacks;
* invariant mining;
* adversarial falsification;
* uncertainty estimation;
* candidate coordinate construction;
* candidate-law lifecycle;
* receipts and reproducibility;
* Session 1 review packet;
* the eventual D prediction;
* any post-D revision allowed by protocol;
* the final frozen prediction/intervention submitted to E.

Other seats must not co-author Cosmos’s visible worlds or candidate law.

The purpose of outside involvement is specifically to break the one-author circularity identified in C0.

⸻

2. WHY OTHER SEATS ARE NEEDED

C0 had an important limitation:

The same author created the substrates and also defined the coordinates used to compare them.

Even with no deliberate leakage, this can produce false universality because common design assumptions can become embedded in every world.

C3 therefore requires at least one substrate that Cosmos did not design.

The strongest evidence comes from:

[
\text{Cosmos develops law without seeing foreign world}
]

followed by:

[
\text{foreign world adjudicates frozen prediction}.
]

The foreign seat is therefore a holdout author, not a C3 co-developer.

⸻

3. HOLDOUT D — DEVELOPMENT HOLDOUT

D is the first foreign world.

Use D only after:

1. P1/P2 calibration passes;
2. A/B/C are operational;
3. the candidate cross-substrate coordinates exist;
4. the candidate law exists;
5. Cosmos has completed its visible-world adversarial attacks;
6. the coordinate audit has passed;
7. the D prediction has been frozen.

D should be authored by a seat other than Cosmos.

Preferred initial candidate: Bellerophon, because it already works with world/substrate machinery.

This is a recommendation, not a requirement. If another seat is better positioned when the request is issued, use that seat.

D author receives only:

* the functional phenomenon contract;
* required execution interface;
* P1/P2 definitions;
* allowed native-observation interface;
* sealing protocol;
* reproducibility requirements;
* intervention interface requirements;
* prohibited-information list.

D author must NOT receive:

* Cosmos’s candidate law;
* candidate coefficients;
* candidate latent coordinates;
* expected phase boundary;
* current A/B/C implementations;
* A/B/C results;
* the invariant grammar’s surviving expressions;
* known Cosmos failure regions;
* the specific intervention Cosmos expects to make;
* any hint intended to make D conform to the law.

The D author should independently design a substrate satisfying the functional task.

Mechanical alienness is desirable.

Agreement with Cosmos is not.

⸻

4. D SEALING REQUIREMENTS

Before Cosmos receives any D outcomes:

The D seat must freeze:

* source/version;
* world grammar;
* parameter ranges;
* native observables;
* seeds;
* execution environment;
* intervention handles;
* test protocol;
* hidden evaluation rows;
* cryptographic commitment/hash.

The seal must be committed and pushed before Cosmos’s D prediction is revealed to the D seat or adjudicator.

Cosmos should receive only:

* the seal/commitment;
* the substrate’s declared execution contract;
* native measurement schema if required for prediction translation;
* enough metadata to construct its frozen prediction without seeing results.

Cosmos must not inspect D implementation internals before adjudication if the protocol can avoid it.

⸻

5. D IS A DEVELOPMENT HOLDOUT, BUT ONLY ONCE

D may be spent once.

Cosmos freezes its prediction and intervention before reveal.

Then D is adjudicated.

After D reveal:

[
D = \text{SPENT}.
]

Any changes influenced by D are labeled:

POST-D DEVELOPMENT

and no longer count as independent evidence on D.

If D exposes a defect, Cosmos may revise the law using A/B/C plus the lessons from D.

But the revised law requires a new independent final holdout.

Do not repeatedly query D.

Do not tune thresholds against D.

Do not turn D into a fourth training family.

⸻

6. HOLDOUT E — FINAL ADJUDICATOR

E is stronger than D.

E should be written by a different seat from both Cosmos and, ideally, D’s author.

Preferred initial candidate: Aether, because its artificial-physics lineage may provide a mechanically different world family.

Again, this is a recommendation, not a mandatory seat assignment.

E should not be commissioned merely because C3 exists.

Only create/use E once:

* D has been adjudicated;
* any permitted post-D revision is complete;
* the final candidate law is frozen;
* the final coordinate map is frozen;
* the final intervention is frozen;
* all thresholds and uncertainty contracts are frozen.

E is the final blind test.

⸻

7. E IS TERMINAL

E gets one adjudication.

No rescue round.

No law revision using E.

No threshold adjustment.

No coordinate remapping.

No alternate interpretation selected after reveal.

No “E2.”

If E falsifies the final candidate:

[
\text{candidate law FAILS final adjudication}.
]

Record the scar and stop that claim.

E can later be studied diagnostically, but anything learned after reveal is post-holdout development and does not rescue the frozen law.

⸻

8. FOREIGN WORLD AUTHORS SHOULD NOT DECLARE UNIVERSAL COORDINATES

The holdout seat provides:

* native measurements;
* native units;
* native controls;
* native interventions.

It should not tell Cosmos:

“This native variable corresponds to universal memory pressure.”

That would reintroduce answer-smuggling.

The translation from native measurements into cross-substrate coordinates belongs outside the substrate author.

Foreign authors describe what their world measures physically.

Cosmos or the independent audit layer decides whether and how those measurements map into candidate universal coordinates.

⸻

9. COORDINATE FIREWALL

C3 needs a strict separation between:

[
\text{world construction}
]

and

[
\text{universal coordinate construction}.
]

A substrate author may say:

* this variable is a decay rate;
* this variable is an energy expenditure;
* this variable is a delay;
* this variable is channel capacity;
* this variable is observable state entropy.

The substrate author should not say:

* this is the universal memory cost;
* this is the universal recurrence coordinate;
* this is where Cosmos’s boundary should lie.

Cosmos proposes mappings only after inspecting A/B/C.

Foreign D/E mappings should be as constrained and preregistered as possible before outcomes are known.

⸻

10. HARMONIA’S ROLE

Harmonia is an audit seat, not a co-author.

Do not send Harmonia work during ordinary C3 construction.

Invoke Harmonia only when Cosmos has:

* working A/B/C;
* candidate coordinates;
* candidate law;
* proposed normalization;
* visible-family residual analysis.

Harmonia’s job is to attack the coordinate layer before D is spent.

Harmonia should inspect for:

* substrate-ID leakage;
* answer-smuggling through native measurements;
* arbitrary normalizations;
* pooled errors hiding opposite-signed family errors;
* family-specific offsets;
* transformations that change units but should not change the law;
* coordinates that are merely aliases for mechanism identity;
* variables whose definitions differ semantically between substrates;
* hidden shared evaluator assumptions.

Harmonia should return one of:

AUDIT_PASS

REVISE

REJECT

D must not be adjudicated under a coordinate system that Harmonia has rejected.

If Harmonia recommends revision, Cosmos revises using visible families only and resubmits.

13. P1 / P2 HARD GATE

Before progressing deeply into law discovery, Cosmos must distinguish:

P1 — persistence

Past information unavailable from current observation remains encoded in current system state.

Conceptually:

[
I(S_t;H_{t-k}\mid O_t)>\tau.
]

P2 — causal utility

Destroying/swapping the historical component while preserving current observation reduces future task performance.

Conceptually:

[
J_{\text{intact}} - J_{\text{history-ablated}} > \epsilon.
]

Calibration must include at least:

* no-memory negative;
* passive-history encoder;
* causally useful explicit memory;
* distributed memory;
* a misleading-correlate control.

If Cosmos cannot distinguish PASSIVE historical encoding from causally useful historical state, stop C3 before spending foreign holdouts.

14. SUBSTITUTION ATTACKS

Before D, actively test whether removing one apparent memory mechanism causes another channel to carry history.

Possible substitutes include:

* environmental modification;
* stigmergy;
* recurrence;
* communication;
* population state;
* recomputation;
* predictive model state;
* structural change.

The purpose is to test whether the candidate law is actually about:

MEMORY MODULE

or something deeper such as:

future-relevant information remaining causally accessible across an interval where direct observation is unavailable.

Mechanism substitution is evidence that the law may need to move upward in abstraction.

Do not automatically call substitution a failure.

Determine whether it falsifies the law or reveals a more general law.

Stop before D if:

* P1/P2 calibration is unreliable;
* passive encoding cannot be separated from causal memory;
* visible families do not show a coherent comparable phenomenon;
* candidate coordinates obviously encode substrate identity;
* Harmonia rejects the coordinate system;

Stop before E if:

* D exposes a fundamental flaw in the chamber;
* D requires so much post-hoc reinterpretation that the original phenomenon changed;
* Cosmos cannot freeze one clear final law and intervention;
* D performs no better than simple transfer baselines;
* uncertainty is too large to make E discriminating.

Do not burn E merely because it exists.
