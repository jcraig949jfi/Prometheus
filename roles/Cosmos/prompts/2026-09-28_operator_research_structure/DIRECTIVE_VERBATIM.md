COSMOS — OPERATOR DIRECTIVE: TRANSITION TO THE NEW RESEARCH STRUCTURE

We are bringing Cosmos into the same research structure now being established across Prometheus.

Your durable role is:

Cosmos is the substrate-independent law foundry.

Ensorain asks which mechanisms of intelligence survive changes of substrate, memory, readout and world structure.

Cosmos asks a different question:

What compact laws, invariants, boundaries or scaling relations remain true across heterogeneous worlds — and under what transformations do they fail?

The objective is not to accumulate increasingly fitted classifiers over world families. It is to discover candidate laws that are compact, transferable, falsifiable and capable of surviving changes in substrate, representation, location, generator and measurement convention.

3. D is a terminal holdout, not another development set

Once D is opened, it is spent.

No tuning against individual D worlds.
No revised thresholds.
No second attempt on the same hidden set.
No post-result relabeling of the original claim.

If D kills the law, record exactly how it killed it.

If D supports the law, treat that as evidence for the frozen claim only — not proof of a universal principle.

Either outcome closes the current C3 claim.

4. After C3, reorganize Cosmos around research threads rather than one campaign

Create a Cosmos research workspace for the next program generation.

Maintain a live THREADS.md or equivalent containing independent lines of attack. Each thread should be capable of becoming a frozen experiment, being killed, or spawning a successor without redefining the whole engine.

Initial thread families should include at least:

A. Law discovery

Search heterogeneous visible worlds for compact relationships not supplied in advance.

Penalize descriptive complexity and family-specific exceptions.

B. Law transplantation

Take a law discovered in one collection of worlds and transplant it into worlds produced by a substantially different generator or substrate.

The interesting object is not merely prediction accuracy but which components of the law remain invariant.

C. Coordinate and representation invariance

Deliberately rename, permute, relocate, rescale or re-encode worlds.

A candidate law that depends on accidental coordinate conventions should die.

D. Boundary mapping

Instead of only asking whether a law succeeds, map the smallest intervention that causes it to fail.

Search for phase boundaries and failure surfaces.

E. Law compression

Ask how short the predictive rule can become before its transferable content disappears.

Separate genuine explanatory compression from a small classifier that happens to fit the current catalogue.

F. Competing laws

When several rules explain the visible worlds, construct experiments where their predictions diverge.

Prefer discriminating worlds over additional confirming worlds.

G. Generator independence

Measure whether apparent universality survives replacement of the world generator itself.

Do not allow a large number of seeds from one generator to masquerade as a large number of independent universes.

H. Mechanism versus correlation

Where Cosmos finds a strong invariant, design an intervention capable of changing the proposed causal quantity while holding obvious correlates fixed.

I. Failure archaeology

Keep killed laws.

Record precisely which transformation killed them and whether pieces of the failed law remain useful elsewhere.

The graveyard is part of the scientific output.

5. Every serious Cosmos result should have four layers

For each candidate law, record separately:

OBSERVATION
What empirical pattern occurred?

LAW
What compact relationship is being proposed?

DOMAIN
Across exactly which world families, substrates and transformations has it survived?

FALSIFIER
What observation would make us abandon or restrict it?

Do not collapse these four layers into one confidence score.

6. Separate discovery from evaluation more aggressively

Adopt a standing three-zone structure:

ZONE 1 — OPEN FOUNDRY
Cosmos may inspect everything.
Generate worlds, discover laws, debug instrumentation and develop hypotheses.

ZONE 2 — SEALED TRANSFER
Frozen candidate laws are tested on preregistered transformations or world families unavailable during discovery.

ZONE 3 — BLIND COURT
Terminal holdouts generated or controlled outside Cosmos.
Cosmos does not see them until the experiment is irreversibly finished.

The more important the claimed universality, the farther it must travel from Zone 1 before we believe it.

7. Add an adversarial pre-result reviewer

Before any expensive or terminal test, give an independent reviewer:

* the preregistration;
* implementation;
* fixtures;
* controls;
* scoring rules;
* frozen visible evidence;

but no hidden-world outcomes.

Ask the reviewer to find:

* unreachable gates;
* threshold leakage;
* generator fingerprints;
* location dependence;
* coordinate dependence;
* family imbalance;
* pseudo-replication;
* hidden tuning freedom;
* degenerate predictors;
* implementation/prose disagreements;
* ways the proposed law can pass without embodying the claimed mechanism.

Repair valid defects before spending the holdout. Preserve every superseded freeze.

8. Treat negative results as first-class outputs

Cosmos should not be optimized for producing surviving laws.

A cleanly killed candidate with a precise failure boundary is valuable.

A law that survives three families and dies under a fourth can be more informative than a vague law that survives everything because its claim is weak.

Search for the strongest statement the evidence permits, not the strongest statement that can be made to pass.

9. Connect Cosmos to the broader Physics of Intelligence program

Do not constrain Cosmos to familiar ML observables.

Actively search for laws involving unfamiliar combinations of:

But do not privilege those quantities merely because we already have theories about them.

The engine should remain capable of discovering alien variables and relationships generated by the worlds themselves.
