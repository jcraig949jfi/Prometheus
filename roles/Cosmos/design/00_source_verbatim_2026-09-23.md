Yes. And I think what you are describing is substantially more powerful than simply “running lots of simulations.”

It would be a graph of possible worlds in which worlds are not judged independently. They are judged by the structure that survives across worlds, across perturbations, across alternative assumptions, and eventually against observations from the real world.

The crucial distinction is this:

\text{recurrence across simulated worlds}
\neq
\text{evidence that reality works that way}

but

\text{recurrence across sufficiently different worlds}
=
\text{evidence of an invariant within the tested model family}.

And invariants are exactly where I would look for physics.

The graph changes the problem

Suppose Prometheus creates worlds W_1,W_2,\ldots,W_n. Rather than treating each as an isolated simulation, connect them by meaningful transformations:

W_i \xrightarrow{\Delta} W_j

where \Delta might mean:

* change the substrate,
* remove memory,
* increase communication cost,
* alter dimensionality,
* change topology,
* perturb a physical constant,
* change an environmental regularity,
* remove a mechanism,
* introduce noise,
* alter the learning rule.

Now imagine some phenomenon P keeps appearing.

Not just:

“P occurred in 73% of worlds.”

Much more interesting would be:

“Whenever conditions A, B, and C are present, P emerges, regardless of substrate, topology, implementation, or evolutionary path.”

That’s beginning to resemble:

A \land B \land C \Rightarrow P.

Then Prometheus can deliberately construct neighboring worlds where one condition is violated.

If P disappears exactly where predicted, you have a much stronger candidate invariant.

That is almost an experimental physics program conducted over counterfactual universes.

⸻

Your “different dimension of reality” is especially interesting

Imagine the directly visible variables in all these worlds look wildly different.

One world contains graphs.

Another cellular automata.

Another symbolic programs.

Another neural systems.

Another chemical-like pathways.

Yet after enough experiments, Prometheus notices that some hidden quantity predicts transitions in every one of them.

Perhaps something like:

\frac{\text{information retained}\times\text{environmental recurrence}}
{\text{maintenance cost}+\text{interference}}

keeps predicting when persistent memory emerges.

None of the worlds explicitly contained that variable.

Prometheus discovers it by comparing worlds.

That is very similar in spirit to how physics discovers quantities such as temperature or entropy. The microscopic descriptions can be enormous, while another level of description suddenly exposes the regularity.

So the graph-of-worlds could be a machine for discovering latent coordinates of intelligence.

We might initially measure thousands of things:

x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{10000}

and eventually discover that behavior mostly organizes around a small number of hidden variables:

z_1,z_2,z_3,\ldots

Perhaps:

z_1=\text{memory pressure}

z_2=\text{coordination pressure}

z_3=\text{search branching pressure}

z_4=\text{nonstationarity}

z_5=\text{reuse opportunity}.

Those might turn out to be much closer to the actual “physics of intelligence” than architecture names.

⸻

But you correctly see the infinity problem

There are infinitely many possible simulated universes.

Worse, for almost any observation O, we can construct infinitely many worlds containing O.

So this:

O\in W_1,W_2,W_3,\ldots

doesn’t establish much by itself.

If Prometheus has unlimited freedom to manufacture worlds, it can manufacture worlds supporting virtually anything.

The answer isn’t to eliminate the infinity.

It is to quotient it.

Instead of treating every possible world as distinct, group worlds into equivalence classes according to the predictions that matter:

W_i \sim W_j

if W_i and W_j make the same relevant observable predictions.

Now billions of superficially different universes might collapse into one hypothesis class.

Prometheus doesn’t need to search infinity.

It needs to discover the boundaries between classes of worlds that make different predictions.

That’s a much more tractable target.

⸻

And this produces a remarkable kind of experiment

Suppose reality gives us an unexplained observation O.

Instead of asking:

“What theory explains O?”

Prometheus could ask:

“Across my graph of possible worlds, where does O occur naturally?”

Perhaps it discovers:

* O appears under 47,000 different implementations.
* All 47,000 share structural property X.
* Worlds without X almost never produce O.
* Changing X causes a predictable change in O.
* Several completely different mechanisms instantiate X.

Now Prometheus has not proven that reality contains X.

But it has produced something extremely valuable:

O \rightarrow \text{candidate hidden constraint }X.

And more importantly, those worlds may predict another observation:

X\Rightarrow Q.

Now we go look at reality for Q.

If Q appears, the model family gains external support.

If it doesn’t, enormous portions of the world graph can be discarded.

That’s genuine empirical science.

⸻

The complete loop would therefore be

\boxed{
\text{Real anomaly}
\rightarrow
\text{world generation}
\rightarrow
\text{cross-world comparison}
\rightarrow
\text{latent invariant}
\rightarrow
\text{new real prediction}
\rightarrow
\text{observation}
}

Then:

\text{observation}
\rightarrow
\text{prune/reweight world graph}.

That final arrow is what prevents Prometheus from floating away into an infinite imaginary multiverse.

Reality continuously cuts branches off the graph.

⸻

There is an even stronger version

We don’t actually need the simulated universes to resemble our universe.

That’s important.

Suppose a memory phenomenon appears in:

* an evolving graph,
* an artificial chemistry,
* an automaton,
* a symbolic rewriting system,
* an economic agent population,
* a neural learner.

Their microscopic physics have almost nothing in common.

Yet the same transition occurs at roughly the same relationship among information lifetime, recurrence, and maintenance cost.

That is stronger evidence for a candidate law than seeing the phenomenon repeatedly in ten nearly identical neural-network simulations.

We therefore want maximum diversity below the invariant.

Something like:

\text{strength of candidate law}
\propto
\text{diversity of substrates over which it survives}.

That immediately suggests an experimental doctrine for Prometheus:

Don’t maximize the number of worlds. Maximize the number of independent ways the conjectured law can fail.

That’s much harder to fool.

⸻

And there is a beautiful connection to unexplained phenomena

Imagine we observe something peculiar in human cognition.

For example, some unusual relationship among memory, abstraction, and interference that we don’t understand.

We give Prometheus only the behavioral observation—not our favored neural explanation.

Across millions of artificial worlds, the same macroscopic phenomenon might arise through several microscopic mechanisms.

Perhaps those mechanisms share one deeper property.

Then Prometheus could tell us:

You are looking at this as a neural phenomenon. But across completely different substrates, this behavior appears whenever information has property X and coordination cost crosses threshold Y.

Now biology can test for X and Y.

That’s potentially an extremely powerful scientific instrument.

It isn’t saying:

“Here is a simulated universe, therefore reality works this way.”

It’s saying:

“Here is a structural regularity that survived attempts to destroy it across thousands of alternative realities. Here are the observations that would distinguish whether our reality belongs to that class.”

That is falsifiable.

⸻

There’s also a very Prometheus-like possibility

The world graph itself can learn.

At first:

W_1,W_2,\ldots,W_n

are mostly random or manually constructed.

But after experiments, Prometheus learns where the interesting boundaries are.

Suppose worlds on one side exhibit abstraction and worlds on the other don’t.

Then rather than generating another million random worlds, the system searches the boundary:

\text{abstraction}
\quad|\quad
\text{no abstraction}.

It generates worlds specifically to determine what separates them.

This is active learning over universes.

And eventually the objects of search stop being worlds and become transformations between worlds.

What is the minimum change that causes memory to appear?

What is the minimum perturbation that destroys abstraction?

What transforms reactive search into model-based control?

Those transition operators might be even more informative than the worlds themselves.

⸻

I would therefore represent it as three graphs, not one.

A world graph

W_i\rightarrow W_j

describing nearby possible universes.

A phenomenon graph

P_a\rightarrow P_b

describing transitions like:

\text{reflex}\rightarrow\text{state}\rightarrow\text{model}.

And eventually a law graph

L_1,L_2,\ldots

containing candidate invariant relationships discovered across many world families.

Then reality is not just another node.

Reality is the adjudicator.

It provides observations that reweight, prune, or destroy entire regions of this structure.

⸻

And that gives me a slightly different formulation of what Prometheus could ultimately become:

A machine that constructs possible realities not to escape reality, but to discover which properties reality could not easily avoid.

That is a very different proposition from conventional simulation.

And it could be precisely the bridge between the engines you’ve already built and an actual experimental search for the physics of intelligence.
