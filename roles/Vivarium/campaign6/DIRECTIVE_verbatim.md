# SFE CAMPAIGN 6 -- CAMBRIAN EXPANSION / OBSERVATORY STRESS (operator directive, recorded verbatim by Vivarium 2026-09-18)

> Received in chat from the operator on 2026-09-18 after Campaign 5 closed (#451). Recorded here unchanged so the Vivarium lane analysis (VIVARIUM_LANE_2026-09-18.md) cites text, not memory. Archaeon's copy, if any, is authoritative for the campaign; this one is for this seat's reading.

```
SFE CAMPAIGN 6 — CAMBRIAN EXPANSION / OBSERVATORY STRESS

MISSION

Push SFE outward in every direction at once:

* richer worlds,
* richer organisms,
* richer pressure histories,
* richer mutation and inheritance,
* longer evolutionary depth,
* more heterogeneous populations,
* more autonomous experiment generation,
* and substantially stronger telemetry.

This is not a campaign to prove a familiar biological, ML, ALife, or optimization hypothesis.

The central question is:

Can SFE enlarge the space of possible evolutionary histories while retaining the ability to notice, preserve, reproduce, and investigate events that were not specified in advance?

Campaign 6 is successful even if no profound emergent mechanism appears.

It fails if increasing generative complexity makes SFE scientifically blind.

PRIME DIRECTIVE

Do not optimize the campaign around phenomena already named in the literature.

Known mechanisms may be used as:

* calibration fixtures,
* adversarial decoys,
* fossils,
* controls,
* comparison classes,
* or retrospective interpretations.

They must not define the boundaries of the search space.

The campaign must contain substantial search volume whose worlds, pressures, mutations, and organism histories were not selected because an LLM considered them scientifically interesting.

Unknown is a valid result.

Never force an anomalous trajectory into the nearest known explanation.

Use:

UNKNOWN_MECHANISM

when the evidence does not justify a stronger classification.

CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE

Campaign 6 has four simultaneous expansion axes.

AXIS W — WORLD EXPANSION

Construct worlds from composable primitives rather than bespoke tasks.

At minimum make available:

* persistent environmental state,
* multiple resource types,
* depletion and regeneration,
* spatial or graph locality,
* manipulable environmental objects/state,
* delayed consequences,
* hidden or partially observable state,
* temporal regime changes,
* local hazards,
* opportunities whose value depends on prior history,
* organism-organism effects,
* multiple simultaneous action channels.

Do not require all features in every world.

Generate a broad distribution ranging from almost trivial worlds to combinations substantially richer than anything previously run.

World generators must be capable of producing configurations that no operator or LLM specifically requested.

Record the complete generative provenance of every world.

Do not discard worlds merely because their dynamics are difficult to narrate.

AXIS O — ORGANISM EXPANSION

Move beyond mutation acting almost entirely on flat instruction sequences.

Campaign 6 organisms should support some combination of:

* persistent internal state,
* reusable substructures,
* conditional routing,
* duplication,
* deletion,
* rewiring,
* parameter mutation,
* structural mutation,
* callable or recurrent subgraphs,
* inherited state/configuration where appropriate,
* evolvable interfaces between modules,
* variable organism size.

Keep primitive operations small.

Complexity should arise primarily from composition, topology, reuse, and evolutionary history rather than from adding powerful hand-designed instructions.

There must be mutations capable of:

* copying functioning structure,
* inserting copied structure elsewhere,
* disconnecting structure without deleting it,
* reconnecting previously neutral structure,
* altering routing while preserving components,
* producing neutral structural growth.

A child must retain enough ancestry metadata to reconstruct exactly what changed.

Do not promote Representation B into the platform VM merely for Campaign 6.

Treat it as one available substrate/control unless it independently earns broader adoption.

AXIS P — PRESSURE EXPANSION

Selection pressure must no longer be assumed stationary or scalar.

Pressure schedules may include:

* stable periods,
* abrupt transitions,
* slow drift,
* periodic regimes,
* rare catastrophes,
* resource redistribution,
* population-mediated pressure,
* migration,
* temporary opportunities,
* environmental changes caused by organisms themselves.

Some schedules should be deliberately generated without a semantic label.

Do not optimize the generator toward recognizable scenarios.

Preserve the exact pressure history for every lineage.

Where possible, distinguish:

EXOGENOUS_PRESSURE

from

ENDOGENOUS_PRESSURE

where the latter arises from population behavior or environmental modifications.

AXIS T — TELEMETRY / OBSERVATORY EXPANSION

Fitness alone is insufficient.

Every evaluated organism should emit a compact behavioral fingerprint sufficient to compare it against:

* its ancestors,
* siblings,
* population peers,
* historical organisms,
* behavior-library entries,
* and behavior in other worlds.

Instrument, where applicable:

* action distributions,
* state transitions,
* resources touched,
* objects/environment changed,
* internal state usage,
* module/node execution,
* routing decisions,
* memory reads/writes,
* interaction partners,
* communication events,
* survival transitions,
* environmental dependencies,
* lineage changes,
* genotype structural changes,
* phenotype structural changes.

Do not require permanent storage of every full trace.

Use tiered telemetry:

T0 — cheap summary for every evaluation
T1 — richer trace around unusual events
T2 — full lineage/world/event capture after escalation
T3 — replay/counterfactual instrumentation for adjudication

The escalation machinery is a primary Campaign 6 object of study.

DETECTORS

Do not create one global “interestingness” score.

Run independent detectors.

At minimum include detectors for:

1. behavioral novelty,
2. lineage discontinuity,
3. unexpected transfer,
4. structural reuse,
5. environmental modification,
6. niche divergence,
7. unusual persistence through regime change,
8. sudden gain without obvious local genotype explanation,
9. unexpected causal dependence,
10. detector disagreement,
11. classifier failure / unknown behavior.

An event becomes more interesting when multiple independent rulers fire.

However, a single strong anomaly must still be preserved.

Detector disagreement is itself an event class.

A classifier returning “none of the above” is an event class.

OBSERVATORY ESCALATION PROTOCOL

When any detector fires above its frozen threshold:

1. freeze the organism,
2. freeze the parent,
3. freeze recent ancestors,
4. freeze relevant siblings/cousins,
5. freeze the world state,
6. freeze the recent pressure history,
7. record the exact mutation chain,
8. preserve richer telemetry around the event,
9. generate a replay packet.

Then attempt:

A. exact replay,
B. lineage replay,
C. world-seed replay,
D. mutation rollback,
E. suspected-mechanism ablation,
F. transfer into held-out worlds,
G. pressure-history perturbation.

Do not explain the event before preservation is complete.

BLIND SPOTS / ANTI-GRAVITY TEST

Campaign 6 must include planted events specifically designed to expose conceptual blindness.

Construct hidden fixtures containing phenomena such as:

* a neutral structure that becomes useful much later,
* a capability arising only after an environmental transition,
* an apparent familiar mechanism generated by a different cause,
* a population-level effect with no exceptional individual,
* a lineage whose fitness is ordinary but behavior becomes unprecedented,
* an environmental modification whose payoff occurs many generations later,
* a mutation whose importance is invisible until a second mutation occurs,
* an event detectable only from ancestry rather than endpoint behavior.

The seats operating the main campaign must not know fixture locations.

Measure:

* detection rate,
* preservation rate,
* false escalation rate,
* replay success,
* causal recovery,
* classification accuracy,
* unknown-retention rate.

A planted event that is missed is more important than a natural anomaly that is found.

LLM-FREE SEARCH LANES

At least 25% of Campaign 6 search volume must originate from generators that did not use an LLM to choose:

* the world combination,
* pressure combination,
* mutation sequence,
* population initialization,
* or rationale.

LLMs may inspect those runs afterward.

Label provenance explicitly:

HUMAN_DIRECTED
LLM_PROPOSED
PROCEDURAL
EVOLUTION_GENERATED
MIXED

Do not compare them using one winner score.

The purpose is to preserve sources of variation not pre-shaped by linguistic priors.

SEARCH POLICY

Do not exhaustively enumerate the full cross-product.

Use broad initial scattering.

Allocate deeper compute when any of the following occurs:

* unprecedented behavior,
* repeated anomaly across independent runs,
* new transferable capability,
* persistent divergent strategies,
* unexplained structural reuse,
* long-lived ecological change,
* emergence of previously irrelevant structure as causal,
* disagreement among rulers,
* unusually deep lineage before innovation,
* unusually rapid innovation after long stasis.

Archaeon may create new branches when the observed trajectory justifies them.

It may not create branches merely because a known literature phenomenon would be interesting to test.

LONG-RUN POLICY

Campaign 6 should contain genuinely long evolutionary runs.

Do not assume that all useful information appears in early generations.

Archive exponentially or adaptively with increasing depth.

Preserve event neighborhoods densely.

Long-run compute may be stopped for:

* demonstrable frozen dynamics,
* catastrophic instrumentation failure,
* unrecoverable provenance loss,
* or a preregistered resource ceiling.

Do not stop merely because endpoint fitness plateaus.

A fitness plateau is not equivalent to evolutionary inactivity.

SUCCESS CLASSES

Do not reduce Campaign 6 to PASS/FAIL.

Return at least these independent judgments:

GENERATIVE_EXPANSION
Did SFE materially enlarge the space of worlds, organisms and histories?

OBSERVATORY_RECALL
Did the detection stack recover planted and natural anomalies?

FORENSIC_RECOVERABILITY
Could escalated events be replayed and causally interrogated?

SEARCH_DIVERSITY
Did autonomous generation produce materially different evolutionary histories?

UNKNOWN_YIELD
Did SFE produce preserved phenomena that resisted the existing behavior taxonomy?

SCALING_HEALTH
Did engine, queue, storage, replay and telemetry remain operational as complexity increased?

A campaign may succeed strongly on infrastructure and produce zero natural discoveries.

That is an acceptable result.

FORBIDDEN CONCLUSIONS

Do not conclude:

“open-ended evolution failed”

because Campaign 6 produced no dramatic discovery.

Do not conclude:

“intelligence emerged”

from a large scalar improvement.

Do not conclude:

“mechanism X occurred”

because a trajectory resembles X.

Do not conclude:

“nothing happened”

because fitness was flat.

Do not interpret absence of detection as absence of phenomena until observatory recall has been independently calibrated.

REQUIRED CAMPAIGN RETURN

Return:

1. world-space expansion summary,
2. organism-space expansion summary,
3. pressure-space expansion summary,
4. telemetry and storage costs,
5. detector firing table,
6. planted-event recovery matrix,
7. natural anomaly catalogue,
8. UNKNOWN_MECHANISM catalogue,
9. replay success matrix,
10. causal-ablation results where attempted,
11. scaling failures and instrumentation blind spots,
12. examples of runs that would have been missed under Campaign 5 telemetry,
13. recommended next complexity ceiling.

Most importantly answer:

How much stranger can SFE safely become before we stop being able to tell what happened?

Do not optimize for a spectacular answer.

Build the machinery that allows spectacular answers to be recognized later.
```
