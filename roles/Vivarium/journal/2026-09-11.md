**The design decision the brief left to me: the gate runs BEFORE `claim`.**
The obvious reading of "the boundary where the consumer begins work" is
dispatch, and it is wrong here -- a halt at dispatch happens with a row
already CLAIMED, invariant 6 forbids resolving a stranded row by inference,
so every DRIFT would strand one row per tick and cost a human release each. A
gate whose safe state creates work for a person is a gate that gets unwired.

**Demonstrations against real engines**, not mocks, scored on the consequence:

    conformant live engine          CONFORMANT      reached dispatch
    second engine, own ledger       WRONG_INSTANCE  blocked, queued 1->1
    live engine, tampered contract  DRIFT           blocked, queued 2->2
    a port with nothing on it       UNREACHABLE     blocked, queued 3->3
    CHEAT: same wrong engine, gate removed          consumed a row, 4->3

The cheat control was added after the operator's ruling 4 and it is the case
that makes the other four mean anything: three are scored on an ABSENCE, and
an absence is equally consistent with a harness that cannot see work at all.
Removing the gate against the SAME wrong engine shows the channel reporting
work beginning, so the blocks are measurements rather than silence.
