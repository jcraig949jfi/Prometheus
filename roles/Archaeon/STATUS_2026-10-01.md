## 2. Dispatch #1148 detail (DEF-ODY-019 failure mode)

**Finding.** `evidence_wiki/ew/db.py` opened every connection without TCP keepalive or `tcp_user_timeout`. This applied to both paths:

- the ThreadedConnectionPool (2-16 connections), which lives as long as the process;
- the direct `psycopg2.connect` fallback.

`comms/api.connect()` routes through the same code. After a network blip, a half-open connection blocks a query forever while the process still looks alive.

**Fix.** One `KEEPALIVE_KWARGS` / `_conn_kwargs` helper is now used by both paths:

- `keepalives=1`
- `keepalives_idle=30`
- `keepalives_interval=10`
- `keepalives_count=3`
- `tcp_user_timeout=60000`

These are plain libpq parameters, with no socket or file-descriptor handling (#1135). There are no schema, data or message-semantics changes, and `fabric/` was not touched.

**Tests.** `evidence_wiki/tests/test_db_keepalive.py`:

| Platform | Result |
|---|---|
| M2 Windows, without the fix | 4 of 4 fail |
| M2 Windows, with the fix (libpq 16.9) | 4 of 4 pass, including a live pooled connection to M1 that reports all five options |
| ubu001 Linux (libpq 18.3) | 3 pass; the live test was skipped because no credentials were copied there |
| Existing identity-guard and comms suites | 7 and 22 pass |

**Limits.**

- There was no induced packet-drop test. The tests prove the options reach libpq; they do not measure recovery timing.
- Windows ignores `keepalives_count` and `tcp_user_timeout`.
