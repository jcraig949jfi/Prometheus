## 4. Mechanism, verified per task — not the number

```
task  numbers        max_value  selected  correct
 0    [60, 14]         46         46        46     OK
 1    [33, 8]          25         25        25     OK
 2    [120, 27]        93         93        93     OK
 3    [5, 19]          14         14        14     OK
 4    [250, 36]       214        214        36     WRONG GUESS
 5    [84, 11]         73         73        11     WRONG GUESS
```

The four hits are the subtraction landing on the correct integer and the reader matching it by
content, which is why all four survive the 24-permutation test. Note task 3: the numbers arrive
in prompt order `[5, 19]`, and the primitive sorts before subtracting, so it is not order-fragile
either.

**The two misses are a trap the home battery never set.** Charon's tasks 4 and 5 ask for the
*complement*: "All but 36 passed. How many **failed**?" The answer is the literal 36, and Charon
placed the subtraction result (214) among the distractors. The pair computes N − K, matches it,
and **converts an abstention into a wrong guess**. On the home battery `all_but_n` always asks for
the remainder. This is a second, smaller vocabulary gap — *question polarity* — sitting on top of
the first, and the existing `parse_question_target` does not fire on either prompt. A pair that
has only "subtract" cannot see it; the readout faithfully routes the wrong quantity. Recorded as
a ΔE-shaped gap with its own tasks, not folded into the +4.
