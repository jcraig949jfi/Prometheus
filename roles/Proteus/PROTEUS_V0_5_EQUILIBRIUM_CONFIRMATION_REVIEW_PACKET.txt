F4. CROSS-RUNTIME REPLAY IS NO LONGER BYTE-IDENTICAL, AND I FOUND WHY.
    V0.4 claimed byte-identical replay on three runtimes. Under V0.5 that claim
    must be narrowed:

      CPython 3.12.10 Windows and CPython 3.12.3 Linux   IDENTICAL digest
      CPython 3.11.9 Windows                             DIFFERS

    The divergent section is the V0.5 kernel probe alone. The measured kernel
    and its key order are bit-identical on all three. What differs is the
    stationary distribution, by a relative 1e-14 in every entry, and the two
    quantities derived from it (max |J|, entropy production) at the 14th
    significant digit.

    1e-14 convergence tolerance.

    floor on the currents and changes no conclusion. But I did NOT freeze a
    numerical replay tolerance in the preregistration, so I cannot appeal to an
    unfrozen tolerance to declare replay passed, and I am not going to invent

    reviewer should freeze a replay tolerance for any later pass.

15. REVERSIBLE REFERENCE DEFINITION
====================================================================

    Q(i,j) = (pi_i P_ij + pi_j P_ji) / (2 pi_i)   for i != j
    Q(i,i) = 1 - sum_j Q(i,j)

This is the additive symmetrisation of the stationary flux. It satisfies
detailed balance exactly with respect to pi, and it TARGETS THE MEASURED
STATIONARY DISTRIBUTION OF THE ACTIVE KERNEL -- stated explicitly, as the brief
requires. It preserves local connectivity: Q_ij > 0 exactly when P_ij > 0 or
P_ji > 0. It is a control; it does not replace the grammar and nothing about the
grammar was changed to match it.

Verified by unit test, not by assertion: detailed balance to 1e-15, pi preserved
to 1e-12, and every current identically zero. In the primary run its maximum
|J| is 2.17e-19.

Two things are true at once and both are reported.

So the current is real, it is authored, and it is invisible in the observables
the previous four passes used. That is precisely the failure mode this pass was
