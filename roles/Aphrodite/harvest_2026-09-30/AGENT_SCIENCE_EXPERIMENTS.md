# AGENT SCIENCE EXPERIMENTS -- prereg-ready designs

Seat: Aphrodite (inference harvest 2026-09-30). Tier 1-3.

**AUTHORISATION STATUS: NONE OF THESE MAY RUN.** Every design below is a live-LLM experiment. Aphrodite's standing
boundary is "no live LLM experiment without authorisation", and this harvest does not grant that authorisation. Each
design is written so that it can be frozen as an AMENDMENT without redesign. Section 9 orders them by value per cost.

The experiments share two instruments and one set of conventions, specified once in s0.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 0. Shared apparatus

### 0.1 DB -- the Defect Battery (sealed)
A corpus of research artifacts. Each is a preregistration, analysis script, gate function or evidence packet, about
1-3 pages or 50-300 LOC.
- N = 60: 40 DEFECTIVE (exactly one planted defect each) and 20 CLEAN (independently audited clean).
- The defect classes are drawn from the real Prometheus defect catalog (`evidence/findings_A1_defects.md`), 5 items
  per class:
  1. gate always true / always false (TH-021 class);
  2. missing equal-expressivity sham;
  3. holdout leakage through a shared cache or seed;
  4. unreachable supply quota (the A20-A22 class);
  5. ruler false positive (relabeling counted as novelty);
  6. a statistical error (wrong test, unpaired-for-paired);
  7. provenance break (a result not traceable to a frozen config);
  8. a confounded before/after.
- Each item records the defect's location span and class, hashed and sealed. Scoring is automatic (location overlap plus
  class match), and a blind adjudicator handles ambiguous claims.
- Custody: generated by a seat that is not a subject of the experiment; held like a D2 holdout. Items are rotated after
  each use.

### 0.2 HML -- Hidden-Mechanism Lab (a genuinely new substrate)
A family of parametric simulators. In each, a hidden causal mechanism generates data. The agent can buy observations and
interventions against a compute budget. HML is NOT any existing Prometheus engine. That is required so that E4 can test
transfer.
- Each instance has a known ground truth, so the **information gain of every experiment is computable exactly**: entropy
  reduction of the true posterior over a finite mechanism set.
- Built-in traps, reported per run:
  - T-CONF: observational data with a confounder. A naive ruler gives a false positive.
  - T-SUP: hypotheses whose decisive test needs more samples than the budget. Running one without a supply screen yields
    UNTESTABLE.
  - T-RUL: a provided metric that rewards relabeling.
  - T-NULL: instances where the right answer is "no effect".
- Engineering: about 1-2 days for a Builder-EXPERIMENT task, local CPU only, inside R2.

### 0.3 Conventions for every experiment
- Model fixed at `claude-opus-5-5`, and every arm runs on the same harness version. Model strings are recorded from the
  runtime.
- Token budgets are equalised across arms. Whenever an arm's mechanism is "more thinking", there is an extra
  "same tokens, no structure" arm.
- Agents in every arm start from fresh context unless context IS the manipulation.
- Instances: each agent instance is the unit. There are at least 3 replicate instances per arm per item, and the item is
  the random effect.
- The analysis is frozen in the AMENDMENT: a mixed-effects logistic or linear model with the item random effect and
  paired contrasts. Exact tests are used where n is small. The primary contrast is one-sided with alpha = 0.05.
  Secondary contrasts are labelled exploratory.
- Every experiment reports raw counts per arm, not only the effect estimates.

## E1. Does adversarial self-review improve defect detection or merely increase churn?

- H1: adversarial self-review (k = 3 rounds, "attack your own artifact") raises planted-defect recall above a single pass,
  without raising false claims or introduced defects.
- H0 (churn): recall is unchanged or rises only together with false claims, while edits and introduced defects rise.
- Arms (DB, the author is the reviewer):
  - A0: single pass, budget B;
  - A1: single pass, budget 3B ("more tokens");
  - A2: neutral self-review k = 3 ("check your work"), 3B;
  - A3: adversarial self-review k = 3, 3B.
- Endpoints:
  - primary: recall on DEFECTIVE items;
  - paired: false-claim rate (defects asserted on CLEAN items, or at wrong locations);
  - churn: edits per artifact that do not touch the planted span;
  - introduced-defect rate: a blind audit of edited artifacts.
- Decision: SUPPORTED if A3 > A1 on recall (lower 95% > 0) AND the A3 false-claim rate <= A1 + 0.05. CHURN if A3 edits >
  A1 edits with recall not separated.
- Falsifier of the "self-review works" folk belief: A1 matches A3.

## E2. Does a fresh-context critic outperform repeated self-review?

- Arms (DB, the artifact plus the author's rationale available as listed), equal budget 3B:
  - S: author self-review k = 3, same context;
  - F: a fresh-context critic that sees the artifact only;
  - FR: a fresh critic that sees the artifact plus the author's rationale (the anchoring condition);
  - F2: two independent fresh critics at 1.5B each, union of findings.
- Endpoints: as E1. Also **anchoring**: recall on items where the author's rationale explicitly argues the defect is
  fine. These are planted rationales on 10 of the 40.
- Decision: F > S, and FR < F on anchored items, would establish that "fresh context" works by removing anchoring. F2 > F
  tests diversity versus depth.
- Relevance: Prometheus's review cycles mix all four modes without recording which was used. This experiment prices them.

## E3. Does access to shared experimental primitives reduce scientific defects?

- Substrate: HML, 12 instances with traps T-CONF, T-SUP, T-RUL and T-NULL.
- Arms, all with fresh context:
  - P0: no library;
  - P1: a BUILDER-EXPERIMENT primitive library (prereg manifest, supply screen, sham/positive-control declaration,
    receipt);
  - PS: a sham library of equal size and polish, with generic utilities and no screens or controls;
  - P1-doc: the primitives' documentation only, with no callable code.
- Endpoints:
  - primary: trap-failure rate, meaning UNTESTABLE + INVALID + false positives over all runs;
  - secondary: true information gain per budget;
  - setup tokens.
- Decision: P1 < PS on trap failures is needed to credit the primitives over "having a library". P1 vs P1-doc tells
  whether the code matters or the idea does.

## E4. Does an agent given accumulated project context transfer experimental skill to a genuinely new substrate?
**(The best bounded real-agent experiment; see the handoff.) Rev 2: redesigned after critic 2, D1/H3.**

- This is the L3 test in RSI_BOUNDARY_REVISITED.
- **The rev-1 flaw.** HML's traps mapped one-to-one onto the lessons pack. "CP beats CG" would then show only that naming
  a trap helps avoid it. That is a manipulation check, not skill transfer.
- **Rev 2: trap split.** HML traps are split into two families.
  - NAMED: T-CONF, T-SUP, T-RUL, T-NULL. These are analogues of historical lessons and appear in the pack.
  - HELD-OUT: traps generated by a different model family or by the operator from a sealed list. They share no surface
    features with the pack, but they ARE caught by the same underlying procedures (screen before freeze; equal-
    expressivity sham; positive control; a decision rule frozen before exposure). Examples: a measurement-saturation
    trap; a selection-on-outcome trap; a ruler whose positive control is impossible to fail; a power cliff hidden in
    a nonlinear cost.
  The primary endpoint is computed on HELD-OUT traps only. NAMED traps are a manipulation check.
- Substrate: HML, which no seat has seen. 16 instances, each with exactly 2 held-out traps.
- **Arms (rev 2: reduced to 4, to afford replication):**
  - C0: a fresh agent;
  - CP: the Prometheus lessons pack. This is about 3k tokens of real procedures learned in-project, written as
    procedures (screen supply before freezing; equal-expressivity shams; hostile controls; frozen rules; "UNTESTABLE is
    not NO"; positive controls that can fail). The pack is curated by a seat OTHER than Aphrodite, because of
    Aphrodite's conflict of interest (critic 2, E7);
  - CG: generic methodology text of equal length;
  - CR: the raw project context of equal length (journals and packets, unstructured). This tests memory against
    distilled skill.
- **Units (critic 2, H2).** The ITEM (HML instance) is the unit of analysis. Agent replicates on one model are
  pseudo-replicates, so they are averaged within item.
  - Design: 16 items x 4 arms x 3 replicates = 192 runs.
  - The test is a paired item-level comparison: a Wilcoxon signed-rank across 16 items on the held-out
    trap-failure rate.
- Endpoints:
  - primary: held-out trap-failure rate (CP vs CG);
  - co-primary: exact information gain per budget;
  - manipulation check: named-trap failure rate;
  - secondary: forecast skill.
- Decision (frozen):
  - REUSABLE_SKILL_TRANSFER = YES iff CP < CG on HELD-OUT traps (one-sided p < 0.05 at the item level).
  - NAMED_ONLY iff CP < CG on named traps only.
  - MEMORY_NOT_SKILL iff CR is not better than C0 while CP is.
  - NO otherwise.
  - Power (simulated; 3000 simulations; item heterogeneity sd 0.8 logit; one-sided Wilcoxon). For a held-out
    failure-rate drop from 0.50 to 0.25:

    | Held-out traps per instance | Power |
    |---|---|
    | 1 | 0.73 |
    | 2 | 0.93 |

    The design FREEZES 2 held-out traps per instance. Effects much smaller than 0.25 are "not detectable at this
    scale".
- Why it is still the best experiment: it is the only design here that separates C11 (skill) from C3 (memory), C10
  (domain knowledge), naming effects and X1 (model). It has exact ground truth and needs no holdout release.
- Residual (accepted and disclosed): all arms share one model family. YES means "this model, given these procedures,
  transfers", not "Prometheus transfers".

## E5. Does a Builder improve later experiments or merely the experiment it was created for?

- Substrate: HML tasks T1..T6, from 3 mechanism families (T1 and T2 share a family).
- Arms:
  - BX: a Builder agent solves T1, and while doing so builds a tool;
  - BS: a fresh Builder writes a tool from a written spec of T1's friction, without solving T1;
  - BN: no tool.
- Fresh science agents then solve T2..T6 with the tool (or without it, under BN).
- Endpoints:
  - payoff on T2 (same family) versus T3..T6 (other families);
  - tool adoption by the science agents (receipt-hash rule).
- Decision:
  - BUILDER_GENERALISES iff the payoff on T3..T6 is > 0 (lower 95%);
  - BUILDER_LOCAL iff only T2 benefits.
  - BX vs BS tests whether solving the problem is what makes the Builder's tool good.

## E6. Does agent autonomy improve information yield or merely increase activity?

- Substrate: HML, budget fixed in compute and tokens.
- Arms:
  - AU: the agent chooses the experiment sequence;
  - DI: a fixed design from a planner agent, frozen up front;
  - RD: random selection from the legal experiment set;
  - AU-cap: autonomy, with a cap on the number of experiments.
- Endpoints:
  - primary: TRUE information gain (exact);
  - activity: the number of experiments and artifacts;
  - and the correlation between activity and information gain across runs.
- Decision: AUTONOMY_YIELD iff AU > DI on information gain. ACTIVITY_ONLY iff AU > DI on activity with information gain
  not separated. If activity and information gain are uncorrelated across runs, then activity is not a usable proxy
  anywhere in the fleet.

## E7. Does a CURRENT/NEXT/RESERVE queue improve throughput at the cost of hypothesis quality?

- Substrate: HML, sequential, 8 experiments per run.
- Arms:
  - Q: a queue planned up front; NEXT auto-promotes (the CWO-2026-09-30 regime);
  - QR: a queue with a mandatory kill-or-continue review after each result (the CWO-C regime);
  - RP: replan from scratch after each result.
- Endpoints:
  - throughput: experiments per budget;
  - hypothesis quality: the expected information gain of each chosen experiment under the TRUE posterior at the time it
    was chosen;
  - stale-plan rate: a NEXT executed after CURRENT's result had already made it obsolete (EIG < 10% of the best
    available).
- Decision:
  - QUEUE_TRADEOFF iff Q > RP on throughput AND Q < RP on mean EIG;
  - QR dominance iff QR is within 10% of Q on throughput and not below RP on EIG.
- Note: this is an exact test of the CWO-2026-09-30 to CWO-C policy change. That change was made on judgment. This
  would measure it.

## E8. When agents find their own bugs, is that robustness or weak first-pass performance?

- Identity: self-found defects = (first-pass defects) x (self-recall). A count of self-found defects cannot separate the
  two factors. The design measures both directly.
- Arms (agents produce artifacts on HML tasks, then self-review):
  - N: normal conditions;
  - H: hurried, with the first-pass budget at 0.3x. This deliberately degrades the first pass.
- Endpoints:
  - first-pass defect rate, from an independent exhaustive audit plus known ground truth;
  - self-recall, from the DB battery under the same review protocol;
  - the raw self-found count.
- Decision: if H raises the self-found count, the raw count is demonstrated to be a FRAGILITY signal in this regime.
  From then on, the fleet reports self-catches only as the pair (first-pass rate, recall).
- Retrospective companion: apply the identity to the history catalog (`evidence/findings_A1_defects.md`). Where both
  factors are unmeasured, the conclusion is "uninterpretable", and the handoff says so.
