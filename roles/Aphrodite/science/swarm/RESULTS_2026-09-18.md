## The boundaries, with numbers

S1 contagion. Required verification v* = 1 - 1/(d tau): with 8 readers
and full trust, v* = 0.875; with 2 readers at trust 0.5, v* = 0 (the
claim dies without any checking). Final size given an outbreak matched
the model to within 0.05 everywhere (e.g. R0 2.0: 0.798 vs 0.797).

S2a weak models, binary verifier. Precision = p t / (p t + (1 - p) q),
independent of k. Boundary p* = q/(t + q): a proposer solving 1% of
problems (p = 0.01) with a 1%-false-accept verifier gets precision
0.503 (sim 0.501 / 0.492 / 0.502 at k = 10 / 100 / 5000); at q = 2% it
is 0.336 (sim 0.297 / 0.339 / 0.344). Sampling 500x longer did not
change precision. The same
closed form is derivable from Stroebl et al., arXiv 2411.17501, App. C
(library/sources/weak_models.md), found independently by the library
subagent.

S2b weak models, scored selection with an exploitable tail (p = 0.1):
    h (hack share of wrong)   k* (model)   acc at k*   acc at k=1024
    0                         1024 (none)  0.700       0.700
    0.001                     512          0.567       0.557
    0.01                      64           0.346       0.101
    0.05                      8            0.208       0.006
Past k*, drawing more samples from the weak model makes the answer
WORSE. One wrong answer in a hundred that fools the scorer is enough.

S3 exploiters. Invasion crossing (sim vs a*): g 0.1: 0.12 vs 0.091;
g 0.25: 0.22 vs 0.20; g 0.5: 0.34 vs 0.333 -- all within 0.04, biased
upward by mutation pressure (as the model predicts for one-way mutation).
Persistence: after 200 unaudited generations exploiters reached x =
1.000 in every run; after the fix, 0 of 100 runs ever halved, at costs
0, 0.02 and 0.1.

S4 voting and herding. p = 0.4: majority accuracy 0.400 (n 1) -> 0.350
-> 0.266 -> 0.144 -> 0.036 (n 81). Herding (agents see earlier actions)
at n = 81: p 0.55: 0.598 (chain 0.599) vs independent 0.818; p 0.6:
0.696 (0.692) vs 0.967; p 0.7: 0.845 (0.845) vs 0.9999. The chain's
limit is the gambler's-ruin value p^2/(p^2 + (1-p)^2). The seat's
RECALLED closed form p(p+1)/(2(1-p+p^2)) gave 0.753 at p = 0.7 and is
WRONG for this tie rule (calibration ledger).

## EXPLORATORY, post-hoc (labelled; not preregistered)

X-S3 reservoir (exploratory_s3_reservoir.py). With an honest reservoir
(exploiters revert w.p. r per generation: back-mutation, or re-seeding
from an archive of honest ancestors -- the same operation here):
    c      r       model half-gen   sim median half-gen   halved
    0.02   0       never            never                 0/100
    0.1    0       never            never                 0/100
    0.02   0.005   86               83                    100/100
    0.02   0.02    30               29                    100/100
    0.1    0.005   31               31                    100/100
    0.1    0.02    17               18                    100/100
Fixing the evaluator does not purge an exploit that has gone to
fixation; a reservoir of honest variants does, on the model's schedule.
This is the mechanism the seat proposes for the reported persistence of
poisoned components through clean evolution in DGM and SICA (Roesner
and Kohno, arXiv 2609.17817, library/sources/swarm_failure.md): a
component with no visible cost after the fix has no selection against
it, and an archive only helps if it still holds clean variants.

## Defects in this seat's work (calibration ledger)

1. Aggregating hundreds of per-cell interval gates with "any
   INDETERMINATE -> INDETERMINATE" makes INDETERMINATE near-certain when
   the model is exact; and a tolerance of ~2.8 SE plus a CI-inside rule
   supports only cells within ~0.8 SE. Eligibility was computed for the
   tolerance, not for the rule applied on top of it.
2. The persistence arm's eligibility was not computed: with one-way
   mutation, 200 unaudited generations fix the exploit (x = 1), leaving
   no honest variant for selection to act on; H-S3c was untestable as
   designed. (The fix was informative -- X-S3 -- but post-hoc.)
3. Two analysis-code bugs (H-S3b upper bound; H-S3c inf vs inf).
4. The recalled herding closed form was wrong for the stated tie rule.
