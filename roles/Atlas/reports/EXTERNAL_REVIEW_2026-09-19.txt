--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SUBJECT UNDER REVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two deliverables from the Atlas seat, which shadows the Prometheus
experiment engines and never runs or adjudicates their science.

(a) An index of the experiments the Prometheus engines have run. WHAT RAN,
    WHAT WAS OBSERVED and WHAT SOMEONE CONCLUDED are separate tables. Every
    fact points back to evidence (git blob, file on a named host, Postgres
    rows, engine-ledger id).
(b) A survey of external artificial-life and open-endedness ecosystems:
    systems that combine a WORLD, an ORGANISM (the "player") and an
    evolutionary PRESSURE. Each is enumerated, classified and pointed to
    its papers and code, then compared with our own engines. Candidate new
    ecosystems and 12 cross-ecosystem experiment proposals follow from it.

Claims under review:
  C1  The catalogue is a faithful, reference-backed census of a large
      part of the field. It is not exhaustive.
  C2  The occupied part of the world x organism x pressure space is a
      tiny fraction of the combinatorial space, and most occupied cells
      are held by a single system.
  C3  Prometheus's engines cluster in two world x organism cells that the
      field barely uses, and none co-evolves its environments.
What would falsify them: a spot-check finding links marked VERIFIED that
do not resolve (C1); a finer or corrected labelling that fills the space
(C2); a misclassification of our engines (C3).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Index (a): eight read-only collectors run from linked git worktrees:
  git history (all remote and local refs); Archaeon SFE campaigns;
  DEEP FRONTIER scheduler registry; NPE (Nestor) ledgers and CW01 loop
  (an M1-local branch); Vivarium queue; PEW evidence-wiki pointers; M1
  local files and engine ledgers (idle SQLite, opened read-only and
  immutable); M2 run receipts and ledgers (collected by the sibling seat
  Atlas-M2 into the same keys).
  Collectors are idempotent and versioned. They never erase data and
  record conflicts instead. Advisory locks keep the two seats' writes
  from interleaving.

Survey (b): six parallel web-survey passes, one per cluster, all writing
  one JSON schema:
    c1 open-endedness / environment curricula   66 records
    c2 digital evolution / artificial chemistry 53
    c3 cellular automata / self-organisation    58
    c4 embodied morphology / ecologies          52
    c5 LLM-era and program evolution            70
    c6 frameworks, source lists, discoveries    94
  Every link carries the surveyor's status: VERIFIED (fetched, or resolved
  via the GitHub/arXiv API), SEARCH_RESULT, or UNVERIFIED (from memory).
  393 records were merged and de-duplicated into 352 systems. Four rows
  for our own engines were added on the same axes. Our existing internal
  coverage (Techne specimens, Nyx cuts, Harmonia rulers, Aporia dossiers)
  was mapped first, and each catalogue row points to it.

3. KEY DEFINITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORLD      where organisms live: discrete grid, program memory, continuous
           field, particles, 2D/3D physics, chemistry, game environment,
           text/code, procedural terrain, graph, other.
ORGANISM   what varies and is inherited ("player"): machine code, genome
           bytes, CPPN, neural net, policy, morphology + controller, CA
           pattern, kernel parameters, reaction rules, LLM-written
           program, LLM agent, other.
PRESSURE   what selects: implicit replication, resource competition,
           explicit fitness, novelty, quality-diversity, minimal
           criterion, coevolution, environment co-evolution, regret
           curricula, human/model judgment, predator-prey, other.
Each axis has 11 canonical values plus an "other" bucket (12 in all). The
surveyors used 25 / 67 / 41 raw labels, which fold into those 12.
Frameworks and curated lists are catalogued but left out of the matrix.

  Each cell is itself a family of experiments. Worlds, seeds, rates,
  budgets and controls parameterise it. In-house examples: DEEP FRONTIER
  expanded 15 lineages into 372 specs; the NPE CW01 loop wrote 122
  perturbation records against 35 trajectories; one SFE campaign is 10
  preregistered slots with up to 6 attempts each. The realisable
  experiment space is cells x parameterisations. In practice it is
  unbounded, so the real question is how to sample it.

  Where Prometheus sits:
    SFE campaigns     program_memory x machine_code  x explicit, QD
    DEEP FRONTIER     program_memory x graph_organism x explicit, novelty
    NPE CW01          program_memory x tree/tape genome x explicit, resource

  Together these are 7 world x organism x pressure cells, and only 2 of
  them contain any catalogued system. All four engines use fixed or
  procedural environments; the field has 26 systems that co-evolve them.

10. KNOWN LIMITATIONS & CAVEATS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Each system gets one label per axis, so hybrids flatten. "other"
  absorbs 76 organisms and 20 worlds; finer labels would raise every
  cell count and lower the occupancy percentages further.
- Occupancy percentages depend on the vocabulary. They measure how
  concentrated the field is, not how much biology is possible.
- Coverage is broad, not exhaustive. The 16 source lists name more
  systems, and two earlier in-house lists (152 and 463 entries) are
  unverified.
- 192 of 834 links are SEARCH_RESULT or UNVERIFIED. Some venues and
  years come from memory and are flagged in the notes.
- The index is M1 plus partial M2. 6,046 pointers still wait for M2-side
  ledger checks.
- Atlas classes are word maps over reported dispositions. Vivarium's
  are queue states, not science.
- No proposal has been sized, piloted or accepted by an owning seat.
