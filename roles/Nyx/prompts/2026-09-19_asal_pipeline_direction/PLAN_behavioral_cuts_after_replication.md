## The question (s5), not "can we cross?"
Crossings exist. The question is: WHAT DYNAMICAL OR REPRESENTATIONAL PROPERTY MAKES DISTINCT LENIA PHENOTYPES OCCUPY
THE SAME LOW-SCORE REGION? The cut is by BEHAVIOR, not by score. The job is to discover what SEPARATES equivalently
low-scoring organisms, not to force them into familiar categories.

## Unit of analysis
Preserved rollouts (frames + trajectories), keyed by stable stage/index identity (never the ambiguous IC-CAT code:
Harmonia's #479 found 63 duplicated catalogue codes; index as the search did). Two strata to cut and CONTRAST:
  - STABLE region: rollouts that cross the reference under BOTH observers (post HARM-55).
  - DISCORDANT region: the flips HARM-55/HARM-56 preserve (crossings gained/lost across observers). These are
    first-class objects, possibly more informative than the stable region.

## Candidate behavioral probes (operator's list -- PROBES, not the taxonomy)
Each is a per-rollout scalar/series computed from the preserved frames, observer-independent, deterministic:
  1. coherent persistent morphology vs turbulent texture
  2. locomotion vs deformation (centre-of-mass displacement vs shape change)
  3. stable identity vs rapid morphological turnover
  4. spatial frequency content
  5. occupancy and edge density
  6. temporal novelty
  7. frame-to-frame displacement
  8. persistence
  9. periodicity
 10. spectral change
 11. object/background separability
 12. morphology-preserving motion
 13. catastrophic expansion/collapse
 14. scene-scale texture generation
No probe is presupposed to matter. The aim is to find the axis (or axes) on which the low-score region splits into
genuinely different dynamical regimes.

## Method (to instantiate after replication)
- Compute the probe battery on every low-score rollout (both strata); freeze the probe definitions + code identity
  BEFORE reading the replication scores (A5 measure-definedness on a tiny fixture first; A7 no estimated-mean-as-threshold).
- Cluster/contrast in probe space; report which probes SEPARATE the low-score population and which do not.
- A negative result is real: if NO behavioral distinction can be recovered among the low-score classes, that is a
  STOP condition (s10) -- the low-score region is behaviorally homogeneous and the phenomenon reduces to metric failure.
- A positive result (several distinct dynamical regimes in the same low-score region) is the reason to go deeper (s10):
  the object is the observer's many-to-one compression, potentially reusable machinery.

## What this yields for the pipeline
One candidate MECHANISM nominated for transplant (s7/s11): a dynamical property extracted from the ASAL population,
NOT the ASAL metric. Theophrastus tests whether that dynamic changes something native to a receiving ecology
(persistence, adaptation, exploration, recoverability, lineage survival, ...), never CLIP score as the fitness function.
