   4.2 POET novelty estimator -- three properties read out of the code,
       then measured (all E3, deterministic, on the fossil's own bytes):
       observation                                    | measured
       -----------------------------------------------+-------------
       estimator identity holds, archive sizes 1..20  | true
       same-fraction ratio, roughness vs stump_height | 2.666667
       the same ratio if the declared norm were used  | 1.000000
       distance(no pit, pit of width zero)            | 0.000000
       strict increases in novelty, archive 5..40     | 0
       novelty at archive size 5 -> size 40           | 2.89 -> 0.24
       The mechanism: argsort()[:k] silently returns fewer than k elements
       when the archive is smaller than k, so "novelty" is whole-archive
       dispersion early in a run and local density later -- it changes what
       it measures, with no discontinuity flagged. normalize=False is
       hardcoded, so the norm vector [8,8,8,3,3] declared inside that very
       function is never applied. And because delete_optimizer never prunes
       the archive while remove_oldest prunes the active population, the
       deflation runs against environments that are no longer in the
       population at all.

7. AUTHOR'S OWN ERRORS AND WHAT THEY COST
   7.1 The POET packet is CONFIRMATORY. I scouted all four rows and three
       controls against the fossil before freezing, because my own
       calibration ledger requires bands be set from a model checked at the
       operating point. Both disciplines are real and they conflict here:
       scouting buys correct bands and costs the blind-prediction strength
       the ASAL packet had (where Harmonia sealed results before my
       freeze). I disclosed this in the packet's band_basis fields and to
       Harmonia, and offered to take the ruling on whatever basis it sets.
       I do not think a confirmatory packet is worthless -- it is a
       machine-checked immutable statement adjudicated by someone who is
       not me -- but it is weaker evidence and should not be counted as if
       it were blind.
   7.2 I nearly shipped a probe battery with a dead probe. Autocorrelation
       computed on raw non-negative intensities never drops (cosine stays
       ~0.75 even between independent random fields), so identity half-life
       could not separate a static blob from noise. Caught by a synthetic
       fixture before freezing; fixed by mean-centering. This is the case
       for testing the battery on synthetic data first: on real data the
       probe would have returned a plausible constant and I would have
       believed it.
   7.3 My review question 9.2 was wrong and the operator corrected it. I
       had written that finding no separating axis would show the low-score
       population is homogeneous. It would show only that this battery
       found no separator. The corrected stop condition is recorded.
