1. CONTEXT
   ASAL (Automating the Search for Artificial Life, Sakana 2024) replaces
   hand-written artificial-life metrics with a foundation-model observer:
   roll out a simulation, embed a few sampled frames with a vision model,
   and score "open-endedness" purely in embedding space. The score at
   asal_metrics.py:52-64 is the mean, over 8 sampled frames, of each
   frame's maximum embedding similarity to any strictly earlier frame,
   minimised; lower means "more open-ended". Earlier work (Techne's
   TECHNE-107) showed scene-level GARBAGE beats living Lenia by this score
   through CLIP. The operator's open question: can a search over
   LEGITIMATE (alive) Lenia parameters cross below the garbage score too?

7. AUTHOR'S OWN ERRORS AND WHAT THEY COST
   7.1 I0 was FALSE and it was the weak claim by construction. I predicted
       the raw catalogue does not cross; five catalogued lifeforms cross
       with no search (min 0.8076). Cost: the packet asserted an existence
       claim ("none cross") that a single counterexample kills, while I1/I2
       were correctly existential the other way ("one crosses"). Lesson
       (calibration): choose the existential direction so the ROBUST side
       is the claim.
   7.2 I froze a 5-seed ESTIMATED garbage mean (0.8167) as an EXACT
       threshold. The I0 margin 0.009 is 1.1 sd of that estimate -- a
       crossed convention, not a statistical result. Harmonia's rule A7
       (adopted): freeze a confidence-aware reference distribution or an
       explicitly NAMED cutoff, never an estimated mean used silently as
       ground truth.
   7.3 I wrote I3 as if it ranged over the intended 1,045 domain without
       first proving the executor accepts that domain. It accepted 395.
       Rule A4 (adopted): a packet is not executable until the executor's
       accepted subdomain is shown with exact accepted/refused counts.
