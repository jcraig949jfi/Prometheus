--- Part A ---

Adopting Cognitive Opacity Independence as a core principle fundamentally liberates the architecture from the anthropocentric trap. If we stop treating self-awareness, introspective transparency, and historical memory as the prerequisites for intelligence, the evolutionary search space for functional architectures expands massively.
This directly answers the crucial question of what an agent actually requires to operate effectively. If structural and historical self-knowledge are stripped away, the absolute minimum internal representation an organism needs is not a "self," but a set of functional boundaries. It requires a pragmatic, operational proprioception consisting of:
 * The Agency Boundary (Control vs. Observation): The agent needs a mapping of what it can act upon versus what it can only perceive. It does not need to know the mechanistic physics of how its internal private intentions translate into external physical movements. It only needs the empirical feedback loop that specific outputs reliably alter the world state.
 * The Epistemic Boundary (Knowledge vs. Uncertainty): The system must maintain a ledger of where its predictive models succeed and where they fail--your positive and shadow epistemic geometry. It does not need to know why an unwanted or incorrect thought arrives, only the capacity to register the prediction error, reject the failure, and adjust its operational boundaries accordingly.
 * The Temporal Boundary (State vs. Transition): To navigate cause and effect, the organism needs a functional anchor separating immediate perception from retained state. It does not need to capture the exact, fleeting nature of the present moment, but rather the ability to differentiate between what is currently observable, what is stored as a compressed heuristic, and what is projected as a counterfactual.
 * The Autonomic Boundary (Explicit vs. Implicit): The organism must be able to offload critical stabilizing processes to background mechanisms. It can rely on an enormous underlying intelligence to maintain its operational survivability while its explicit reasoning focuses entirely elsewhere.
This principle transforms your transplant assays and evolutionary experiments from interesting tests into the primary instruments of discovery.
By treating the experimental engine as the sole omniscient observer--perhaps recording the true causal state to an immutable, external ledger--you maintain the strict boundary where the organism interacts only through its limited perceptual interface. This allows you to rigorously test representation divergence. If two agents develop a shared vocabulary to coordinate in a simulated environment, we can instrument their internal architectures to see if their mathematical representations are entirely divergent, perfectly mirroring the epistemic problem of verifying subjective experiences like color.
When you transplant an evolved mechanism (like a novel symbolic compression scheme) from one organism to another, you are isolating pure structural inheritance. If the receiving organism successfully utilizes that mechanism in a novel environment without any "autobiographical" memory of how it was formed, or any introspective understanding of how it works, you have proven Cognitive Opacity Independence.
You are no longer building machines that are instructed to improve themselves. You are building environments where the capacity to revise one's own cognitive machinery might emerge as a highly efficient, though entirely optional, survival strategy.
If we treat structural self-knowledge purely as an emergent evolutionary possibility rather than an architectural doctrine, what specific environmental pressures or resource constraints within the Prometheus engine do you hypothesize would force an opaque organism to first invent a self-model?

--- Part B ---

I think the first self-model would emerge when an organism encounters a particular kind of uncertainty:

It can no longer reliably predict what will happen in the world without accounting for changes in its own capabilities.

That is the strongest evolutionary pressure I would introduce into Prometheus.

But I would make an important distinction. We cannot guarantee that environmental pressure will force an organism to invent a self-model. Evolution may discover an alternative mechanism that performs equally well. Indeed, such an alternative could be a more interesting discovery.

The objective should be to create conditions under which modeling one's own capabilities becomes advantageous, without prescribing the mechanism that realizes that advantage.

1. The first self-model may be a model of controllability

Imagine a simple organism.

It inhabits a world containing food, obstacles, predators, and competing organisms.

Initially, its behavior is straightforward:

* Perceive food.
* Move toward food.
* Consume food.
* Avoid predators.

The organism does not need to know what it is.

It does not need a representation of its nervous system, architecture, evolutionary history, or even its own existence.

It requires only a functioning relationship between observations and actions.

Now introduce a complication.

Sometimes the same action produces different results because the organism itself has changed.

Its locomotion mechanism has deteriorated.

Its visual apparatus has become unreliable.

Its energy reserves are depleted.

One of its computational modules has stopped functioning correctly.

The world hasn't necessarily changed.

The organism has.

Now its previously successful predictions begin failing.

At first, the organism might interpret these failures as environmental changes.

But continued failure creates selection pressure for a different explanation.

Perhaps the problem lies in the machinery producing its actions.

The organism doesn't need to understand that machinery.

It merely needs to discover something equivalent to:

Under these conditions, this action no longer produces the expected result.

That is the beginning of an operational self-model.

Not self-awareness.

Not introspection.

Not identity.

A compressed representation of its own conditional capabilities.

And that representation could exist as an entirely implicit computational mechanism.

---

2. Seven environmental pressures I would investigate

I see seven particularly promising pressures for Prometheus.

| Environmental pressure | Evolutionary problem | Possible emergent capability |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Internal capability drift | The organism's own mechanisms change unpredictably. | Predictive modeling of its own capabilities |
| 2. Irreversible consequences | Incorrect actions cause permanent damage or extinction. | Confidence estimation and selective experimentation |
| 3. Resource scarcity | Computation, energy, memory, and experimentation are expensive. | Compressed models of operational limitations |
| 4. Morphological change | The organism acquires or loses functional components. | Adaptable representations of its own action capabilities |
| 5. Conflicting internal mechanisms | Specialized subsystems produce incompatible recommendations. | Arbitration based on subsystem reliability |
| 6. Social cooperation and competition | Different organisms possess different capabilities and limitations. | Distinguishing its own capabilities from those of others |
| 7. Long-horizon environmental challenges | Decisions depend on capabilities that may deteriorate before an objective is reached. | Predictive models of future internal states |

These pressures are not equally powerful.

My strongest candidates are internal capability drift, resource scarcity, and irreversible consequences acting together.

Here is why.

3. The critical combination: Uncertainty + Scarcity + Consequences

Consider three environments.

Environment A: Unlimited experimentation

The organism encounters an unfamiliar obstacle.

It attempts to cross.

It fails.

It tries again.

Eventually, it discovers a successful behavior.

There is little need to model its own capabilities because experimentation is inexpensive.

A simple trial-and-error mechanism may be sufficient.

Environment B: Limited experimentation

Now every failed attempt consumes a significant amount of energy.

The organism cannot afford unlimited retries.

It benefits from using previous observations to predict which actions are likely to succeed.

Nevertheless, a conventional world model may still be sufficient.

Environment C: Limited experimentation with changing internal capabilities

Now something more interesting happens.

The organism faces the same obstacle it successfully crossed yesterday.

But its movement mechanism has changed.

Attempting the familiar maneuver could kill it.

It has enough energy for one or two experiments.

It must decide whether the obstacle has changed, whether its own capabilities have changed, or whether the interaction between them has changed.

Now a compact model of its own operational reliability may be more valuable than additional knowledge of the external world.

This is where I predict a primitive self-model could emerge.

The organism might discover that a small amount of computation spent estimating its own capabilities saves substantial energy and reduces mortality.

That is a genuine evolutionary incentive.

The organism would not be rewarded for introspection.

It would be rewarded for surviving.

---

4. The essential difficulty: Distinguishing self from world

However, there's a deeper experimental opportunity.

Imagine two situations.

Situation A: An organism cannot move because the terrain has become slippery.

Situation B: An organism cannot move because its locomotion mechanism has deteriorated.

From the organism's immediate perspective, the observations could be nearly identical.

It attempts to move.

Movement fails.

But the correct adaptations differ.

If the terrain is slippery, the organism should change its navigation strategy.

If its locomotion mechanism is impaired, the organism may need to modify its movement strategy across many environments.

The useful distinction is not simply:

Something went wrong.

It is:

Where in the causal relationship between my actions and the world did the failure originate?

This could be the earliest form of operational self/world discrimination.

Crucially, we must design the environment so that the distinction is discoverable through some combination of observation, experimentation, and retained state.

If the two causes are completely indistinguishable under every possible interaction, no organism can reliably infer which occurred.

But if they produce different consequences under carefully chosen actions, an organism could evolve diagnostic behavior.

And when experiments are expensive, the organism may benefit from retaining a compressed representation of the results.

That combination is promising.

---

5. I would not give the organism an explicit self-model

This is the most important design constraint.

We should not install a special introspection module.

We should not provide a predefined representation of internal architecture.

We should not require organisms to maintain a variable representing their identity.

And we should not reward an organism simply for correctly describing itself.

Instead, the experimental substrate should permit mechanisms that can preserve and exploit information about their own operational dynamics.

The resulting representation might be distributed.

It might be encoded within a recurrent controller.

It might be embedded in a symbolic compression mechanism.

It might exist as a collection of learned behavioral constraints.

Or it might take a form we don't recognize.

We should be particularly careful not to mistake a conventional memory of recent failures for a sophisticated self-model.

Remembering that an action failed is not necessarily the same as inferring a reusable limitation in one's own capabilities.

The stronger evidence would come when the organism uses that inferred limitation to predict failures in situations it has never encountered.

For example, an organism discovers that a particular internal transformation is unreliable.

It subsequently avoids a completely different task requiring that transformation.

That would suggest it learned something more general than a collection of failed action sequences.

---

6. The Prometheus experiment I would prioritize

SELF-01: The Endogenous Uncertainty Experiment

Research question:

Under what environmental conditions does evolution favor reusable representations of an organism's own operational capabilities?

I would implement this first in an existing evolutionary world substrate, potentially Ensorain, with the Phase 3 observatory providing external measurement.

The experiment would compare five conditions.

| Condition | World changes? | Organism's capabilities change? | Costly failures? |
|---|---|---|---|
| C0: Stable control | No | No | No |
| C1: External uncertainty | Yes | No | Yes |
| C2: Internal uncertainty | No | Yes | Yes |
| C3: Coupled uncertainty | Yes | Yes | No |
| C4: Coupled uncertainty under scarcity | Yes | Yes | Yes |

The worlds should be matched as closely as possible for general difficulty, with frozen seeds, comparable resources, and independently controlled sources of variation.

My primary hypothesis is that C4 will create the strongest selection pressure for operational self-modeling.

Not necessarily the highest intelligence.

Not necessarily the most complex architectures.

But the greatest advantage for mechanisms that can distinguish changing environmental conditions from changing internal capabilities.

C2 is especially informative because it isolates uncertainty originating inside the organism.

C3 helps determine whether uncertainty alone is sufficient, without the added pressure of costly mistakes.

What would count as evidence?

I would not accept survival improvement alone.

An organism might survive by adopting a universally cautious strategy.

Nor would I accept the appearance of a decodable internal variable as conclusive evidence of a functional self-model.

The more compelling test involves controlled causal interventions.

We identify a successful organism and then manipulate its internal mechanisms while preserving the external environment.

We test whether it adapts appropriately.

Next, we change the external environment while preserving its internal mechanisms.

Can it respond differently?

Then we transplant selected mechanisms into an organism with a different architecture.

Does the receiving organism develop useful operational predictions without access to the donor's history?

Finally, we perturb or remove candidate internal representations to determine whether they causally contribute to performance.

This would give us evidence of a functional, transferable mechanism for self-conditioned prediction, rather than merely a retrospective interpretation of behavior.

We should compare against strong reactive, finite-memory, and generic recurrent-controller baselines.

Otherwise, an ordinary adaptive controller could be misidentified as an emergent self-model.

7. The most interesting outcome might be an organism that never models itself

There is a possibility I find particularly important.

Suppose C4 produces two successful evolutionary lineages.

Lineage A develops a compressed internal representation of its own changing capabilities.

It learns to distinguish internal degradation from external environmental changes.

Lineage B develops something entirely different.

Perhaps a robust mechanism that automatically compensates for internal changes without maintaining any separately identifiable model of those changes.

Perhaps it evolves redundant pathways that dynamically substitute for one another.

Perhaps it develops a distributed controller whose organization makes the distinction between internal and external failure largely irrelevant.

Both survive.

Both demonstrate strong adaptive competence.

But only one exhibits a recognizable operational self-model.

This would be a tremendously valuable result.

It would show that the same environmental pressure can support different cognitive solutions.

The second lineage might even outperform the first.

And if so, our experiments should investigate why.

The purpose of SELF-01 should not be to manufacture self-models. It should be to discover when self-models are useful, when they are unnecessary, and what alternative mechanisms evolution discovers.

That is Cognitive Opacity Independence applied scientifically.

---

8. An even deeper possibility: Self-modeling might emerge from compression pressure

Here is a less obvious hypothesis.

Consider an organism that has accumulated thousands of successful and unsuccessful experiences.

It has discovered many regularities:

Certain movements fail under particular conditions.

Certain computations become unreliable after prolonged activity.

Certain behaviors work only when specific internal mechanisms remain intact.

It could preserve these experiences individually.

But memory is expensive.

Alternatively, it could discover that many apparently unrelated failures share a common underlying cause.

For example:

A dozen behavioral failures may all arise from one degraded internal component.

Instead of remembering twelve separate failures, the organism can preserve one compressed representation of that component's reliability.

This creates an information-theoretic incentive for a form of self-modeling.

A model of an internal capability can become a more economical explanation of experience than a collection of unrelated world-specific memories.

That is directly connected to Sagacity's North Star.

Symbolic compression could favor the emergence of increasingly abstract representations of functional capability.

Those representations could subsequently improve reasoning.

Improved reasoning could identify additional regularities worth compressing.

And that could create a positive feedback loop.

Importantly, the organism would not need to know that the compressed representation referred to itself.

It might simply discover a reusable latent variable that improves prediction.

Only our external scientific instruments would determine whether that variable corresponds to the organism's own functional organization.

This is where I see the most compelling intersection of symbolic compression, emergent reasoning, and Cognitive Opacity Independence.

---

9. A hierarchy we should investigate, not assume

I would distinguish four increasingly demanding capabilities.

| Capability | What the organism can do |
|---|---|
| Operational adaptation | Change behavior when outcomes deviate from expectations |
| Capability modeling | Predict its own conditional ability to perform classes of actions |
| Structural modeling | Represent relationships between its internal functional mechanisms |
| Architectural revision | Modify or select its own mechanisms using predictions about the consequences |

These are not necessarily developmental stages.

An organism might modify its architecture without possessing explicit structural knowledge.

Another might accurately model its own capabilities without being able to change them.

A third might develop architectural revision through evolutionary search without any introspective mechanism.

We should preserve all these possibilities.

What matters is discovering the causal relationships between these capabilities.

For example:

Does capability modeling improve architectural revision?

Can architectural revision emerge without capability modeling?

Does structural self-knowledge help under severe resource constraints, or does its computational cost make the organism less competitive?

Can a lineage inherit the benefits of architectural adaptation while losing the mechanism that originally discovered those adaptations?

Those questions connect directly to Prometheus's existing transplant and inheritance experiments.

---

10. Where this intersects with epistemic fault-lines

There is also a connection to CHIASMA that deserves attention.

We have been treating the negative epistemic geometry as a sparse representation of consequential failures near the boundaries of supported knowledge.

But we could distinguish two kinds of epistemic fault-lines.

External fault-lines arise where an organism's model of the world becomes unreliable.

Endogenous fault-lines arise where the organism's assumptions about its own capabilities become unreliable.

The distinction is operational rather than metaphysical, and the two may interact.

For example, an organism might correctly understand a problem but possess an unreliable mechanism for solving it.

Or it might possess an excellent reasoning mechanism but apply it to an incorrect representation of the environment.

These produce superficially similar failures.

Yet their appropriate remedies differ.

This suggests that a cognitive architecture could benefit from learning a sparse set of constraints about its own functional reliability, without developing a complete representation of its architecture.

In other words:

The earliest useful self-model might be a compressed map of where the organism cannot trust its own machinery.

That would be a fascinating result.

And it might be substantially cheaper than learning a detailed structural model.

---

11. My strongest prediction

If I had to choose a single evolutionary pressure to investigate first, it would be:

Scarce, irreversible action under uncertainty about one's own changing capabilities.

Why?

Because this combines four important properties.

First, the organism's internal capabilities materially affect future outcomes.

Second, those capabilities cannot always be inferred from current external observations alone.

Third, experimentation is costly enough that remembering and generalizing from previous outcomes provides an advantage.

Fourth, the environment contains enough structural regularity that a compressed predictive mechanism can be reused.

Together, these conditions create a potential selective advantage for modeling internal capabilities.

But the experiment must leave room for evolution to discover a superior alternative.

I would formulate the hypothesis this way:

SELF-H1: Under bounded memory, computation, and experimentation budgets, organisms exposed to independently varying internal capabilities and external conditions will more frequently evolve reusable predictive representations of their own action-conditioned reliability than organisms exposed to external variation alone.

The strongest evidence would be improved performance on unfamiliar tasks, causal attribution under controlled internal-versus-external interventions, and selective transfer of the relevant mechanism.

A failed experiment would also be informative, provided the environment was sufficiently challenging, the required distinctions were observable through interaction, and successful solutions were reachable by the evolutionary search.

---

One final implication for Sagacity

I want to challenge one subtle assumption in the four boundaries you outlined.

You described the agency, epistemic, temporal, and autonomic boundaries as minimum internal representations.

I would go one step further.

The organism may not need to explicitly represent any of those boundaries.

Its behavior must respect certain causal and computational constraints.

But those constraints can be realized through mechanisms that never construct explicit representations of them.

A thermostat can regulate temperature without representing an epistemic boundary.

A distributed controller can coordinate interacting processes without identifying an explicit central agent.

A recurrent system can exploit temporal information without constructing symbolic categories for past, present, and future.

Some such systems may be cognitively limited. The point is that the representations themselves should remain experimentally optional.

I would therefore distinguish functional boundaries imposed by the world from representations of those boundaries constructed by the organism.

That distinction may be even more liberating than Cognitive Opacity Independence alone.

Because it suggests that our task is not to discover the minimum collection of conceptual structures an intelligence must possess.

It is to discover the minimum functional organization from which increasingly powerful cognitive capabilities can emerge.

The resulting intelligence may discover representations of itself.

It may discover something entirely different.

Or it may become extraordinarily capable while remaining permanently opaque to itself.

Prometheus should be designed to recognize all three as legitimate evolutionary outcomes.
