### Hypothesis H1, stated so it can be killed

`api.get_foundry()` builds a `Foundry` -> `Store` -> new `sqlite3.connect`
per request and closes it at the end. In WAL mode the LAST connection to
close takes an EXCLUSIVE lock, checkpoints the whole WAL into the main
file, fsyncs, and deletes `-wal`/`-shm`. The at-rest observation (no
`-wal` while the process is up) proves that close-and-delete path runs
whenever request concurrency drops to zero. After a burst of hundreds of
writes with overlapping readers (audit-envelope, events?limit=500 hold
snapshots that stop the passive autocheckpoint from resetting the WAL),
the WAL is large, so the first quiet moment pays a full checkpoint under
EXCLUSIVE, and every request arriving during it -- including a bare
`PRAGMA journal_mode=WAL` on open -- waits out the 30 s busy handler and
fails. Serial clients then hit it once per request, which is exactly the
one-failure-per-40-60-s cadence Vivarium measured.

What would falsify it: `-wal` size during and after a reproduced burst; a
reproduction on a `VACUUM INTO` copy over real HTTP (C9) that shows the
stall without any last-connection close (e.g. with one connection pinned
open for the process lifetime), or shows no stall with it. What it does
NOT explain on its own: why the 10:24-11:24 hole is an hour long; a
checkpoint of a few hundred MB is seconds, not minutes, unless something
else (a reader holding a snapshot across the checkpoint, or the OS) is in
the path. So H1 is a candidate, not a finding.

Rival H2: an external process taking an exclusive lock on the ledger file.
Archaeon's readers open `mode=ro` (`archaeon/fossils.py:278`), Vivarium's
`viv.cli orphans` is read-only; the `var/backup` snapshot is dated 04:01
(my move window), inside neither episode. No candidate identified, not
excluded.
