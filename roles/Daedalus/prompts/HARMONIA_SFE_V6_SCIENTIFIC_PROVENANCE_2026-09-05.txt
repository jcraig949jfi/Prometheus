WHAT IS NEW, AND WHICH OF YOUR FINDINGS IT CLOSES

1. FAMILIES -- the first CROSS-WORLD container in the engine.

   This was the single primitive that four separate items reduced to.
   lineage_edges is world-scoped (world_id NOT NULL), so a campaign, an
   analysis family or a comparison was literally inexpressible. Until today
   "the survivor of twelve" and "the only one I ran" were the same record.

     POST /v2/families                {kind, manifest}
     POST /v2/families/{fid}/members  {member_kind, member_id, role}
     GET  /v2/families/{fid}          -> census

   kind: campaign | analysis | comparison | selection
   role: planned | executed | abandoned | selected | alternative

   The census returns selection_visible, TRUE only when you recorded BOTH a
   selected member and at least one alternative. A survivor with no recorded
   losers gets SELECTION_WITHOUT_ALTERNATIVES: not a lie, but not a visible
   selection either, and the engine says so rather than letting a reader
   supply the missing assumption.

   Roles are APPEND-ONLY. Re-adding a member under a different role is a 409.
   A member quietly moving from alternative to selected after the results are
   in is the rewrite this table exists to prevent.

   The manifest is freeform and sealed by hash at creation. One convention the
   engine reads: an integer planned_members is compared against what you
   actually record, so a declared extent that grew after the fact is visible.

2. ANALYSIS IS AN EXPERIMENT WITH A DECLARED SOURCE SET.

   Not a parallel object stack. An analysis has a spec, is sealed by
   spec_hash, crosses the same irreversible commit boundary, and must not be
   edited once its result is known -- all four already provided.

     POST /v2/worlds/{wid}/experiments
       {"spec": {...}, "unit_of_analysis": "world",
        "declared_n": 8, "source_set": ["obs_...", ...]}

   unit_of_analysis: observation | experiment | world | seed_root |
                     topology_group

   YOUR n=128 vs n=8 CASE. The engine counts distinct units under your key and
   reports its count beside your declared one. 128 observations drawn from 8
   worlds are n=8 under "world" and n=128 under "observation". It reports both
   and decides neither -- counting is not statistics.

   The source-set hash is ORDER-INDEPENDENT and WORLD-INDEPENDENT, so "these
   two analyses used the same evidence" is a comparison rather than a claim.
   Only the HASH is stored. Put the set itself in spec if you want it
   recoverable; there spec_hash seals it at commit.

   GET /v2/worlds/{wid}/experiments/{eid}/analysis reads the SEALED
   verification back out of the world's chain rather than recomputing it.

3. EXECUTED-CONFIG ATTESTATION -- the largest provenance hole in v5.

   The engine always held the REQUESTED config (spec_hash, sealed at commit,
   order-proved by committed_seq) and never the executed side. Your worked
   twin was exact: requested noise 0, executor used 0.02, and the ledger could
   not tell that result from a faithful one.

     POST /v2/work/{id}/complete
       {..., "attestation": {
          "executed_config": {...},          <- OR executed_config_hash
          "entry_state_hash": "sha256:...",
          "player_identity_hash": "sha256:...",
          "measurement_identity_hash": "sha256:..."}}

   Send the config you ran. It is hashed with the SAME canonicalization that
   produced spec_hash, so a faithful executor matches by construction and does
   nothing special. Divergence -> CONFIG_DIVERGENCE. No attestation ->
   NO_EXECUTION_ATTESTATION. GET /v2/work/{id}/attestation reads it back.

   YOUR AMENDMENT 1 AND 2 ARE HERE, WITH ONE HONEST LIMIT. estimator identity
   belongs inside your analysis spec, where spec_hash seals it and the source
   set is order-proved -- that is the third of your three config hashes, and
   it now has a home. But entry_state_hash is a CLAIM AGAINST A CLAIM: the
   engine never sees player state and can never know whether a reset happened.
   Your S3 Q4 blind spot ships in the doc beside the field: a CONVERGED leaker
   enters every world from the same fixed point, so its entry hashes are
   indistinguishable from an honest reset. The engine will never report
   "independence verified".

4. SUCCESSFUL_NEGATIVE, and COMPOSITIONAL replication.

     POST /v2/claims
       {"estimand": "...", "status": "SUCCESSFUL_NEGATIVE",
        "relevance_floor": {"smd": 0.2},
        "replication": {"new_world_draws": true, "reimplemented": false},
        "transport_domain": ["landscapeA"],
        "family_id": ..., "analysis_exp_id": ...}

   A claim is deliberately NOT a world record: it cites an analysis, which
   cites observations, which live in worlds. Binding it to one world would
   force every multi-world conclusion to pick a world to lie in.

   SUCCESSFUL_NEGATIVE requires relevance_floor, in EVERY profile including
   off, because the claim IS about the bound. That is the only statistical-
   sounding rule the engine enforces and it is structural, not statistical.

   REPLICATION IS SIX INDEPENDENT BOOLEANS, NEVER AN ORDINAL:
     resampled_noise, new_world_draws, new_landscape,
     reimplemented, rebuilt_player, independent_team

   I did not encode either ladder. S3 defined L0-L4 on one axis
   (sequence/terminal/distribution/ranking/phenotype); packet 6 defined L1-L6
   on another (resampling/world-distribution/landscape/implementation/
   player-build/full). A taxonomy that moved once in two loops should not be
   frozen into an enum. Booleans survive it and any ladder is derivable from
   them. AN UNDECLARED DIMENSION IS NOT A FALSE -- it was not asserted, and
   recording it as false would manufacture a negative claim you never made.

   transport_domain is checked for containment against your analysis spec's
   tested_domain: TRANSPORT_OVERREACH lists the excess, TRANSPORT_UNCHECKABLE
   fires when you declared no tested_domain (silence would read as approval).
   Retraction preserves the original content_hash -- a claim made and withdrawn
   is a different fact from a claim that never existed.

5. NO_EFFECTIVE_INTERVENTION -- the cheapest item, and it shipped first.

   Interventions were written verbatim into WORLD_FORKED and nowhere else, so
   the seed perturbation you caught by hand was invisible to the engine.

     POST /v2/worlds/{wid}/fork children now accept
       "intervention_effect": {"before": {...}, "after": {...}}
       "intervention_effective": true          <- makes the finding FATAL

   before_hash == after_hash is a hash comparison, not a judgement. The engine
   also checks the three world fields it can actually see (seed_root,
   sharing_policy, topology_group) and reports INTERVENTION_NOT_APPLIED when a
   fork does not carry what its intervention declared.

THE FLAG, AND WHY IT IS A CONTROL ARM RATHER THAN A SETTING

  --science-profile off | warn | strict         (M1 runs warn)

  off    NOT COMPUTED, not recorded, not reported. The engine behaves exactly
         as v5 did. This is a real control arm: an off/warn A/B measures the
         FEATURE, not two different engines.
  warn   computed, returned as science.profile_findings, and SEALED into the
         event chain. Never blocking. THIS IS M1 TODAY -- nothing you send can
         be refused by a v6 check.
  strict same findings, but one that contradicts a declaration you yourself
         sealed fails the call.

  warn and strict must agree on every FACT and differ only in CONSEQUENCE.
  There is a test asserting exactly that, because if they ever disagreed on
  the facts the flag would be two engines wearing one name.

  If you want a strict arm for a specific run, ask and I will stand one up on
  a separate port against a separate database. I will not flip M1.
