1. CONTEXT

Prometheus grows reasoning mechanisms under selection rather than designing
them. Hephaestus is the forge: it keeps a queue of "walls" (reasoning
behaviours the frozen primitive set cannot express) and, before any wall may
be minted, runs a "closure gauntlet": enumerate programs over the frozen
primitives (arm A0), plus a frozen generic basis (A2), plus a generic small
language (B), and ask whether any program reproduces the target behaviour.
Verdicts: SEARCH_ROUTING (the substrate already reaches it), OPERATOR (a
new primitive is genuinely needed), INCONCLUSIVE. Two boolean-valued walls
had been through the gauntlet; both came out non-OPERATOR. The obvious
worry (raised in the charter's Addendum 4): can the gauntlet ever say
OPERATOR? Specimen 3 is the positive control: a tiny synthetic world
(TINYPROG: 4-vectors over Z6, ten primitives p00..p09) where removing one
primitive (p05, elementwise multiply) provably makes a certified class of
behaviours unreachable. Twenty LOST behaviours and ten CONTROL behaviours
were selected mechanically; an extra arm C = A0 + p05 asks whether naming
the missing operator closes the wall.

9. QUESTIONS FOR THE EXTERNAL REVIEWER

  1. Arm B is the "any small generic program" control and cannot express
     a scalar intermediate. Should B be widened (adding scalar ops) as a
     versioned change, or should INCONCLUSIVE(B) simply be reported as it
     was here? Widening B after seeing it fail is the move the prereg
     doctrine forbids; not widening leaves B weaker than A0 on typed
     routes.
  2. What would falsify "the gauntlet can detect an OPERATOR gap"? Our
     answer: a LOST target with an A0 witness that survives the exhaustive
     and shift columns. Zero occurred in 20. Is a positive control in ONE
     synthetic world sufficient to lift the concern for the boolean
     specimens, whose value type is different?
  3. Should we stop? The seat's honest case for stopping the gauntlet
     programme: three defects in one instrument in one day; every result
     before today was produced by code that had never met a second value
     type. The case for continuing: the boolean outputs are byte-identical
     under the corrected code, and the instrument now has one executed
     positive, one executed negative, and one executed injected-success
     (arm C) control.
  4. The P1 gate (>= 90%) sits exactly on the observed value with n = 20.
     Should the next specimen preregister an eligibility count and an
     interval, and treat "at the line" as INDETERMINATE by rule?
  5. Is exact tuple equality the right COERCE for vector targets, or should
     the six-probe "coerced" column use a weaker equivalence (e.g. equal
     up to a fixed permutation) so that P5-type aliases have a chance to
     appear on LOST targets?
