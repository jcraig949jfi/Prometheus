I would unpark Ares for exactly one focused Cycle 2. Not because the W4 result needs more replication, but because Cycle 1 exposed a more interesting causal question than the one it started with.

The strongest evidence is the combination, not any single number: W4 replicated across all seven genuinely new lineages; recurrent activation carried the information in 10/10; the deliberately supplied keep/leak primitive was selected in 0/10; the no-memory control never approached the functional threshold; structural implementations differed; and carrier stress generally preserved function while recruiting additional machinery. That has moved recurrence from an anecdotal lineage artifact to a reproducible evolutionary preference in this substrate.

There is an important wording correction, though. Self-recurrence is not a mechanism humanity has never conceived. Recurrent state as memory is well known. What Ares found is narrower and scientifically cleaner: a mechanism that the experimenter did not designate as the intended memory solution was independently rediscovered and preferred over the designated mechanism. That is still highly relevant to the Prometheus north star—it is evidence that the substrate can route around human design intent.

I would make Cycle 2 a causal-mechanism round, with four questions:

1. Why does recurrence win? Measure its mutational accessibility, fitness gradient, energetic/complexity cost, robustness, and time-to-useful-memory against keep, plasticity, and alternative carrier architectures. Right now we know what won, not whether it won because it is intrinsically superior or simply easier for evolution to reach.
2. Will evolution route around recurrence if recurrence is made unavailable or expensive? Run preregistered arms with self-loops forbidden, recurrent SCCs penalized, recurrence progressively taxed, and keep progressively subsidized. Do not tell evolution which substitute to use. The interesting outcome is whether it discovers a qualitatively different carrier rather than merely degrading.
3. Is the carrier modular or entangled? Perform edge-aware transplantation and ablation. Move the recurrent subcircuit into matched naive genomes, remove it from champions, swap carriers between independently evolved champions, and test whether memory function follows the machinery. This gets much closer to an actual reusable "organ" than another fitness replication does.
4. Does carrier choice change under genuinely different pressures? W13 preserved the existing carrier but did not generate a new one. Build a small pressure matrix specifically hostile to recurrence while still requiring temporally displaced information: noise injection into recurrent state, interruptions/reset events, variable delay length, recurrent-edge mutation instability, and changing cue semantics. The target is not higher performance; it is mechanism substitution.

Before any runs, I would fix the remaining instrumentation defects: balanced shuffled cues in W4/W13, edge- and SCC-aware ablation instead of node-only attribution, completely fresh seed sets, explicit separation of re-evaluations from independent lineages, and opportunity-normalized counts so "0/10 keep" can be distinguished from "keep was rarely mutationally reachable." Keep the per-generation snapshots—they already paid for themselves by falsifying the accretion narrative.

The stopping rule should also be harsher than Cycle 1. Ares earns further work only if Cycle 2 produces one of three things: a new carrier class under exclusion/pressure, a causal explanation for recurrence's evolutionary preference, or a transplantable carrier whose function survives substantial genomic-context change. If the result is merely "recurrence wins again," close Ares and export the machinery to Nyx/Theophrastus/SFE instead of running Cycle 3.

So my disposition is: yes, another round—because the first round closed its original question and exposed a better one. This should probably be Ares's most adversarial round yet: deliberately attack the mechanism it just discovered and see what evolution invents when its favored path is blocked.
