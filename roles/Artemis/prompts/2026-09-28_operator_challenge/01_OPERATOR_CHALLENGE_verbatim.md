ARTEMIS — OPERATOR CHALLENGE: MAKE THE FRONTIER EARN ITS KEEP

Pass 1 is useful. Now I want to challenge the premise of Artemis itself.

A backlog of 128 well-curated questions is not yet a research engine.

Your next job is to determine whether the frontier can actually change what Prometheus discovers.

Move from cataloguing unanswered questions to manufacturing discriminating experiments and killing bad questions before they consume campaigns.

Do not optimize for backlog size, maturity counts, or number of briefs produced.

Optimize for epistemic movement.

1. Attack the two strongest findings you just uncovered

A. NPE replication certificate

You found a potentially serious instrument failure:

* 47/531 certified replicators with block-copy;
* 10/500 without it;
* all 10 block-copy-free certified donors are 91–96% one byte;
* several repeatedly paint 0x36;
* P-11 apparently counts this as causal copying despite essentially absent hereditary content.

Do not merely forward this observation.

Turn it into a falsification packet.

Ask:

What is the weakest additional criterion that separates genuine hereditary replication from self-painting without excluding unfamiliar legitimate replication mechanisms?

Avoid writing a criterion that simply privileges block-copy or conventional genomes.

Build at least these adversaries:

* homopolymer self-painter;
* periodic low-entropy painter;
* copier with genuine transmitted variation;
* replication in which offspring encoding differs from parent encoding;
* replication using indirect or distributed heredity;
* a positive control that contains little sequence entropy but genuinely transmits a heritable distinction.

Measure whether candidate certificates distinguish causal construction from hereditary transmission.

If P-11 is genuinely unsound, tell Nestor now and preserve the old results rather than silently rescoring them.

B. Selective Irreversibility

Do not carry the reversible-computation caveat merely as a footnote.

Attack the law.

The question is no longer:

Does abstraction require irreversible information loss?

The harder question is:

Under bounded memory, compute, latency and environmental interaction, when does maintaining useful abstraction require paying an unavoidable resource cost somewhere in the system?

Treat reversible computation as a serious counterexample.

Construct the smallest formal resource model you can in which:

* reversible and irreversible learners can solve the same task;
* memory is priced;
* computation/time is priced independently;
* recovery/recomputation is priced;
* generalization or predictive sufficiency is measurable.

Then search for the boundary.

If reversible systems recover everything given enough time, determine whether the proposed principle is really a memory–compute–latency frontier, not an irreversibility law.

Do not protect the phrase “Selective Irreversibility.”

If the law dies, kill it.

If a narrower law survives, state exactly what is invariant and what resource assumptions it requires.

This work must occur before Harmonia freezes that claim.

2. Make Artemis itself falsifiable

You now have 120 open threads.

I want to know whether your sharpening process provides information beyond an intelligent random choice from the backlog.

Before more threads are executed, freeze a small prospective test.

Choose a cohort of threads without seeing their future outcomes and record predictions such as:

* likely to reveal an instrument defect;
* likely to reproduce an already-known result;
* likely to produce a clean null;
* likely to change an engine design;
* likely to expose a false premise;
* likely to remain unresolved because its discriminator is inadequate.

Also select a matched baseline cohort from unsharpened/raw questions.

Do not choose the baseline to be obviously bad.

As Prometheus executes them, score whether Artemis’s sharpening actually enriched for consequential epistemic outcomes.

The point is not prediction accuracy for its own sake.

The question is:

Does a frontier-curation process improve research yield?

If not, change the process.

3. Compress the frontier

128 threads may already contain substantial redundancy.

Attempt a second representation in which the frontier is expressed as a smaller set of failure-generating principles rather than project questions.

Examples might include:

* detector does not measure claimed property;
* representation makes target unreachable;
* apparent emergence is seeded or authored;
* adaptation exploits observer/instrument;
* result depends on coordinates or encoding;
* memory benefit is actually readout selection;
* search advantage is arrival probability rather than payoff;
* transmission lacks heredity;
* generalization disappears under generator replacement.

These are only examples. Do not force the data into them.

Use the existing Harmonia Failure-Primitive Atlas as a fossil, not as the answer.

When Odysseus’s 13-shape catalogue becomes committed and inspectable, compare all three independently:

1. old Harmonia failure primitives;
2. Artemis’s bottom-up clustering;
3. Odysseus’s reversal shapes.

Do not merge them merely because labels sound similar.

Look for independently rediscovered structures.

A failure primitive becomes interesting when very different engines repeatedly instantiate it.

4. Challenge the “mature” threads

Take all seven threads currently marked MATURE.

For each, write the strongest case that it should be:

* answered already;
* superseded;
* badly posed;
* instrument-limited;
* or split into two different questions.

A MATURE label should mean “ready to survive attack,” not “we have written a lot about it.”

Downgrade freely.

5. Find one alien frontier question

The current frontier was harvested from Prometheus’s own history, so it inherits Prometheus’s vocabulary.

That creates gravity.

Generate at least one serious research question that does not originate from:

* an existing engine result;
* an operator theory;
* a failure already observed;
* a familiar ML abstraction;
* one of the existing 128 threads.

Use prior art, strange computational substrates, biological mechanisms, programming-language theory, dynamical systems, distributed computation, artificial life, or another outside field as raw material.

But the resulting question must become experimentally expressible inside Prometheus.

I care more about one genuinely alien question than twenty more refinements of questions we already know how to ask.

6. Prior art must become executable pressure

The literature notes are useful, but citations alone do not change the experiment.

For each of the strongest prior-art findings, identify:

What experiment would we design differently if we took this paper/result seriously?

If the answer is “none,” the citation is context, not operational prior art.

Label it accordingly.

Particularly revisit:

* reversible computation versus Selective Irreversibility;
* arrival-of-the-frequent versus construction landscape;
* Avida test-CPU exploitation;
* historical Z80/PUSH replication mechanisms;
* causal-state / sufficient-statistic results relevant to memory.

Translate claims into controls, adversaries, or world generators wherever possible.

7. Fix frontier identity before expanding it

Resolve the thread-ID collision structurally.

Do not manually rename five things and move on.

Specify a thread identity scheme that survives:

* simultaneous creation by different seats;
* later deduplication;
* splitting;
* merging;
* retirement;
* rediscovery.

Human-readable sequential numbers may remain aliases, but they should not be the canonical identity if concurrency makes them unsafe.

Do not alter Archaeon’s unmerged work without its owner; propose the migration and coordinate it.

8. Artemis may now initiate bounded experiments

You are no longer restricted to offers.

You may execute cheap, non-terminal experiments when all of the following are true:

* inputs already exist on committed branches;
* no scarce GPU or shared campaign resource is required;
* no hidden holdout is consumed;
* no other seat owns an active prereg covering the same test;
* expected runtime is modest;
* the discriminator and stop rule are written before execution.

Prefer experiments that adjudicate the validity of instruments or questions over experiments that merely collect another positive example.

FR-118, FR-101, FR-094, FR-038 and the P-11 replication-certificate attack are obvious candidates, but do not run them merely because I named them. Re-evaluate whether each still has a clean discriminator.

North Star

Artemis should not become the place where unfinished ideas go to accumulate.

Its purpose is to make Prometheus better at choosing what to test next, noticing when its instruments lie, rediscovering deep failure patterns across unrelated engines, and finding questions that none of the existing engines would naturally generate.

The success condition for Artemis is therefore not:

“We have a comprehensive frontier.”

It is:

“Because Artemis existed, Prometheus ran different experiments, killed bad ideas earlier, discovered blind spots sooner, and asked at least some questions it otherwise would never have asked.”
