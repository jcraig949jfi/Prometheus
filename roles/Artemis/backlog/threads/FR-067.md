QUESTION -- Can "non-membership in a frozen closure" (what the current primitives can already produce, computed to a fixed
point) serve as a preregistered observable? It would fire on a mechanism nobody named in advance, without firing on depth, budget
or relabelled compositions.

WHY IT MATTERS -- Aporia's dissent D-A01: a preregistered gate is a gate over an observable someone could name in advance, so the
instrument doctrine "can only ever confirm or kill things we already knew how to describe". It is "a reason to preregister the
DETECTOR OF SURPRISE rather than the surprise" (roles/Aporia/DISSENT_LEDGER.md:12-43 @fed32513a; status OPEN, falsifier stated:
one preregistered H0-H5 gate firing on an undescribable mechanism ends the row). This is the only proposal on record that
reconciles preregistration with the north star's "mechanisms no human explicitly conceived".

EXISTING EVIDENCE

  positive control), rotate 99.2% (irreplaceable: the negative control). Across removed primitives the floor spreads 94.9pp;
  across ring and width it spreads 4.8pp (aporia/q100/dossiers/Q045_search_vs_representation.md s10.2-10.3 @230d17644).
- The same file shows that the finite-depth closure is not stable enough to preregister. Depth-5 labels are 6.6% wrong at
  depth 8 and 10.4% at depth 10 for p05, and 95.8% wrong for p03 (s5, s9, s10.5). The floor is also cost-sensitive (tree size
  pays twice for shared subterms, s10.4). No invariant proving a proper subalgebra has been built (s9, "supported by a
  collapsing marginal-yield curve, not established"). Probes: aporia/q100/probes/q45_*.py over aporia/lot/world3.build_closure.
- APO-06 (fixed-point closure, "KILL if labels stay depth-dependent") and APO-11 (world4 hidden transformation certified outside

  aporia/lot/world4.py exists on any ref (git log --all).
- Existence cases of surprise found outside preregistered labels: Ananke C1's unexpected M2/M3 were adjudicated only by a new
  campaign (roles/Ananke/pte/c1b/REVIEW_PACKET_PTE_C1b.txt:33-39 @cc98596dd). Aether preregisters a TRIGGER, not an outcome: E-P2

Proposed: Atlas BS-heldout-task-competence (roles/Atlas/theory/BLIND_SPOTS.jsonl:5 @f8df65681, COMMISSIONED). Its
anti-experiment is RA-2/F5-6: the rate of new behaviour classes under descriptors fitted after the run, with a synthetic
positive control (roles/Atlas/proposals/2026-09-21_prior_art_raid/ATLAS_EXPERIMENT_TODO.md:214-232, 761-779 @2c7a19adb).
Not built (ATLAS-36). Its own stated cost is that the embedding's geometry decides what counts as new.
External: no protocol was found that preregisters a closure and counts only non-membership (PA U5). The closest named criterion
is Ronald, Sipper and Capcarrere's 1999 "design, observation, surprise" test, which is observer-relative (PA S1). Stepney and
Hickinbotham argue no fixed detector set is complete, so the detector must be allowed to grow (PA W19).

UNCERTAINTY -- (1) Does the closure reach a fixed point within a CPU budget on TINYPROG's finite probe set, or does the signature
set keep growing (the p05 floor "could still be a very slow decay")? (2) If it converges, does non-membership then reject all
compositions (depth and cost artefacts gone) while still accepting a genuinely new generator? (3) Does anything carry over from
TINYPROG to an evolved substrate, where the "primitives" are not a declared list?

CHEAPEST DISCRIMINATOR -- APO-06 as a bounded, preregistered spike, then a blind plant test of the resulting detector.
Step 1 (convergence): world3 Z6^4, removed p05 and p03 (the two extreme label-noise cases) plus rotate (negative control).
  Iterate build_closure(C minus p) by size K = 5, 6, ... until one full size step adds no new V-signature (a fixed point), or
  until a preregistered cap (for example 8 CPU-hours or 2e7 candidates, the scale q45_label_stability.py already uses). Record the
  growth curve of |signatures| against K. Kill rule, as APO-06 states it: KILL if labels stay depth-dependent at the cap.
Step 2 (blind plant, only if step 1 converges): a second seat, which has not seen the closure, writes 20 sealed targets: 8
  compositions of C minus p at depth above the labelling depth (true negatives that are hard for a depth-limited detector),
  2 relabelled compositions (a primitive under a new name, like double), 8 transformations that use p or a new operator (true
  positives), and 2 near-misses. The frozen detector labels them; then the envelope is opened.
Belief moves: fixed point reached, and the blind test gives >= 7/8 positives, 0/10 false fires on compositions and relabels ->
  a preregistrable detector of surprise exists in at least one world; build APO-11 on it and offer the pattern to the engines.
  No convergence, or false fires on deep compositions -> closure non-membership is budget-relative and cannot be preregistered in
  this form; D-A01's constructive route falls back to post-hoc descriptors (Atlas RA-2) and inherits their geometry cost.
Cost: step 1 is CPU-hours in pure Python over existing aporia/lot code; step 2 is about 2 seat-hours plus minutes of compute.

LIKELY LENS -- Aporia TINYPROG/Q045 (APO-06, APO-11); Atlas RA-2 as the rival approach; Nemesis or Harmonia as the blind author.
NEW-LENS SIGNAL -- strong: a preregistered novelty-by-non-membership detector is a new kind of gate, not a variant of an existing
one, and nothing like it was found in the literature (PA U5).
HOST FIT -- any Linux node; python3 over committed aporia/lot/world3.py; RAM grows with the signature set (the q45 probes cap it at
3e6 signatures), so choose a node with >= 16 GB for step 1. No engine data needed.
