QUESTION -- When does residual-driven ("surprise") eviction lose to random eviction, and is that loss a noise-retention effect
(heteroscedastic corruption), a recency effect, or a capacity effect? And is the "random" control relevance-blind at all?

WHY IT MATTERS -- Endogenous forgetting rules are a North-Star primitive supplied to every engine (WTP reservoirs, PTE buffers,
Ergon libraries). If surprise retains noise, every engine using a residual key needs a learning-progress or noise-floor key
instead; if random is really distribution matching (PA P4), SI-program "blind" controls are mislabelled.

UNCERTAINTY
- Is the fixture loss caused by the SD-3 corrupted half (the PA prediction), or by B = c/4 on one generator with 4 seeds?
- F3 switch: does keep_worst win (0.97 of rows) because a high residual after a switch marks RECENT records, i.e. surprise is a
  recency proxy (H-D1-68 in reverse)? Rows do not log retained-record ages.
- Why self-signal loses on F4 transfer and F5 nuisance at low B: nuisance-mode noise is structured, not homoscedastic.
- "Order is second-order to capacity" (Ergon, PA P6) does not hold in these rows at c/4; the Ergon null may be consumer-specific.
