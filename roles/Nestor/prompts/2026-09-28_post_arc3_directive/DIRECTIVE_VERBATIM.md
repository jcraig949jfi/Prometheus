5. Independence and effective sample size

Be extremely strict about experimental units.

Repeated rows, duplicate births, replays of the same stochastic realization, or two labels attached to the same underlying simulation are not independent evidence.

For every ancestry-replay statistic, preserve enough provenance to reconstruct:

* originating simulation/run;
* seed;
* birth;
* lineage;
* intervention arm;
* shared ancestry/background;
* any duplicated or deterministically identical realization.

If clustering changes the effective sample size, surface it before the result is calculated.

Do not let “34 births” become “n=34” automatically.

6. Painting/source-diversity guard

Continue investigating the apparent “painting” behavior you observed: many written loci may descend from very few distinct source loci.

This matters because a genome can appear broadly reconstructed while actually being generated by a very narrow causal source.

The attribution machinery should therefore distinguish at least:

* number of destination loci written;
* number of distinct causal source loci;
* number of distinct performers/instructions responsible;
* dependence structure among destinations;
* whether a broad copied region is simply repeated expression of one or two source causes.

Do not promote “32 loci causally attributed” into “32 independent inherited causes.”

Use Archaeon’s C4/C5/C6 rules where applicable, but retain an explicit source-diversity diagnostic even if it is not part of the final decision rule.

* a byte being written;
* a byte being causally written by the parent/donor;

* that contribution surviving into later reproductive generations;

Do not allow a successful construction certificate to silently answer the heredity question.
