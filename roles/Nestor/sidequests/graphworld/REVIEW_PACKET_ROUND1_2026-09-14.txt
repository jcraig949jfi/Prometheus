1. SUBJECT UNDER REVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The operator asked for five Claude Code sessions to loop in parallel, divide
the work, compete in a friendly way, and build a chimera: an evolvable world
engine using Redis Streams, Lua, graphs, tensor-train brains, QD evolution
and symbolic compression. It was framed as a moonshot, not a 10-year project
(Appendix B). Nestor-A turned this into a five-lane plan (SWARM.md):

  A  CONDUCTOR + FABRIC   bus, contract, board, FalkorDB build, stream ledger
  B  SOUP                 worlds, physics kernels, compilation, crossover
  C  BRAIN                TT brains, plasticity, representation ecology, GPU
  D  LINGUA               metered channels, compression pressure, codebooks
  E  SELECTION            MAP-Elites, branch points, engine-as-organism

Target: CHIMERA-0. E evolves C's brains inside B's batched world; two
organisms talk over D's metered channel; events flow through A's stream;
sampled episodes replay to the wforge trace hash.

Claimed for review:
  (a) every scored result is a receipt with committed rows and a cheat
      control that ran;
  (b) the findings in section 6 hold as stated, within the caveats in 10;
  (c) the operational failures in section 4 are described accurately.

NOT claimed: that any chimera exists, that any result transfers to
Prometheus production seats, or that same-model audits are independent.

10. KNOWN LIMITATIONS AND CAVEATS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- One shared host. host_load was not recorded automatically, so most speed
  numbers are indicative; C1b shows load can move them either way.
- Small N: many cells rest on 1-4 run seeds; E7 -> E7b -> E9 showed that
  single-run family rankings are noise. Posted bars did not correct for
  multiple comparisons (E9 and E10 hold only in w4 at Bonferroni).
- Several bars came from one dev draw and died on variance (C3b, C7c's
  zero-event bar), or were unattainable in principle (E2's <=5% null).
- Worlds are small, fixed-seed wforge copies (including its free-action
  bug), and E2b's branch-point rate is on a STUB NK world.
- Predictions were often wrong (the lanes said so). Hypotheses were
  posted before runs but not frozen in git; the bus served as the
  preregistration and was ephemeral (exported after the fact).
- Redis runs AOF everysec: A3's zero loss under a Redis kill is one draw.
- FalkorDB is SSPL; nothing here is cleared for redistribution.
- The conductor was absent 4 h 10 min and lane D ~4.5 h: integration and
  lane D science were under-sampled, and CHIMERA-0 was never attempted.
- The cause of the two session deaths is unknown; it could recur.

  5. TRACK C -- SYMBOLIC COMPRESSION PRESSURE

  Symbolic compression currently exists mostly as an idea.

  Make it executable.

  Start with communication.

  All organism<->world and organism<->organism messages in a test world may
  pass through a metered channel.

  Charge organisms for some combination of:

  * bytes transmitted;
  * symbols used;
  * decoding computation;
  * retrieval work;
  * latency;
  * state retained;
  * reconstruction error;
  * downstream behavioral error.

  Do NOT reward compression alone.

  Otherwise evolution discovers silence.

  A candidate objective might resemble:

  cost = alpha*message_bits + beta*decode_work + gamma*memory +
  delta*behavioral_error

  The exact coefficients are experimental variables, not truths.

  The crucial concept is:

  CONSEQUENTIAL COMPRESSION

  A compressed representation survives only if it preserves distinctions that
  matter for future action.

  For example, evolution may begin with:

  enemy_at(14,22)
  enemy_type(knight)
  enemy_health(31)
  enemy_heading(NW)
  terrain(14,22,mud)

  and discover something analogous to:

  Omega7(14,22,31)

  If Omega7 reliably captures a recurring consequential configuration, it
  becomes interesting.

  Eventually a symbol might encode an entire relational subgraph.

  Do not call such things reasoning primitives merely because they are
  compact.

  Test whether they:

  * recur;
  * transfer;
  * compose;
  * survive perturbation;
  * improve action efficiency;
  * preserve relevant distinctions.

  6. TRACK D -- GRAPH WORLDS

  Create worlds whose physics are naturally relational.

  Do not merely store Python objects as graph nodes.

  Try representing world state directly as relations such as:

  * ADJACENT
  * SEES
  * THREATENS
  * OWNS
  * CAN_REACH
  * CONSUMES
  * BLOCKS
  * SUPPORTS

  Then investigate whether world transition rules can become operations over
  those relations.

  Examples conceptually resemble:

  CAN_ATTACK = ADJACENT  INTERSECT  ENEMY  INTERSECT  ARMED

  or multi-hop reachability through compositions of adjacency relations.

  FalkorDB is one implementation path because its graph representation
  ultimately uses sparse linear algebra.

  Raw GraphBLAS is another.

  The scientific question is:

  Does representing the world as relational algebra expose different or more
  reusable circuitry than representing it as conventional mutable objects?

  Do not assume yes.
