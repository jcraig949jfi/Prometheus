What revision 2 corrects

Revision 1 was written from the campaign's human-readable packet rather than from the machine-readable record. Four claims were wrong in consequence. Each is recomputed below and each now has a test that fails if it regresses.

R-01 · Sign inversion in two headline effects

Removing explicit fitness raised scores; forced read order beat answer-before-read.

Both reversed. The effect is defined as experiment minus control and the axis label reads experiment then control, so a positive value means the left-hand level scored higher. Explicit fitness beat implicit, and answer-before-read beat forced read.

R-02 · Wrong run named as the admissible instance

Named a run whose final held-out score was 0.75, which cannot satisfy the 0.90 rule.

That run adjudicates weak. The single admissible instance is a different run entirely, at 1.0 against a control at 0.0. The wrong identity came from the preserved-flag stream, which carries no verdict.

R-03 · Categorical claims the record does not carry

The reservoir question is answerable from per-run lineage records; first-replication times show no sharp accessibility cliff.

Lineage keeps a truncated tail and logs no migration events, so the ancestry certificate the reservoir claim needs cannot generally be rebuilt. The epoch range pools tiers and cells and identifies nothing about topology.

R-04 · Incomplete effect table

Twenty-four axes, inherited from what the packet's summary happened to print.

All twenty-eight axes in the record, recomputed from the run index.

Findings

Explicit fitness outperformed implicit selection. Across 513 matched pairs the explicit-fitness arm finished a mean of 0.3036 higher on held-out score, and crossed in 85 pairs where its implicit control did not against 11 the other way. This is the largest effect in the table and it rests on the second-largest pair count. Revision 1 stated this backwards.
Answer-before-read outperformed forced read, opposite to cycle 8. Both read-order rows agree and they are independent sets of pairs. With answer-before-read as the experiment the effect is +0.1029 over 334 pairs, 37 crossings to 13. With the flip run the other way it is −0.0687 over 304 pairs, 14 to 33. Cycle 8 concluded the opposite: that the plateau organism answers before reading the ask tick, that no edit of the answer makes it conditional until a read is added, and that every partial read is deleterious. This campaign does not reproduce that advantage. The discrepancy is recorded as an open result, not resolved. Three candidate explanations are separable by experiment and none is adopted here: the endpoint differs, since cycle 8 scored crossing a specific world while this scores final held-out competence; forcing the read costs instructions in a byte VM where the read must be executed and timed; and the moat tag couples read order to the bridge factor, so the two arms may not be exchangeable. Cycle 9 should settle which.
Replication events are common; self-sustaining lineages are rare. 1,031 randomly initialised, unseeded populations produced at least one birth backed by evidence that the organism placed the child's bytes, at fidelity 0.906 to 0.990. That is a causal claim about single events and it holds. It is not a claim about lineages. Reconstructing ancestry from the stored parent pointers gives a maximum chain depth of 1 in 911 of the 1,031 runs: one parent, several children, no grandchild. Only 120 runs reach depth 2 or more and only 9 reach depth 3. Counting events rather than generations is far more generous, at 952 runs with two or more and 308 with ten or more, because a single parent replicating repeatedly inflates the event count without extending the lineage. No run in this set had its lineage tail truncated, so the depth figures are exact rather than a floor.

The single admissible endogenous-reproduction instance. Run 64dea50f417efb02-s1203-tL-a0 finished at 1.0 on held-out score with its matched external-reproduction control at 0.0, a margin of 1.0. Its cell is a graph world under a coevolving environment, partial endogenous reproduction, block copy, periodic-migration niches, and forced read order. It is one run. The other 64 instances of this flag fail on the defect below: 50 are below threshold at final state and 14 have the exogenous control finishing ahead.
The reservoir question is not answerable from the current record. All 124 reservoir flags are inadmissible, in two exact groups: 117 fired on seeded instrument populations, which cannot bear on what evolution finds unaided, and 7 fired on runs whose reproduction is external, which is the exogenous control rather than a reservoir result. None is weak, so none is a near miss. Supporting the claim the flag names needs a certificate that a lineage arose in the easy niche, migrated, and crossed in a hard one. The record cannot generally supply it: the stored lineage is the last 400 births and is thinned to 50 under disk pressure, each entry carries only the parent's niche at the moment of birth, and migrations are counted in aggregate without being logged as events. A lineage that migrated between two births leaves no trace of having done so.
First-replication timing identifies nothing on its own. Across the admissible set the first evidence-backed replication appears anywhere from epoch 0 to epoch 3,946. That range is not a statement about accessibility. It pools two tiers with different epoch budgets, 979 runs at 4,000 epochs and 52 at 2,000, and 35 distinct combinations of reproduction physics, ecological structure, and representation, one of which supplies a third of the set on its own. Revision 1 read the range as evidence against a sharp threshold. It is not evidence either way.

Defects in the campaign's own flag layer

Found during execution, recorded rather than patched: the campaign froze its flag rules before launch, so changing them after seeing results would make the two halves of the record incomparable. All three are handled by the post-hoc adjudicator.

Z80A-D01 — historical event measured against final state. A run counts as having crossed if any organism reached the threshold at any validation pass, but the score reported beside it is the best among organisms alive at the end. These disagree whenever a crossing lineage later dies, which is common in contested worlds. This is why 50 endogenous instances adjudicate weak and 14 invert outright.
Z80A-D02 — the reservoir flag never checks the reservoir contributed. It fires on structure, moat, and any historical crossing, without asking whether the easy niche supplied the lineage that crossed. See the reservoir finding above for why the existing record cannot close this.
Z80A-D03 — replication evidence absent from the index. The index whitelist carries the replication rate but not the two counters that separate an evidence-backed birth from a resemblance copying never caused. The first adjudicator read them there, found nothing, and wrongly declared every spontaneity flag inadmissible. The index schema is deliberately unchanged; the adjudicator now reads the per-run record.
