# Forensic: the fragile-to-robust transition in 7ae3 / seed 16000006

Delegate for Nestor, arc 3 (npe-arc3-2026-09-28), 2026-09-28. This is computational artificial life: integer
programs on the z8 VM. Nothing biological is involved.

## Verdict: KILLED as a single heritable change. The lineage descent is real, and the robustness is real but distributed across many bytes.

- **The claim that one localized heritable change caused robustness is KILLED by the pre-registered rule**
  (`PREREG_CAUSAL.md`). The knock-in scored 0 of 5, the revert 0 of 8 (all 8 reverts were destructive), and
  the literal graft 0 of 12.
- **The "first STATE-ROBUST descendant" under the task's rule (k=1) is a measurement artefact.**
  - The D0 founders are not state-fragile in general. Their carried state cycles with period 2 to 3, so they
    copy after 0, 2 and 5 own executions and fail after 1, 3 and 4.
  - The first member that the k=1 rule labels robust (vid 320, epoch 352, 2 births from D0) is the same
    oscillation shifted in phase. It copies after k = 0, 1, 4, 5 and fails after k = 2, 3, 6. It also fails
    from CONST and from RANDOM starts.
  - The X-DD-SELFSTATE rule (rate_1 >= 0.25 rate_0) reads a single phase of this cycle.
- **Genuine robustness appears later.** By epoch 700 the lineage's modal genomes copy after every k = 1..6 and
  from 0x5A and random register starts. They descend from D0 member vid 211 through 118 to 126 accepted
  replications. They differ from every D0 genome at 49 to 54 of their 64 bytes.
  - This rules out the replacement explanation: there is no takeover by an unrelated lineage, because L_share
    is 1.0 and every path is rooted in D0.
  - The robustness cannot be put back into D0 by transplanting any single change, or even the 20-position
    consensus of the late genomes.

## Localized change and mechanism (disassembly and block-copy trace)

The copier is the same throughout:

- `LD HL,553F ; INC HL` fixes HL = 0x5540, which is the partner half's first byte mod 128.
- `LD (HL),B`, then `LDDR*` (E7 alias) at 0x18.

Only DE (destination) and BC (count) come from state (`localize2.json` traces; `corpus_analysis.trace_blockcopy`):

- **D0.** DE is never set before the LDDR.
  - FRESH gives DE = 0000 and BC = 0000. The copy is aligned (dst = src + 64 mod 128) and runs for the whole
    budget.
  - CONST gives DE = 5A5A. The copy is misaligned, so it fails.
- **vid 1005 (first FR).**
  - Bytes 0..2 = `01 00 00` = `LD BC,0000`, followed later by `INC C`. This forces the first LDDR to copy one
    byte.
  - The loop then reaches a second LDDR whose DE (xx00) it computes itself. That DE is aligned whatever the
    start state (CONST: DE = 0D00).
  - **Byte 0 is not heritable in this copier.** The descending copy writes the victim's byte 0 over the
    donor's byte 0 before copying it, so every child's byte 0 is recomputed.
  - Of 158 descendants of 1005, none kept `01` at byte 0; the child values were 00, FF, F4, 9F, and so on.
  - So the first FR genome's key bytes did not propagate.
- **Epoch-700 genomes.**
  - Bytes 20..22 = `11 00 32` = **`LD DE,3200`** immediately before `LD (HL),B ; LDDR*`. This is the
    candidate heritable change: it sets the destination explicitly and aligns it (0x3200 = 0 mod 128).
  - The trace from every start state shows HL = 4BC0 and DE = 3200. BC is taken from state (00FF, 5A59, 2307,
    46BD) and is always large enough.
  - This triplet arose at vid 4887 (byte 20 changed 00 -> 11; the victim held A2 at that position, so the new
    byte came from neither parent). It is present in all 8 modal genomes.

## Causal tests (thresholds declared in `PREREG_CAUSAL.md` before `causal.py` ran; 25 seeds x 2 sides)

**(a) Knock-in of `LD DE,3200` into the 5 D0 genomes: 0 of 5 FR, so FAIL.**
- The knock-in is not without effect:
  - RANDOM rose from 0 to between 0.32 and 0.34 in all 5 D0 genomes.
  - One D0 genome (31b094..., vid 63) became STATE_ROBUST under the k=1 rule (SELF1 0.44).
- CONST stayed at 0 in all 5, and SELF1 stayed at 0 in 4.
- The change therefore contributes part of the robustness and is not sufficient on its own.

**(b) Revert of bytes 20..22 to D0's `E2 1A E2`, or byte 20 to `00`, in the 8 epoch-700 genomes: FAIL.**
- 0 of 8 reverts count, because all 8 x 2 reverts are destructive: FRESH copying drops to 0.
- In the late background, `LD DE` is **necessary for copying at all**, so its role in robustness cannot be
  separated from its role in function.
- One of the 8 control genomes (ffff19...) itself had FRESH 0 on this ruler, although it is COMPETENT.

**(c) Cross-graft into 12 fragile competent 7ae3 DENSE genomes from other runs (q1_partial.jsonl):**
- Literal `11 00 32` before the executed block copy: 0 of 12 robust. It destroyed copying in 12 of 12.
- Adapted graft (reported only, not scored), DE = HL_at_copy + 0x40, which applies the alignment principle
  rather than the literal bytes:
  - 7 of 12 remained FUNCTIONAL;
  - 1 of 12 (4e236166...) became FR (SELF1 0.5, CONST 0.48, RANDOM 0.40).

**(d) All start states** are reported for every genome in `causal.json`. The pre-registered prediction
(CONST and RANDOM move to >= 0.25 FRESH on knock-in) held for RANDOM (5 of 5) and failed for CONST (0 of 5).

**Post-hoc test (not pre-registered;** `posthoc_multilocus.py`**).**
- Knocking the full 20-position consensus of the epoch-700 genomes into D0 gives FRESH 0.46, SELF1 0,
  CONST 0, RANDOM 0.28. This is not robust.
- Leave-one-out shows that positions 20, 21 and 45 are needed for function.
- Removing position 12 (CD back to ED) paradoxically gives STATE_ROBUST at k=1, but still not FR.
- Robustness in the late genomes depends on non-consensus, variable positions: it is distributed and depends
  on the genetic background.

## What the explanation seems to be

1. **The fragile-to-robust appearance at the D0 boundary is a ruler artefact.**
   - Under the k=1 rule a phase shift of a periodic state cycle counts as robustness.
   - The D0 founders already copy from their own state at k = 2 and k = 5.
2. **Real, stable robustness did evolve within D0's lineage.**
   - It is not sorting, replacement, or recombination with an unrelated lineage: all paths are rooted in D0,
     and victims were overwhelmingly L members by epoch 500.
   - It accumulated over about 120 accepted replications and about 50 byte changes.
   - Along the way it was lost and regained several times. Some steps were caused by non-heritable,
     state-written bytes: byte 0 is always rewritten by the copy.
3. **Its main visible ingredient is an explicit, aligned destination (`LD DE,xx00`) before the block copy.**
   - This helps: RANDOM-state robustness appears in every D0 knock-in, and the adapted graft transferred to 1 of
     12 genomes.
   - It is neither sufficient (the knock-in fails) nor separable from function in the late background (the
     revert is destructive).

## Caveats

- Genealogy and screening stop at epoch 700. Robustness at epochs 700 to 2000 was not traced; X-P2-LINEAGE
  reports robust genomes are 96 to 100% of competent after epoch 1000.
- The rates are near-deterministic (about 0.5 or about 0), so 25 seeds resolve the categories well. They do not
  resolve small effect sizes.
- The copy criterion uses a blank partner and the carried-state protocol of X-DD-SELFSTATE. The world's real
  carried states come from non-blank partners, and a newborn inherits the **victim's** registers, not its
  parent's. RANDOM and CONST are the proxies for this, and they are why FR was added.
- The world attributes a birth to the donor, but children differ from the donor at up to 3 of 64 bytes.
  These bytes are victim residue, copy error, or state-written bytes (byte 0 always). This is a small,
  uncontrolled recombination and mutation channel.
- The one-byte-per-generation copy of byte 0 means any analysis that treats byte 0 as heritable is wrong for
  this copier.
