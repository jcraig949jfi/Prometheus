## §0 Headline

The h2 black hole was never the label. **It is the mechanism.**

The structured-label refactor transmits the claim coordinates faithfully (good
hygiene), but the information *available* to transmit is ≈ 0.01 bits per kill —
because h2's evaluation (polyfit over independently-resampled catalog pairs)
has a near-constant outcome across its entire 24-cell claim space
(P(kill | pair) ∈ [0.9988, 1.0]). An *infinite* h2 ledger saturates at
**≈ 4 bits total** of predictive information about fresh evaluations. The 87K
historical kills (43.68% of corpus volume) re-measured a ≤ 4-bit object.

"44% of kill volume converted from opaque to named" (kill-topography
recommendation #2) over-promised in exactly the way Proposal E §4 feared: it
fixed the transmission layer of a channel whose source has almost no entropy
to send. Honest restatement: **44% of volume was, and remains, ≈ 0% of
information — now with better labels.**

This is reported with the same pride as a recovery, per the directive: it is a
law-grade negative, it generalizes (§3), and it converts directly into an
instrument (§5) and into substrate policy (§6).

## §2 The Information-Recovery Law

**When does finer labeling of failure RECOVER information vs merely REPAINT
the black hole?** [Working theory — analytic core proven for the
independence case; one full empirical anchor (h2); 13 code-audit
classifications awaiting their own empirical anchors]

1. **Transmission clause.** A kill label transmits information about anything
   beyond the record itself only through its *claim-coordinate* content (what
   was tested). Label components derived from the *evaluation path* (which
   methods voted, R² values, margins, agreement counts) add label entropy but
   carry **exactly zero** information about any independent re-evaluation,
   conditional on the coordinates. Finer labeling RECOVERS information iff
   the refinement adds coordinate content; path-derived refinement is
   REPAINT — it makes the black hole *look* differentiated (entropy goes up)
   while transmitting nothing the coordinates didn't.
2. **Availability clause.** Per-record available information,
   I(coordinates; fresh outcome), is a property of the generator's
   *mechanism*, not its labeling. A label refactor cannot raise it
   (data-processing inequality); it can only stop withholding it. h2's
   mechanism — independent resampling — makes the outcome a measurement of
   the instrument-catalog null, nearly constant in the coordinates:
   available ≈ 0.01 bits/kill.
3. **Accumulation clause.** A ledger's total predictive value saturates at
   Σ_cells H(outcome | cell). Volume beyond saturation re-measures known
   cells. This mechanizes kill-topography Finding 1 ("99% of kill volume is
   catalog-uniform low-information") and explains Finding 3 quantitatively:
   h2's saturation is ≈ 4 bits, so its 87K records were ≈ 87K − 24
   re-measurements.

**Scope condition (the law's boundary, found by the audit, not assumed):**
clause 1 requires evaluations independent given coordinates. Of 14 generators
audited, 13 hold the premise; **d4 violates it** (its evaluation consumes
prior records' values — the `eps` in its label is corpus-state-derived). For
state-coupled generators, path/state components *can* carry temporal
information — and their labels are not comparable across time. The auditor's
`BEYOND_COORDINATE_SIGNAL` verdict detects this boundary empirically (or
flags hidden coordinates — either way: investigate, don't celebrate).

**Corollary (lossy proxy).** A path component can act as a degraded
coordinate proxy *only when the coordinates are absent from the label*
(DPI: I(path; Y) ≤ I(coords; Y)). h2's tag is pure decoration because the
pair is present; d3's `mean_r2={:.3f}` label is the pure pathological case —
hundreds of distinct labels, zero coordinate content, the maximal repaint.

**Unified reading of the kill-topography findings:** Finding 1 (volume-driven
kills) = clause 3 saturation; Finding 2 (a3's lattice is the only real
structure) = deterministic mechanism (availability = full) + coordinate-
bearing labels (transmission = full); Finding 3 (h2 black hole) = label
starvation stacked on a near-empty mechanism. One law, three findings.

**Three label pathologies** (the failure shapes, not verdict lines):
- **Coordinate starvation** (a4, d1, h4; partially f2/f4/c1/b5): the label
  drops coordinates the payload retains. For *deterministic* mechanisms
  (most of these), each cell's first kill carries full information and
  repeats carry none — the ledger's value is the **occupancy map over
  coordinate cells**, which starved labels make unbuildable without payload
  joins. These are the cases where finer (coordinate) labeling genuinely
  recovers information. a4 is the sharp irony: h2's own parent generator has
  the same 24-pair space and emits ONE string.
- **Path decoration** (h2 tag/agr, d3, d2's raw margin): label entropy up,
  transmitted information unchanged. FP-002's top-1-share detector is
  *blind* to d3 (healthy-looking entropy) — this is a distinct primitive
  (proposed FP-004, §6).
- **State leakage** (d4): outside the law's premise; labels embed
  accumulated-state values; needs its own treatment.

**Substrate-wide invariant found:** `payload_rederivable = true` for **all
14 generators** — every kill label (including every pathological one) is a
pure function of its retained payload. The schema-retention failure Proposal
E §5 Q4 worried about **does not exist in the current substrate**. The right
move is a lint that *pins* this invariant at generator-addition time, not a
repair (§6 R4).
