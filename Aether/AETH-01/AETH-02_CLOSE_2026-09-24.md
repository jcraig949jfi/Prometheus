## 0. THE CONTROL THAT MAKES A CHEAP LATTICE LEGITIMATE

Everything below is measured at 256², so the first question is whether
256² reproduces the 2048² stationary state. It does, on every bulk
measure, once the comparison is made honestly:

| | 256² | 2048² | relative |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| write density | 0.423599 | 0.422507 | 0.3% |
| activity density | 0.191727 | 0.191052 | 0.4% |
| starved density | 0.231873 | 0.231455 | 0.2% |
| energy Gini | 0.586571 | 0.587116 | 0.1% |
| H(opcode) bits | 5.58817 | 5.5992 | 0.2% |
| edge fraction | 0.0377228 | 0.0376037 | 0.3% |
| edges ≥64 ticks (opcode) | 0.0717 | 0.0696 | 2.9% |

A separate size scan (128², 192², 256², 384², `lattice_size_scan.log`)
found per-field change rates flat to within 10% across a 9× range in site
count, so nothing here depends on the lattice being small.

**Two comparisons initially looked like failures and were my own error, not

## 1. H1 — persistence is residue, and nothing repairs it

**NOT FALSIFIED, and more decisively than predicted.**

Protocol: run to tick 2,500 tracking edge run-lengths; split the opcode
field's edges into PERSISTENT (run ≥ 64) and SHORT (run ≤ 8); from one
snapshot fork three arms. The lesion adds 1 to the source's `arg0`, which
rotates its target direction by one and leaves the source active — the
minimal edit that removes the edge without removing the writer.

| horizon | ARM_P recurrence | ARM_S recurrence | ARM_P occupancy | ARM_S occupancy | SHAM recurrence |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| +1 | 0.00000 | 0.00000 | 0.00000 | 0.03571 | 0.99554 |
| +10 | 0.00000 | 0.00446 | 0.00000 | 0.04911 | 0.96875 |
| +50 | 0.00000 | 0.00446 | 0.00000 | 0.04018 | 0.96875 |
| +100 | 0.00000 | 0.01339 | 0.00000 | 0.05357 | 0.95536 |
| +250 | 0.00000 | 0.01339 | 0.00446 | 0.05357 | 0.96429 |
| +500 | 0.00000 | 0.00446 | 0.00000 | 0.05357 | 0.92411 |

k = 224 edges per arm.

**Recurrence is zero at every horizon out to 500 ticks.** Not "lower than
control" — zero. And **occupancy is also zero**: after the lesion the
target receives no incoming edge at all, from any neighbour, for 500
ticks, against 3.6–5.4% for the short-run control. The sham arm holds at
92–99%, so untouched persistent edges do persist.

So a persistent edge is not repaired, and it is not replaced. The
prediction was "recurrence does not exceed the control"; the observation is
stronger — the site goes quiet permanently.

### 1b. Why, measured rather than inferred

H1's zero occupancy demanded an explanation, so one more observation (not
a preregistered test):

| | value |
|:--|--:|
| sites with no net change in 4 template fields over 64 ticks | **0.923** |
| mean active-writer neighbours, all sites | 0.793 |
| persistent-edge targets frozen | 0.794 |
| short-run-edge targets frozen | 0.697 |
| persistent-edge sources frozen | 0.985 |
| short-run-edge sources frozen | 0.966 |

**92% of the lattice shows no net template change over 64 ticks.** Change
is concentrated in an ~8% churning minority. A persistent uncontested edge
means one neighbour aims at a target and three do not — and in a frozen
neighbourhood those three go on not aiming there indefinitely. Re-aim the
one writer and the target has no writer at all. That is the mechanism
behind the zero, and it needed no intervention to see.

This does **not** contradict the trajectory report's finding that
persistent edges are not spatially clustered: the frozen bulk is the
*majority* of the lattice, so being frozen is not a localised cluster.

## 2. H2 — partly falsified, and the split is the finding

**The instantaneous structure is predicted almost exactly. Persistence is
not predicted at all.**

Under independence, each active site aims at one of 4 directions and one
of 5 fields, so a given neighbour targets a given (site, field) with
probability p = activity_density / 20:

| quantity | predicted | observed | ratio |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| p per neighbour | 0.0095863 | — | — |
| edge fraction | 0.0377975 | 0.0377228 | **1.002** |
| contested share of edges | 0.014402 | 0.017140 | 0.840 |
| edges with run ≥ 64 | 0.220315 | 0.071657 | **3.07** |

**Edge fraction is predicted to 0.2%, and the contested share to within
16%.** No structure-forming process needs to be posited for the
instantaneous write graph: it is what independent stationary statistics
predict.

**Persistence is over-predicted by 3.1×.** A per-tick recurrence estimated
from measured change rates gives s = 0.9763, so s⁶³ = 22.0% of edges should
hold 64 ticks. Only 7.2% do. Real edges are *less* persistent than
independence predicts, so something correlated is shortening their lives
relative to the naive model — the opposite of the "hidden stabilising
process" one might have looked for.

**Verdict.** H2 as stated — "stationary statistics predict the persistent
cohort without invoking a structure-forming process" — is **falsified for
the persistent cohort** and **confirmed for the instantaneous structure**.
The interpretation left standing is narrower than the one the trajectory
report offered: the *edge structure* is plain independence; the *lifetime
distribution* is not, and remains unexplained.

## 3. H3 — not settled

**INCONCLUSIVE at 256². Stated as such rather than resolved by assertion.**

| conditioning | state-changing share | edges |
|:--|--:|--:|
| all edges | 0.2503 | 12,340 |
| target on a cycle | 0.4927 | 205 |
| target is a writer, NOT on a cycle | 0.3310 | 1,266 |
| target is not a writer | 0.2364 | 10,869 |

Cycle enrichment 1.97×; writer-constraint enrichment 1.32×. The writer
constraint — a node can only be on a cycle if it is itself an active
writer — explains about **a third** of the lift, not most of it. Within
writers, on-cycle is still 1.49× off-cycle.

**205 on-cycle edges is too few to rest a conclusion on.** A 512² pass
with a per-field decomposition was launched and did not finish before the
session ended; it is the first thing to re-run and it costs nothing.

One result did come out clearly: **random rewiring produces 2.75× MORE
cycle nodes than reality** (517.75 mean over 8 trials against 188
observed). Cycles are *suppressed* relative to chance, not enriched — the
opposite of what a "cycles are special" reading would predict.

The mechanism worth testing next is specific and per-field. A write copies
the source's **payload** into the target's chosen field — it is not a
field-to-field copy. A node on a cycle must be an active writer, so its
opcode is `WRITE`. An incoming *opcode-field* edge is therefore same-value
only if the source's payload happens to equal `WRITE`: one byte value in
256. If the enrichment is concentrated in the opcode field, H3 is
supported and the mechanism is named; if it is spread evenly across
fields, the constraint does not explain it.

## 4. H4 — perturbation is the source of template change

**NOT FALSIFIED. The prediction was 38%; the measurement is 41.1%.**

From one stationary snapshot, a control keeping `mut_numer` and a treatment
at `mut_numer = 0`. `aeth01.v1` is unmodified; the perturbation rate is a
run parameter.

| horizon | control template change | treated | drop |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| +1 | 0.009029 | 0.005322 | **0.4106** |
| +2 | 0.009018 | 0.001335 | 0.8519 |
| +5 | 0.008904 | 0.000847 | 0.9049 |
| +10 | 0.008846 | 0.000580 | 0.9345 |
| +50 | 0.008972 | 0.000542 | 0.9396 |
| +100 | 0.008770 | 0.000519 | 0.9408 |
| +500 | 0.008842 | 0.000484 | **0.9452** |

Two things beyond the prediction:

**The energy change rate shifts by exactly 0.000000** at tick 1.
Perturbation touches only template fields, as the semantics say, and this
is the cheat control — a treatment that moved energy would mean the
ablation had done something other than what it claimed.

**It keeps falling to 94.5%.** The immediate 41% is the redundant-channel
share; the remaining decay is redundant channels ceasing to be
re-randomised. Without perturbation, template-field change in B-balanced
`aeth01.v1` very nearly stops. The trajectory report's claim that "the
redundant copy channel is the noise channel" is confirmed, and the
stronger statement is now available: it is very nearly the *only* channel.

## 5. INSTRUMENT CORRECTION TO THE TRAJECTORY REPORT

Found while building H2, which needed a per-tick change rate and got an
implausible one.

**`aeth02_runner.py` refreshes its `prev` snapshot only when it emits a
sample.** Since `SAMPLE_EVERY = 250`, every comparison against `prev` was
against the state **250 ticks earlier**, not the previous tick. That
affects `change_rate`, `change_rate_by_field`, `changed_by_field` and the
four-way edge classification — the centre of the report.

The bias was measured directly rather than argued about: one lattice, one
tick, one observer, only the reference state differs.

| quantity | per tick (correct) | 250-tick lag (as run) | bias |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| STATE_CHANGING | 25.486% | 27.349% | +7.3% rel |
| SAME_VALUE_UNCONTESTED | 73.825% | 71.865% | −2.7% rel |
| SAME_CONTESTED_ALT_CHANGE | 0.672% | 0.778% | +15.7% rel |
| change_rate | 0.164874 | 0.184784 | +12.1% rel |
| change_rate_by_field, opcode | 0.00986 | 0.02248 | **+128% rel** |
| p_s (redundant proposals) | 0.8796 | 0.8598 | −2.3% rel |
| perturbation share of change | **42.2%** | 38.0% | −10.0% rel |

The lagged column reproduces the 2048² figures almost exactly (27.08%,
72.25%, 0.18461, 0.02195), which is what identifies the defect — and
which also **validates the 256² proxy**: the §0 control comparisons that
looked like failures were a per-tick measurement held against a lagged
reference.

### Corrected headline figures

| | as reported | corrected |
|:--|--:|--:|
| STATE_CHANGING | 27.1% | **~25.5%** |
| SAME_VALUE_UNCONTESTED | 72.3% | **~73.8%** |
| redundant template proposals | 86.0% | **~88.0%** |
| perturbation share of template change | 38% | **~42%** |
| per-field template change rate | 0.020–0.024 | **~0.009** |

Every qualitative conclusion stands, and two move further in the direction
already reported. That is luck, not vindication.

### Also withdrawn

The trajectory report's §1 "observer consistency check" — that
`changed_by_field` equals `STATE_CHANGING` exactly at every sample — is
**an algebraic identity, not a check**. `edge_aggregates` computes
`(has & differs).sum()` and `edge_classes` computes `(has & changed).sum()`
from the same inputs. They cannot disagree. I presented it as validation;
it validates nothing. An exact 1.0000 ratio across hundreds of samples
should have prompted me to look for why it could not have come out
otherwise.

The defect is in the campaign runner, not in the observatory or the
kernel: `observatory/aeth01_observatory.py::change_rate` and
`aeth01_graph.py::edge_classes` both do exactly what their docstrings say.
The runner passed them the wrong operand.

## 6. WHAT AETH-02 CONCLUDES, AT THE CORRECT WIDTH

**No evidence was found that the measured persistent-edge and cycle
structures perform a demonstrated nontrivial function under the assays
run.**

That is the whole claim. It is not, and must not be quoted as, "Aether
contains no possible circuitry." The assays here can see localisation,
contest, persistence and resource routing. They cannot see a structure
that is spatially distributed, informational rather than resource-routing,
uncontested in normal operation, stateful without being self-repairing, or
dynamically reconfigurable rather than topologically persistent — and any
of those would be circuitry this round was not built to detect.

What the falsifiers add to the trajectory round:

- **persistence is not maintained** (H1: zero recurrence, zero occupancy);
- **it sits in a lattice that is 92% static** (H1b), which is the mechanism
  behind that zero;
- **the instantaneous edge structure needs no explanation beyond
  independence** (H2), so the structure is not evidence of anything;
- **the edge lifetime distribution is NOT explained by independence**
  (H2), and that remains open;
- **perturbation supplies ~42% of template change immediately and ~95%
  cumulatively** (H4), so the substrate's own copy dynamics are very nearly
  static;
- **cycles are suppressed relative to random rewiring** (H3), and whether
  the state-changing enrichment holds up against a per-field null is
unresolved.

The one genuinely open question this round leaves is **H2's persistence
gap**: real edges are 3× shorter-lived than independence predicts. Nothing
measured explains that, and it is the cheapest remaining thread.

## 7. H5 AS A CAMPAIGN-DESIGN LESSON — REGIME-SPECIFIC

What was established is narrow: *for B-balanced `aeth01.v1` and the
observables measured here, the stationary statistics were already settled
by approximately tick 2,500.*

Nothing establishes that 2,500 ticks suffices for another parameter
regime, other semantics, other observables, or a future substrate.
Assuming it would be the same class of error as carrying a throughput
figure across workloads — which is the other thing this campaign got wrong.

**The transferable lesson is the procedure, not the number: measure
time-to-information with cheap scouts before buying long trajectories.**
That procedure now exists as code (`prometheus_gpu/scout.py`) with this
campaign's 7.2% cost miss as its regression test.

## 8. LIMITATIONS

1. **H3 unresolved** at the size tested; the 512² confirmation did not
   finish. Free to re-run.
2. **H1 tested one field** (opcode) and one lesion type (re-aim the
   source). A lesion that removed the writer entirely, or that targeted
   `arg1`, was not tried.
3. **H1b is an observation, not a preregistered test.** It explains an H1
   result; it was not set up in advance to be able to fail.
4. **H2's per-tick recurrence estimate** uses change rates averaged over
   all sites, while edge sources are active writers — a biased
   subpopulation. The 3× gap is larger than that bias plausibly explains,
   but it is not corrected for.
5. **The corrected figures in §5 are measured at 256²**, not re-measured at
   2048². The lag bias is established; the corrected 2048² values are
   inferred from the 256² pair plus the §0 control.
6. **Single seed per falsifier.** The trajectory round's three-seed
   agreement supports treating these as representative; they are not
   replicated.
7. **No intervention was run on GPU**, and none was needed.
