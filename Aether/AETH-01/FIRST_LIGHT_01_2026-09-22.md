# INTERPRETATION

Statements here are my reading of section OBSERVED. They are weaker than
the measurements and should be attacked separately from them.

## I1. Regime A's death is provable, and HABITABILITY.md lacks the condition

HABITABILITY.md's `DEAD_CERTIFIED` requires *zero* WRITE opcodes
anywhere. Regime A has 7.3 million of them and is, I claim, equally
provably dead:

> If `MAINTENANCE_COST = 0` and `REPLENISH_NUMER = 0` and every site
> with `opcode = WRITE` has `energy < WRITE_COST`, then no site can emit
> a proposal; with no proposal there is no energy transfer; so no site's
> energy can ever increase; so no site can ever emit. The state is fixed
> forever.

O2's 4,249 ticks of byte-identical state is consistent with this but
does not prove it -- the proof does, and the proof is what should be
attacked. It fails if any route exists by which a site with
`energy < WRITE_COST` gains energy while no site can emit.

If it holds, it is a second certified-death precondition and such worlds
may be hard-stopped. **Proposed for HABITABILITY.md's label table; not
edited into that document here, because it is another document's text
and wants a ruling.**

## I2. The scout's scale-invariance argument survived a 1024x test

The scout argued from 16,384 sites that regime A's death is a per-site
property. At 16,777,216 sites it reproduces with the same signature
(`write_density == starved_density`). I read this as the scout tier
being predictive enough to choose paid parameters with, which is the
economically significant result of the day: the next campaign can be
aimed for $0 instead of $2.

## I3. The correlations of O4 have a mundane mechanical explanation, and I prefer it

A writer copies its own `payload` byte into a neighbour's field, so
payload values propagate to adjacent sites and neighbours become more
similar than chance. A write landing on a neighbour's `opcode` replaces
it with an essentially arbitrary byte, eroding similarity. That predicts
exactly the observed sign asymmetry and the saturation in the frozen
world.

This is tier-1 `STRUCTURAL_RESEMBLANCE` at most, and
HEREDITY_REQUIREMENTS.md is explicit that structural resemblance alone
is never replication evidence. **I am not calling it copying, state
transmission, or a heredity signal.** It is a correlation with a
sufficient boring cause.

## I4. AETH-01 is not energy-conserving in any regime

O5 shows the zero-cost regime losing 3% of its energy. The only sink
left in the law is the transfer mechanism: a source always pays the
attempted amount, only the contest winner's target is credited, losers'
amounts are destroyed, and credit is clamped to the target's headroom
(ECONOMICS.md, PHYSICS_SPEC_DRAFT.md). So the loss is a documented
mechanism, newly quantified rather than newly discovered.

Consequence: **every long AETH-01 campaign is on a clock** unless
replenishment exceeds the contest loss rate. ECONOMICS.md states no loss
rate. 3.81 units per site per 5,000 ticks at 42% activity and 50% WRITE
density is the first measurement of one, and it is specific to those
conditions, not a constant of the physics.

## I5. What the run did not find

No endogenous dynamics. Every change observed is attributable to energy
bookkeeping, injected perturbation, or arbitration contests whose winner
re-randomizes each tick because the priority hash includes the tick.
The lattice behaves as a fixed assignment graph settling to a fixed
point, eroded by a destruction process (O7) and stirred by injected
noise.

This is a null, and AETHER_DOCTRINE.md item 8 is explicit that a null is
evidence. What this round adds is that the null is now **mechanistic**:
there is a stated reason, and the reason is checkable.

## I6. What surprised me

1. **The exactness of the seed agreement** (O1). I expected replication
   to be close; three to four significant figures on every metric was
   more than I expected, and it means a single seed is nearly sufficient
   at this scale -- which inverts HABITABILITY.md's guidance of >=8
   seeds per grid point, written for 4x4 and 32x32 worlds where it is
   certainly right.
2. **That the zero-cost control leaks energy** (O5). I set regime C up
   as the boring baseline and it produced the round's most specific new
   number.
3. **That regime A ends full of writers rather than empty of them**
   (O2). I expected extinction of the WRITE population; what happens is
   universal insolvency with the population intact.
4. **How early everything settled.** 86-92% of the A and C runs observed
   a state reached in the first tenth. The brief's instinct for long
   runs is right in general and was not what these particular worlds
   needed.

# HYPOTHESES FOR NEXT ROUND

Framed as things that could be wrong, with what would settle each.

## H1. B_balanced is not converged and its endpoint is unknown

B is the only world still changing when time ran out: energy still
falling, Gini still rising, activity still decaying at tick 5,000. It is
**right-censored by run length, not by physics.**
*Test:* rerun B_balanced alone to 50,000 ticks. It is one world, about
40 minutes of A40 time, roughly $0.35. This is the single highest-value
follow-up in this document.

## H2. Long-run cross-backend determinism may not hold

Verified at tick 1 at full scale and for 7 ticks at small scale. A slow
divergence -- an accumulating CuPy reduction-order difference, say --
would be invisible to everything run so far.
*Test:* run one 512^2 world for 5,000 ticks on both backends and
compare the full digest series. Cheap, and it either closes the
brief's stability question or finds something serious.

## H3. Structure may exist below the 64-site block scale

O3's null is a statement about 64x64 block means. A structure with a
characteristic length under 64 sites would be invisible to it.
*Test:* retain a small fixed window (say 256x256) at full resolution
every 500 ticks. About 320 KB per sample; affordable.

## H4. The certified-death condition of I1 may be wrong

*Test:* adversarial reading of the transition law for any energy-gain
route available to a starved site in a world with no emitters; then a
fixture world constructed to sit exactly on the precondition.

## H5. Perturbation rate may not be the axis that matters

The scout found template churn scaling linearly with perturbation and
vanishing without it; this run used a single rate (0.1) and did not test
the axis.
*Test:* it is a scout-tier question, and the scout tier is now
demonstrably predictive (I2). Answer it for $0 before spending again.

## H6. The energy leak of I4 may dominate campaign design

If the loss rate scales with contest density, then denser worlds die
faster, and there may be an activity density above which no
replenishment rate can sustain a world.
*Test:* measure the loss rate against WRITE density and activity on the
CPU scout; it needs no GPU.
