HYPOTHESIS
  Whatever predictive signal the assay does carry is obtainable at a
  cost NEGLIGIBLE against the search it is meant to precede. The
  campaign's question is not only "does geometry predict" but "can
  Prometheus decide CHEAPLY and PROSPECTIVELY", and a ruler that costs
  as much as the search it replaces answers nothing.

NULL / COMPETING EXPLANATION
  The signal only appears at assay budgets comparable to search itself,
  in which case prospective qualification buys nothing and the honest
  recommendation is to just run the search.

INTERVENTION
  Recompute the assay at draw budgets k in {10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 400}
  edits per object, 60 objects, on the nine real search cells. For each
  k: (a) the rank correlation of the k-budget estimate with the
  full-budget (k=400) estimate -- STABILITY; (b) the rank correlation
  of the k-budget estimate with E5's measured search outcome --
  PREDICTIVE POWER at that price.

FROZEN METRIC
  ASSAY COST   = n_objects x k phenotype evaluations.
  SEARCH COST  = 800 evals x 10 targets x 15 seeds = 120,000 per cell.
  COST RATIO   = assay cost / search cost.
  E7-M1  the smallest k whose stability rho >= 0.95 against k=400.
  E7-M2  predictive rho at that k, versus predictive rho at k=400.
  E7-M3  the cost ratio at that k.

  NOTHING IS GATED. E5 and E6 already decided whether the ruler
  predicts; E7 measures only what it costs to learn what it knows.
  INSTRUCTION TO MYSELF, FROZEN: reporting an unhelpfully weak ruler as
  CHEAP is not a rescue, and the final packet must never present
  cheapness as if it were validity. A cheap ruler that does not
  transfer is a cheap ruler that does not transfer.

CONTROLS
  The three designed controls are excluded, as in E6.

WHAT THE RESULT CHANGES
  It fixes the practical clause of the final recommendation: whether a
  SUBSTRATE QUALIFICATION GATE, IF one were justified at all, would be
  affordable. Given E5/E6 this is expected to be a conditional answer,
  and it is written that way in advance.
