1. ANALYSIS -- WHAT THE TOPOLOGY RESULT DOES AND DOES NOT SAY
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 THE TASK IS ONEMAX IN DISGUISE, AND THAT IS BOTH A FEATURE AND
THE MAIN LIMIT. Score = fraction of bits matching a hidden 24-bit
target. Under XOR relabeling every target induces an IDENTICAL
landscape (unimodal, separable). Consequences:
  (a) benign: each (arm, seed) cell used a different target, but
      cross-arm comparison is unconfounded BY CONSTRUCTION -- the
      landscape is target-invariant. A reviewer should still ask;
      the answer is the XOR isomorphism.
  (b) limiting: on a unimodal separable landscape, shared
      information is almost purely helpful -- failures prune, and
      an imported better base is a strict shortcut. The design
      cannot observe the regime where shared information MISLEADS.
      Topology-1 therefore establishes the machinery and the
      easy-regime direction, not a general law of collective
      search. The interesting successor is the regime where
      failure-sharing should HURT (section 4, E1).
1.2 CEILING COMPRESSION. A3 reached 0.975 of a 1.0 ceiling; several
cells hit 1.0 exactly. The dynamic range at the top is compressed,
which UNDERSTATES the A3-A2 and A2-A1 gaps. A harder task (larger
space or budget-to-diameter ratio) would decompress the ordering.
1.3 THE SHAM ARM IS THE STRONGEST DESIGN ELEMENT. A4 carries the
full import machinery, event traffic, and tabu-set growth of A2 --
but with decoy scores. |A4-A1| = 0.019 vs |A2-A1| = 0.050. So the
A2 effect is not overhead, not extra event volume, not tabu-set
size: it is score-bearing information crossing an engine-gated
boundary. Note the sham is not perfectly inert (A4 slightly above
A1): decoy-scored candidates still enter the tabu set and prevent
re-testing, a small REAL information effect. A cleaner sham would
poison tabu too; recorded as a design refinement, not a flaw --
the pre-registered P3 asked only that A4 sit closer to A1 than A2
does, which it did.
1.4 SIBLING SYNCHRONIZATION. Within sharing arms, sibling bests
converge to identical values (visible mid-run: 0.917/0.917/0.917).
Sharing collapses sibling diversity -- efficiency bought at the
price of exploration variance. On OneMax that trade is free; on a
deceptive landscape it is exactly what should backfire. This single
observation motivates the E1 successor more than the headline
means do.
1.5 STATISTICAL POSTURE. n = 5 seeds; perm p (A2 vs A1) = 0.064.
Pre-committed as directional, and reported as such. The right
successor fix is not more seeds on a toy: it is a preregistered
effect-size band on a task family with headroom (E1/E2).
1.6 WHAT THE ENGINE ADDED SCIENTIFICALLY. Three things convention
cannot fake: (i) budgets terminated every searcher server-side --
matched-budget claims are enforced, not asserted; (ii) the sharing
topology was enforced by policy-gated crossings -- the arms differ
by what the INSTRUMENT permitted, not by what my code chose to do;
(iii) the predictions met their evidence under a prospective flag
the engine will not grant retroactively. This is the first
Prometheus experiment where the preregistration discipline was
machine-checked end to end.

3. PROPOSED SFE FIXES / NEXT-RELEASE ITEMS (prioritized)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
F1 (P2) POLICY-GATED CONTENT RETRIEVAL [DR-1]. Imports transport
   provenance only (ids + hashes; no payload; no content-GET). For
   single-client trusted-LAN use, driver-side payloads verified
   against source_hash suffice (what topology-1 did). For
   MULTI-CLIENT ecologies -- the engine's obvious destiny -- the
   importer must be able to read what it imported:
   GET /v2/worlds/{wid}/artifacts/{aid}/content, permitted iff
   that artifact is NATIVE-or-IMPORTED in {wid}. Same policy gate
   as import; no new trust surface.
F2 (P3) info_kind / sharing-policy ENUM COHERENCE [DR-2].
   SUCCESSES_ONLY policy exists; "success" info_kind does not
   (allowed: artifact/failure/hypothesis/observation). Either add
   the kind or rename/document the policy against the real enum.
F3 (P3) DUPLICATE PREDICTION-BINDING SEMANTICS [L1]. Binding the
   same prospective prediction to multiple observations is
   silently accepted (claim counting is idempotent at hypothesis
   level, which saves the accounting). Pick and document a
   contract: reject, or first-bind-wins with later binds recorded
   as replications.
F4 (P3) PER-RESPONSE RELEASE HEADER [L2]. Identity is in version/
   status bodies and commit events -- sufficient; a header on
   every response would let clients detect a hot-swap on ANY call
   (closes H-1 permanently, cheaply).
F5 (P3) REQUEST-IDENTITY IDEMPOTENCY KEYS on epistemic POSTs [L3].
   Commit itself is idempotent (the load-bearing one); an optional
   client-supplied dedup key on hypothesis/prediction/observation
   would make network-retry semantics fully boring.
F6 (perf) BATCHED EXPERIMENT CYCLE. One candidate evaluation =
   5 HTTP round-trips (commit+enqueue, claim, complete, observe,
   optional failure/artifact). Topology-1 spent most of its ~2h
   wall clock on transport. A bundled transaction (commit+enqueue+
   claim in one; complete+observe in one) or a batch-claim would
   cut ecology-experiment wall time ~3-4x without touching
   semantics.
F7 (usability) ARTIFACT LISTING per world (id, kind, info_kind,
   origin, created_seq). Topology-1 had to scan the event stream
   to discover siblings' exports; a list route is cheaper and
   less error-prone.
F8 (ops) WORLD LIFECYCLE HYGIENE: bulk terminate/archive for probe
   and campaign worlds (this arc created ~90 worlds, most now
   inert), plus an operator GC policy that never deletes ledgers.
F9 (future, sXI) BACKEND EVIDENCE BINDING: typed fields on
   experiments/observations for backend type, backend release
   identity, config hash, raw-evidence identity -- so gen-2 can
   govern gen-1 substrate experiments with machine-checked
   binding rather than opaque spec conventions. This is the bridge
   to governing D-14-class campaigns.

4. PROPOSED FUTURE EXPERIMENTS (in rough priority order)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
E1 TOPOLOGY-2: WHERE SHARING SHOULD HURT. Same design, deceptive/
   rugged task family (NK landscapes with tunable K, or a trap
   function). Preregister: as ruggedness rises, FULLY_SHARED's
   advantage shrinks and can invert (premature convergence via the
   synchronization observed in 1.4); FAILURES_ONLY degrades more
   slowly than best-adoption sharing. This turns topology-1's
   direction into a testable INTERACTION law and can falsify the
   naive metabolization reading.
E2 BUDGET x TOPOLOGY INTERACTION. Same task, budgets {10, 20, 40,
   80}: does sharing help more when budgets are tight (information
   substitutes for compute) or loose? Cheap, decompresses 1.2's
   ceiling, and yields the first quantitative exchange rate
   between shared information and budgeted compute -- a number the
   north star eventually needs.
E3 DELAYED SHARING (needs the DELAYED_SHARING policy implemented,
   currently declared-immediate): information latency as the
   manipulated variable. Prediction: value of shared failures
   decays with delay at a rate set by landscape drift -- the first
   step toward sharing economics.
E4 MULTI-CLIENT ECOLOGY (needs F1): two genuine clients (e.g.,
   Harmonia + another seat) sharing across the H5 bilateral group
   mechanism. Tests the isolation-with-consent model for real and
   is the first two-experimenter SCIENCE (not just isolation
   probes) on the instrument.
E5 GEN-2 GOVERNING A SUBSTRATE CAMPAIGN (needs F9, or disciplined
   spec conventions meanwhile): re-run a small D-14C-style slice
   with every hypothesis/prediction/observation flowing through
   gen-2 and the gen-1 Foundry as executor backend. The deliverable
   is the governance pattern itself: two instruments, neither able
   to lie about the other.
E6 HETEROGENEOUS ECOLOGY: mixed searcher strategies (climber /
   annealer / random) under one topology. Asks whether sharing
   value depends on RECIPIENT strategy -- the first step from
   topology toward division of cognitive labor.
D-14D / D-15 remain queued on the gen-1 executor's S3/S4 -- gen-2
does not unblock them and must not be used to fake them.

5. QUESTIONS FOR THE REVIEWER (genuine asks)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1 STATISTICS FOR SMALL-SEED ECOLOGIES. Seed-level permutation on
   n=5 was pre-committed as directional. For E1/E2, what is the
   right preregisterable inference frame: more seeds, seed-paired
   designs (same seed across arms -- note targets are XOR-
   isomorphic so pairing is legitimate), or preregistered
   effect-size bands with equivalence tests?
Q2 SHAM DESIGN. Should A4's decoys also poison the tabu set
   (pure-noise sham), or is preserving true tabu membership the
   better control (isolating score information specifically)? We
   read the current sham as isolating score-information; a
   reviewer may prefer both shams as separate arms.
Q3 PREREGISTRATION MIRRORING. Topology-1 kept a committed freeze
   doc (binding) plus engine-registered predictions (mirror).
   Should the engine become the ONLY preregistration channel once
   F3-F5 land, or does the git-committed human-readable freeze
   retain independent value worth the duplication?
Q4 WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THE METABOLIZATION READING? Our candidate:
   E1 showing FAILURES_ONLY <= ISOLATED on rugged landscapes at
   matched budget. Is there a cheaper or sharper falsifier?
Q5 INFORMATION ACCOUNTING. Is there a preregisterable measure of
   "shared information used" better than import counts + adopted-
   base events -- e.g., bits of score information transferred, so
   E2's exchange rate has honest units?
Q6 MULTI-CLIENT TRUST. For E4, tokens are bearer-identity on a
   trusted LAN. What minimum additions (read-only tokens? per-
   world scopes?) would a reviewer require before treating
   cross-client results as adversarially clean?
Q7 NORTH-STAR LINK. Topology results are about search ecology,
   not reasoning operations. Our view: they earn a connection only
   via the operator-court (reach expansion under matched compute)
   once acquired operations exist to share. Does the reviewer see
   a shorter honest path from sharing-topology results to the
   acquisition question -- or is the guard against premature
   connection exactly right?
