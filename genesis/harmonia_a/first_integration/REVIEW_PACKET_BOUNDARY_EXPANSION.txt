5. UNEXPECTED FINDINGS (the ones that matter)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
    - A directive can pass every durability check and still be
      non-executable (V2/I-FREEZE-EXEC). The gate we JUST built to
      prevent lost directives does not prevent broken ones.
    - The system's failures are quiet, not loud: unattested
      "evidence", duplicated worlds on retry, and a fossil anchored on
      the wrong (but real) event ALL return success. Fail-closed holds
      for malformed input; it does NOT hold for well-formed-but-wrong
      input. That is the throughput hazard.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
6. IDENTITY / PROVENANCE FINDINGS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
    - SFE is content-addressed and does NOT verify organism identity
      (A3). blob_hash == sha256:organism_id is a CLIENT assertion; SFE
      accepts corrupt bytes with a valid-looking blob_hash. The gate
      is load-bearing and unenforced by the engine -> it MUST live in
      the shared primitive, never be re-typed per caller
      (I-CLIENT-GATE-UNENFORCED).
    - Content identity vs envelope identity is clean: same organism in
      two worlds -> identical blob_hash, distinct artifact_id (A2). No
      collision. This boundary is trustworthy.
    - Causal linkage at the PEW boundary is CLIENT-asserted: PEW
      validates sfe_entry_hash shape and (event_id, entry_hash)
      existence, not that the event is the run's observation (D1). A
      wrong-but-real anchor fossilizes clean.

8. REPLAY FINDINGS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
    Replay is NOT self-contained. Recover-fossil YES; reconstruct-
    inputs NO (input + encounter seed live in the frozen directive /
    integration_record.json in the repo, not the fossil); instantiate-
    equivalent-world PARTIAL (seed_root recoverable, sharing_policy/
    budget ambient); run-same-action NO (action not in fossil).
    "Derivable while today's checkout exists" is NOT durable
    preservation (I-REPLAY-NOT-SELFCONTAINED, depends on 7).

WHAT BREAKS WHEN WE TRY TO GO FAST?
---------------------------------------------------------------------
    1. RETRIES DOUBLE WORLDS. create_world has no idempotency key
       (H2/B1). An orchestrator that retries a timed-out create silently
       runs the organism in TWO worlds -> two divergent causal chains,
       two fossils, one intended encounter. This is the first thing
       that breaks under load.
    2. EVIDENCE GOES UNATTESTED SILENTLY. Drop work_id (easy to do when
       hand-wiring 14 steps) and the observation is CLIENT_ASSERTED with
       no error (C3). At speed, unattested rows enter the record and
       only a status read would ever reveal it.
    3. FOSSILS ANCHOR ON THE WRONG EVENT. Pick any real event and PEW
       accepts it (D1). Fast hand-wiring + a loose event match =
       fossils that cite a real ledger row that is not the one that
       happened. Correlation masquerading as causation, and it passes.
    4. REPLAY DIES. The fossil lacks action/input/seed/full-config
       (sec 7-8). A run recorded today cannot be replayed from its
       fossil tomorrow without the exact repo checkout -- so "we ran
       10,000 encounters" does not mean "we can reproduce any of them".
    5. THE CLIENT BREAKS ON SHAPE DRIFT. events() returned a dict then
       a list (K4); this harness broke mid-run. Every hand-rolled
       client is one shape change from a crash.
    6. YOU LOSE TRACK. 189 unscoped worlds already (K1); no way to ask
       "which are mine, which are running, which are done".
